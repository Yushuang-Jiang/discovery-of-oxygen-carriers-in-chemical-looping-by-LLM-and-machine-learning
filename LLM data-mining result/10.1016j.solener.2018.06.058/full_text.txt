Partial substitution of B cation in La0.6Sr0.4MnO3 perovskites: A promising
strategy to improve the redox properties useful for solar thermochemical
water and carbon dioxide splitting

A B S T R A C T
Mixed perovskites, described by the formula La0.6Sr0.4Mn1−xFexO3−δ with x ranging from 0 to 1, were prepared
and tested as potential catalysts for the solar thermochemical water and carbon dioxide splitting. The increase of
iron amount promotes the reduction step according to the trend of the starting reduction temperatures; on the
other hand, the reactivity towards H2O and CO2 seems to be fostered by high Mn contents. The highest amounts
of oxygen evolved during thermogravimetric analysis and hydrogen produced during splitting tests occur to the
sample with x = 1 even if a non-monotonic trend with iron substitution is appreciated. Instead, CO amount
progressively decreases by increasing iron content. The results reported in this work indicate that the for-
mulation of bi-functional systems with two active sites, one showing good self-reduction the other good re-
activity towards splitting reactions, is the best route to prepare materials with larger intrinsic H2 and CO pro-
ductivity as well as lower reduction/oxidation temperature.
1. Introduction
Thermochemical cycles have received a renewed interest for H2
production through water splitting since more eﬃcient solar radiation
concentration systems have been developed (Rao and Dey, 2015).
Thermochemical water splitting cycles can be divided into low (LT) and
high (HT) temperature cycles (Rao and Dey, 2015). The main diﬀerence
is related to the number of steps required to complete the cycle, two for
the HT cycles, more than two for the LT cycles. Despite their high op-
erating temperatures, two-step cycles appear more promising due to
their higher theoretical eﬃciency and lower complexity (Rao and Dey,
2015).
As shown in some interesting reviews on this topic (Agraﬁotis et al.,
2015; Miller et al., 2014; Muhich et al., 2016; Rao and Dey, 2015;
Stechel and Miller, 2013), the development of a solar thermochemical
H2O and CO2 splitting process is a multidisciplinary issue involving
connecting expertise on solar receivers, chemical reactors, heat ex-
change/recovery and redox materials.
An ideal material for solar thermochemical splitting cycles should
exhibit high oxygen release at temperature as low as possible, fast H2
and/or CO production in H2O/CO2 streams and structure stability (al-
lowing re-utilization for several cycles).
Several materials were proposed, including volatile (ZnO, SnO2,
In2O3) and non-volatile oxides, the latter classiﬁed into stoichiometric
(iron oxide, ferrite, hercynite) and non-stoichiometric (undoped and
doped ceria, perovskite) oxides (Agraﬁotis et al., 2015; Ambrosini et al.,
2010; Fu et al., 2016; Gao et al., 2018, 2016; Scheﬀe and Steinfeld,
2014; Zhang et al., 2009). According to Scheﬀe and Steinfeld (2014),
volatile and melting oxides are not suitable materials for thermo-
chemical splitting cycles due to technical drawbacks related to the se-
paration from the gas phase (volatile oxides) or the need of crushing
and sieving (melting oxides). On the contrary, although non-stoichio-
metric oxides can desorb lower O2 amounts, in principle they should
show an improved structure stability and keep the same (solid) phase
during the whole cycle; as a result, they are suitable materials for
practical applications.
Several results showed that it is diﬃcult to get a material exhibiting
both good self-reducibility at low temperature and good activity to-
wards splitting reactions due to thermodynamic and/or kinetic limita-
tions (Miller et al., 2014; Yang et al., 2014). Trovarelli and co-workers
(Pappacena et al., 2017, 2016) demonstrated that compositional het-
erogeneity at the nanoscale level is beneﬁcial for the splitting process.
Due to their signiﬁcant redox properties, structure stability and
wide range of formulations, perovskites (ABO3 as general formula) were

claimed as catalysts for several reactions, such as partial and complete

Actually, the perovskite structure can host several elements among al-
kaline, alkaline earth and rare earth metals as A cation and among
several metals from the 3d, 4d, or 5d conﬁguration as B cation (Royer
et al., 2014); a huge number of compositions can be thus obtained.
LaMnO3 has received a great interest as splitting material, showing
good redox properties during reduction/oxidation cycles as well as fa-
vourable thermodynamics (Scheﬀe et al., 2013). In order to improve the
redox behaviour under the typical splitting conditions, the substitution
on the A site was explored (Cooper et al., 2015; Dey et al., 2015a,
2015b; Yang et al., 2014). Results showed that La substitution with
either Sr or Ca increased oxygen production and lowered the reduction
temperature; however, the oxidation kinetics can be negatively af-
fected.
A positive eﬀect on splitting properties of partial substitution of the
B cation with Al in the LaxSr1−xMnO3 structure was reported (Cooper
et al., 2015; McDaniel et al., 2013a, 2013b). Because Al does not exhibit
any redox properties, the improved splitting activity was related to the
crystal structure modiﬁcations. Demont et al. (2015) showed that the
partial substitution of Mn with Mg in LaxSr1−xMnO3 system enhances
CO production through CO2 splitting. This behaviour was related to the
improved thermal stability and resistance to sintering. On the contrary,
Jiang et al. (2014) studied the eﬀect of B site substitution with Mn or Co
in LaFeO3 perovskites on CO2 splitting. They found no signiﬁcant CO
production on unsupported materials, while good activity was detected
on SiO2 supported perovskites, such as LaFe0.7Co0.3O3.
The eﬀect of the partial substitution of the B cation with reducible
species was studied for energy storage applications (Babiniec et al.,
2016, 2015). Results showed that improved reducibility can be ob-
tained starting from a Mn-based perovskite by partial substitution with
either Fe or Co (Babiniec et al., 2015) and with either Al or Ti (Babiniec
et al., 2016).
In the case of splitting reaction, few works were developed to study
the eﬀect of the partial substitution of B cation with reducible elements.
Bork et al. (2015) showed that La0.6Sr0.4Cr1−xCoxO3 shows interesting
splitting performance while the systems Ba/Sr or La/Sr perovskites with
Co/Fe as B cations (Demont et al., 2014) showed lower activity than
La0.5Sr0.5MnO3. Recently, Nair and Abanades (2018) showed that the
partial substitution of A and/or B cation improves the catalyst perfor-
mances (Nair and Abanades, 2018).
According to the evidence of the literature, the emerging material
has to be formulated by an intimate mixing of diﬀerent active species at
the atomic scale, one able to produce molecular oxygen by self-reduc-
tion and the other active towards water/carbon dioxide splitting.
Following this approach, in this work, a novel strategy for the pre-
paration of materials active in the solar thermochemical splitting is
proposed. In particular, the partial substitution of manganese with iron
in La0.6Sr0.4MnO3 perovskite proved to be highly eﬀective and pro-
mising as a tool to manage and tune the redox features toward CO2/
H2O splitting reactions.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials preparation
Perovskites with La/Sr = 1.5 and variable Mn/Fe molar ratios were
prepared according to the following procedure. Aqueous solutions were
prepared by mixing stoichiometric amounts of manganese (II) nitrate
tetrahydrate, strontium nitrate, lanthanum nitrate hydrate and iron (III)
nitrate nonahydrate (Sigma Aldrich) in 75 ml bi-distilled water and
stirred for 3 h. Then these solutions were heated in a MW oven (CEM
SAM-155) up to the formation of a homogeneous gel. Gel formation was
usually obtained by heating at 90 °C under continuous stirring (Cooper
et al., 2015); in this work the use of MW heating allowed to get full
geliﬁcation in few minutes (10–15 min). Then the obtained gel was
further calcined at 1400 °C for 4 h in a MAB high temperature furnace
(heating rate: 20 °C/min). The nominal composition of the prepared
samples can be described by the general formula La0.6Sr0.4Mn1−xFexO3
(0 ≤x ≤1). The La/Sr ratio was chosen as the best value for manganite
perovskites in water splitting cycles from both experimental and theo-
retical data (Bork et al., 2017). In Table 1 the general formulas and
labels for the prepared materials are reported.
2.2. Materials characterization
The actual composition was determined by means of ICP-MS ana-
lysis using an Agilent 7500CE instrument. Results showed diﬀerences
with the nominal content within the experimental error ( ± 5%).
X-ray diﬀraction spectra were collected with a XRD diﬀractometer
PANalytical X'Pert Pro. The measurements were carried out with a step
size of 0.02° and a counting time of 80 s per step.
Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) transmittance spectra were re-
corded in 400–4000 cm−1 using a Nicolet 5700 FT-IR spectrometer.
2.3. TG analysis
TG analysis was performed in a TGA/DSC TA Instrument Q600SDT,
sketched in Fig. 1. 30 mg sample were placed in an alumina crucible.
Each sample was pre-treated in air at 1200 °C (heating rate: 20 °C/min)
in order to remove the chemisorbed CO2 (Di Benedetto et al., 2013) and
then cooled down to room temperature. After a 1 h purging under a
nitrogen ﬂow, the sample was heated up to 1350 °C (heating rate:
20 °C/min) under a nitrogen ﬂow (0.1 l(STP)/min) and kept for 20 min
at 1350 °C. Then it was cooled down to 1000 °C and the atmosphere was
switched to CO2, followed by an isothermal step at 1000 °C for 50 min
(oxidation step).

3. Results
3.1. Characterization of the materials (XRD, FTIR, SEM)
Fig. 3a shows XRD proﬁles of the studied perovskites. In LSM
sample a single phase with a tetragonal structure (JCPDS 01-089-0786)
was detected.
The coexistence of both tetragonal and orthorhombic structures
(JCPDS 01-089-0786 and JCPDS 01-088-0061) was revealed for LSMF-
0.2, whereas single rhombohedral perovskite phase was ascertained for
the LSMF-0.4 and LSMF-0.6 compositions ((JCPDS 00-051-0118).
In pure LSF sample both rhombohedral (JCPDS 01-082-1963) and
tetragonal (JCPDS 01-071-1745) perovskite structures were identiﬁed;
furthermore, other peaks appear revealing formation of SrLaFeO4 phase
(JCPDS 29-1305).
In the range 2θ = 32–34° (Fig. 3b) the peaks appear shifted towards
lower angles with increasing the Fe-content. This eﬀect is related to an
increase of the volume of the unit cell due to the partial substitution of
Mn4+ by Fe3+ which exhibits larger ionic ratio (0.640 and 0.645 Å,
respectively).
The average crystalline size, evaluated by Sherrer formula, was
about 15 nm in the LSM, LSMF-0.2, LSMF-0.4 and LSMF-0.6, whereas it
was lower in the LSF sample (about 5 nm).
Fig. 4 shows FTIR spectra of LSM, LSMF-0.6 and LSF samples. The
band at 3420 cm−1 is typical of OeH stretching vibration mode of
physically absorbed water (Silvestri et al., 2012). In addition, the broad
band at 600 cm−1 (highlighted in the red box of Fig. 4) can be assigned
to the stretching of MeeO (Me = Fe, Mn) bonds in perovskite structure
(Bernard et al., 2004). This band shifts to higher wavenumbers in
LSMF-0.6 sample, since the strength of FeeO bonds (407 ± 1 kJ/mol)
is larger than that of MneO bonds (362 ± 25 kJ/mol) (Luo, 2007).
Furthermore, this band splits into two peaks in LSF sample; in particular
the band at 630 cm−1 is usually assigned to stretching vibrations of Fe-
O bonds in FeO4 tetrahedra (Andronache and Racolta, 2014), thus

3.2. TG analysis
TG measurements in N2 atmosphere were performed to assess O2
evolution at temperatures lower than 1400 °C. Fig. 5 shows the weight
loss proﬁles (Fig. 5a) and the corresponding derivatives (Fig. 5b) of the
samples pre-treated in air. TG curve of LSM shows a decrease starting at
about 800 °C, in agreement with the literature results (Demont et al.,
2014). On the other hand, from the TG proﬁles it can be drawn that LSF
is the most reducible catalyst, showing both the lowest reduction
temperature (signiﬁcant O2 evolution starting at about 400 °C) and the
highest O2 production, in agreement with Demont et al. (2014). As
displayed in Fig. 5a, mixed perovskites show a qualitative behaviour
like LSM rather than to LSF. For the samples with x = 0.4 and 0.6 the
main reduction starts at quite higher temperatures than that detected
for LSM. However, as displayed in Fig. 5b, LSMF samples show at low
temperature a small reduction phenomenon, more evident by in-
creasing the iron content.
As reported in Table 2 the overall O2 released amount is comparable
with those reported in the literature (Demont et al., 2015, 2014; Dey
et al., 2015b; Jiang et al., 2014; McDaniel et al., 2013a, 2013b), thus
suggesting that our materials are comparable with perovskites prepared
by other research groups. The largest oxygen production was obtained
on LSF, while for the mixed perovskites a lower oxygen production was
observed. According to literature results, the reduction path and the
catalytic activity of Mn-based perovskites are related to the abundance
of Mn4+ species, also causing an oxygen non-stoichiometry (Ciambelli
et al., 2000; Irusta et al., 1998; Oliva et al., 2012; Ponce et al., 2000;
Rossetti et al., 2009; Royer et al., 2014). In the LSMF materials with low
iron content (x ranging from 0.2 to 0.4) the reduced amount of released
oxygen could be ascribed to the preferential substitution of Fe3+ ions to
Mn4+ species, thus decreasing the overall reduction behaviour.
As reported in the Section 2.3, reduced samples were exposed to
CO2 after cooling down to 1000 °C. The subsequent weight increases
were addressed to the materials re-oxidation and simultaneous CO
production. As shown in Fig. 5, all samples show partial re-oxidations,
that are however associated to signiﬁcant CO production for LSM,
LSMF-0.2, LSMF-0.4. In Table 2 the corresponding CO amount are re-
ported; CO production shows a progressive decrease as Fe3+ ions are
substituted to Mn3+/Mn4+ species in the B sites of the ABO3 perovskite
structure. Accordingly, in CO2 atmosphere mixed perovskites show re-
oxidation properties intermediate between those of the LSF and LSM
samples. The re-oxidation degree (Table 2) decreases from 67% for LSM
down to 29% for LSF, in agreement with the higher activity of man-
ganese based perovskites towards the oxidation step (Demont et al.,
2014).

Fig. 7 shows the H2 proﬁles obtained during the ﬁrst (Fig. 7a) and
the second (Fig. 7b) splitting cycles on LSMF-x pre-reduced samples.
LSM shows two well-deﬁned peaks at 500 °C and 900 °C, while oxida-
tion of LSF occurs in the range 600–800 °C (maximum is detected at
about 750 °C with a shoulder at lower temperature). The LSMF-0.2
sample shows three H2 peaks at about 500, 700 and 900 °C, respec-
tively, suggesting the co-presence of sites resembling those of the pure
perovskites (LSM and LSF). However, the peak temperatures related to
LSM sites slightly increase, while those associated with iron sites de-
crease. By increasing the iron content, peak temperatures further shift
to higher values, lower temperature peak progressively disappears and
intermediate oxidation peak signiﬁcantly increases. Therefore, while
the H2 production steadily increases by increasing the Fe content,
oxidation (i.e. lower oxidation temperatures) is favoured by the Mn
content.
H2 proﬁles obtained during the second H2O splitting tests (Fig. 7b)
show few diﬀerences with respect to those reported in Fig. 7a. In par-
ticular, samples LSF, LSMF-0.6, and LSMF-0.4 show better deﬁned H2
production peaks, suggesting a more homogeneous sites distribution.
The atomic oxygen amounts released from the samples (calculated
as H2 consumption) during pre-treatment and those adsorbed during
H2O splitting tests (calculated as H2 production) in two successive cy-
cles are shown in Fig. 8, together with the atomic oxygen amounts
evolved in the TGA tests and gained during the TGA in CO2. Table 3
gives the H2 amounts evolved during H2O splitting tests and the cor-
responding re-oxidation degrees. Even if more tests are needed to assess
the repeatability and thus the stability of these materials, results of
repeated experiments are in good agreement.
As shown in Fig. 8, the overall oxygen amount evolved/leaved
during pre-treatment/H2O splitting are signiﬁcantly higher than those
obtained in the TGA experiments. However, the same trend is obtained
as function of the material composition. This suggests that the com-
parison among the diﬀerent samples can be carried out despite the
overestimation of the H2 production.
All the samples show an incomplete re-oxidation during the ﬁrst
cycle (oxygen evolved during the ﬁrst pre-treatment is higher than that
leaved during the ﬁrst H2O splitting test), suggesting that water does
not react with all the reduced sites, in agreement with previous lit-
erature results (Demont et al., 2014) and our results in CO2 atmosphere.
However, this diﬀerence decreases by increasing the Fe content, as
underlined by the re-oxidation degrees (i.e. the ratio between the hy-
drogen produced during the splitting test and that consumed during the
pre-treatment) given in Table 3. Re-oxidation degrees of the second
cycles are higher for all samples; interestingly, a complete re-oxidation
is obtained for samples with Fe fraction starting from 0.4, suggesting a
more repeatable behaviour of these materials.

further investigated.
These results suggest that the good strategy for developing pro-
mising STS catalysts is to formulate materials with two diﬀerent active
sites, the former showing good self-reduction (i.e. LSF), the latter good
reactivity towards splitting (i.e. LSM).
4. Conclusions
Perovskites with La and Sr as A cations (La/Sr = 1.5) and with Mn
(very active towards splitting reactions as LaxSr1−xMnO3) and Fe
(showing good self-reduction properties as LaxSr1−xFeO3) as B cations
were prepared to study the eﬀect of the Mn/Fe ratio on their mor-
phological, structural and redox catalytic properties.
Structural characterization of the calcined materials revealed that
all of them have the typical perovskite structure with the additional
appearance of a tetragonal SrLaFeO4 phase only in the sample without
manganese. The structural features of the samples move from those
corresponding to La0.6Sr0.4MnO3 towards those corresponding to
La0.6Sr0.4FeO3 by increasing the substitution degree of manganese with
iron.
TG analysis showed that both starting reduction temperature and
oxygen produced through self-reduction follow a non-monotonic trend
with iron content. This could be related to a preferential substitution of
Mn4+ with Fe3+. On the other hand, H2O splitting tests showed that
reactivity is higher and starting oxidation temperature is lower on
samples with larger Mn amount. H2 production as a function of the iron
content shows the same trend detected during TG experiments.
Repeated tests showed good reproducibility especially on samples with
high iron content.
CO2 shift tests in TG show that all samples undergo only partial re-
oxidations, but with signiﬁcant CO production, that progressively de-
creases with Fe content.
Results of the characterization, TGA and H2O splitting tests com-
bined with structural evidences prove that structural features as well as
reduction/oxidation properties can be tuned by opportunely changing
the nature and amount of B cations.
Therefore, an improved CO/H2 production at lower temperature can
be obtained modulating the relative amount of Mn3+/Mn4+ and Fe3+
at B site in the material. This opens the way to design materials with
larger intrinsic CO/H2 productivity and lower reduction/oxidation
temperatures.
This also suggests that the redox properties of perovskite materials
can be adapted to the features of the reactor and of the sunlight con-
centrating system.
Further insights into the mechanism of self-reduction, H2O/CO2
oxidation and surface-to-bulk and bulk-to-bulk oxygen transfer would
be useful in order to deepen the knowledge of the features providing
good performance for splitting cycle. However, this study is beyond the
aim of this work and will be further reported.
