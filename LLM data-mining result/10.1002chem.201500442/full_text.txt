Ln0.5A0.5MnO3 (Ln=Lanthanide, A= Ca, Sr) Perovskites Exhibiting
Remarkable Performance in the Thermochemical Generation of
CO and H2 from CO2 and H2O
Sunita Dey, B. S. Naidu, and C. N. R. Rao*[a]
Abstract: Perovskite oxides of the Ln0.5A0.5MnO3 (Ln=lan-
thanide, A=Sr, Ca) family have been investigated for the
thermochemical splitting of H2O and CO2 to produce H2
and CO respectively. The amounts of O2 and CO produced
strongly depend on the size of the rare earth ions and al-
kaline earth ions. The manganite with the smallest rare
earth possessing the highest distortion and size disorder
as well as the smallest tolerance factor, gives out the max-
imum amount of O2, and, hence, the maximum amount of
CO. Thus, the best results are found with Y0.5Sr0.5MnO3,
which possesses the highest distortion and size disorder.
Y0.5Sr0.5MnO3 shows remarkable fuel production activity
even at the reduction and oxidation temperatures as low
as 12008C and 9008C, respectively.
The solar thermal method of splitting H2O and CO2 to generate
H2 and CO provides an important alternative route to generate
H2 and CO, which are of great relevance to our energy and en-
vironmental needs. The two-step thermochemical treatment to
split H2O and CO2 by using oxide materials has been of interest
in this context.[1,2] The basic reactions involved in the thermo-
chemical splitting of CO2 and H2O are the following endother-
mal (reduction) and exothermal (oxidation) reactions of the
oxide.

Oxides that readily lose oxygen on heating (reduction) and
get reoxidized reversibly on reaction with H2O and CO2 (oxida-
tion) are ideally suited for such a process.[3] An important oxide
that has been investigated for this purpose is CeO2.[4,5] Howev-
er, the amounts of CO or H2 produced by the thermochemical
process using ceria have not been satisfactory. Thus, CeO2 pro-
duces about 105 mmolg¢1 of CO and 144 mmolg¢1 of H2 at the
reduction and oxidation temperatures of 1400 and 10008C, re-
spectively.[6]
Recently,
perovskite
oxides
of
the
type
La1¢xSrxMnO3 and La1¢xSrxFeO3 are reported to give rise to im-
proved generation of CO and H2 and the results are encourag-
ing.[7–11] McDaniel et al.[12] have found an improved production
of fuel using Sr- and Al-doped LaMnO3 in comparison with
CeO2. Further improvement has been obtained by using
the
La1¢xCaxMnO3
family
of
perovskites.[13]
For
example,
La0.5Ca0.5MnO3
shows
much
superior
performance
than
La0.5Sr0.5MnO3 and CeO2, producing 525 mmolg¢1 of CO at the
reduction and oxidation temperatures of 1400 and 11008C, re-
spectively. Since the properties of the rare earth manganites
(Ln0.5A0.5MnO3, Ln=lanthanide, A=Sr, Ca) are strongly depend
on the size of the rare earth ion,[14,15] we considered it is impor-
tant to investigate the performance of various members of the
rare earth manganites family, by varying the size of the rare
earth ion. With this purpose, we have employed two series of
perovskite oxides, Ln0.5Sr0.5MnO3 and Ln0.5Ca0.5MnO3 (Ln=La,
Nd, Sm, Gd, Dy, and Y) and find that the amount of O2 evolved
and the amount of CO produced depends strongly on the size
of the rare earth ion. The manganite containing the smallest
rare earth ion, such as yttrium, shows the highest distortion
and size disorder having smallest tolerance factor, gives out
the maximum amount of O2 and, hence, the maximum
amount of CO. Furthermore, the reaction temperature can be
substantially reduced by using the smaller lanthanide ion for
producing CO and H2 from CO2 and H2O. Thus, Y0.5Sr0.5MnO3
shows remarkable fuel production activity even at reduction
and oxidation temperatures of 1200 and 9008C, respectively.
Ln0.5Sr0.5MnO3 (LnSM50) and Ln0.5Ca0.5MnO3 (LnCM50) perov-
skites crystallize in the orthorhombic structure except LaSM50
which adopts the rhombohedral structure (Figure S1 in the
Supporting Information). The Bragg peaks shift to higher
angles as the size of the rare earth ion decreases (Figure S2 in
the Supporting Information). The morphology and composi-
tions of the manganites were also checked by field emission
scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) and energy-dispersive
X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis, respectively, as shown in the
case of YSM50 and YCM50 in Figure S3 and Table S1 in the
Supporting Information. The as-synthesized materials are com-
posed of micron-sized particles, as observed from FESEM
images. The BET surface areas of the samples are less than
1 m2g¢1.

we have investigated the water splitting of these materials.
The O2 as well as the H2 evolution kinetics of YSM50 and
YCM50 were measured using a gas chromatograph. The

Experimental Section
Synthesis and characterizations: All the LnSM50 compounds
were synthesized by solid-state reactions of a mixture of stoichio-
metric quantities of Ln2O3, SrCO3, and MnO2. Ln2O3 was heated at
9508C for 12 h prior to weighing. These precursors were mixed,
ground well, and the powders transferred in an alumina crucible
and heated at 10008C for 2 h and 14508C for 8 h. Intermediate
grinding was carried out after every 4 h of heating. CaCO3 was
used as a precursor instead of SrCO3 to synthesize LnCM50, keep-
ing the other reaction conditions the same. Phase identification
was performed by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) analysis with
a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer using Cu Ka radiation. FEI
Nova Nanosem 600 was used to perform the Energy Dispersive X-
Ray analysis (EDAX). BET measurements were carried out in
Quanta-chrome Autosorb instrument at 77 K from N2 absorption.
Reactivity tests: Thermal reduction and CO2 splitting experiments
were performed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) with a Perki-
nElmer Pyris1 instrument. About 100 mg sample was introduced in
a platinum crucible and kept inside the furnace chamber. The
sample was heated to 14008C at a constant heating rate of
208Cmin¢1 under continuous Ar (99.9999% grade) flow of 40 sccm
and maintained for 45 min. To perform CO2 splitting, 40% CO2
mixed with Ar was injected inside the furnace chamber after cool-
ing the temperature down to11008C. The quantification of O2 and
CO produced was done by calculating the change in mass (Equa-
tion (S1) and (S4) in the Supporting Information). The water split-
ting activity of YSM 50 and YCM 50 was investigated using an ex-
perimental set up described previously.[13] About 500 mg of the
sample was placed in an alumina crucible and pushed inside a tub-
ular furnace. The sample temperature was ramped up to 14008C
(rate 108Cmin¢1) and maintained under inert atmosphere until the
reduction was complete . The furnace was cooled down to 11008C
and steam injected along with continuous flow of inert gas to per-
form the H2 generation step. The detection and measurement of
evolved gases were carried out with a gas chromatograph (Perki-
nElmer Clarus ARNEL 580) equipped with a thermal conductivity
detector (TCD). Numerical integration of molar flow rate-time
curves gives the total amount of O2 evolved, whereas the total
amount of H2 was calculated by the water displacement method.
