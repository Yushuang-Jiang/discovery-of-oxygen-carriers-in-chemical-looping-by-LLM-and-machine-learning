A B S T R A C T   
In this study, two Ca/Mn/Fe perovskite oxygen carriers (i.e., CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ and CaMn0.75Fe0.25O3−δ, 
respectively) were prepared using red mud and two pure reagents to recycle the red mud for the chemical 
looping combustion of biomass. The oxygen uncoupling (OU) capacity and redox cycle characteristics of pe­
rovskites were examined by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under a gaseous (H2, O2, N2) environment. With 
the same Mn/Fe mole ratio, reagent-based perovskites exhibited a higher total oxygen transport capacity, while 
perovskites derived from red mud exhibited a better cycling stability. The addition of red mud did not affect the 
reactivity of perovskites. The curves of conversion level vs time revealed that two control mechanisms are 
involved in the reaction between oxygen carriers and gaseous compounds. That is, the oxidation kinetics of 
perovskite oxygen carriers were mainly controlled by a chemical reaction, while the reduction kinetics were 
controlled by a chemical reaction at a low conversion level, and with the increase in the reduction degree, gas 
diffusion through the solid product layer was enhanced. In addition, kinetic parameters of OU and redox were 
obtained, and the grain model was considered to be suitable to describe reduction and oxidation reactions.   
1. Introduction 
Currently, CO2 emission reduction is a common global problem, and 
chemical looping combustion (CLC) technology is considered to 
demonstrate immense application potential for cost-effective CO2 cap­
ture [1]. In CLC technology, an oxygen carrier (OC) is used to flow be­
tween the fuel reactor (FR) and air reactor (AR) to continuously transfer 
the heat and lattice oxygen required for the reactions, and fuel com­
bustion is conducted in the absence of nitrogen [1,2]. Generally, OC 
performance is the key factor that affects CLC efficiency. For decades, a 
number of studies on the properties of Fe-based OCs [3,4], Mn-based 
OCs [5,6], Cu-based OCs [5,7], Ni-based OCs [8], and other OCs have 
been conducted. Most OCs exhibit good reactivity in the initial stage of 
redox cycles; however, with sintering, phase separation and carbon 
deposition occur; hence, the OC activity decreases significantly [9,10]. 
In addition, solid fuels such as coal are fired in the FR, and some un­
converted char particles are entrapped by the OC flow to the AR, thereby 
reducing the carbon capture efficiency [11,12]. 
To solve the above problems, perovskite-composite OC with an 
oxygen uncoupling (OU) property have attracted increasing attention. 
The formula of the perovskite structure is ABO3, which exhibits better 
stability in a wide range of oxygen partial pressures compared with 
those of other composite OCs [13]. Lyngfelt et al. [14] have proposed 
chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) and reported that OC 
can release O2(g) in an inert atmosphere and react with fuel directly, 
thereby improving the fuel conversion efficiency. Therefore, CLOU is 
considered to be particularly suitable for solid-fuel CLC processes [15]. 
Several studies have reported that Cu-based [5,16] and Mn-based 
[5,17,18] metal oxides exhibit more suitable OU thermodynamics. For 
example, CaMnO3−δ perovskite OC, where δ is the oxygen vacancy, ex­
hibits a good OU property, high reactivity with reducing gases, and 
cycling stability in experimental tests. By doping different metal ele­
ments on the B site, the reactivity and stability of perovskites can be 
improved further [19,20]. The CLC of CaMn0.5Ti0.375Fe0.125O3−δ [21] 
and CaMn0.9Mg0.1O3−δ [22] perovskites has been investigated. Perov­
skite OC exhibits immense development prospects in the near future. 
The preparation cost of OC should be considered while ensuring its 
good reactivity characteristics, which is crucial to scaling up CLC from 
the laboratory level to the industrial level. Currently, several studies

Chemical impregnation was employed to prepare CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-

dry mixture was subjected to calcination in air at 900 ◦C for 6 h in a 
muffle furnace to obtain CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-red mud OC with a perovskite 
structure. The red mud was replaced with Fe2O3 powder, and the above 
procedure was repeated, affording CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-lab perovskite OC.

Corporation, Japan) patterns of CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-red mud and

characteristic peak. Two types of CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-red mud were 
composed of not only the above perovskite phases but also the calcium- 
aluminosilicate phase, which was attributed to the presence of Si and Al 
in the red mud. Hence, red mud can be used as raw material to prepare 
perovskite OC.

2.3. Experimental setup 
Temperature-programmed reduction (TPR) was first employed to 
determine the total oxygen transport capacity of each perovskite OC. 
Under 5 vol% H2/N2, samples were heated from 313 K to 1173 K at a 
rate of 10 K/min. Then, the temperature was maintained for 25 min to 
ensure that the sample achieved a fully reduced state. After TPRs, redox 
cycles at a constant temperature (1173 K) were conducted to evaluate 
the cycling stability of each perovskite OC. A cycle reaction was divided 
into three stages: 1. The reduced sample was oxidized under 21 vol% 
O2/N2 for 10 min to obtain lattice oxygen; 2. High-purity N2 was passed 
during TGA for 10 min to prevent the reaction between O2 and the 
subsequent reducing gas; 3. Next, 5 vol% H2/N2 was introduced again 
for 25 min to complete a single redox cycle. 
For the oxygen uncoupling kinetics test, under 21 vol% O2/N2, each 
sample was first heated from 313 K to 1173 K at 20 K/min. Then, the 
final temperature was maintained for 25 min to ensure that the perov­
skites achieved a stable oxidated state at 1173 K. Next, four OU and 
regeneration cycles were conducted at 1173 K, with the release of 
gaseous oxygen in high-purity N2 for 25 min, followed by the oxidation 
of the sample under 21 vol% O2/N2 for 25 min again.

4.1. Oxygen transport capacity 
4.1.1. Redox properties 
The initial weight loss of the four samples in the TPR test (the 1st 
cycle) was greater than those in the 2nd and 3rd cycles, but all samples 
did not achieve complete regeneration (ω = 100 %), as shown in Fig. 3. 
Abad et al. [32] also have reported that CaMn0.775Ti0.125Mg0.1O2.9−δ 
perovskite OC can be fully regenerated in the low reduction state (ω >
95 %); however, with the decrease in the ω value, the oxygen transport 
capacity of OC exhibited a downward trend, possibly related to the 
change in the perovskite structure in OC. 
This phenomenon indicates that in the process, deep reduction is 
observed, and perovskite OC exhibits good cyclic stability, albeit the 
effective oxygen transport capacity decreases. 
Therefore, the mass change in the TPR test is defined as the total 
oxygen transfer capacity of fresh OC. Please check ROC,t (fresh) in 
Table 4. ROC,t (fresh) was greater than the theoretical value ROC,t 
calculated by Eqs. (7) and (8). For example, the ROC,t (fresh) values of 
CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ-lab and CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ-red mud were 13.961 % 
and 10.989 %, respectively, but the ROC,t values were 5.405 % and 
4.076 %, respectively. This result is related to the fact that the ther­
modynamical data of CaMnO3−δ’ used for theoretical calculation do not 
include Fe-based oxides, while Fe-based oxides can provide lattice ox­
ygen as active components. In addition, the ROC,t (fresh) of the red mud- 
based OC was less than that of the lab-based OC due to the presence of 
inert carriers such as Al and Si oxides. The curve of ω in the 2nd and 3rd 
cycles was considered to be the stable redox cycle state of OC at 1173 K. 
The total oxygen transfer capacity of OC at this stage was defined as ROC, 
t (stable), as shown in Table 4. These values are further used to analyze 
and construct the redox reaction kinetics model.

4.1.2. Oxygen uncoupling and regeneration properties 
All samples exhibited a slight mass loss during gradual heating up to 
1173 K in air (Fig. 4(a) and (b)) because the oxygen vacancy increased 
with the increase in the temperature, and O2 (g) was released [32]. 
Then, with the increase in the number of cycles, the OU capacity of each 
sample decreased first and finally became stable: Two types of 
CaMn0.75Fe0.25O3−δ perovskites were even more obvious; for example, 
compared with that in the 1st cycle, the weight loss of 
CaMn0.75Fe0.25O3−δ-lab and CaMn0.75Fe0.25O3−δ-red mud in the 4th 
cycle decreased by 41.5 % and 36.9 %, respectively, while that of 
CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ-lab and CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ-red mud decreased by 19.8 
% and 5.9 %, respectively. This phenomenon may be caused by the 
disintegration of the perovskite phase into the Ca-Mn mixed oxide salt or 
the phase without OU ability under harsh reduction conditions, leading 
to the decline in the OU capacity [33]. The decrease trend of the two 
types of CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-red mud OC was less than that of the 
CaMnxFe1−xO3−δ-lab OC. Hence, as the raw material for preparing Ca/ 
Mn/Fe perovskites, red mud can significantly improve the cycling sta­
bility of the OU reaction, which may be related to the inert components 
in red mud. 
To ignore the effects of the OC structure changes, the 1st cycle was 
analyzed to obtain the experimental value of the OU capacity (please 
check ROC,ou (fresh) in Table 4), and ROC,ou (fresh) was similar to the 
theoretical value ROC,ou. The rate of OU was extremely low because the 
driving force (CO2,eq −CO2) decreased significantly [32]; hence, the inert 
period is not prolonged further. The stable OU capacity ROC,ou (stable) 
can be obtained in the 4th cycle. 
4.2. Reaction conversion analysis 
To analyze the reaction kinetics model of the redox reaction and OU

5. Models 
It is imperative to design reactors and determine the residence time 
of OC in reactors according to the reaction characteristics of OC and 
kinetics models; as a result, kinetics models of the perovskite reaction 
are determined.

6. Conclusions 
In this study, red mud was used as a raw material to prepare two 
types of OC perovskites (i.e., CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ-red mud and 
CaMn0.75Fe0.25O3−δ-red mud, respectively). The physical and chemical 
properties were compared with the perovskites prepared by laboratory 
reagents (CaMn0.5Fe0.5O3−δ-lab and CaMn0.75Fe0.25O3−δ-lab). The redox 
and oxygen uncoupling kinetics of these perovskites OC were 
determined.

The total oxygen transport capacity ROC,t and oxygen uncoupling 
capacity ROC,ou of the red mud-based perovskites were less than those of 
the lab-based perovskites, which may be related to inert carriers such as 
Al2O3 in red mud. However, the red mud-based perovskites exhibited 
better stability in redox cycles and oxygen uncoupling reactions. The 
results confirmed that, as a type of solid waste, red mud can become a 
suitable material for the preparation of OC perovskites for subsequent 
application to chemical looping combustion. 
The grain model was used to determine redox and oxygen uncou­
pling kinetic parameters. Kinetics for reduction and oxidation were 
mainly controlled by the chemical reaction during the initial reaction 
period. However, a slow reaction stage controlled by gas diffusion 
through the product layer occurred with the increase in the conversion 
level. The activation energy Echem for the reaction of red mud-based 
perovskites with 5 vol% H2 and 21 vol% O2 controlled by the chemi­
cal reaction stage was similar to that for the lab-based perovskites. The 
grain model modified with Ψ, corresponding to the ratio of the con­
version level at the fast reaction stage, accurately predicted the con­
version curves for all reaction types.
