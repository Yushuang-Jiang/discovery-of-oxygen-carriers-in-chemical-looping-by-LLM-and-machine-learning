reactivity of La-based perovskites. By maintaining the fuel (glycine) to metal nitrate ratio ( ) = 1, the LaMO3

1. Introduction
Thermochemical conversion of thermodynamically stable H2O/
CO2 into syngas (H2/CO mixture) by using redox materials and con-
centrated solar power has emerged as one of the effectual approaches
for the generation of renewable liquid fuels [1–4]. This process is
beneficial as it aids to lessen the greenhouse effect by commendably
upgrading the CO2 into synthetic fuels and provides a sustainable al-
ternative for the consumption of fossil fuels [5].
To be a favorable candidate for the thermochemical redox cycle, the
essential properties the metal oxides should possess includes higher and
sustained levels of O2 evolution (formation of a large number of O2
vacancies) and CO production in multiple cycles, faster reaction ki-
netics, highest solar-to-fuel energy conversion efficiency, and long term
thermal stability. In pursuit of the best redox material, numerous vo-
latile and non-volatile (both stoichiometric and nonstoichiometric)
metal oxides such as ZnO [6–9], SnO2 [10–12], ceria [13–20], ferrites
[21–29], perovskites [30–35], and others [36–38] have been in-
vestigated. Among these, ceria based redox materials are competent of
producing consistent amounts of O2 and CO in successive cycles with a
faster reaction kinetics. Alike ceria, perovskite-based redox materials
(ABO3) also displayed encouraging results towards splitting H2O/CO2
thermochemically. The redox reactions involved in a perovskite-based
thermochemical cycle are as follows:

The preceding studies indicate that analogous to the ceria-based ma-
terials, the redox properties of the perovskites upgraded via integrating
various combinations of the metal cations in the perovskite crystal
structure. Doping of the metal cations appreciably influences the O2 re-
leasing and H2O/CO2 splitting capability of the perovskites. Additionally,
the doped perovskites were also competent of being thermally reduced at
lower temperatures as compared to ceria-based materials.
Scheffe et al. [39] have explored the La-Sr-Mn-O perovskites by
varying the composition of Sr as 0.3 and 0.4 and reported favorable
redox outcomes with better solar-to-fuel energy conversion efficiency.

Similar to the study mentioned above, the effect of variation in the Sr
composition in the La-Sr-Mn-O perovskite on the water-splitting ability
was thermodynamically and kinetically investigated by Yang et al. [40]
and the obtained results indicate that the composition of Sr in the range
of 0.4–0.6 is suitable for the elevated amounts of fuel production. Dey
et al. [41] reported that the La-Sr-Mn-O perovskite and the Y-Sr-Mn-O
perovskite possess higher fuel production capacity and better redox
performance as compared to the ceria and Zr-doped ceria materials.
Ezbiri et al. [42] investigated the performance of the (La-Sr)-(Mn-Al)-O
perovskite and (La-Ca)-(Mn-Al)-O perovskite towards H2O/CO2 split-
ting reactions by varying the compositions on the A site. Based on the
obtained results, these authors have concluded that these two per-
ovskites are suitable for the H2O/CO2 splitting reactions at reduced
operating temperatures. McDaniel et al. [43] indicated that the Sr- and
Mn-doped La-Al-O perovskites were capable of producing a consider-
able amount of H2 and CO at lower temperatures (approximately by
300 °C) as compared to the ceria.
Multiple researchers have reported that, by employing the Ca-, Sr-,
and Al-doped La-Mn-O perovskites [33,34,44], the unwanted carbona-
tion reaction avoided by using re-oxidation temperature above 900 °C.
Such combinations of cations on A and B sites favor the enhancement in
the redox outcome of the perovskites as compared to ceria (for the
thermal reduction temperatures in the range of 1200–1400 °C). Bork
et al.[45] reported that the use of La-Sr- combination on the A site and
Cr-Co- combination on the B site is beneficial to split CO2 25 times higher
as compared to ceria (without any carbonation) for the operating tem-
peratures in the range of 800–1200 °C. Demont and Abanades [46] ex-
amined the crystal chemistry and phase composition of the Ba-, Ca-, Y-,
Al-, and Mg-doped La-Sr-Mn-O perovskites towards H2O splitting reac-
tion performed in the temperature range of 1000–1400 °C. Likewise,
Babiniec et al. [47] explored an extensive range of composition of (La-
Sr)-(Co-Mn)-O perovskites and (La-Sr)-(Co-Fe)-O perovskites towards
H2O splitting using a thermogravimetric analyzer.
From the list, it is evident that the La-based perovskites were con-
siderably exercised for the thermochemical splitting of H2O/CO2.
Moreover, to enrich the redox reactivity of the La-based perovskites, Sr,
Mn, Fe, Co, Al, and Cr were employed as dopants. Based on the literature
survey conducted by our team, we identified that the initial selection of
the dopants was primarily made based on the thermodynamic analysis
(no experimental reasoning provided). Therefore, in this investigation,
our emphasis was to assess the efficacy of the different dopants, in-
dividually and experimentally, towards enhancing the redox reactivity of
the La-based perovskites (LaMO3). The LaMO3 (where M = Co, Fe, Mn,
Ni, Al, Cr, Sr) perovskites were synthesized utilizing the solution com-
bustion synthesis (SCS) approach where glycine acts as the fuel. The
effectiveness of each LaMO3 material towards the thermochemical
splitting of CO2 was determined by performing multiple cycles using a
high-temperature thermos-gravimetric analyzer (TGA).

3. Results and discussion
As stated earlier, the compositional purity of the SCS synthesized
LaMO3 redox materials was identified by performing the PXRD analysis.
The PXRD peaks allied with each LaMO3 material are reported in Fig. 3.
For all the perovskite materials (except for LaNiO3), the phase identi-
fication conducted by using the PANalytical XPert software designates
the formation of the respective perovskite phase with no traces of any
metal/metal oxide impurities. Besides phase identification, the average
crystallite size of each perovskite material was computed by using the
Scherrer formula. The numbers acquired after performing the calcula-
tions indicate that the average crystallite size of all the LaMO3 materials
was in the range of 50–70 nm. The uppermost crystallite size was de-
tected in the case of the LaFeO3 (69.6 nm), whereas the lowest one
observed in the case of LaSrO3 (50.9 nm).
The microstructural morphological analysis was also conducted, and
the SEM images obtained for each LaMO3 perovskite are presented in
Fig. 4. All the SCS synthesized perovskites showed more extensive irregular
shaped porous morphology. The elemental composition of the perovskites
was also determined by using the EDS, and the obtained results confirmed
1:1 M ratio for all La:M (M = Co, Fe, Mn, Ni, Al, Cr, Sr) perovskites.
The O2 releasing and CO producing capacity of the calcined LaMO3
perovskites was determined by performing thermochemical redox ex-
periments using a high-temperature TGA. In the beginning, the redox
reactivity of the SCS synthesized LaMO3 perovskites were compared
with each other based on the findings obtained in the first thermo-
chemical cycle. Each perovskite was reduced at 1400 °C for 60 min and
re-oxidized at 1000 °C for 30 min, and an exemplified TGA profile (for
the LaAlO3) obtained during the first cycle is reported in Fig. 5.
The amount of mass lost by each perovskite during the reduction step
was calculated starting from 1000 °C up to 1400 °C (steady for 60 min)
whereas, the sum of mass gain via splitting of CO2 estimated at 1000 °C
(steady for 30 min). The obtained mass variation values were converted into
the amounts of O2 released, and CO produced using Eqs. (5) and (6). The
numbers obtained specify that the O2 releasing and CO producing aptitude
of LaSrO3 perovskite was significantly higher than the other perovskites.

perovskite was noticeably active as compared to other perovskites to-

perovskites were further evaluated in three O2 releasing and CO2
splitting cycles at operating conditions identical to the first cycle. The
TGA profiles obtained for all the perovskite materials for three cycles
are reported in Fig. 6, and the quantities of O2 released and CO pro-
duced are listed in Table 1.
The tabulated numbers display that the thermal reduction aptitude
of all the LaMO3 perovskites lessens in the second cycle. The %decrease
in the amount of O2 liberated by each perovskite in the second cycle as
compared to the first cycle was as follows: LaMn (40.3%), LaCo
(77.8%), LaNi (74.1%), LaFe (17.4%), LaCr (27.9%), LaSr (55.1%), and
LaAl (22.1%). The O2 releasing competence of most of the LaMO3
perovskites (except for LaMn and LaNi) further reduced in the third
cycle. For instance, the %decrease in the quantity of O2 freed by each
perovskite in the third cycle as compared to the second cycle was as
follows: LaMn (2.2%), LaCo (39.8%), LaNi (2.4%), LaFe (14.8%), LaCr
(18.95%), LaSr (88.5%), and LaAl (7.9%). The obtained findings in-
dicate that, in terms of their thermal reduction ability, the LaMn, LaNi,
and partially LaAl became stable after three thermochemical cycles,
whereas the remaining perovskites were still unsteady.
The potential of the SCS synthesized LaMO3 perovskites towards the
splitting of CO2, in terms of the amounts of CO produced in three cycles,
is presented in Table 1. Similar to the thermal reduction section, the
capacity of each perovskite (except LiCo) to split CO2 was decreased
considerably in the second cycle. For instance, the %reduction in the CO
generated by each perovskite in the second cycle as compared to the first
one was as follows: LaMn (51.3%), LaCo (3.0%), LaNi (43.2%), LaFe
(41.3%), LaCr (44.9%), LaSr (88.5%), and LaAl (35.9%). In the third
cycle also the CO production ability of LaMn, LaNi, LaFe, LaCr, and LaSr
diminished significantly in comparison to the CO generated in cycle 2
(22.2%, 6.3%, 23.7%, 50.0%, and 37.9%, individually). Nevertheless,
LaCo and LaAl appear to be proficient in producing comparable amounts
of CO in the third cycle as compared to the second one.

second-best material, in terms of the thermal reduction aptitude, was
observed to be LaSr perovskite. Similar to the LaMn perovskite, the O2

but the sum of O2 released in each cycle was lower than the LaMn and
LaSr perovskites. Identical to LaAl perovskite, the LaFe and LaCr per-
ovskites also showed unchanging O2 release for all the cycles. None-
theless, the quantity of O2 liberated by the LaFe and LaCr perovskites was
the lowest as compared to other perovskites investigated in this study.
As per Fig. 8, the uppermost CO production was observed in the case
of LaSr perovskite. The amount of CO produced by the LaSr perovskite
decreased from cycle 2 (222.2 μmol/g) to cycle 6 (79.5 μmol/g).
However, from cycle 6 (79.5 μmol/g) to cycle 10 (86.6 μmol/g), this
perovskite was capable of producing a roughly a similar quantity of CO.
Based on the sum of CO generated, the second-best option was the LaAl
perovskite. Although the LaAl perovskite produced a considerable vo-
lume of CO, the amounts were fluctuating per cycle. For instance, in
cycle 3 the LaAl produced 86.6 μmol/g of CO, which was reduced to
59.2 μmol/g in cycle 4 and again increased up to 70.7 μmol/g in cycle
6. Identical to LaAl perovskite, the LaCo perovskite also generated the
sizable sum of CO, but the trend of the CO production per cycle was of
increasing-decreasing order. The remaining perovskites produced in-
ferior amounts of CO as compared to LaSr, LaAl, and LaCo perovskites.

4. Summary and conclusions
In this study, various La-based perovskites were synthesized and
tested towards thermochemical CO2 splitting reactions. The results as-
sociated with the compositional characterization (performed using
PXRD) confirmed the formation of targeted perovskites with pure phase
composition. The outcomes of the elemental analysis of the derived
perovskite samples agree with the results obtained during the PXRD
investigation. The SEM analysis of the La-based perovskites showed
more significant irregular shaped porous morphology.

Several thermochemical redox cycles were performed to determine
the redox reactivity (amount of O2 released and CO produced) of each
LaMO3 perovskite (thermal reduction at 1400 °C and CO2 splitting at
1000 °C). The obtained findings indicate that the inclusion of Mn into
the La-based perovskite was considerably beneficial towards improving
the O2 release through the lattice during the thermal reduction step.
Likewise, to enhance the production of CO during the CO2 splitting
step, the incorporation of Sr in the La-based perovskite was essential. In
overall, La-Mn-O perovskite was capable of liberating 184.8 μmol of
O2/g·cycle, and the aptitude of the La-Sr-O perovskite towards produ-
cing CO via CO2 splitting was equal to 124.1 μmol of CO/g·cycle.
