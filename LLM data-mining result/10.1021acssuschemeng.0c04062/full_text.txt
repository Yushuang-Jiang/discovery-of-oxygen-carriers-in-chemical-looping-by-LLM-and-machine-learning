ABSTRACT: As a key to realize the chemical looping air
separation (CLAS) process, CuO is a good oxygen carrier
candidate because of its high oxygen transport capacity and
excellent thermodynamic characteristics. However, it is hampered
by easy deactivation during redox cycles. In this work, a Cu−Zr
oxygen carrier is developed via a metal−organic framework
(MOF) self-templated method to realize high redox reactivity
and favorable thermal stability for the CLAS process. The
physicochemistry property and reduction kinetics are investigated
through isothermal redox cycles, material characterizations, and
reduction kinetic modeling. Compared to the coprecipitated
oxygen carrier (Copr.), the MOF-derived oxygen carrier (MOF-
D) obtains a 35% faster average reaction rate at the temperature
interval of 800−900 °C, especially a 60% reduction rate increase at 850 °C. This enhanced reaction rate is mainly ascribed to the
lower intrinsic activation energy (126.3 kJ·mol−1 for MOF-D vs 157.3 kJ·mol−1 for Copr.) rather than the amount of active oxygen
species. The oxygen-releasing mechanisms for the MOF-derived and coprecipitated oxygen carriers follow the nucleation and nuclei
growth model with a transition of one-dimensional growth to two-dimensional nucleation at two temperature ranges (800−825 and
850−900 °C). This study oﬀers a new route to develop an eﬃcient oxygen carrier for the CLAS process.
KEYWORDS: chemical looping, air separation, oxygen carrier, metal−organic framework, reaction kinetics
■INTRODUCTION
Based on the chemical looping concept, chemical looping air
separation (CLAS) is proposed as a promising method for
oxygen production.1 Compared to other air separation
technologies (e.g., cryogenic distillation, pressure swing
adsorption, and membrane separation), CLAS attracts much
attention because of low exergy penalty and highly eﬃcient
production.1,2 Typically, the CLAS system consists of two
interconnected reactors: an oxidation reactor and a reduction
reactor to realize the cyclic oxidation and reduction of a
looping medium (i.e., an oxygen carrier).2 Oxygen carriers
release gaseous oxygen (eq 1) in the reduction reactor, while
reduced oxygen carriers are then regenerated in the oxidation
reactor (eq 2).

As a cyclic medium, the oxygen carrier plays the most
important role in chemical looping systems. Until now, many
metal oxides have been selected and developed as oxygen
carriers to release gaseous oxygen in CLAS.3−7 Because of the
properties of high oxygen transport capacity (0.1 gO2/gCuO),
high redox reactivity, excellent thermodynamic characteristics,
and less solid inventory, CuO stands out as a promising oxygen
carrier for the CLAS process.8,9 Meanwhile, it exhibits a
desirable property that a modest equilibrium oxygen partial
pressure diminishes the loss of eﬀective oxygen capacity during
the conveying in the two reactors.10 In this way, Cu-based
oxygen carriers can help to achieve the heat balance for the
practical CLAS application. For instance, using CuO as the
active component, reaction optimization and heat integration
realize the thermal management for CLAS,2 while adjusting
inert solid ﬂow and CO2 feeding makes the CLAS suitable for
oxy-fuel combustion.11 However, CuO deactivates fast during
redox cycles,12 indicating that great challenges still exist for this
oxygen carrier for further applications.8,13 Therefore, eﬀective

Fortunately, three solutions can be applied to deal with the
above observations: (i) add a support with high Tammann
temperature to maintain high thermal stability; (ii) combine
with a highly active oxygen carrier to enhance redox reactivity;
and (iii) rationally design an oxygen carrier via advanced
synthesis techniques to accelerate the reaction kinetics. In the
ﬁrst aspect, single or blended supports have been intensively
investigated and compared, such as Al2O3, ZrO2, MgAl2O4,
MgO, ZnO, CeO2, and their mixtures.14−16 Among them,
ZrO2 would be a competitive candidate because it can
maintain high thermal stability and increase the oxygen-
releasing rate.17−19 Certainly, the interaction between the
active component and inert support should be considered to
avoid the formation of inactive complexes20,21 and to
implement a synergistic eﬀect from intensiﬁed ion con-
duction.22 With respect to the second solution, the improved
performance is mainly derived from the positive interactions
between two active components.23 When compared to that of
a single oxygen carrier, the combination of CuO and Fe2O3 can
attain high redox reactivity and stable performance.24,25 Finally,
using an advanced synthesis method to rationally design an
oxygen carrier can be a way to improve redox reactivity and
thermal stability. For instance, a templated synthesis approach
would be a good choice because it realizes a rational design of
solid materials with high performance (e.g., catalyst, oxygen
carrier, and CO2 sorbent). A self-assembly template
combustion-synthesized core−shell structured CuO@TiO2−
Al2O3 showed superior redox performance without sintering,26
while layered double-hydroxide-derived Cu−Al oxygen carriers
with a nanoplatelet morphology demonstrated stable oxygen
release and oxygen storage capacities.27 Hence, ﬁnding a
suitable templated synthesis method would be a promising
solution to obtain desirable and eﬃcient oxygen carriers.
Metal−organic frameworks (MOFs), also known as porous
coordination polymers, construct an inﬁnite and periodic
network with backbones of metal ions as connectors and
ligands as linkers.28 Their tunable textural and chemical
properties make them promising materials for the application
in gas adsorption, catalysis, energy conversion and storage,
etc.29 More importantly, MOFs are also appropriate self-
templates to synthesize nanostructured materials with superior
activity. Signiﬁcant research studies on developing solid
materials via MOF-templated methods have been applied to
low-temperature electrocatalysis30,31 and photocatalysis;32,33
however, limited attempts have been made to apply this
method for high-temperature processes. Lithium orthosilicate
(Li4SiO4) can be prepared from a Li−Si-based MOF template
to achieve high sorption capacity and fast adsorption rates for
CO2 capture.34 As the ﬁrst attempt to synthesize a Ca-based
sorbent for calcium looping, a Ca-MOF-derived sorbent with a
low inert support content showed 500% times CO2 sorption
capacity and high thermal stability.35 These eﬀorts demon-
strate a promising future for adopting the MOF-templated
approach to rationally design solid materials for chemical
looping applications. However, to the best of our knowledge,
no such study uses the MOF-templated method to develop
oxygen carriers for the CLAS process.
Toward this end, in this work, a Cu-based oxygen carrier is
ﬁrst synthesized using an MOF as a self-template to research
its feasibility and reactivity for the CLAS process. In order to
examine its redox performance, some characterization
techniques and cyclic redox experiments have been applied
for the comparison with a reference oxygen carrier prepared via
a coprecipitation method. Moreover, a comprehensive reaction
kinetic analysis is conducted to explain why the new CuO/
ZrO2 oxygen carrier can yield an enhanced reduction reaction
rate.
■MATERIALS SYNTHESIS AND EXPERIMENTS
Synthesis of Oxygen Carriers. A pristine Cu-MOF precursor
was prepared by a modiﬁed solvothermal method.29,36−38 Typically,
1116.9 mg of 1,3,5-benzenetricarboxylic acid (H3BTC) was dissolved
in 30 mL of absolute ethanol as solution A, while 2172.8 mg of
Cu(NO3)2·3H2O was dissolved in 30 mL of deionized water as
solution B. Then, the two solutions were mixed, stirred, and
transferred into a Teﬂon-lined stainless-steel autoclave. After being
treated in an oven at 100 °C for 20 h, the resulting Cu-MOF
precipitate was ﬁnally collected by ﬁltration, washed with deionized
water, and dried at 100 °C under vacuum for 10 h.
The CuO/ZrO2 oxygen carrier was obtained by the thermal
transformation of a Zr-coated Cu-MOF precursor. Because this
oxygen carrier is designed to have a nominal composition of 90 wt %
CuO and 10 wt % ZrO2, a predetermined weight of ZrOCl2·8H2O
was dissolved in ethanol. Then, the Cu-MOF precursor was added
into the solution. The mixed solution was stirred for 6 h, dried for 10
h, and calcined for 8 h at 950 °C with a heating rate of 2 K·min−1.
Finally, the Cu-based oxygen carrier was obtained and denoted as
MOF-D.
In order to examine and compare the performance of the newly
developed oxygen carrier, a copper/zirconium oxide with an identical
CuO/ZrO2 mass ratio (9:1) was prepared by a coprecipitation
method39 and selected as a reference material. The predetermined
amounts of copper nitrate Cu(NO3)2·3H2O and ZrOCl2·8H2O were
ﬁrst dissolved in deionized water, and sodium carbonate was then
added in excess to precipitate the cupric ions and ZrO2+ ions through
adjusting the pH close to be nearly alkaline with stirring. At last, the
solid precipitate was ﬁltered, washed, dried, and calcined using the
same condition as for the MOF-derived CuO/ZrO2 oxygen carrier.
This reference sample is represented as Copr.. The obtained oxygen
carriers were sieved into three groups: 100−200, 200−300, and 300−
400 mesh. In order to eliminate the internal mass-transfer limitation,
the eﬀect of these particle sizes was investigated during the reaction
kinetic tests.
Materials Characterization. The phase compositions of the as-
prepared and used oxygen carriers were analyzed using a Shimadzu
powder X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) 6100 diﬀractometer with Ni-ﬁltered
Cu Kα radiation. Scans were performed at 10−80° at a rate of 4° per
minute with an accelerating voltage and a ﬁlament current of 40 kV
and 30 mA, respectively. The morphological images of the fresh and
used materials were examined via scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) which was performed on a Hitachi S-4800. The textural
property was measured using nitrogen-adsorption/desorption iso-
therms at 77 K on a JWGB Sci & Tech. instrument JW-BK200C,
China.
Redox Reactivity Test. The redox behavior of Cu-based oxygen
carriers was examined at diﬀerent operating temperatures (800, 825,
850, 875, and 900 °C) under atmospheric pressure using a
thermogravimetric analyzer (NETZSCH STA 2500, Germany). The
samples were placed in a 4 mm deep and 6 mm diameter quartz
crucible. In a typical thermal decomposition experiment, a 5−10 mg
sample was heated in a quartz bowl with a heating rate of 20 K min−1
from ambient temperature to the desired temperature under N2 at 50
mL·min−1. The 10 redox cycles were conducted within the
temperature range of 800−900 °C using N2 as a reduction agent
and air as an oxidation medium. The reduction and oxidation reaction
time periods were set as 20 min and 15 min, respectively. Three
diﬀerent oxygen concentrations (0, 0.8, and 1.2 vol %) are employed
for the oxygen-releasing reaction.

in the thermogravimetric analyzer during redox cycles. The oxygen

■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Characterization of the Synthesized Precursor and
Oxygen Carrier. Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns of the
synthesized Cu-MOF and the thermogravimetry (TG) curves
for the decomposition of the precursors. It aims to conﬁrm the
successful synthesis of the MOF precursor and to justify the
formation of the oxygen carriers from the precursor. The good
agreement of the characteristic peaks for the prepared Cu-
MOF with the simulated XRD pattern indicates the successful
synthesis of the MOF precursor.40 As shown in Figure 1b, the
molecular formula of the Cu-MOF can be determined as
Cu3(BTC)2·3H2O based on the calculation of weight loss from
the thermal decomposition proﬁle, which is consistent with the
previous literature results.38,41 Moreover, during the thermal
transformation process from ambient temperature to calcina-
tion temperature under an air atmosphere with a heating rate
of 5 K·min−1, two thermal decomposition stages appeared for
the Cu-MOF and Zr-precursor. For the Cu-MOF, the ﬁrst
stage occurs at a temperature below 300 °C approximately,
which is attributed to the loss of the absorbed water and
ethanol in the ﬁrst rapid drop in weight and the removal of
crystal water in the second drop in weight. The sharp weight
loss in the second stage is due to the structural decomposition
of organic linkages or guest molecules. Subsequently, new
crystallized CuO and zirconia phases are formed. However, the
Zr-loaded Cu-MOF precursor shows 65.90% weight loss
(compared to a theoretical 63.74% weight loss) with the
presence of one more decomposition stage because the
primary decomposition stage of ZrOCl2·8H2O is diﬀerent
from that of the Cu-MOF.
The phase component of the MOF-D and Copr. oxygen
carriers before and after redox cycles is illustrated in Figure 2.
It is found that pure CuO and ZrO2 are detected without other
impurities. The presence of only monoclinic zirconia before
and after redox cycles indicates no incorporation of Cu into the
Zr lattice because the dopants would destabilize and cause a
phase transformation for ZrO2 between monoclinic and
tetragonal phases.42,43 In other words, ZrO2 plays the role of
an inert support to maintain the thermal stability of the two
synthesized oxygen carriers. Determined from the Scherrer
equation by ﬁtting a Gaussian function with deconvolution
parameter D (equal to 2) to the three most intense
characteristic peaks of each compound,44 the averaged
crystalline size for the two samples is obtained. Owing to
high-temperature calcination and redox reaction, a large
crystalline size (for CuO: 46.2 nm for MOF-D vs 51.1 nm
for Copr.) and a low surface area (Table S1, 3.7 m2·g−1 for
MOF-D vs 2.4 m2·g−1 for Copr.) are obtained for the two
oxygen carriers, even though the MOF-D oxygen carriers show
a surface area and a crystal size more favorable than that of the
Copr. oxygen carrier. Moreover, a 10% inert support can

Redox Reactivity and Stability. Figure 4 shows the mass
variations of two CuO/ZrO2 oxygen carriers at 850 and 900
°C under 10 redox cycles. Because of the morphology
evolution promoted at high temperature, the unstable
maximum and minimum weight values of the two oxygen
carriers at the ﬁrst four cycles are measured. These two solid
materials become stable gradually beginning at the ﬁfth cycle,
which is a common phenomenon which occurred in the
previous studies.45,46 Thus, as discussed in the next section, the
mass loss data at the ﬁfth cycle were selected to obtain the
reaction rate and realize kinetic analysis at each temperature.
The fact that there is no distinct diﬀerence in the stability of
the two oxygen carriers obtained by two preparation methods
is possibly due to the lower inert carrier content. Under these
conditions, the metal oxides have already become dense during
the calcination step of the preparation process. In addition,
although the weight fraction of the inert zirconia support is
only 10% and signiﬁcantly lower than that reported in the
previous studies,10,47,48 the durability of the synthesized oxygen
carriers can be guaranteed when compared to the circumstance
of pure CuO.49
Figure 5a illustrates the enhanced conversion rate for the
CuO/ZrO2 oxygen carrier derived from the Cu-MOF
precursor when compared to that of the coprecipitated
CuO/ZrO2 oxygen carrier in the temperature range from
800 to 900 °C. Figure 5b shows the maximum diﬀerence in
conversion for the two oxygen carriers, in which the MOF-D
oxygen carrier shows a conversion 52% higher than that of the
Copr. oxygen carrier at 850 °C. However, the slight diﬀerence
in the conversion for the two samples at 900 °C might be due
to the high diﬀusional barrier caused by sintering eﬀects at high
temperatures.50 As illustrated in Figure 6a, because the largest
conversion gap between MOF-D and Copr. samples is
obtained at 850 °C, their reduction reaction rates are
compared at this temperature, indicating that the MOF-D
oxygen carrier shows a 60% faster average reaction rate with a
10.5 min oxygen-releasing period than the Copr. oxygen
carrier. Similarly, the 31 and 11% increase in the average
reaction rate are obtained for the MOF-D oxygen carrier at
875 and 900 °C, respectively, compared to that for the Copr.
material. Accordingly, the MOF-D oxygen carrier achieved a
35% increase in the average oxygen-releasing rate at the
temperature interval of 800−900 °C. This higher reactivity for
the MOF-D oxygen carrier might be ascribed to the larger
amount of active oxygen species and/or the lower intrinsic
activation energy because more lattice distortion would be
introduced using the MOF-templated method. However,
temperature-programmed reduction experimental results (Fig-
ure 6b) for the two oxygen carriers show almost an identical
released oxygen amount (2.89 mmolO2/gOC for MOF-D and
2.93 mmolO2/gOC for Copr.). This implies that the enhanced
reaction activity for the MOF-D oxygen carrier is a result of its
intrinsic kinetics, which will be comprehensively discussed in
the next section.
■REACTION KINETICS
Controlling Resistances. In general, three resistance types
(external mass transport limitation, internal diﬀusion resist-
ance, and intrinsic chemical reaction) would potentially control
the reaction rate individually or collectively in the gas−solid
reaction. For the determination of the kinetic parameters, it is
recommended that the physical transfer behaviors should be
avoided. Therefore, the experiments on the elimination of
internal and external diﬀusions were conducted. As illustrated
in Figure S1, the eﬀects of nitrogen sweep ﬂow rate, sample
mass, and particle size on the solid conversion were
investigated. The results show that an adequate purge gas
velocity (≥40 mL·min−1) and a less loading mass (≤6 mg)
lower the oxygen partial pressure around the particles,
minimizing the reaction rate limitation due to the thermody-
namic eﬀects.51 In addition, the particles in the size range of
75−150 μm have a negligible internal mass-transfer resistance
according to the law of additive reaction times.52 Moreover, as
discussed by a previous study,53 the temperature diﬀerence
between particles and the bed during redox reactions can be
neglected for oxygen carrier particles with a small size.
Therefore, 6 mg oxygen carriers with diameters of 100−200
mesh (75−150 μm) were employed in thermogravimetric

Based on the identiﬁed nucleation and nuclei growth model,

acceleration, and deceleration regions). As illustrated in Figure

■CONCLUSIONS
A facile MOF-templated synthesis method is applied to
develop a Cu−Zr oxygen carrier with high redox reactivity
and favorable thermal stability for CLAS. Because of the
diﬀerent thermal decomposition manner of the Zr and Cu
precursors, the formation of the oxygen carrier from the Zr-
coated Cu-MOF experiences three transformation stages with
a maximum structure disassembly of organic linkages or guest
molecules. Compared to the referenced oxygen carrier
prepared by a typical coprecipitation approach, the MOF-
derived oxygen carrier shows a smaller crystal size and a larger
surface area. The two oxygen carriers show good thermal
stability and high oxygen release capacity during 10 redox
cycles. Moreover, the MOF-derived oxygen carrier achieves
1.35 times average reduction rate of that for the coprecipitated
oxygen carrier at the temperature interval of 800−900 °C and
especially a 60% increase in average reduction rate at 850 °C.
This is attributed to the fact that the former material provides a
lower intrinsic activation energy (126.3 and 157.3 kJ·mol−1 for
the MOF-derived and coprecipitated oxygen carriers, respec-
tively) rather than a greater amount of active oxygen species.
Although the reduction reaction kinetics for the two oxygen
carriers can be described by the nucleation and consequent
nuclei growth model, a transition from one-dimensional
growth to two-dimensional nucleation occurs in diﬀerent
temperature ranges. This is likely a result of the oxygen-ion
diﬀusion rate and the rate control step change at high
operating temperatures.
