Reaction mechanism of spinel CuFe 2 O 4 with CO during 
chemical-looping combustion: An experimental and 
theoretical study 
Feng Liu, Jing Liu ∗, Yingju Yang, Zhen Wang, Chuguang Zheng 
State Key Laboratory of Coal Combustion, School of Energy and Power Engineering, Huazhong University of Science and 
Technology, Wuhan 430074, China 
Received 1 December 2017; accepted 27 June 2018 
Available online 17 July 2018 
Abstract 
Spinel CuFe 2 O 4 is a promising oxygen carrier due to its synergistic enhanced performance. A fundamental 
understanding of the reaction mechanism between oxygen carrier and fuels is important for a rational design 
of highly efficient oxygen carrier. The reaction mechanism of spinel CuFe 2 O 4 with CO during chemical- 
looping combustion (CLC) was studied based on thermogravimetric analyses (TGA) and density functional 
theory (DFT) calculations. Two distinct reaction stages were clearly observed. CuFe 2 O 4 was mainly trans- 
formed into Cu and Fe 3 O 4 with a rapid reaction rate in the initial stage, and then product Fe 3 O 4 was slowly 
reduced to FeO or even to Fe. The reactivity of CuFe 2 O 4 is much higher than that of Fe 2 O 3 , which is ascribed 
to the existence of Cu. The enhanced oxygen evolution activity of CuFe 2 O 4 at low temperature is validated 
by both the experimental and theoretical methods. Three types of surface oxygen coordinated with different 
metal atoms show different reactivity. Two kinds of reaction pathways are involved in CO oxidation over 
CuFe 2 O 4 . In the one-step reaction pathway, CO directly reacts with the oxygen bonding to two octahedral 
Cu and one octahedral Fe atoms to form a CO 2 molecule without an energy barrier, which corresponds to the 
surface oxygen consumption observed in TGA experiments. In the possible two-step reaction pathway, CO 
first adsorbs on the surface, and then reacts with the oxygen bound to one octahedral Cu and two octahedral 
Fe atoms to generate CO 2 by surmounting an energy barrier of 10.84 kJ/mol, which is the most kinetically 
and thermodynamically favorable pathway.

1. Introduction 
Chemical-looping combustion (CLC) is an in- 
novative combustion technology for CO 2 capture 
with low cost and energy penalty [1–3] . Being differ- 
ent from the conventional combustion, fuel and air

in CLC are never in direct contact. Instead, oxygen 
carrier is circulated between a fuel reactor and an 
air reactor to transfer oxygen for fuel combustion. 
Consequently, oxygen carrier is the foundation of 
CLC and is identified as one of the determining fac- 
tors for the successful operation of CLC. 
In recent years, a lot of efforts have been made 
for the selection and optimization of oxygen carri- 
ers with improved redox and mechanical properties 
[4–10] . Compared to other potential oxygen carrier 
materials, Fe 2 O 3 shows a lot of positive properties 
such as low cost, high mechanical strength and en- 
vironment friendliness. However, the redox charac- 
teristic of Fe 2 O 3 is relatively weaker than that of 
other metal oxides, especially for the CLC with coal 
or methane. In addition, Fe 2 O 3 with the thermody- 
namic limitation leads to a low oxygen transport ca- 
pacity, because only the transformation from Fe 2 O 3 
to Fe 3 O 4 is applicable for CLC system to produce a 
high concentration of CO 2 [11] . On the other hand, 
CuO is a very attractive oxygen carrier due to its 
high redox reactivity, high oxygen transfer capacity 
and a favorable thermodynamics for complete fuel 
conversion [4] . Nevertheless, the main challenge of 
CuO is its low melting point and high tendency to 
agglomeration. As a result, a single metal oxide of 
Fe 2 O 3 or CuO is difficult to adequately satisfy the 
requirements of practical CLC application. 
Recently, in order to address the disadvantages 
of single metal oxide, bimetallic Cu/Fe oxygen car- 
riers with synergistic effect, which takes advantages 
of the favorable characteristics of each of them, 
have been studied to increase the reactivity of Fe- 
based oxides and the stability of Cu-based oxides 
[5–7] . In the previous studies, the synergistic im- 
provements were mainly attributed to the forma- 
tion of new crystalline phases, such as spinels and 
perovskites [12] . Siriwardane et al. [5,6] found that 
the spinel CuFe 2 O 4 could be easily formed even in 
the presence of a small amount of Fe 2 O 3 above 
488 °C. They also suggested that the mixed oxides 
with Cu/Fe ratio about 1:2 exhibited better reac- 
tivity and improved physical stability as compared 
to the corresponding single metal oxide during the 
coal or CH 4 CLC. This indicates that the newly 
formed CuFe 2 O 4 plays a critical role in the pro- 
moted performance of mixed oxides. Furthermore, 
Wang et al. [13–15] synthesized CuFe 2 O 4 using sol- 
gel combustion synthesis method, and revealed that 
spinel CuFe 2 O 4 had better reactivity than Fe 2 O 3 
with coal and no agglomeration occurred at 900 °C. 
Besides, the performance of various ferrites were 
investigated, and it was found that bimetallic fer- 
rites had better reactivity than pure Fe 2 O 3 , and no- 
tably decreased the particle agglomeration during 
the cyclic tests [7,12] . In addition, CuFe 2 O 4 with 
spinel structure is well known to be a magnetic ma- 
terial, which can greatly facilitate the separation of 
oxygen carrier from the fuel ash. Therefore, spinel 
CuFe 2 O 4 is a promising oxygen carrier candidate 
for the application of CLC. 
The knowledge of reaction mechanism between 
fuel molecules and oxygen carrier is useful for un- 
derstanding the reaction behaviors accompanying 
with the evolution of lattice oxygen, crystalline 
phase and microstructure, which will facilitate the 
design and optimization of more efficient oxygen 
carriers. Although previous studies characterized 
the reactivity and stability of CuFe 2 O 4 during CH 4 
or coal CLC process, no attempts have yet been 
made to study the reactivity of spinel CuFe 2 O 4 with 
CO as a reducing gas. Since CO is a key compo- 
nent in the syngas. Furthermore, the reaction mech- 
anism of CuFe 2 O 4 with CO during CLC process is 
still unknown. 
The objectives herein are to investigate the re- 
activity of CuFe 2 O 4 with CO and reveal the re- 
lated reaction mechanism. TGA experiments were 
conducted to acquire the reduction behavior of 
CuFe 2 O 4 by CO. Theoretical investigation based 
on the density functional theory (DFT) calcu- 
lations was performed to identify the oxidation 
mechanism of CO over spinel CuFe 2 O 4 . Based on 
the TGA, XRD, XPS and DFT analysis, the con- 
nection between reduction behavior and reaction 
mechanism was introduced. These results will be 
helpful for the practical application of the com- 
bined Cu/Fe oxygen carriers. 
2. Experimental and computational details 
2.1. Preparation and characterization of oxygen 
carrier 
The spinel CuFe 2 O 4 oxygen carrier particles 
were prepared by sol-gel combustion method. The 
preparation route starts from molecular precursors 
and forms an oxide network through the inorganic 
polymerization reactions. At first, Fe(NO 3 ) 3 ·9H 2 O, 
Cu(NO 3 ) 2 ·3H 2 O and citric acid (C 6 H 8 O 7 ·H 2 O) in 
stoichiometric molar ratio were mixed and dis- 
solved in deionized water. Then, the mixed solution 
was stirred in a water bath at 85 °C until the gel 
formed. The achieved gel was dried in an oven at 
100 °C for 12 h to expel residual water. After that, 
the dried gel was calcined in a muffle furnace at 
500 °C for 0.5 h to remove organic compounds, fol- 
lowed by calcination at 950 °C for 6 h (with a heat- 
ing rate of 20 °C/min). By using this preparation 
method, the particles with good compositional ho- 
mogeneity, high phase purity and surface activity 
were obtained. Besides, Fe 2 O 3 was also synthetized 
by using this method for the comparison. These 
oxygen carrier particles were crushed and screened 
to the size smaller than 125 μm. 
The crystallographic phases of oxygen carrier 
were determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD) in a 
Shimadzu XRD-7000 diffractometer with Cu K α
radiation ( λ = 1.5418 ˚A) in the 2 θ range of 20 ° to 
80 ° with a step of 5 °/min operating at 40 kV and 
30 mA. The chemical states of O, Cu and Fe ele-

2.2. Experimental setup and procedure 
The experiments were conducted in a thermo- 
gravimetric analyzer (TA SDT-Q600). A sample of 
20 mg was heated in an alumina crucible from room 
temperature to reaction temperature. The sweep 
gas enters the balance room to keep it in an inert 
environment, and then passes the reaction cham- 
ber. The pure CO is directly introduced in the reac- 
tion chamber. The total gas flow rate for each test 
was set to 100 mL/min. To characterize the reduc- 
tion behavior of CuFe 2 O 4 , CO temperature pro- 
grammed reduction (CO-TPR) was conducted at a 
heating rate of 20 °C/min and from 25 to 900 °C. 
CO-TPR of Fe 2 O 3 was also conducted for the 
comparison. To account the oxygen release prop- 
erty of CuFe 2 O 4 , the temperature programmed de- 
composition (TPD) of CuFe 2 O 4 in pure N 2 was 
performed with a heating rate of 20 °C/min and 
from 25 to 1000 °C. The duration at 1000 °C is 
set to 20 min to ensure the sufficient conversion 
of CuFe 2 O 4 . Isother mal and non-isother mal tests 
were conducted to explore the effect of CO con- 
centration, heating rate and temperature on reduc- 
tion performance of CuFe 2 O 4 . In isothermal tests, 
sample temperature was raised from ambient to the 
temperatures of 700, 800 and 900 °C with a rate of 
20 °C/min in the air atmosphere. To avoid mixing 
the air and CO, the air was cut off for 10 min before 
switch to CO. The reaction time is set to 50 min for 
full conversion. In non-isothermal tests, the heat- 
ing rate of 10, 15 and 20 °C/min are used to rise the 
sample temperature from 25 to 900 °C in 5% CO at- 
mosphere. CO concentrations of 3%, 5%, and 8% 
were employed to study its effect on the reduction 
performance of CuFe 2 O 4 . 
2.3. DFT model and calculations 
Spinels with the formula of AB 2 O 4 (where A 
and B are metal ions) are usually categorized into 
normal and inverse spinel according to the distri- 
bution of A and B cations filling the tetrahedral 
and octahedral sites in the face-centered cubic sub- 
lattice of O atoms. Previous experimental studies 
have demonstrated that CuFe 2 O 4 usually has an in- 
verse spinel structure [16] . Besides, the magnetism 
of spinel is primarily inferred as antiferromagnetic 
between the magnetic ions at A and B sites [17] . 
Accordingly, CuFe 2 O 4 with the antiferromagnetic 
arrangement was adopted in the present study (see 
Fig. 1 a). 
All calculations were carried out within spin- 
polarized density functional theory framework 
by employing CASTEP codes [18] . The gener- 
alized gradient approximation (GGA) functional 
as parameterized by Perdew, Burke, and Ernz- 
erhof (PBE) [19] was employed to account for 
the exchange-correlation energy, as this has been 
shown to give accurately structural and electronic 
properties of spinels [20,21] . The Vanderbilt ultra- 
soft pseudopotentials (USPP) [22] was adopted to 
describe the electron-ion potential, and the valence 
orbitals were represented by a plane-wave basis set 
[23,24] with a cutoff energy of 340 eV. For the k- 
points sampling of the Brillouin zone, Monkhorst- 
Pack scheme [25] was used. A 4 × 4 × 4 set of k- 
points for bulk optimization and a 4 × 4 × 1 set 
for surface relaxation as well as for CO adsorption 
were used. The self-consistent field (SCF) tolerance 
was converged when the energy differences between 
electronic iterations were smaller than 1.0 × 10 –6 
eV/atom. Atomic positions were optimized by the 
BFGS algorithm [26] , and convergence criterions 
were: (i) an energy of 1.0 × 10 –5 eV/atom; (ii) an 
atomic force of 0.03 eV/ ˚A; (iii) a maximum dis- 
placement tolerance of 0.001 ˚A. 
At first, a full optimization of the cell parame- 
ters for bulk structure of CuFe 2 O 4 was performed. 
The optimized structure of CuFe 2 O 4 is tetrago- 
nal with the lattice parameters of a = b = 8.221 ˚A 
and c = 8.903 ˚A, which is in good agreement with 
the experimental values of a = b = 8.216 ˚A and 
c = 8.709 ˚A [27] . CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface is the ther- 
modynamically stable [28] and catalytically active 
[29] surface. Moreover, all the possible active sites, 
for instance, Cu and Fe atoms, and the O atoms 
with different Cu/Fe coordination are exposed on 
this surface. Thus CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface was em- 
ployed in this study. Alone the (100) orientation, 
the structure of CuFe 2 O 4 is composed alternatively 
of a Fe atomic layer and an oxygen-bearing Cu- 
Fe atomic layer. As a result, an eight-layer modeled 
surface with top four layers fully relaxed was built 
in this study, as shown in Fig. 1 b. A vacuum region 
of 15 ˚A was used to eliminate the repulsive force 
between adsorbates. Due to the asymmetrical force 
near surface, atomic relaxation was taken into ac- 
count to provide more accurate energy.

3.1.2. Effects of CO concentration, heating rate 
and temperature 
To further explore the reaction performance of 
CuFe 2 O 4 , the effect of CO concentration, heating 
rate and temperature were considered. It is noted 
that these factors are influential for the chemical re- 
action behaviors [14,31] . As shown in Fig. 3 , the re- 
duction reaction also includes two reaction stages 
according to the weight loss (100% ∼91.35% and 
91.35% ∼73.25%) and the abovementioned reac- 
tion mechanism. 
The effect of CO concentration (3%, 5% and 
8%) on isothermal and non-isothermal reduction 
of CuFe 2 O 4 was evaluated, as displayed in Fig. 3 a 
and b. In the case of isothermal reduction, an in- 
crease in the reaction rate was observed during the 
entire reduction stage as CO concentration was in- 
creased. At the beginning of the reduction, the re- 
action rate was very fast especially for 5% and 8% 
CO, then it decreased rapidly in the second stage. 
As for non-isothermal reduction, the reaction rate 
also revealed two stages. In both cases, the increase 
of CO concentration only resulted in an increase of 
reduction rate, which is applicable in practical CLC 
process with a higher CO concentration. In addi- 
tion, the full conversion of CuFe 2 O 4 with 3% CO 
is hardly achieved in about 90 min. Thus low CO 
concentration ( < 5%) is not recommended, and 5% 
is used in the following study. The CO concentra- 
tion can affect the reaction rate rather than reaction 
stage, which indicates that the corresponding reac- 
tions are controlled by two different resistances.

reaction rate) is mainly dependent on the heating 
rate. Figure 3 c shows the effect of heating rate (10,

ing rate scarcely affects the reaction, because the 
weight loss profiles are almost parallel. However, 
at 10 °C/min heating rate, there is an increase in 
reaction rate around 575 °C corresponding to the 
reducing transition of Fe 3 O 4 → FeO in the second 
stage. The most possible reason is that the low

CuFe 2 O 4 , reduced and oxygen uncoupled CuFe 2 O 4

be seen that all the perceptible peaks are associ-

3.2. CO adsorption and oxidation mechanism on 
CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface 
3.2.1. CO adsorption on CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface 
To evaluate the adsorption activity of CuFe 2 O 4 
surface, all the possible adsorption sites have been 
examined with the CO molecular axis perpendicu- 
lar (C-down and O-down orientations) and paral- 
lel to the surfaces. Five distinguishable adsorption 
sites at this surface are displayed in Fig. 1 b. The 
coordinately unsaturated 5-fold Fe (Fe O ) and Cu 
(Cu O ) sites, and three kinds of 3-fold O sites (O 1 , 
O 2 and O 3 ) are exposed on the CuFe 2 O 4 (100) sur- 
face. In detail, O 1 bonds with two Cu O and one Fe O 
atoms, O 2 bonds with one Cu O and two Fe O atoms, 
and O 3 bonds with one Fe O , one Fe T and one Cu O 
atoms, respectively. After optimization, the most 
stable configurations with adsorption energies and 
structural parameters are given in Fig. 6 . The re- 
sults indicate that only the C-down orientation of 
CO has strong interaction with the surface. The 
stability of interaction system follows the order of 
A > B > C > D. 
As shown in configuration A, CO interacts in- 
tensively with the surface O 1 -site atom forming a 
CO 2 molecule directly. A new C–O double-bond 
forms and the bond length is 1.182 ˚A. Meanwhile, 
the C–O triple-bond in CO is converted to double- 
bond after adsorption and elongated to 1.186 ˚A. 
Besides, the adsorption energy of this configuration 
is −217.07 kJ/mol. The process of breaking two 
Cu–O and one Fe–O bonds and complete detach- 
ment of CO 2 molecule from surface suggest that 
CO oxidation via surface O 1 atoms seems to have 
proceeded without an energy barrier. 
For the configuration B, CO interacts with the 
Fe O - and O 2 -site atoms to form C–Fe and C–
O bonds with the bond lengths of 1.969 ˚A and 
1.242 ˚A, respectively. At the same time, the C–O 
bond length of CO is elongated to 1.196 ˚A. The 
interaction between CO and O 2 site results in the 
breakage of one Cu–O and one Fe–O bonds and 
the formation of a new C–O bond generating a sur- 
face CO 2 complex. The adsorption energy for this 
interaction system is −109.99 kJ/mol. 
For configuration C, CO is adsorbed to the 
bridge site of Cu O and O 3 atoms by C–Cu and C–O 
bonding. The formed C–Cu and C–O bond lengths 
are 1.911 ˚A and 1.300 ˚A, respectively. Meantime, 
the C–O bond distance of CO is elongated to 
1.208 ˚A. The adsorption energy is −60.92 kJ/mol.

3.2.2. CO oxidation on CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface 
Obviously, the CO adsorption behavior study 
suggests that there are two reaction channels for 
CO oxidation on the CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface. The 
first channel is a one-step reaction, as shown in the 
configuration A, where CO directly reacts with O 1 
to generate a linear CO 2 . The reason for this may 
be due to the 3-fold O coordinated with two Cu 
atoms thus resulting in a high activity. This reac- 
tion channel for CO oxidation could occur at low 
temperature, which is consistent with our observa- 
tion (191 ∼312 °C) in TGA experiments. 
The second reaction channel is a two-step pro- 
cess including CO adsorption and CO 2 desorp- 
tion. The reaction pathways and energy profiles of

two-step reaction pathway for CO oxidation is the

From the above discussion, it can be concluded 
that CO oxidation on the CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface fol- 
lows the Mars-van Krevelen mechanism: CO first 
interacts with the surface O, forming a CO 2 directly 
(one-step reaction) or a surface CO 2 complex (two- 
step reaction) which bonds to the metal atoms, then 
CO 2 desorption occurs by surmounting an energy 
barrier. Moreover, the kinetics of CO oxidation 
reaction on CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface is mainly con- 
trolled by desorption. The surface O atom bonding 
to the Cu atom exhibits a superior activity, which 
can explain the high reactivity observed in the TGA 
experiments. Further, it should be noted that H 2 
would also be present in the CLC process, and it 
may have the synergistic effect for CO adsorption 
and oxidation. H 2 will compete with CO for active 
sites on oxygen carrier surface, which may lead to

4. Conclusions 
A comprehensive investigation of the reaction 
mechanism of spinel CuFe 2 O 4 with CO in CLC 
was performed by experiments and DFT calcula- 
tions. XRD and XPS were employed to character- 
ize the evolution of oxygen carrier phases during 
the reduction. Two main reduction stages were ob- 
served for the reaction of CO with CuFe 2 O 4 . In the 
first stage, CuFe 2 O 4 is reduced into Cu and Fe 3 O 4 
with a high reaction rate. Notably, this stage begins 
with the surface oxygen consumption at low tem- 
perature (191 °C). In the second stage, the product 
Fe 3 O 4 is slowly transformed to FeO even to Fe. The 
whole reaction process of CuFe 2 O 4 with CO ex- 
hibits a superior reactivity as compared to Fe 2 O 3 , 
implying that the existence of Cu enhanced the re- 
activity of CuFe 2 O 4 . The CO concentration, tem- 
perature and heating rate can hardly affect the re- 
action stage. CO oxidation over the CuFe 2 O 4 (100) 
surface follows the Mars-van Krevelen mechanism: 
CO first interacts with a surface O, forming a CO 2 
directly (one-step reaction) or a surface CO 2 com- 
plex (two-step reaction) which bonds to the metal 
atoms, then CO 2 desorption occurs by surmount- 
ing an energy barrier. The kinetics of CO oxidation 
reaction on the CuFe 2 O 4 (100) surface is controlled 
by desorption. The surface O atom bonding to the 
Cu atom exhibits a superior activity, which can ex- 
plain the high reactivity observed in the TGA ex- 
periments.
