LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d, and BaySr1-yCoO3-d perovskite oxide powders were investigated as potential
thermochemical energy storage (TES) materials operated at high temperatures above 600 C. The pur-
pose of the research is to provide complete characterization of the impact of partial A- and B-site sub-
stitution on the reactivity, kinetics, redox reaction repeatability and charging/discharging storage
capacity. The perovskite oxides were investigated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential
scanning calorimetry (DSC) at temperatures of 500e1100 C. Thermal energy storage was evaluated in
terms of the enthalpy of the reversible reactions of oxygen release (reduction) and uptake (oxidation)
upon heating the oxide materials in air stream. Among the perovskites tested, Ba0.3Sr0.7CoO3-d and
Ba0.7Sr0.3CoO3-d powders were suitable thermochemical storage materials operating at above 600 C in
terms of chemical reactivity, charging/discharging temperatures and storage capacities, kinetics of ox-
ygen uptake/release, and repeatability of thermochemical cycling. Further, charging/discharging capacity
for both perovskites was comparable to that for Fe-doped manganese oxide.

1. Introduction
Thermal energy can be stored as sensible, latent, or thermo-
chemical heat [1]. Thermochemical heat storage (TCS) possesses
greater energy density than sensible or latent heat and can be used
at higher temperatures [2]. TCS consists of charging, storage, and
discharging steps. The heat provided by concentrated solar radia-
tion drives a reversible, endothermic chemical reaction during the
charging step, which stores the heat as reaction products. Heat is
recovered from the exothermic reverse of this chemical reaction
during the discharge step. The TCS system allows long-term and
round-the-clock operation in concentrated solar power (CSP) plant
to achieve CO2-free power generation. Redox pairs operating in the
range of 600e1000 C, which is much greater than the 600 C
operational limit of current state-of-the-art molten nitrate heat-
transfer ﬂuids, are consistent with the needs of next-generation
CSP plants [3e5]. 800e1000 C redox pairs are applicable to solar
methane reforming and gasiﬁcation of carbonaceous materials via
endothermic processes producing hydrogen and synthetic gas [6].
Thermodynamics, working temperature, energy storage capac-
ity, material costs, reaction kinetics, toxicity, and cyclic behavior are
important factors in the choice of the redox pair for TCS [7]. A redox
system based on a hercynite (FeAl2O4) cycle has been studied at the
University of Colorado [8], and a CuO/Cu2O redox pair has been
tested for TCS [9]. Wong evaluated various metal oxides as a TCS
medium by thermodynamic calculation and experimental analysis
[10]. Among the systems suitable for TCS, a Co3O4/CoO redox pair
exhibited the best re-oxidation kinetics, whereas a Mn2O3/Mn3O4
system exhibited cost-effectiveness and favorable redox tempera-
tures between 800 and 1000 C. The TCS performance of the Co3O4/
CoO redox pair has been studied from the viewpoint of thermo-
dynamic analysis, materials testing, and reactor/heat exchangers
[4,11e16]. The TCS properties of Co3O4 are consistent with the
operating temperatures of next-generation CSP plants [10] and
solar chemical reactors. A promising concept for CSP plants

however, has drawbacks of slow kinetics in its discharge step and

Perovskite oxides are thermodynamically more attractive than
Co3O4/CoO and doped manganese oxides as a redox material for
TCS [25e34]. Perovskite oxides of the general formula, ABO3, have
large concentrations of vacancies in their structure and can
reversibly accumulate and release oxygen at high temperatures. In
addition, their crystal structure is essentially invariant during the
high-temperature redox reaction that did not accompanies the
phase transition between different crystal structures, which results
in a small volume change upon oxygen uptake and release. Thus,
perovskite oxides should exhibit favorable redox cycle repeat-
ability. The TCS process using a perovskite oxide redox pair is
represented as:
Heat Charge (HC) step: ABO3 /ABO3-d
þ d/2O2 (endothermic, T1)

A and B are the A- and B-site ions in the ABO3 perovskite
structure, ABO3-d is the reduced oxide, and d is a stoichiometric
factor that deﬁnes the change in oxygen content during oxidation
and reduction. The cationic A and B sites can be substituted to
control changes in oxygen stoichiometry, kinetics, and thermody-
namic properties over a wide range of compositions. Perovskite
oxides remain in the solid state during redox reactions and retain
their structure over a wide range of oxygen stoichiometries.
Perovskite oxide reactivity is improved by A-site alkaline earth
or B-site transition metal cation substitution in the crystal struc-
ture, which creates oxygen vacancies and produces changes in
oxygen uptake/release capacities for TCS [25]. Babiniec et al. stud-
ied LaxSr1-xCoyM1-yO3-d (M ¼ Mn, Fe) families by thermogravi-
metric analysis (TGA) [26]. The HC step was performed at a heating
rate of 20 C/min from 200 to 1250 C followed by cooling of the
sample to 200 C in the HD step at rate of 20 C/min. Air and Ar
were used as heat transfer ﬂuids (HTFs) to evaluate reactivity. A La
content of x ¼ 0.3 provided the greatest redox reactivity in both
families. The authors reported that the B-site composition does not
affect redox reactivity. Ashoka et al. synthesized LaMO3 (M ¼ Cr,
Mn, Fe, Co, Ni) families [27], and Agraﬁotis et al. subjected LaxSr1-
xFeO3/LaxSr1-xFeO3-d redox pairs to TGA testing [28]. TGA testing
of La0.3Sr0.7FeO3 was performed cyclically between 1200 and 800 C
with a heating/cooling rate of 5 C/min. The HTFs were air in the 1st
cycle and Ar gas in the 2nd cycle. Imponenti et al. investigated Ca1-
xSrxMnO3-d (x ¼ 0, 0.05, 0.1) families as potential TCS materials by
TGA and reported the impact of A-site substitution [29]. Samples
were tested cyclically between 500 C in air and 900 C at low ox-
ygen partial pressure of PO2 ¼ 104 bar to evaluate durability.

Although LaSre and BaSr-based perovskites have been studied
as redox materials for TCS and as participants in thermochemical
H2O and H2O/CO2 splitting cycles [35e37], complete characteriza-
tion of the impact of partial A- and B-site substitution on the
reactivity, kinetics, and redox reaction repeatability in air has not
been performed for these materials. This study aims to ﬁll that gap
by providing information on the applicability of LaSre and BaSr-
based perovskites as TCS materials through studies of their
behavior in a stream of air. The impacts of partial A- and B-site
substitution on the thermodynamics, kinetics, and redox reaction
repeatability are compared and evaluated for both perovskite
families.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Preparation of LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d, and BaySr1-yCoO3-
d redox powders
LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d and BaySr1-yCoO3-d were synthesized by
a modiﬁed Pechini process [38]. The La/Sr ratio of perovskite was
expressed as “x” in the chemical formula, while the Ba/Sr ratio was
denoted as “y”. Stoichiometric amounts of La(NO3)3$9H2O (purity
99.9%), Sr(NO3)2 (purity 98.0%), Ba(NO3)$6H2O (purity 99.0%),
Mn(NO3)3$9H2O (purity 98.0%), Fe(NO3)3$6H2O (purity 99.9%), and
Co(NO3)$6H2O (purity 99.0%) were dissolved in deionized, distilled
water (200 mL). Ethylene glycol and citric acid were added to the
aqueous solution. The mole ratio of the sum of metallic cations,
ethylene glycol, and citric acid was 4:5:5. The solution was heated
at 80 C with stirring for 1 h, after which the temperature was
increased to 180 C for 0.5 h to form a gel. The gel was maintained
for 4.5 h for drying. The resulting back powder was calcined at
1200 C for 6 h in an air stream using an electric heater. The
chemical compositions of the LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d, and BaySr1-
yCoO3-d powders are listed in Table 1.
Solid phase identiﬁcation was performed by X-ray powder
diffraction (XRD) (MXeLabo, MAC Science) using CuKa radiation
(l ¼ 0.15418 nm) (40 kV - 25 mA) at room temperature. Diffraction

2.2. Reactivity and repeatability test of LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d and
BaySr1-yCoO3-d redox powders
The thermochemical storage performance of LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe,
Co)O3-d and BaySr1-yCoO3-d redox powders was examined by the
following procedure. Approximately 100 mg of sample powder was
packed into a platinum cup (5 mm diameter  5 mm deep) and
mounted on the balance in a ceramic reaction chamber. Fig.1 shows
the experimental set-up of the thermogravimetric reactor (Rigaku
Thermo plus EVO TG-DTA, weight resolution of 0.1 mg, temperature
resolution of ±0.0025  |t|
C) used for endothermic thermal
reduction (TR) and subsequent exothermic air oxidation (AO) of the
sample powder. The powder was ﬁrst heated to 1100 C at a rate of
30 C/min using an electric furnace while passing 99.9% synthetic
air through the reactor at a ﬂow rate of 0.2 dm3/min controlled by a
mass ﬂow controller (KOFLOC RK1700). The TR (heat charging)
reaction is accomplished in this step. The temperature of the
sample powder was monitored using an R-type thermocouple in
contact with the platinum cup. The powder was heated at a con-
stant temperature of 1100 C for 20 min and subsequently cooled to
500 C at a rate of 30 C/min at the air ﬂow indicated above. The
sample maintained at 500 C for 30 min in the electric furnace of
the reactor to complete the AO (heat discharging) step. The TR and
AO steps were repeated three to six times in sequence to study the
repeatability of charging and discharging. The sample powder
weight changed in the reactor furnace by O2 release during the TR
step and O2 uptake during the AO step. The weight change of the
powder was recorded as a function of time to monitor the fractional
extent of reaction in the TR and AO steps.

samples obtained after the energy storage cycle were examined by
powder XRD to identify the crystal phase.

3.2. Inﬂuence of A- and B -site partial substitution on oxygen
uptake/release behavior
Fig. 3(a) shows the thermogravimetric proﬁles of La0.3Sr0.7CoO3-
d (LS37C) and Ba0.3Sr0.7CoO3-d (BS37C). The results illustrate the
effect of La or Ba substitution at the A site of perovskite oxide
(A0.3Sr0.7CoO3-d, A ¼ La, Ba). The weight of LS37C decreases linearly
with increasing temperature from 350 to 1100 C due to thermal
reduction and remains constant at 1100 C. The weight loss upon
reduction is nearly a ¼ 2%. When temperature is decreased from
1100 to 500 C, the weight increases linearly due to oxygen uptake
and remains constant at 500 C. The weight gain increases to within
a ¼ 0.34% of the initial value. The reduction-oxidation cycle was
repeated 3 times with similar results. The weight of BS37C rapidly
decreases at 900 C and then continues to decrease with increasing
temperature from 900 to 1100 C, before stabilizing at 1100 C.
When the temperature of BS37C is decreased from 1100 to 500 C,
the sample weight increases monotonically, but a sharp spike is
reproducibly observed between 750 and 500 C. The overall weight
changes of BS37C upon reduction and oxidation are similar to those
of LS37C. The results suggest that ionic species of the A site of
perovskite oxide does not impact on reactivity but charging/dis-
charging rate. However, a substitution of different valance states
(La3þ, Ba2þ, Sr2þ) in the A site of perovskite generally induces ox-
ygen vacancy, leading to improving reactivity. Thus, the authors
will quantitatively compare and evaluate OSC based on the weight

3.3. Inﬂuence of A- and B -site partial substitution on oxygen
storage capacity and reaction rate
Fig. 4(a) shows the redox reactivity of AxSr1-xCoO3-d (A ¼ La, Ba;
x ¼ 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) during the 1st-3rd cycles of TR and AO. The b values
of oxygen storage capacity were calculated by eq. (4) from the
weight change of each material for each step of the three cycles. The
results illustrate the redox reactivity and repeatability upon La and
Ba substitution and changes in composition at site A of AxSr1-xCoO3-
d. The absolute magnitude of b decreases with increasing La content
in the LaxSr1-xCoO3-d series (LS37C, LS55C, and LS73C). However,
the opposite behavior is observed upon increasing the Ba content in
BaxSr1-xCoO3-d series (BS37C, BS55C, and BS73C). The repeatability
of both series is nearly the same as that reported above based on
the observation that b for the TR step is only slightly smaller in
cycles 2 and 3 than in cycle 1. The AxSr1-xCoO3-d results mean that a
large Ba ion population at site A produces good redox reactivity and
repeatability. As seen in Fig. 4(a), the redox reactivity of both steps
for LS37C is comparable for to BS37C. The results are good agree-
ment with the results for Fig. 3(a). However, the redox reactivity for
LS73C is quite different from that for BS73C. The results indicate
that Sr-substituted A2þB4þO3-d structure is higher OSC than Sr-
substituted A3þB3þO3-d structure. For BaxSr1-xCoO3-d series, Ba2þ-
Sr2þ substitution cannot enhance oxygen vacancy in the perovskite,
but the difference of ionic radius between Sr2þ (1.44 Å) and Ba2þ
(1.61 Å) may alleviate lattice distortion of perovskite and induce
oxygen uptake/release.
Fig. 4(b) shows b values for La0.5Sr0.5BO3-d (B ¼ Mn, Fe, Co) for
the 1st-3rd cycles of TR and AO. The results describe the redox
reactivity and repeatability produced by substitution of Mn, Fe, and
Co at site B of La0.5Sr0.5BO3-d. The average values of b are 0.015,
0.094, and 0.176 for LS55M, LS55F, and LS55C, respectively. Redox
reactivity depends on the identity of the species at the B site. Cobalt
substitution produces the greatest redox reactivity, which is 12
times greater than that of Mn and 1.9 times greater than that of Fe.
Repeatability is not inﬂuenced by the ion at site B. A tolerance
factors for La0.5Sr0.5BO3-d series are in a range of 0.97e1.02, indi-
cating that the perovskites are small lattice distortion and stable
cubic crystal structure. In the case of LaxSr1-xCoO3-d series, oxygen
vacancy increases with increasing Sr contents due to La3þ-Sr2þ
substitution, leading to enhancement of OSC in the perovskite.
Reaction rate of oxygen release was measured for the perov-
skites during TR step of thermochemical cycles. Fig. 5 shows values
of db/dt as a function of temperature during the 1st-3rd cycles of TR
for (a) LS37C, LS55C, and LS73C and (b) BS73C, BS55C, and BS37C.
The proﬁles of all three cycles overlap. As seen in Fig. 5(a), there are
no major endothermic peaks in the proﬁles. This observation in-
dicates that the kinetics of endothermic oxygen release are slow
and independent of composition for LaxSr1-xCoO3-d (x ¼ 0.3, 0.5,
0.7) and that the reaction proceeds at a nearly constant rate across a
wide temperature range as seen in Fig. 3(b). On the other hand, as
seen in Fig. 5(b), the proﬁles of BaxSr1-xCoO3-d (x ¼ 0.3, 0.5, 0.7)
exhibit a sharp endothermic peak at 860e980 C during TR. The
result means that a series of the perovskite can cyclically charge
heat from air stream at speciﬁc temperatures. The response at
1000 C for BS55C is the artifact of a programed temperature
change in the thermogravimetry unit and does not represent an
authentic endothermic reaction. The peak values of db/dt at
860e980 C are 0.176 (BS73C), 0.130 (BS37C), and 0.081 min1
(BS55C). The order of charging rate was not identical to that of
reactivity as seen in Fig. 3 (b). The facts for both series indicate that
a reaction rate for TR is not correlated with OSC, and chemical
composition and temperature are dominant factors for charging/
discharging rate in a thermochemical cycle. The oxygen release
kinetics of BS73C is superior to those of the other examples, and its
peak position is almost unchanged throughout the 1st-3rd cycles.
Reaction rate of oxygen uptake was measured for the perov-
skites during AO step of thermochemical cycles. Fig. 6 shows the
variations in db/dt versus temperature during the 1st-3rd cycles of
AO for (a) LS37C, LS55C, and LS73C and (b) BS73C, BS55C, and
BS37C. The proﬁles of all cycles overlap. As seen in Fig. 6(a), there
are no major peaks in the proﬁles as well as the 1st-3rd cycles of TR
as seen in Fig. 5(a). This result indicates that the exothermic uptake
of oxygen is kinetically slow and independent of chemical
composition for LaxSr1-xCoO3-d (x ¼ 0.3, 0.5, 0.7). From the results of
Figs. 5(a) and 6(a), the LaxSr1-xCoO3-d series provided slow thermal
response over the wide temperature range for TR and AO in air
stream, although an enough OSC based on weight change was
observed in the thermogravimetric analysis. The heat-charging/
discharging capacity for the series was validated using DSC anal-
ysis in following section.
Deﬁnitive peaks are observed in Fig. 6(b) for BaxSr1-xCoO3-
d (x ¼ 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) with maximum values of db/dt ¼ 0.157 (BS73C),
0.096 (BS55C), and 0.056 min1 (BS37C). The db/dt peak maxima
increase with increasing Ba content at site A. Moreover, the peak
position shifts to higher temperatures, and the peak shape
sharpens as the Ba content increases. The results indicate that a
tendency of heat-discharging kinetics for BaxSr1-xCoO3-d series is a
different from heat-charging kinetics (Fig. 5(b)). It appears that the
order of discharging rate was identical to that of redox reactivity as
seen in Fig. 3 (b). The kinetics of oxygen uptake depend on the
chemical composition at the A site with BS73C producing the
greatest reaction rate over the smallest temperature range. Thus,
BaxSr1-xCoO3-d series provided quick thermal response over the
wide temperature range for TR and AO in air stream as well as redox
reactivity based on weight change in the thermogravimetric anal-
ysis. The heat-charging/discharging capacity for BaxSr1-xCoO3-d se-
ries will be validated using DSC analysis in following section.

4. Conclusions
Thermochemical energy storage of LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d and
BaySr1-yCoO3-d perovskite oxides was studied to develop a TES
operating at temperatures above 600 C. The materials were cycli-
cally tested under a stream of air without inert gas to evaluate the
impact of partial substitution at their A and B sites on the reactivity,
kinetics, and repeatability of oxygen uptake and release. XRD
demonstrated that A- and B-site substitutions in LaxSr1-x(Mn, Fe,
Co)O3-d series are accommodated within the perovskite oxide cubic
crystal structure without phase transformation. A series of vaterite-
type oxide BaySr1-yCoO3-d materials with a hexagonal crystal
structure was also prepared and investigated.
The reactivity and repeatability of LaxSr1-x (Mn, Fe, Co)O3-d, and
BaySr1-yCoO3-d redox powders were examined in a thermogravi-
metric reactor. The oxygen uptake/release kinetics of both series is
evaluated using weight changing proﬁles. The charging/discharg-
ing capacity is measured for redox reaction in air stream by using
DSC device. BS37C provides a greatest charging capacity among
BaxSr1-xCoO3-d series, and BS37C and BS73C exhibited relatively
large discharging capacity. The tendency of heat capacity was not
directly related with that of OSC and reaction rate. Thermochemical
capacity should be evaluated by direct measurement of charging/
discharging heat using DSC device. The charging capacity for BS37C
and BS73C is similar level to Fe-doped manganese oxides that are
superior to Mn2O3/Mn3O4 in terms of repeatability and charging/
discharging capacity. Especially, the charging/discharging capacity
for BS37C is greater than Fe-doped manganese oxides. BS73C is one
of suitable thermochemical storage materials in terms of OSC and
charging/discharging kinetics, while BS37C is superior to BS73C
with regard to reactivity and storage capacity. Their materials can
be chosen depending on charging/discharging temperature level
above 600 C.
