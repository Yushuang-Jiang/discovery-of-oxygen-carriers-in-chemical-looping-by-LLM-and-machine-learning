A B S T R A C T
In this study, Ni-doped iron oxide (NixFe3−xO4) materials were synthesized via the 1,2-epoxypropane assisted
sol-gel method by varying the molar concentration of Ni from x=0.2 to 1. Sol-gel derived NixFe3−xO4 gels were
dried and the dried powder was further calcined upto 600 °C in air for 90 min. Obtained calcined NixFe3−xO4
powders were further analyzed to determine the phase composition, crystallite size, speciﬁc surface area, pore
volume, and morphology via powder X-ray diﬀraction (PXRD), BET surface area analysis (BET), as well as
scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM and TEM). The obtained results in the synthesis and
characterization section indicate formation of NixFe3−xO4 nanoparticles with high speciﬁc surface area. Thermal
reduction and re-oxidation of the sol-gel synthesized NixFe3−xO4 materials were determined by using the high
temperature thermogravimetry. Obtained results indicate that the amount of O2 released during the thermal
reduction step (at 1400 °C) and quantity of CO produced during the CO2 splitting step (at 1000 °C) increases as
the concentration of Ni inside the iron oxide crystal structure increases. The highest amounts of O2 released
(221.88 μmol/g) and CO produced (375.01 μmol/g) in case of NiFe2O4 (NF10 material).
1. Introduction
Syngas i.e. a mixture of H2 and CO is utilized as the feedstock for
the production of liquid transportation fuels such as gasoline, diesel,
and jet fuel via the Fischer Tropsch process [1]. Solar driven thermo-
chemical splitting of H2O and CO2 using metal oxide based redox
reactions is considered as a promising new technology for the produc-
tion of syngas, bypassing the utilization of fossil fuels [2,3]. As only
(atmospheric) CO2 and H2O are consumed, the syngas produced can be
considered as a renewable product. Among the various metal oxides
investigated in past, ferrites (doped iron oxides) are considered as
potential materials due to their excellent redox reactivity and the high
fuel production capacity.
In recent years, several ferrite including Ni-ferrite, Zn-ferrite, Co-
ferrite, Ni-Zn-ferrite, Ni-Mn-ferrite, Cu-ferrite, and others have been
studied towards thermochemical H2O splitting cycles [4–22] and
among all these, Ni-ferrite has been investigated heavily. Sano et al.
[23] synthesized a carbon bearing Ni-ferrite for the production of H2
via thermochemical H2O splitting reaction at 300 °C. Kodama et al.[24]
reported that the Ni-ferrite supported on ZrO2 exhibits higher activity
and a sustainable H2 production in multiple water splitting cycles due
to the alleviation of sintering. A solar heated, internally circulating
ﬂuidized bed reactor containing Ni-ferrite/ZrO2 particles as the
reactive medium for the water splitting reaction was successfully
designed and tested by Gokon et al.[25]. Allendorf et al.[7] performed
the thermodynamic analysis of the thermal reduction and re-oxidation
of Ni-ferrite by thermochemical water splitting cycle using the FactSage
thermodynamic software package. Fresno et al.[26] compared the
activity for H2 production of commercially available ferrites such as
Ni-ferrite, Zn-ferrite, Cu-ferrite, Ni-Zn-ferrite and Cu-Zn-ferrite (pur-
chased from Sigma Aldrich) by performing thermochemical water
splitting cycles and reported that Ni-ferrite produces the highest
amount of H2 compared to other ferrites. Agraﬁotis et al.[4] synthe-
sized diﬀerent ferrite materials by the combustion synthesis and
reported that the Ni-ferrite and Co-ferrite are the two most promising
candidates as they are capable of producing the highest amounts of H2
and of O2 compared to other ferrite based redox materials. Agraﬁotis
et al.[4] also investigated the kinetics of ferrite based water splitting

To produce syngas, it is essential that the redox system is also
capable towards the production of CO in addition to H2. Therefore,
diﬀerent Ni-ferrites are assessed in this study for their ability to
produce CO from CO2. The eﬀect of Ni incorporation in the iron oxide
crystal structure towards the thermochemical CO2 splitting reaction is
investigated by comparing the Ni-ferrites with diﬀerent Ni content. The
NixFe3−xO4 redox materials (where, x=0.2 to 1) were synthesized using
a 1,2-epoxypropane assisted sol-gel method. The redox inactive Fe+2 is
replaced by Ni+2 and due to this, it is expected that the redox reactivity
in terms of O2 release and CO production will be higher in case of
NixFe3−xO4 compared to pure iron oxide (Fe3O4). The derived
NixFe3−xO4 redox materials are characterized using the powder XRD
(PXRD), BET surface area analysis (BET), as well as scanning and
transmission electron microscopy (SEM and TEM). The thermal
reduction and re-oxidation by CO2 is assessed by using a thermogravi-
metric analyzer (TGA). It is noteworthy that, several researchers
investigated Ni-ferrite for other applications [27–31], however; the
sol-gel synthesized Ni-ferrite was never studied towards thermoche-
mical CO2 splitting reaction.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials
The sol-gel synthesis of NixFe3−xO4 was performed by using
following precursors and chemicals: nitrate salts of nickel and iron,
ethanol as the solvent, 1,2-epoxypropane as the proton scavenger
(gelation agent). All of these materials were purchased from Sigma
Aldrich and Alfa Aesar. No pretreatment was carried out on any of
these materials.
2.2. Synthesis of NixFe3−xO4 redox materials
Diﬀerent NixFe3−xO4 redox active materials (x=0.2 to 1) were
synthesized by applying the 1,2-epoxypropane assisted sol-gel method.
The value of x=1.0 represents the complete replacement of the redox-
inactive Fe2+ by Ni2+ in the ferrite structure. Later on, the following

dissolution, 20 ml of 1,2-epoxypropane was added slowly to the mixed
solution and the gel formation was induced. The gel was aged at room
temperature for 24 h and then dried at 100 °C for 2 h using a
temperature-controlled hot plate. The dried powders were calcined in
a muﬄe furnace at 600 °C for 90 min in air to achieve crystalline phase
formation. The obtained calcined powders were stored in dry atmo-
sphere and characterized using diﬀerent analytical methods. The sol-
gel synthesis approach is schematically summarized in Fig. 1.
2.3. Characterization of NixFe3−xO4 redox materials
A Panalytical XPert MPD/DY636 powder X-ray diﬀractometer was
used to determine the compositional purity and crystallite size (by
using Scherrer's equation) of the synthesized NixFe3−xO4 materials. A
BET surface area analyzer, Micromeritics ASAP 2420 was used to
determine the speciﬁc surface area (SSA) and pore volume of the
NixFe3−xO4 materials. The morphology of the synthesized NixFe3−xO4
material was scrutinized using a Zeiss Supra 55VP ﬁeld-emission
scanning electron microscope (SEM). In addition, the formation of
NixFe3−xO4 nanoparticles was conﬁrmed by using a high resolution
transmission electron microscope (TECNAI G2 F20 and JEM-2010).

409). The details about the set-up used have been published in our
recent investigations [32,33]. Approximately, 50 mg of Ni-ferrite
powder was placed in an Al2O3 crucible and this crucible was
introduced into the TGA. The TGA temperature was increased upto
1400 °C at a rate of 25 °C/min and the TGA was held at this
temperature for 1 h in an Ar ﬂow of 100 ml/min. The observed
decrease of the mass was directly attributed to the amount of O2

3. Results and discussion
In Fig. 2, we report the PXRD patterns of the calcined Ni-ferrite
powders. The patterns are characterized by strong reﬂections indicat-
ing highly crystalline samples. All reﬂections observed can be attrib-
uted to the cubic structure of the spinel phase. Notably, peaks that
could be attributed to impurity phases such as NiO are completely
absent. Thus, the PXRD patterns conﬁrm complete incorporation of
Ni2+ into the Fe3O4 crystal structure. As the concentration of nickel
increases, the peaks shift to lower angles conﬁrming the formation of
NixFe3−xO4 with an expanded crystal lattice.
Close inspection of the PXRD peaks revealed that the peak width
increases with the increase in the Ni2+ concentration. This observation
indicates that the crystallite size decreases as more Ni2+ was incorpo-
rated into the ferrite crystal structure. The quantitative evaluation of
the crystallite size (see Fig. 3) was performed by using the Scherrer

The speciﬁc surface area and the pore volume of the Ni-ferrites as

The morphology of all the sol-gel derived NixFe3−xO4 materials was
analyzed using SEM and TEM. The SEM analysis shows formation of
smaller grains and indicated nanoparticles morphology of sol-gel
derived NixFe3−xO4. TEM analysis further conﬁrms the formation of
NixFe3−xO4 nanoparticles in the range of 10–50 nm. No evidence of the
eﬀect of Ni incorporation in the iron oxide crystal structure on the
morphology of the NixFe3−xO4 materials synthesized was observed.
Fig. 5a and b show representative SEM and TEM images for NF8 redox
material. Similar material morphology was observed in case of other
NixFe3−xO4 and hence the SEM and TEM images for these materials are
not included in this paper.
The activity of the Ni-ferrites materials towards production of CO
via the thermochemical CO2 splitting reaction was examined using a
high temperature TGA. The typical TGA proﬁle obtained during the
thermal reduction of NF8 is shown in Fig. 6. The overall loss in the Ni-
ferrite mass was computed from the weight diﬀerence between the
starting ferrite material and the material left over after performing
thermal reduction for 60 min at 1400 °C. The mass of Ni-ferrite
material decreases due to the release of O2 during thermal reduction
resulting into formation of O2 vacancies in the NixFe3−xO4 crystal
structure. The mass loss (Δmloss) was determined by using TGA proﬁles
and this mass loss was converted into the amount of O2 released by
using Eq. (1). In case of NF2, the percentage mass loss was 0.20% and
as the nickel content increases, the percentage mass loss increases
considerably. The highest mass loss was observed in the case of NF10
where all Fe2+ has been substituted by Ni2+.

The obtained results indicate that substitution of Fe+2 by Ni+2 in
Fe3O4 has a beneﬁcial eﬀect. For low substitution levels up to about
60%, only a small increase of the redox capacity in terms of the amount
of O2 that can be removed from the crystal structure is observed. At
high substitution levels and in particular in the fully substituted
NiFe2O4 (NF10), a drastic increase of the redox performance is
experienced.
4. Summary and conclusions
The eﬀect of the substitution of Fe+2 by Ni+2 in iron oxide crystal
structure on its physico-chemical properties and CO2 splitting ability
was investigated in detail. The PXRD patterns indicate that the sol-gel
synthesis results into the formation of phase pure NixFe3−xO4 with
highly crystalline spinel cubic structure in absence of any impurity
phases such as NiO. Nickel substitution leads to a signiﬁcant decrease
in the crystallite size from about 28 nm for NF2 to 20 nm for the fully
substituted NF10. The decrease in the crystallite size is accompanied by
an increase in the speciﬁc surface area and the pore volume by 28.76%
and 54.08%, respectively. The SEM and TEM analysis conﬁrms the
formation of highly crystalline NixFe3−xO4 nanoparticles. The thermo-
gravimetric experiments indicate that the redox performance of Ni-
ferrite increases with increasing nickel content. Interestingly, substitu-
tion of less than about 60% of the Fe+2 results into slightly improved
redox performance of Ni-ferrite. At higher substitution levels and most
notably in the case of the fully substituted NiFe2O4, the redox
performance of the Ni-ferrite material drastically increases. CO pro-
duction is enhanced by a factor of 6.67 in case of fully substituted NF10
in comparison to the least substituted NF2. Not only does the amount
of CO produced increases but also the ratio of CO/O2 increases by
46.75% as the substitution level increases from x=0.2 to 1. The best
performance was observed with NiFe2O4 (NF10) when compared to the
other sol-gel derived NixFe3−xO4. However, it is important to note that
the present study only concerns an initial assessment as the materials
are only subjected to one complete thermochemical cycle. For a more
complete assessment and to allow a comparison with previously
investigated ceria based redox materials, it will be essential to
investigate the redox reactivity and thermal stability of the sol-gel
derived NixFe3−xO4 materials in multiple thermochemical cycles. This
study is the subject of our ongoing work.
