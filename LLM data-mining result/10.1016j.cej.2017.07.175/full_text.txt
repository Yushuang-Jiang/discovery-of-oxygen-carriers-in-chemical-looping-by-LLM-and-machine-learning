A B S T R A C T
Chemical looping is a promising low-cost technology for CO2 capture with a low energy penalty. In this study,
CuO supported on olivine (CuO/olivine) was investigated as an oxygen carrier (OC) in chemical looping com-
bustion (CLC) and in chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU). The reactivity and oxygen uncoupling
behavior were evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). CLC and CLOU experiments using pine sawdust
(PS) as fuel were conducted in a ﬂuidized-bed reactor. The CLC cyclic performance and the oxygen release and
uptake behaviors of CuO/olivine were explored. In CLC experiments, both carbon conversion (χc) and carbon
capture eﬃciency (ηc) increased with increasing temperature and with increases in OC-to-fuel ratio (Φ); the
maximum value of χc and ηc—96.81% and 95.54%, respectively—were achieved at 900 °C with Φ = 1.50. In
CLC cyclic tests, the CuO/olivine displayed stable reactivity, and the values of χc and ηc were 94.64% and
92.59% at the 20th cycle, respectively. In the CLOU tests, oxygen transport capacity (Ro) of 3.55% was obtained
via TGA. Complete conversion of pine PS and ηc = 99.29% were also achieved at 950 °C in a ﬂuidized bed
reactor. MgCu2O4 was found in the fresh CuO/olivine sample and it disappeared immediately during the ﬁrst
redox cycle; most of CuO accumulated on the olivine surface and then gradually spread and covered the olivine
upon additional cycles. After 20 CLC cycles. CuO content of the CuO/olivine OC decreased slightly from 38.81%
to 35.22%; a crushing strength of 4.8 N was maintained, and no signiﬁcant agglomeration was observed. The
CuO/olivine has an excellent cyclic performance and it can be used as a good OC candidate in chemical looping
processes.
1. Introduction
Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technologies have received ex-
tensive attention over the past few decades as a potential way to reduce
CO2 emissions. CCS technologies are aimed at capturing CO2, trans-
porting it and ﬁnally storing it away from the atmosphere. Three major
CO2 capture technologies—oxy-fuel combustion, pre-combustion and
post-combustion—involve high-energy-consuming and high-cost gas
separation steps. Chemical looping combustion (CLC) is a novel tech-
nology for CO2 capture in which CO2 separation is inherent. In CLC, fuel
combustion occurs in the fuel reactor (FR) where oxygen is provided by
an oxygen carrier (OC) instead of by air. The fuel and air are never
mixed during this process, circumventing the need for gas separation
steps. A high concentration of CO2 can be obtained by condensing the
ﬂue gas. The reduced OC in the FR is transferred into the air reactor
(AR) where it is oxidized to its initial state by air.
Chemical looping technologies have been reviewed in detailed by
Adanez et al. [1]. Most research eﬀorts concerning the chemical looping
processes have been focused on gaseous fuels. Chemical looping with
solid fuels, such as biomass [2,3], coal [4,5] and biomass char [6], has
drawn increasing attention in recent years. Biomass is a promising
neutral renewable energy resource for the reduction of CO2 emissions.
Even negative CO2 emissions can be achieved in biomass CLC, because
the CO2 is previously absorbed by photosynthesis. Compared with coal,
biomass has low N and S contents, which reduce SO2 and NOx emis-
sions.
OCs are the cornerstone of any chemical looping processes. More
than 1000 OCs have been reported. Cu-based OCs have been shown to
be a promising OC candidate because high oxygen transport capacity

However, the reactivity, stability and mechanical properties of Cu-

A naturally occurring iron-magnesium mineral, olivine, has been
extensively used in biomass gasiﬁcation as a support or as a catalyst
because of its low cost, high mechanical strength and high reactivity
toward tar destruction [28–31]. The chemical looping capabilities of
olivine has been reported and is attributed to the iron segregates out of
the olivine matrix forming free iron oxide phases, which are capable of
transferring oxygen to the reactor [32]. However, the literature con-
tains relatively few studies devoted to the use of olivine as a bed ma-
terial in chemical looping processes.
In this study, CuO supported on calcined olivine (i.e. CuO/olivine)
was evaluated as an OC in CLC and CLOU. The reactivity and oxygen
uncoupling behavior of the CuO/olivine were investigated by thermo-
gravimetric analysis (TGA). The CLC and CLOU experiments were
conducted in a ﬂuidized-bed reactor using pine sawdust (PS) as fuel.
The carbon conversion (χc), CO2 capture eﬃciency (ηc) and gas con-
centration were measured. The cyclic performance of the CuO/olivine
was further investigated. Finally, physical properties of the CuO/olivine
carrier were characterized using various techniques.
2. Experimental
2.1. Biomass feedstock
PS was used as the biomass feedstock and obtained from a wood
factory in Bijie City, Guizhou Province, China. The PS sample was
naturally dried, crushed, and passed through a 150 mesh sieve, re-
sulting in a particle size of less than 104 μm. The ultimate analysis and
proximate analysis and the ash oxides content were determined, results
are shown in Table 1. Ultimate analysis gave the weight percent of
carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, and sulfur in the sample simultaneously.
The weight percent of oxygen was determined by diﬀerence.
2.2. Preparation of the CuO/olivine OC
Raw olivine was collected from Yichang City, Hubei Province,
China. The raw olivine was crushed and calcined at 1000 °C for 6 h
under an air atmosphere, and then the resulting product was cooled,
ground and sieved into particles with a size range of 100–250 μm to
obtain the calcined olivine sample. The composition of the calcined
olivine sample was determined by X-ray ﬂuorescence (XRF); results are
listed in Table 4.
It was veriﬁed that the formation of Cu was responsible for the
agglomeration process of the Cu-based OCs in CLC process. The high
content of CuO-based OC increases the risk of agglomeration in che-
mical looping processes. The agglomeration can be avoided by using
lower loading amount of CuO, while a low content of CuO will lead to a
high solids circulation rate and a high solids inventory in the reactor.
Additionally, the calcine olivine has a lower porosity in general. Higher
loading amount of CuO may have contributed to the enrichment of
copper on the surface of olivine, which further increase the risk of
agglomeration. In this study, CuO/olivine OC with a mass fraction of
40 wt% CuO was synthesized by the impregnation method. The pro-
cedure of impregnation method was brieﬂy described below. First, Cu
(NO3)2·3H2O and calcined olivine were accurately weighted with a
mass ratio of Cu(NO3)2·3H2O/calcined olivine of 2.03. The Cu
(NO3)2·3H2O (reagent grade) was put into a beaker, and then an
amount of deionized water was added to make a nitrate aqueous so-
lution. After that, the prepared aqueous solution was transferred to
another beaker, where the calcined olivine powder was placed.
Subsequently, the mixture was stirred slowly for 10 h at 80 °C. Next, the
sample was dried at 105 °C for 48 h to remove water, and then calcined

2.3. TGA
2.3.1. Reactivity with biomass in CLC
The reaction reactivity of OC and biomass was investigated in a TGA
(SDT Q600, TA Instruments). First, the PS was mixed thoroughly with
the OC in a mortar at a mass ratio of 1:15. Second, approximately 15 mg
sample was added to a ceramic crucible, heated from room temperature
to 950 °C in a nitrogen environment and then maintained at this tem-
perature for 20 min. The heating rate and the nitrogen ﬂow were 20 °C/
min and 80 ml/min, respectively.
2.3.2. Oxygen transport capacity in CLOU
The RO of CuO/olivine was measured using TGA. In this test, CuO
released all of gaseous oxygen and then it was completely reduced into
Cu2O; the olivine was acting as an inert support. First, approximately of
15 mg CuO/olivine was heated in air (80 ml/min, consisting of 20.8%
O2 in N2) from ambient temperature to 950 °C at a heating rate of
20 °C/min, and then maintained at this temperature for 15 min. The
gaseous oxygen was released by the CuO/olivine in nitrogen (80 ml/
min, 99.999% N2) for 60 min. Subsequently, nitrogen was switched into
air (80 ml/min, consisting of 20.8% O2 in N2) for 15 min to oxidize the
CuO/olivine. A total of 10 redox cycles were performed.
2.4. Fluidized-bed tests
The biomass CLC and CLOU experiments were performed in a la-
boratory-scale ﬂuidized-bed reactor. The experimental setup of the
ﬂuidized-bed reactor system is illustrated in Fig. 1; more details of this
reactor system are available elsewhere [33].
At the start of each experiment, the PS sample was loaded in the
biomass feeder (PEF-90AL, Sanki Co. Ltd., Japan), and the feeding rate
was determined by adjusting the vibrational frequency of the biomass
feeder. The OC particles were placed on the porous distributor plate of
ﬂuidized-bed reactor, heated from room temperature to the desired
temperature with an air ﬂow rate of 600 ml/min. When the tempera-
ture stabilized, nitrogen ﬂow (100 ml/min) was used to purge the
biomass feeder from the top; this ﬂowing nitrogen could be used as a
balance gas during the feeding process. Additional nitrogen ﬂow
(600 ml/min) was supplied to the reactor from the bottom as a ﬂui-
dizing gas. After 5 min, the PS was fed into the reactor and reacted with
the OC. After the reaction, the nitrogen ﬂowing from the bottom of the
ﬂuidized-bed reactor was switched into air (600 ml/min) to oxidize the
reduced OC particles.
The ﬂue gases passed through a cyclone separator, a condenser and
an on-line gas analyzer (Gasboard-3100) in sequence. The ﬁne particles
were collected; the condensable components of ﬂue gases were re-
moved; and the gas concentrations (including CO, CO2, CH4, H2, CnHm
(2 ≤n ≤3, m ≤8) and O2) were analyzed and recorded continuously
during this process.

oxygen release from the OC and combustion of residual char. The
oxygen release earlier than combustion of the residual char, thus, the

(912.79 °C) of CuO/olivine without PS.
To evaluate the eﬀect of CLC cyclic tests on the reactivity of the
CuO/olivine, the CuO/olivine after 20 CLC redox cycles was analyzed.
This mixture exhibited the highest mass-loss rate (2.103 wt%/min) at
368.30 °C, which is likely attributable to the multiple redox cycles
changing the distribution of CuO, thereby enhancing the reactions of
volatiles with the CuO. After 20 cycles, the ﬁnal mass loss (89.15 wt%)
of CuO/olivine and PS was slightly lower than that (89.85 wt%) of with
fresh CuO/olivine and PS, this is attributed to a portion of the CuO was
lost during the redox processes.
3.1.2. ﬂuidized-bed tests
Biomass CLC tests were conducted in a ﬂuidized-bed reactor be-
tween 700 °C to 900 °C. In each run, PS powders (3.0 g) were fed at a
rate of 0.20 g/min, and the reduction time was 25 min. Four OC-to-fuel
ratios (Φ = 1.0, 1.10, 1.25 and 1.50) were used, corresponding to the
CuO/olivine masses of 48.0 g, 53.0 g, 60.0 g and 72.0 g, respectively. A
total 3 cycles was performed at each Φ by feeding additional PS pow-
ders at the start of new cycle; the χc and ηc were taken as the average
values.

more OC was used, the higher solids inventory was required in the
reactor.

3.2. CLOU tests
3.2.1. Oxygen transport capacity evaluated by TGA
The ability of the OCs to maintain oxygen transport capacity over
many oxidation/reduction cycles was evaluated in the TGA by cycling
the gas between air and nitrogen; results for 10 cycles of the CuO/
olivine carrier at 950 °C are shown in Fig. 6. The ﬁnal weight loss in-
creased with increasing number of cycles during the ﬁrst four cycles in
the reduction process, potentially due to the decomposition of olivine.
The weight variation of CuO/olivine between the reduced and oxidized
processes was nearly constant after the 4th cycle, conﬁrming that the
CuO/olivine had a stable reactivity in the CLOU process. A Ro value of
3.59% was obtained for the10th cycle, corresponding to a 35.67 wt%
theoretical weight fraction of CuO. This CuO content was lower than
that of fresh CuO/olivine (38.18 wt%, see Section 3.3.4) because the
CuO was lost during the redox process. Pilar Gayán et al. [34] in-
vestigated the Ro of ﬁve OCs which CuO was supported on Al2O3- and
MgAl2O4-supportedCuO OCs synthesized using an incipient wet im-
pregnation method, and the maximum value of Ro, 3.30%, was ob-
served for Cu33γ-Al_850 at 1000 °C, which is lower than the RO of CuO/
olivine at 950 °C. Additionally, RO values for two other CuO/olivine
samples derived from the 10th and 20th cycles in CLC cyclic tests were
further investigated. The RO of CuO/olivine after 10 and 20 CLC cycles
were 3.44% and 3.37% (results not shown), respectively. These results
indicated that the CLC tests only weakly aﬀected the oxygen uncoupling
performance.

3.2.3. CLOU ﬂuidized-bed tests with PS
The CLOU ﬂuidized-bed tests with PS were conducted at 950 °C
using 1.0 g of PS and 60.0 g of CuO/olivine in each experiment. The PS
was fed into the reactor in a shorter period time. The gas concentrations
are displayed in Fig. 8, the concentration of CO2 ﬁrst increased sub-
stantially, and then decreased sharply. CO, CnHm and tar were not de-
tected during the reduction process, although, a small peak of H2 and
the trace amounts of CH4 resulting from devolatilization of PS [37]. An
oxygen peak was detected after PS feeding during the reduction pro-
cess, which we attributed to the exothermic reaction of PS with CuO.
This reaction resulted in an increase in the reactor temperature, which
promoted the release of oxygen. Similar results have been reported by
Mattisson et al. [38] and Zhao et al. [39]. No gas other than oxygen was
present during the oxidation process. Full conversion of PS and a very
high carbon capture eﬃciency, ηc = 99.29%, were achieved in the fuel
reactor. In this case, unburnt compounds were present barely in the fuel
reactor outlet. Thus, the CLOU process should not require special
measures such as an oxygen-polishing step.

3.3.4. XRF analyses
The elemental contents of the OCs were determined by XRF; the
oxide contents are listed in Table 4. CuO content of fresh CuO/olivine,
38.18 wt%, was slightly lower than the theoretical value of 40.00 wt%,
and CuO content decreased to 35.22 wt% after 20 redox cycles. A small
portion of CuO was lost during the OC preparation process and the
redox cycles process. The loss of CuO is unfavorable for the chemical
looping processes because it may cause a decrease in oxygen transport
capacity. Compared with fresh CuO/olivine, the CuO/olivine after 20
redox cycles exhibited slight increases in the Fe2O3, Cr2O3, NiO and
Al2O3 contents; these increases are attributable to Fe, Cr, Ni and Al
were being transferred to the surface during the redox cycles [32]. This
transfer is conducive to the chemical looping process because some of
these species can act as OCs and catalysts. It also may explain why both
χc and ηc values were maintained higher level after 20 redox cycles even
a fraction of CuO was lost in CLC process.
The contributions of the active components in olivine are not dis-
cussed in the study because low content of them; further studies will be
needed to explore the function of olivine in chemical looping processes.
In addition, the eﬀects of ash on the reactivity of CuO/olivine were
insigniﬁcant, possibly because of the low content (1.09 wt%) of ash in
PS. Role of ash in CuO/olivine chemical looping processes should be
further explored in future studies.
4. Conclusions
The CuO supported on calcined olivine was investigated as an OC
using TGA and a ﬂuidized-bed reactor in CLC and CLOU. The oxygen
uncoupling properties, mechanical properties, and reactivity of the
CuO/olivine were evaluated. The following conclusions were drawn:
In the CLC with PS, both χc and ηc increase with the increasing
temperature and OC-to-fuel ratio (Φ). The maximum values for χc and
ηc, 96.81% and 95.54%, respectively, were achieved at 900 °C with
Φ = 1.50. In the CLC cyclic tests, both χc and ηc increased gradually
during the ﬁrst ﬁve cycles and then stabilized. Values of 94.64%% and
92.59%, respectively, were recorded in the 20th cycle.
In CLOU tests, Ro of CuO/olivine, 3.55 wt% was obtained after 10
redox cycles in TGA. The oxygen concentrations in the reactor were less
than those predicted based on thermodynamic equilibrium. Complete
conversion of PS and a high carbon capture eﬃciency (99.29%) were
achieved at 950 °C in the ﬂuidized-bed reactor.
MgCu2O4 was detected in the fresh and reduced CuO/olivine sam-
ples, however, it is disappeared immediately during the ﬁrst redox
cycle, indicated that the interaction between olivine and CuO is insig-
niﬁcant. SEM image indicated that the majority of CuO accumulated on
the olivine surface at the beginning, and then gradually spread out and
covered the olivine particles as the number of cycles increased. A slight
increase in speciﬁc surface area of the CuO/olivine was observed be-
cause of an increase in the number of pores among CuO grains. The XRF
analysis results conﬁrmed that a small quantity of CuO was lost in the
cyclic tests. After 20 CLC cycles, a crushing strength of 4.8 N was ob-
tained, and no signiﬁcant agglomeration was observed for CuO/olivine.
The results indicated that CuO/olivine as an oxygen carrier in chemical
looping processes is feasible.
