A B S T R A C T
To identify the best suitable metal oxide (MO) to split H2O/CO2, it is critical to investigate the formation of O2
vacancies during thermal reduction (TR) step and the re-oxidation of the MOs via H2O or CO2 splitting (CS)
reaction. Accordingly, in the present work, we have experimentally evaluated the redox reactivity of sol-gel
synthesized Ni-ferrite (NiF) in multiple temperature-swing redox cycles. The calcined powders of NiF materials
were characterized for the determination of phase purity, crystallite size, and particle morphology. In case of the
TGA cycles, the TR was accomplished at 1400 °C (for 60 min) and the reduced NiF was again re-oxidized via CS
reaction at 1000 °C (for 30 min). The obtained outcomes indicated that, among all the NiF materials investigated
i.e. Ni0.2Fe2.8O4 (NiF2), Ni0.4Fe2.6O4 (NiF4), Ni0.5Fe2.5O4 (NiF5), Ni0.6Fe2.4O4 (NiF6), Ni0.8Fe2.2O4 (NiF8),
NiFe2O4 (NiF10), the NF10 has the aptitude to produce maximum quantities of O2 (nO2) and CO (nCO). When
compared with the benchmark CeO2 material, the average nO2 and nCO by the NiF10 (from cycle 2 to cycle 9) was
greater by 15.9 μmol/g·cycle and 30.9 μmol/g·cycle, respectively.
1. Introduction
Owing to the detrimental consequences of CO2 driven global
warming, plentiful research teams are focused towards converting CO2
into valuable chemicals [1–5]. Among the numerous CO2 conversion
processes, solar driven thermochemical splitting of H2O/CO2 is one of
the capable means of transforming CO2 into liquid transportation fuels
[6–9]. This solar thermal process proceeds on the metal oxide (MO)
based redox reactions. Researchers have examined several MOs for the
solar thermochemical H2O/CO2 splitting cycle [10–25], and amid all
these, Ni-ferrite (NiF) appears to be a striking option.
Sano et al. [26] attained the H2 production at 300 °C by thermo-
chemical water splitting (WS) reaction using a carbon bearing NiF. By
engaging a zirconia supported NiF, Kodama et al. [27] achieved a
sustainable H2 generation in multiple WS cycles. Allendorf et al. [28]
thermodynamically probed the eﬀect of inclusion of Co, Ni, and Zn
metal cations into iron oxide spinel structure towards H2 generation via
WS reaction. Fresno et al. [29,30] reported that the NiF possess the
upmost H2 production competence in multiple WS cycles as compared
to the commercially obtained Zn-ferrite, Cu-ferrite, Ni-Zn-ferrite and
Cu-Zn-ferrite. The outcomes published by Agraﬁotis et al. [31] implied
that both NiF and Co-ferrite produced maximum quantity of H2 via WS
reaction in comparison with the iron oxide, Zn-ferrite, Mn-ferrite Ni-Zn
ferrite, and Mn-Zn ferrite. By performing a detailed Rietveld analysis,
the O2 liberation aptitude of zirconia supported and unsupported NiF
was estimated by Gokon et al. [32]. Fernández-Saavedra et al. [33]
scrutinized the kinetics of the NiF based thermochemical WS cycle and
reported activation energies of 261.7 kJ/mol and 34.5 kJ/mol for the
thermal reduction (TR) and WS steps, respectively.
From the preceding investigations, it was understood that the NiF
was applied mainly in case of the WS reaction and its utilization to-
wards CO2 splitting (CS) reaction was very limited [34,35]. The sys-
tematic assessment of the performance of MOs towards multiple ther-
mochemical cycles is vital in order to unveil their long-term redox
reactivity and thermal stability. Thus, to make an appropriate com-
parison with the previously examined MOs, the sol-gel derived NiF
materials synthesized in this investigation were inspected in ten ther-
mochemical CS cycles using a thermogravimetric analyzer (TGA). Sol-
gel method was selected, as it possesses unique advantages in terms of
stoichiometry and powder characteristics. Molecular lever mixing and
networking of the chemical components is one of the major features of
this synthesis approach, which improves the chemical homogeneity,
stoichiometry, phase purity and powder characteristics [36–38].

The compositional purity of the sol-gel synthesized NiF materials

The particle morphology of the derived NiF materials was ascertained
by utilizing the Scanning Electron Microscope (Nova Nano 450, FEI).

4. Summary and conclusions
By substituting the redox active Ni+2 in place of the redox inactive
Fe+2, synthesis of NixFe3−xO4 (where x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, and
1.0) i.e. NiF materials was successfully achieved by using the sol-gel
approach. PXRD analysis conﬁrmed formation of phase pure NiF2,
NiF4, NiF5, NiF6, NiF8, and NiF10 materials. The calculations per-
formed using the Scherrer formula indicated that the CS of NiF mate-
rials decreased from 30.2 to 23.9 nm as the Ni+2 atomic concentration
increases from 0.2 (NiF2) to 1.0 (NiF10). The results obtained via SEM
analysis showed that each NiF material attained a nanoparticulated
morphology. The redox reactivity of each NiF material was estimated
by performing ten thermochemical CS cycles (using a TGA set-up). The
TGA results indicated that all the NiF materials were capable of pro-
ducing stable nO2 and nCO from cycle 2 to cycle 9. Furthermore, it was
understood that the nO2 and nCO upsurged with the rise in the atomic
concentration of Ni+2. For instance, the highest nO2 = 64.4 μmol/
g·cycle and nCO = 125.9 μmol/g·cycle was observed in case of NiF10
material. At identical experimental conditions, the average nO2 and nCO
by the NiF10 material (from cycle 2 to cycle 9) was considerably higher
than the CeO2.
