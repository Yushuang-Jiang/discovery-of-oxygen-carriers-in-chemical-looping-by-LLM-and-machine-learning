A B S T R A C T   
Concentrated solar power coupled with thermochemical energy storage (TCES) has emerged as an effective 
approach for renewable energy utilization. TCES based on perovskites attracts much attention because of tem­
perature tunability and high flexibility. In this work, the electronic structures are simulated by the density 
functional theory (DFT) method, revealing the reduction temperatures is related to the formation energy of 
oxygen vacancy (Evac). The compounds of Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ with x = 0–1 are synthesized and investigated by 
experimental methods. According to the results, Ba content improves the redox capacities and reaction kinetics, 
while Sr content expands the reaction temperatures and enhances the micro morphologies. In addition, all the 
samples present a greater reversibility after 150 cycles, because the extended pores are beneficial to the oxygen 
diffusion. Overall, Ba0.5Sr0.5CoO3-δ is suggested as a suitable TCES material with a reaction enthalpy of 202.20 kJ 
kg−1. This study provides a design principle of composite perovskites for thermochemical energy storage.   
1. Introduction 
Renewable energy is a key solution to achieve climate stability and 
sustainable development. Solar energy has attracted much attention due 
to the advantages of cleanness and abundance [1]. The approach for 
solar energy utilization can be divided into photovoltaic and concen­
trated solar power (CSP). CSP coupled with thermal energy storage 
(TES) is becoming more and more competitive, enabling a continuous 
power generation and remarkable dispatchability [2–5]. TES systems 
can be integrated to store excess solar energy and supply electricity on 
demand during solar transients [6]. In order to improve the system ef­
ficiency and service life, TES materials should present high energy 
storage densities, fast reaction kinetics and long-term durability [7]. 
According to the working principles, TES technologies can be classified 
into sensible, latent and thermochemical energy storage [4]. Sensible 
energy storage, such as molten salt, has been successfully incorporated 
to commercial CSP plants, but the energy storage density is relatively 
small. Latent energy storage using phase change materials (PCM) covers 
a wide range of temperatures with higher energy storage density. 
However, some of PCMs such as chlorides and hydroxides are corrosive 
[4]. Thermochemical energy storage (TCES) makes use of reversible 
chemical reactions, including gas-gas reactions and gas-solid reactions. 
Among these technologies, TCES stands out because of large energy 
storage density and long storage periods [8]. 
TCES based on redox reactions has been widely studied because heat 
can be absorbed or released at air atmosphere, which is safe and 
convenient. The Co3O4/CoO couple exhibits high energy storage density 
and fully reversible reactions, but its toxicity may affect the system 
safety, which requires special attention in practical applications [9]. The 
CuO/Cu2O couple suffers from poor oxidation kinetics, because the re­
action temperature (~1030 ◦C) is close to the melting temperature 
(~1077 ◦C) [10,11]. Wong investigated the thermal properties of the 
Mn2O3/Mn3O4 couple and found the re-oxidation could hardly happen 
[12]. Agrafiotis et al. proposed that the addition of Fe2O3 could promote 
the re-oxidation of Mn3O4 significantly [13]. Xiang et al. reported a self- 
assembled core-shell structure of the Mn–Fe binary couple. It was 
demonstrated that the lower formation energy of oxygen vacancies in 
the shell led to an improved oxidation rate [14]. However, these metal 
oxide systems can only store heat in a specific temperature range 
(800–1100 ◦C), which is too high for current commercial CSP plants. 
Thus flexible TCES materials with a broad temperature window should 
be developed to better match various applications [15]. 
Perovskites with an ABO3 formula have emerged as promising can­
didates for TCES due to high oxygen diffusion rates and favorable

cyclability [16–21]. Another feature of perovskites is that they can 
exhibit a continuous reaction over a wide temperature range of 
300–1000 ◦C [20]. The thermochemical properties of these perovskites 
can be tuned by chemical substitutions [22,23]. The previous study 
demonstrated crystal structures are mainly related to A-site metals, 
while redox thermodynamics are mainly governed by B-site metals [24]. 
Mastronardo et al. proposed B-site doping with Fe could extend the re­
action temperature range of CaMnO3 [15]. Ti-doped and Al-doped 
CaMnO3 were also be investigated by Sandia National Laboratories. 
The results showed these dopants could increase the average B–O bond 
strength and thus the reduction enthalpy [25]. Vieten et al. reported the 
oxygen storage capacity could be improved by Cu-doping in SrFeO3-δ. 
The SrFe1-xCuxO3-δ with high Cu levels exhibited a good redox perfor­
mance at high temperature [26]. Krzystowczyk et al. chose Mn, Co, Cu 
and Ni as B-site dopants to assess the thermochemical performance of 
substituted SrFeO3. Cu-doping increased the redox capacity, but 
agglomeration was observed. The Mn doped sample showed a good 
cyclability after 1000 cycles, indicating a robust stability [27]. Many 
studies on B-site substitutions have been reported, but studies on A-site 
substitutions are sparse. Actually, the structural changes caused by A- 
site substitutions will also affect the redox properties. Imponenti et al. 
found Sr substitution on A-site could inhibit the phase decomposition of 
CaMnO3, leading to an increased redox capacity [28]. Popczun et al. 
investigated the temperature tunability of Sr1-xCaxFeO3-δ (x = 0–0.4) for 
different operating temperatures between 400 ◦C and 700 ◦C [19]. This 
work mainly concerned the oxygen storage capacity of the materials, so 
the reaction enthalpy was not studied. Zhang et al. studied the ther­
mochemical redox behavior of Ba and Sr series perovskites, revealing 
BaCoO3-δ could be fully oxidized with an energy storage density of 292 
kJ kg−1 [29]. Gokon et al. studied the impact of partial A-site sub­
stitutions on the thermal behaviors of BaxSr1-xCoO3-δ (x = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) 
and suggested Ba0.7Sr0.3CoO3-δ as a suitable TCES material [30]. 
Although some important works on the Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ system can be 
found, these works focus on the experimental assessment of thermal 
characteristics. The micro morphology and long-term cyclability are not 
fully investigated. Besides, most of these studies do not cover the entire 
compositional range, so a systematic investigation with x = 0–1 is 
required. For a comprehensive understanding of the composite system, 
the computational methods can be performed in advance of the exper­
iments to guide material design. 
In this work, the thermochemical energy storage performance of the 
Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ (x = 0–1) system is investigated by computational and 
experimental methods. The density functional theory (DFT) method is 
used to predict the thermal characteristics of Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ perovskites 
by analyzing the electronic structures. The formation energy of oxygen 
vacancy (Evac) is also calculated and considered as a theoretical 
descriptor for the reduction temperatures of the Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ system. 
Different roles of Ba and Sr on A-sites are compared in terms of redox 
capacities and micro morphologies by TG-DSC and SEM. The multi-cycle 
cyclability and structural stability are demonstrated by in situ XRD. This 
study provides a design principle of composite perovskites for thermo­
chemical energy storage. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Computational methods 
In this work, the DFT calculations were carried out in Materials 
Studio 2019 using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with 
Perdew-Burke-Erzernhof (PBE) type. The cubic perovskites belonged to 
the Pm3m (#221) space group. The lattice parameter of BaCoO3 and 
SrCoO3 were 3.95 Å and 3.86 Å [31]. To improve computing efficiency, 
a 5 × 2 × 1 supercell with 50 atoms was created, enabling a substitution 
level of x = 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9 and 1. The Ba0.5Sr0.5CoO3 (BSC55) 
model was created by replacing 50 % Ba atoms Sr by atoms, as shown in 
Fig. S1. A convergence test of calculation parameters was carried out to 
ensure the computational accuracy, as shown in Fig. S2. According to 
the convergence test, the energy cut-off was 600 eV and the k-point 
mesh was 1 × 1 × 3. The energy convergence was 2 × 10−5 eV. All 
structures were relaxed until the forces on each ion were <0.05 eV Å−1. 
The formation energy of oxygen vacancy was calculated by Eq. (1) [19]. 
Evac = Edefect + 1

Here, Edefect is the total energy of the supercell with an oxygen va­
cancy, EO2 is the chemical potential of a free oxygen molecule, and Ebulk 
is the total energy of a defect-free supercell. 
2.2. Materials synthesis 
The Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ samples were synthesized by a modified Pechini 
process [32]. The Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ (x = 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1) sam­
ples were abbreviated as BC, BSC91, BSC73, BSC55, BSC37, BSC19 and 
SC respectively. The chemical compositions of different samples are 
listed in Table 1. The preparation precursors were Ba(NO3)2 (purity 
99.0 %), Sr(NO3)2 (purity 99.5 %), Co(NO3)2⋅6H2O (purity 99.0 %), 
citric acid (purity 99.5 %), EDTA (purity 99.5 %), ammonia solution 
(purity 25–28 %) and ethylene glycol (purity 99.5 %). These chemical 
reagents were provided by Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. 
Stoichiometric amounts of metal nitrates, citric acid and EDTA were 
dissolved in de-ionized water. The mole ratio of citric acid, EDTA and 
total metal ions was 1.2:0.6:1. Then ethylene glycol and ammonia so­
lution were added to adjust the pH to 8. The mixed solution was stirred 
in a thermostat water bath at 80 ◦C for 5 h to obtain a resin. Then, the 
resin was dried in an electrothermal drier at 200 ◦C for 3 h. The pre­
cursor was pre-calcined in a muffle furnace at 300 ◦C for 3 h to remove 
organic components and then calcined at 1000 ◦C for 5 h. After cooling 
to room temperature, the samples were ground into powder in a ball 
crusher. To obtain the volumetric energy storage densities, 40 g 
perovskite powder was pressed into cubic honeycombs (3 cm × 3 cm ×
3 cm) using a plate vulcanization machine (1000 kN, 20 MPa). 20 wt% 
polyvinyl alcohol was used as a binder. The honeycombs were calcined 
at 800 ◦C for 8 h. The density of the perovskite honeycomb is about 2.07 
g cm−3.

200 mesh. The samples were put into the sample rack and flattened

range of 25–35◦. The sample was heated to 1000 ◦C and then cooled to

photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed on a ThermoFisher K-

bration. The powder samples were prepared on aluminum foil tape (1 
cm × 1 cm) for XPS analysis. The morphologies of fresh and cycled 
samples were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) on a

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. DFT calculations 
The DFT method was used to predict the microscopic properties of 
different models and guide subsequent experiments. This will help to 
better understand the micro mechanism of substitution modification. 
The impact of substitution on A-sites was analyzed in terms of crystal 
structures and electronic distribution. The 5 × 2 × 1 supercell models of 
BaCoO3 (BC), Ba0.5Sr0.5CoO3 (BSC55) and SrCoO3 (SC) were created and 
optimized to find the minimum total energy. The optimized lattice pa­
rameters were in excellent agreement with the experimental values with 
a relative error within ±0.5 %, as shown in Table S1. The lattice con­
stants decrease after Sr-substitution because ion radius of Sr2+ is smaller 
than that of Ba2+. The Mulliken charges of BC, BSC55 and SC models are 
listed in Table 2. For all the models, the effective charges of Ba/Sr are 
close to +2e, which means Ba and Sr show strong ionic properties in the 
lattices. The ionic interaction between A-sites and O mainly includes 
long-range attraction and short-range repulsion. The long-range 
attraction of Ba–O and Sr–O are close because of similar arrange­
ment of outer electrons. However, the short-range repulsion will decline 
with the ion radius reduction. Thus, Sr-substitution will weaken the 
repulsion of A-O ion pair, as well as the structural stability [33]. The 
effective charges of Co are about 0.72e, which is much smaller than +4e. 
This result can be attributed to the covalent nature of B–O bonds in

To further estimate the electronic structures, the partial density of 
states (PDOS) for BC, BSC55 and SC models are compared in Fig. 1. Here, 
zero corresponds to the Fermi level (EF). The peaks at −10 eV and 13 eV 
belong to Ba5p and Ba6s respectively. Sr4p and Sr5s peaks lie in lower 
energy of −15 eV and 12 eV. They have little contribution to O2p, 
revealing Ba and Sr mainly exist in the form of ions. In the BSC55 model, 
the reduction of Ba states can be observed because of the addition of Sr. 
The energy region between −10 eV and 5 eV is dominated by O2p and 
Co3d. The effective overlap indicates Co and O will form strong covalent 
bonds, which is consistent with the Mulliken charge analysis. Compared 
to BSC55 and SC, Co3d and O2p show greater overlap in the BC model, 
suggesting stronger Co–O bonds. The results indicate tuning the Ba/Sr 
content will negligibly affect the bonding types in the Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ 
system, but the bond strength tend to be weakened by the replacement 
of Ba with Sr.

oxygen vacancies were considered according to the number of adjacent

The Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ samples were synthesized by a modified Pechini 
process. The samples are abbreviated as BC, BSC91, BSC73, BSC55,

3.3. Thermal characteristics 
Thermal characteristics of Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ samples were analyzed by 
TG and DSC measurements. BC shows a large reaction capacity, with a 
reduction onset temperature of 442 ◦C (Fig. 5a). δox is slightly smaller 
than δre due to some irreversible mass losses of impurities [17]. SC ex­
hibits a relatively slow reaction rate and poor reversibility in a wide 
temperature range (Fig. 5b). Similarly, the reduction of SC also shows an 
inflection point, because perovskite-type SrCoO2.52 will transform into 
brownmillerite-type Sr2Co2O5 at elevated temperatures [36]. This phase 
transition is irreversible, so δox is significantly less than δre. The com­
posite samples show complex thermal characteristics, as shown in 
Fig. 6a and Table 3. The composite samples show a temperature 
tunability in the range of 298–312 ◦C. Generally, the reduction onset 
temperature Tre, as well as the reaction capacity δ, tend to decrease with 
increasing Sr levels. This phenomenon is consistent with DFT prediction 
results, indicating the Evac can be considered as a theoretical descriptor 
as the reduction temperatures. The redox pattern of BSC91 is similar 
with BC, but the Tre decreases by 130 ◦C. This means Sr substitution on 
A-site can expand the reaction temperature range. However, it should be 
noted that the temperature deviation between rapid reduction and 
oxidation reactions expands with the increment of Sr content. Compared 
to other composite samples, BSC19 also shows a low oxidation rate. This 
means excessive concentration of Sr may lead to kinetic hindrance. In 
terms of redox reactivity, BSC19 suffers from poor reversibility, indi­
cating Ba-rich samples produce large redox capacities and good 
reversibility. Table 3 shows the reaction enthalpies of different samples. 
The deviation between reduction enthalpies and oxidation enthalpies is 
probably caused by the instrument measurement error. In this work, the

3.4. Cyclability and structural stability 
Thermochemical energy storage materials should have an excellent 
cyclability for long-term applications. In order to estimate the cyclic 
stability of Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ perovskites, the samples underwent 150 cy­
cles in a tubular furnace between 400 and 1050 ◦C. The cycle perfor­
mance is shown in Fig. 7. An overall downward trend of δre can be seen 
for undoped BC and SC samples. Comparatively, the composite samples 
show better cyclic stability with small fluctuations. As for the oxidation 
process, BC shows a slight degradation after 150 cycles. Other samples 
exhibit even larger δox after 150 cycles, though a small drift can be 
detected during cycling. The TG curves of the cycled samples are shown 
in Fig. 8a. After 150 cycles, the samples can still realize the reversible 
redox reactions. Significantly, the reversibility of cycled samples is 
obviously better than fresh samples as displayed in Fig. 8b. This can be 
explained by morphology changes. As revealed by SEM images in Fig. 9, 
the Ba-rich samples exhibit terraced structures with point defects on the 
surfaces [34]. The Sr-rich samples appear to have porous structures in 
favor of the diffusion of oxygen. After 150 cycles, some large holes 
instead of point defects can be distinguished in the Ba-rich samples, 
which may accelerate oxygen exchange rates. The Sr-rich samples 
maintain the favorable structures with high porosity, though slight 
agglomeration can be observed. The oxygen diffusion is related to the 
porosity and pore size distribution. As shown in Fig. 10, some small 
pores aggregate into large pores after multiple cycles, which is condu­
cive to the oxidation progress [37]. The results indicate the composite 
perovskites have the desired cyclability with incredibly robust 
microstructures. 
To assess the structural stability of the composite perovskites, XRD 
patterns of the cycled samples are shown in Fig. 11. The main crystal 
phases remain the same as the prepared samples. No impurity phases are 
generated, indicating a high structural stability of the perovskite mate­
rials. In situ XRD was performed to further study the crystalline phase 
evolution during the redox process. The contour map of cycled BSC55 
during the reduction and oxidation processes is displayed in Fig. 12a. 
Two main reflections of the hexagonal phase, (101) peak and (110) 
peak, shift to lower angles under heating conditions, which is related to 
oxygen release. Above 900 ◦C, the hexagonal phase exhibits a fast

ble, so the cubic-hexagonal transition is more difficult, which is 
responsible for the temperature deviation observed in Fig. 6b. 
4. Conclusions 
Perovskites is a kind of promising thermochemical energy storage 
materials because of flexible reaction temperatures, large energy storage 
densities and long storage periods. In this work, the compounds of Ba1- 
xSrxCoO3-δ are studied by computational and experimental methods. To 
entirely estimate different roles of Ba/Sr contents, a wide compositional 
range of x = 0–1 is covered in this work. To predict the thermal char­
acteristics of Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ perovskites, the crystal and electronic 
structures are calculated by DFT method. It is demonstrated that A-site 
substitution has negligible impact on the bonding types in the perov­
skites, but the formation energy of oxygen vacancy turns out to decrease 
with the increasing Sr content. Therefore, it is predicted that the 
reduction reactions become more easily in Sr-rich samples, resulting in 
lower reduction temperatures. According to the experimental results, 
the reduction onset temperatures can be tuned from 440 ◦C to 290 ◦C

Overall, Ba0.5Sr0.5CoO3-δ is suggested as a suitable TCES material, due to 
a large reduction enthalpy of 202.20 kJ kg−1 (116.27 kWh m−3) and 
excellent cycling stability for long-term applications. This study offers a 
rational guideline for the material design of thermochemical energy 
storage perovskites. In summary, the Ba1-xSrxCoO3-δ perovskites provide 
a wide temperature range, so they can be integrated with different 
systems flexibly. In addition, they show robust structural stability after 
multiple cycles, which is beneficial to extend the service life. However, 
TCES technology based on perovskites is still in the laboratory stage due 
to high technical difficulty and expensive cost. This work focuses on the 
energy storage performance of perovskite powder. In the future, per­

scale systems.
