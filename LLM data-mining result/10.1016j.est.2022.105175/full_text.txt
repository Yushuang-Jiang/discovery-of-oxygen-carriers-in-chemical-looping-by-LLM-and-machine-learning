A B S T R A C T   
The structural stability, thermal expansion, oxygen exchange thermodynamics, and thermochemical storage 
(TCS) capacity of perovskite-like Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ and Ruddlesden-Popper SrCa3Mn3O10-δ manganites are 
studied by the combined use of experimental techniques, thermodynamic modeling of the defect formation re­
actions, and energy calculations utilizing density functional theory (DFT). It is argued that conservative estimates 
of the storage capacity can be made by the use of the Dulong-Petit limit for high-temperature heat capacity and 
oxygen partial enthalpy independent of both temperature and oxygen content. The respectively recalculated 
literature data and the obtained results show that the thermodynamic limit for the TCS capacity (~800 kJ/kg) of 
Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ is one of the highest at thermal cycling within the oxygen partial pressure range of 10 
−4–0.21 atm. At the same time, the storage capacity of SrCa3Mn3O10-δ achieves only about 600 kJ/kg. The TCS 
cycling tests demonstrate the stability of SrCa3Mn3O10-δ while a decline in the energy storage capacity is 
observed for Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ. According to the EDX analysis, this effect may reflect surface degradation of 
Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ. The energy storage capacities of SrCa3Mn3O10-δ and Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ decrease to 510 and 
560 kJ/kg, respectively, because of the rather sluggish reduction kinetics. It is concluded that further 
improvement of manganites as energy storage materials can be achieved using new doping strategies.   
1. Introduction 
The thermochemical storage of solar energy (TCS) is considered a 
promising approach to address the problem of intermittent operation of 
solar energy-based plants [1–5]. The technology rests on the capture of 
heat energy by an oxide material at elevated temperatures, followed by 
the release of the stored energy at backward oxidation when the tem­
perature is decreased. As a result, the cyclic energy capture/release can 
be used to smooth out the peaks of energy consumption. This method is 
distinguished by the higher density of the stored energy and a wider 
temperature range of use compared to the storage of sensible and latent 
heat [4]. Owing to the inherent simplicity, the TCS is more robust and 
less expensive to implement than the battery technology commonly 
applied in photovoltaic plants [6]. The main function of the TCS unit 
coupled with an electric power plant is to accumulate the excess heat 
produced throughout the day and release the stored energy into the 
energy generating cycle for continuous operation of the plant. The en­
ergy storage capacities of perovskite-like manganites SrxCa1-xMnO3–δ 
have been found to be particularly appealing [7,8]. These oxides evince 
a promising properties' combination as they are cheap, non-toxic, stable 
under TCS conditions, and exhibit a significant density of chemical en­
ergy associated with oxidation of manganese ions Mn3+ → Mn4+ and 
filling of oxygen vacancies in the crystal lattice at 500–1100 ◦C. Similar 
to the doping of Mn sites [9], the strontium incorporation in the Ca sub- 
lattice thwarts the tendency of CaMnO3–δ to phase transition above 900 
◦C [10]. Additionally, it favors enhanced oxidation/reduction kinetics 
and enlarged oxygen non-stoichiometry (δ) at elevated temperatures 
[11]. Alternative attempts at developing more advanced TCS materials

based on the use of other dopants or combined doping of Ca and Mn sub- 
lattices have not resulted in any significant improvement in functional 
characteristics. Most possibly, it occurs because dopants either do not 
change or even decrease the concentration of electroactive manganese 
cations. Respectively, it is interesting to consider compounds with a 
larger concentration of manganese in the crystal lattice, e.g., CaMn2O4 
with a marokite structure type [12]. This manganite is characterized by 
a higher concentration of manganese (28.6 % at) compared to CaMnO3 
(20 % at), and all manganese cations are in the 3+ oxidation state. These 
features potentially benefit considerable oxidation enthalpy because 
reduction of Mn3+ to Mn2+ takes a lot of energy [13]. Unfortunately, the 
structure of CaMn2O4 formed by edge-sharing MnO6 octahedra is un­
favorable for a wide homogeneous range of oxygen content [12]. Hence, 
the chemical contribution to the stored energy in this manganite is 
small. Alternatively, one can try to use manganites with the increased 
oxidation enthalpy for Mn3+ cations even if their concentration is 
somewhat smaller than in CaMnO3–δ. The larger oxidation enthalpy, i.e., 
enhanced Mn – O bonding, can be attained by the insertion of additional 
CaO structural units in perovskite-like framework of CaMnO3. Indeed, 
the experimentally observed value of −222 kJ⋅mol−1 for Ca2MnO4–δ is 
considerably larger compared to the oxidation enthalpy of −178 
kJ⋅mol−1 in CaMnO3-δ [14]. Therefore, the available data suggests that 
the strontium doped manganites Can+1MnnO3n+1–δ (n = 1, 2, or 3) in the 
Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) series may be promising for TCS applications. 
To the best of our knowledge, no study has been conducted yet on en­
ergy storage and high-temperature oxygen exchange thermodynamics in 
layered manganites. In selecting which member of the RP series may 
better fit the TCS demands, Ca4Mn3O10-δ attracts attention because thick 
(CaMnO3–δ)3 slabs are expected to tolerate wide variations of oxygen 
content, and the value of Ca/Mn = 4/3 is fairly close to the ratio Ca/Mn 
= 1 in CaMnO3–δ. On the other hand, the sheets of CaO that keep the 
quasi-2D crystalline lattice in Ca4Mn3O10–δ may favorably influence the 
energy characteristics and mobility of the labile oxygen [15]. Another 
advantageous feature of Ca4Mn3O10–δ is that the incorporation of 
strontium in the n = 3 RP structure enables a well-ordered phase with a 
minimal concentration of stacking and intergrowth defects, which can 
interfere with obtaining unambiguous results [16]. With the above 
consideration, we resolved to carry out a contrasting study of the ther­
mochemical energy storage properties and oxygen exchange thermo­
dynamics in the strontium substituted layered RP manganite 
SrCa3Mn3O10–δ and the perovskite-like Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ with the same 
Ca/Sr = 3/1 ratio. 
Considering the obtained results and literature data, we argue that 
the conservative approach based on the Dulong–Petit limit for heat ca­
pacity and permanent oxygen enthalpy results in a viable evaluation of 
the thermodynamic maximum for the energy storage capacity in man­
ganites. Despite having superior TCS characteristics, manganites require 
further development to ensure fast reduction and oxidation kinetics and 
good reversibility during redox cycling. 
2. Methods 
2.1. Synthesis and characterization 
The samples were synthesized by a sol-gel technique from high- 
purity grade CaCO3 (99 + %), SrCO3 (99.9 + %) and Mn2O3 (99.9 +
%). The starting materials were pre-heated at 523 K for 2 h in order to 
remove adsorbates, weighed in desirable proportions, and dissolved in a 
light excess of 2 M HNO3 (99.5 %). The obtained solutions were mixed 
together and added with glycine (99 %) in an amount sufficient for the 
reduction of chemically bound nitrogen to a gaseous state. Then, the 
liquor was heated on a hot plate, resulting in the formation of a gel. The 
gel was dried at 573 K, and then heated at 1173 K for 10 h in order to 
remove 
carbonaceous 
residues.

1 kbar, fired at 1673 K for 75 h in the air with three intermittent 
grindings in ethanol media and repelletizations, and furnace cooled to 
room temperature. Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ was fired in the air for 10 h at 
1473 K, followed by slow cooling. Some parts of the obtained materials 
were pulverized for further experiments. 
Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) in Bragg-Brentano mode at room 
temperature was used for phase purity control and structure determi­
nation of the synthesized materials. A Shimadzu XRD-7000 diffrac­
tometer (Cu-Kα radiation) was used to collect data in the 2Θ range of 
10–90◦with a 0.03◦step size and a 5 s acquisition time. Another set of 
data from room temperature to 1200 K in the air was obtained for the 
SrCa3Mn3O10–δ sample with the help of a D8 Advance diffractometer 
equipped with a high-temperature chamber HTK 1200 N and a position 
sensitive detector VÅNTEC-1. The XRD spectra were taken in the 2Θ 
range of 8–137◦with a 0.021◦step size, a heating rate of 0.5◦/min, a 
temperature step of 10◦, and an acquisition time of 493 s at each tem­
perature. Rietveld profile refinement of the data was performed using 
the FullProf calculation package [17]. The behavior of the synthesized 
materials at heating in various gas media was studied with the help of a 
TG-DTA-92 (Setaram) thermoanalyzer. Initially, weighed portions of the 
studied compounds were heated in the air to 1223 K, after which they 
were slowly cooled to 423 K and kept at this temperature for 5 h in order 
to circumvent the effect of trace amounts of moisture on the results of 
the experiment. The resulting mass (m0) was further taken as a refer­
ence. Then, the hydrogen purge was switched on and the samples were 
heated to 1223 K, followed by a 10 h dwell. Cyclic tests were carried out 
using a program that switched the gas medium from synthetic air to Ar 
(>99.99 %), and adjusted the temperature regime accordingly. 
The oxygen content changes in the oxides in conditions with varied 
temperature and oxygen partial pressure were measured by the coulo­
metric titration technique within 973–1223 K. The experimental setup 
and measurement features can be found elsewhere [18,19]. Morpho­
logical studies were conducted by using scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM, JEOL JSM 6390-LA). The chemical analysis was performed along 
with the morphological studies by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy 
(EDX, EX-23010BU) at an acceleration voltage of 20 kV.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Structural features 
The X-ray diffraction spectra in Fig. 2 give evidence of the formation 
of single–phase Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ and SrCa3Mn3O10–δ specimens. The 
obtained set of reflections was unambiguously ascribed to orthorhombic 
(S.G. Pnma) and tetragonal (S.G. I/4 mmm) symmetry for the elementary 
cell of Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ and SrCa3Mn3O10–δ oxides, respectively. The 
refined elementary cell parameters in Table 1 are close to those observed 
in works [16,23] for similar compounds. The Rietveld refinement results 
for SrCa3Mn3O10–δ show a strong preference for strontium atoms to 
occupy Ca1 positions in perovskite–like slabs, as shown in Table 1. 
This conclusion is corroborated by DFT calculations showing about 
100 kJ/mol less energy for the structure in Fig. 1(b) compared to the one 
in Fig. 1(a). This observation is also supported by a very good coinci­
dence of the experimental and calculated elementary cell parameters. 
Quite differently, strontium atoms in Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3 are not expected 
to exhibit positional preference in the crystal lattice as the energy dif­
ference between the supercells in Fig. 1(c) and (d) does not exceed 10 
kJ/mol. Therefore, the energy-based arguments confirm the random 
distribution of Sr dopants over Ca sites in Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ and rein­
force the X-ray diffraction results. The preferential location of the Sr 
impurity in RP–manganite is most likely due to strontium's large ionic 
radius. As a result, the residence of 9-fold coordinated calcium in rock- 
salt layers is accompanied by pronounced local deformations, while the 
replacement of calcium in 12-fold coordination in perovskite-like slabs 
is less energy demanding. This is not the case for the perovskite-like 
manganite, where oxygen always coordinates Ca/Sr– positions 
12–fold, favoring a randomized distribution of Sr and Ca in 
Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3. 
The materials for high-temperature applications are supposed to be 
thermally robust. The respective studies of phase and thermodynamic 
stability of manganites in the series Sr1-xCaxMnO3-δ were carried out 
earlier in works [24,25], where the orthorhombic structure of 
Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ was found to exist at wide variations of temperature 
and oxygen partial pressure. At the same time, little is known about the 
stability limits of RP– manganite SrCa3Mn3O10–δ. In order to eliminate 
this deficiency, we performed X – ray diffraction experiments at elevated 
temperatures. The obtained data is presented in Fig. 3. It is seen that 
heating in the air up to 1500 K is not accompanied by structural tran­
sitions or phase decomposition. The temperature-dependent variations 
of the elementary cell parameters in SrCa3Mn3O10–δ show an expressed 
anisotropy of thermal expansion, Fig. 4. According to the data [25] [26], 
the main contribution to the increase in elementary cell volume V(T)

3.2. Oxygen content and non-stoichiometry variations 
The oxygen stoichiometry changes of as synthesized samples 
Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ and SrCa3Mn3O10-δ were determined by TG mea­
surements at heating in air atmosphere. The results are presented in 
Fig. 5(a). As can be seen, the heating of the samples is accompanied by a 
significant decrease in their relative mass (Δm/m0). In both phases, the 
oxygen exchange practically halts below 900 K. For the case of the RP 
manganite this observation is additionally corroborated by strong 
changes of the volumetric expansion coefficient, Fig. 4. The value of 
Δm/m0 for SrCa3Mn3O10-δ is somewhat smaller compared to 
Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ, which most possibly reflects the respectively smaller 
concentration of manganese in the crystalline lattice of SrCa3Mn3O10-δ. 
Still, both samples exhibit quite large variations of oxygen non- 
stoichiometry, a feature of importance in the design of TCS materials. 
The behavior of the manganites at heating in the flow of the 5%H2/Ar 
gas mixture is shown in Fig. 5(b). The X-raying revealed simple metal 
oxides CaO, SrO and MnO in the products after heat treatment at 1200 K.

Prior to the use of Eqs. (14) and (15), we have to draw attention to 
several points. First of all, measuring high-temperature heat capacity is a 
complicated experimental task. As a result, it is not surprizing that the 
data for SrCa3Mn3O10–δ are lacking in the literature, and there is only 
one work presenting adiabatic calorimetry results for heat capacity in 
CaMnO3 and CaMnO2.562 up to 600 K [33]. The reliability of the data 
follows from the excellent coincidence with the low temperature mea­
surements [34,35]. Importantly, the heat capacity values in CaMnO3 
(121.3 J⋅mol−1 K−1) and CaMnO2.562 (110.9 J⋅mol−1 K−1) at 600 K are 
only 3 % smaller than the respective high-temperature limits calculated 
from the Dulong-Petit law. The extrapolation of the data [33] to higher 
temperatures does not appear reliable [36]. For instance, the extrapo­
lated heat capacity of CaMnO2.8 at 1273 K attains 125 J⋅mol−1 K−1, 
which is >10 % larger than the Dulong-Petit limit, and the difference is 
too large to ascribe it to the effects of anharmonicity and/or the 
appearance of oxygen vacancies [37]. Even larger divergence with the 
experiment is obtained at theoretical estimates [38] where the calcu­
lated heat capacity of CaMnO3 is in excess of 300 J⋅mol−1 K−1 at 600 K. 
Considering the experimental and theoretical difficulties, it seems that 
the Dulong-Petit limit is sufficient for conservative estimates of the high- 
temperature heat capacity and sensible heat. 
The evaluation of the chemical contribution to the stored energy 
typically involves the use of ΔHO(T, δ) obtained from the experimental 
equilibrium plots for δ = δ(PO2,T) according to relations (10) and (11). 
For instance, authors [9] apply this approach to CaTi0.2Mn0.8O3-δ and 
CaAl0.2Mn0.8O3-δ and find that the derived values of ΔHO(T, δ) do not 
depend on temperature. It is also shown that the impact of oxygen non- 
stoichiometry on the enthalpy in CaTi0.2Mn0.8O3-δ is practically insig­
nificant. An alternative method is utilized in work [7] where a defect 
model is developed for Ca0.95Sr0.05MnO3-δ and Ca0.9Sr0.1MnO3-δ in order 
to describe experimental δ = δ(PO2,T) plots and reproduce the enthalpy 
behavior from the model parameters. The simulated enthalpy is found to 
be practically independent on temperature and non-stoichiometry at δ 
> 0.2. At the same time, the theoretical values strongly decrease with δ 
from −190 ÷ −200 to −240 ÷ -260 kJ/mol in the range of 0 < δ < 0.2. 
In order to support their model calculations, the authors carried out 
high-temperature DSC measurements. The typical scatter of the ob­
tained values reached 25 %. In view of the experimental uncertainties, a 
stoichiometry independent constant enthalpy appears most appropriate 
for the representation of the experimental results. Nonetheless, the au­
thors fitted the data following their model, i.e., by a curve bending 
sharply from −200 kJ/mol at δ > 0.2 to −260 kJ/mol at δ → 0. Mean­
while, due to the strong enthalpy dependence, the interval 0 < δ < 0.2 
makes an unusually large contribution to the record high stored chem­
ical energy calculated from Eq. (15) for Ca0.95Sr0.05MnO3-δ at integration 
within the experimental range 0 < δ < 0.3 [7]. The results obtained in 
work [40] are very instructive in deciding which approach is best suited 
for the estimates. The authors show that the use of a free-fit approach to 
the experimental data may give significantly different enthalpies of 
oxidation in perovskite-related oxides. On the other hand, they 
demonstrate that the composition-independent enthalpic terms can be 
used as a good approximation to describe the redox energetics of non-

nearly identical within determination uncertainties. It is also worth 
noting that, while the TCS capacity of the layered SrCa3Mn3O10–δ ap­
proaches that of 3D perovskite-like manganites, it does not exceed their 
energy storage characteristics within the accepted (T, PO2) cycling 
limits. Therefore, for further scrutiny of the applicability of the man­
ganites for TCS technologies, we need to compare cyclability and kinetic 
aspects of their behavior in conditions simulating a TCS reactor.

4. Conclusions 
In the framework of this study, we synthesized complex oxide 
manganites Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3-δ and SrCa3Mn3O10-δ via combustion of 
organometallic precursors. The single-phase compositions of the 
resulting oxides were confirmed by X-ray diffraction. According to 
experimental data and DFT calculations, strontium atoms preferentially 
reside in the perovskite slabs of the layered SrCa3Mn3O10, as opposed to 
random distribution in Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ. Moreover, DFT calculation 
results suggest that the O1 and O3 positions in perovskite-like slabs are 
mostly active in the oxygen exchange of SrCa3Mn3O10-δ with the 
ambient gas phase. The DFT calculations agree with the thermodynamic 
analysis of SrCa3Mn3O10-δ equilibration with gas-phase oxygen, 
demonstrating that the involvement of two structurally different oxygen 
positions in oxygen exchange allows for an excellent simulation of 
experimental data for variations of non-stoichiometry stoichiometry 
δ(T, PO2) with temperature and oxygen partial pressure. The combina­
tion of the experimental results with consideration of defect equilibria 
and literature data gives strong arguments in proof of the oxygen partial 
enthalpy independent of temperature and oxygen content in the man­
ganites. It is also reasoned that the Dulong-Petit law gives rather reliable 
estimates of high-temperature heat capacity in non-stoichiometric 
manganites. The respectively reconsidered literature data and the re­
sults in the present work show that the thermodynamic limit for the TCS 
capacity (~800 kJ/kg) of quasi– 3D manganite Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ is 
one of the highest. Simultaneously, it is only about 600 kJ/kg in quasi– 
2D manganite SrCa3Mn3O10-δ in the same span of variations of tem­
perature (773–1273 K) and oxygen partial pressure (0.21–10−4 atm) 
values. It is shown that the potentially larger energy storage capacity of 
SrCa3Mn3O10-δ is not attained because of the oxygen vacancy repulsive 
interaction, preventing active participation of equatorial O4 positions in 
oxygen exchange. The TCS cycling tests reveal superior stability of 
SrCa3Mn3O10-δ compared to Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ. The EDX analysis data 
suggest that the poor cyclability of Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ is the result of a 
surface degradation. SrCa3Mn3O10–δ and Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ energy 
storage efficiencies converge at thermal cycling in the practicable oxy­
gen pressure range of 10−2–0.21 atm, where TCS capacity is only about 
510 and 560 kJ/kg in SrCa3Mn3O10–δ and Sr0.25Ca0.75MnO3–δ, respec­
tively. The cyclability tests and thermal analysis results show that the 
decreased TCS performance is mostly due to the sluggish reduction ki­
netics of the manganites. Overall, the findings indicate that further ad­
vancements in manganites as energy storage materials are possible 
through the development of doping strategies that ensure better surface 
stability and faster bulk oxygen exchange.
