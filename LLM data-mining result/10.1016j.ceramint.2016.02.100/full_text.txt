In this paper, we report the synthesis, characterization, and assessment of CexZryHfzO2 (CZH) materials
for solar thermochemical splitting of CO2 in up to 20 consecutive thermochemical cycles. CZH materials
with different stoichiometries were synthesized by using the co-precipitation of hydroxides method. CZH
materials acquired after calcination were characterized using powder X-ray diffraction, scanning electron
microscopy, energy dispersive spectroscopy, and BET surface area. The thermochemical CO2 splitting
ability of the derived CZH materials was assessed by performing multiple thermochemical cycles using a
thermogravimetric analyzer. All CZH materials studied exhibit a higher activity than ceria modiﬁed with
Hf or Zr alone. Thus a synergistic effect seems to be present. Among the different CZH materials ex-
amined, Ce0.895Zr0.046Hf0.053O1.988 (CZH5) was observed to be the most active exhibiting a sustained
redox activity in terms of CO production/O2 release in the temperature range of 1000–1400 °C. Moreover,
CZH5 was subjected to 20 consecutive thermochemical cycles during which no degradation could be
observed. The results obtained indicate that CZH5 is a promising candidate for solar thermochemical
conversion of CO2 as it is capable of producing stable amounts of O2 and CO in multiple thermochemical
cycles.

1. Introduction
Solar fuel production via metal oxide based thermochemical
splitting of H2O and CO2 is a promising technology for meeting the
future energy demand and to limit greenhouse gas concentrations
in the earth's atmosphere. In a solar thermochemical cycle a metal
oxide, in our case CexZryHfzO2, is thermally reduced at high tem-
peratures provided by concentrated solar radiation. Then the re-
duced material is reoxidized by H2O and/or CO2 to yield the initial
oxide to close the cycle producing H2 and/or CO as schematically
illustrated in Fig. 1.
In comparison to the high temperature direct thermolysis of
H2O and/or CO2 (42500 °C), metal oxide based thermochemical
cycles are advantageous because 1) these cycles require lower
operating temperatures (r1600 °C), 2) avoiding the formation of
explosive product gas mixtures (as the production of H2/CO and O2
can be carried out in two separate steps), and 3) these cycles are,
environmentally
and
thermodynamically,
more
favorable
compared to the direct thermolysis [1]. Previously, two types of
metal oxide redox systems were examined as active materials in
solar thermochemical H2O and/or CO2 splitting cycles, namely: a)
volatile metal oxide pairs such as ZnO/Zn [2–12] and SnO2/SnO
[11–16], and b) non-volatile metal oxide pairs such as Fe3O4/FeO
[3,4,17–25], ferrites [26–40], ceria based redox materials [41–58],
and perovskite based redox materials [59–69] respectively. Among
all these redox systems, ceria-based oxides are of particular in-
terest because of their faster reaction kinetics due to their in-
trinsically high oxygen mobility and better thermal stability as
compared to the other volatile and non-volatile metal oxide sys-
tems. Abanades and Flamant [42] investigated the CeO2/Ce2O3
redox system for solar thermochemical H2 production for the ﬁrst
time. The following two reactions that compose the stoichiometric

The non-stoichiometric ceria based redox cycle is also a two-
step process. Firstly, CeO2 is reduced at 1500–1600 °C via a solar
thermochemical reduction to its nonstoichiometric phase CeO2δ.
Then, the reduced CeO2δ can be re-oxidized to CeO2 by the

Recently, Abanades and Le Gal [56–58] synthesized ceria–zir-
conia solid solutions with the help of various soft chemistry routes
(such as the co-precipitation of hydroxides, the sol-gel method,
the Pechini method and hydrothermal treatment). The potential of
the derived ceria–zirconia materials in solar thermochemical H2O
or CO2 splitting cycles was examined by performing thermo-
gravimetric experiments. The synthesized ceria–zirconia materials
were observed to be very promising as the required thermal re-
duction temperature was reduced to 1400 °C. It was also reported
that at high Zr concentration (Ce0.5Zr0.5O2), the reduction yield
was maximum. However, the H2O/CO2 splitting yield and thermal
stability was observed to be better at low Zr doping (Ce0.9Zr0.1O2
and Ce0.75Zr0.25O2). According to the studies reported until now,
ceria–zirconia and ceria–hafnia binary oxides are among the most
promising candidates for solar thermochemical cycles. In recent
investigations, researchers have also reported that some ternary
oxides based on Zr–CeO2 doped with Gd3þ, La3þ, Y3þ exhibit an
even better performance compared to binary ceria oxide [54].
In this publication we report on ternary metal oxides (CZH)
based on Ce, Zr, and Hf, a potentially interesting system which has
not been examined so far. Our aim is to increase the amount of
oxygen that can be removed at a given temperature during the
thermal reduction. This will make a thermochemical cycle more
efﬁcient as a larger amount of product is obtained per cycle and
per unit mass of redox active material. Due to their redox potential
(Zr/Zr4þ: 1.529 V, Hf/Hf4þ: 1.70 V) the dopants will not be
redox active. Still, we expect that the modiﬁed materials are easier
to reduce because the signiﬁcantly smaller ionic radius of the
dopants compared to Ce4þ leads to large local strain in the host
lattice. In addition, also the tendency for sintering is expected to
decrease as both, ZrO2 and HfO2 are refractory oxides with a

The CZH materials were prepared by co-precipitation of hy-
droxides method. Their phase and elemental composition, surface
area, porosity, and morphology was determined by powder X-ray
diffraction (PXRD), BET surface area analysis (BET), scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive spectroscopy
(EDS). Their performance in terms of redox reactivity was ex-
amined during multiple thermochemical CO2 splitting cycles using
a thermogravimetric analyser (TGA).
2. Experimental section
2.1. Synthesis of CexZryHfzO2 materials
CexZryOzO2 (CZH) materials with various chemical stoichiome-
tries (x¼0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, and 0.9, y¼0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.20, and 0.25,
z¼0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.20, and 0.25) were synthesized using the co-
precipitation of hydroxides method according to Fig. 2. Precursors
salts cerium nitrate hexahydrate (Ce(NO3)3  6H2O), zirconium
chloride (ZrCl4), hafnium chloride (HfCl4) and ammonium hydro-
xide (NH4OH, 28%) were purchased from Alfa Aesar and used as
received. To synthesize CZH materials, desired amounts of metal
precursors were dissolved in demineralized water under mild
stirring conditions. Once completely dissolved and well mixed, an
excess amount of aqueous ammonium hydroxide (NH4OH, 28%)
was added slowly to the solution until precipitation was complete
(pH9). The aqueous dispersion was further stirred for 24 h, the
color of the precipitate changes brownish to pale yellow. Then the
dispersion was left to settle overnight. The supernatant liquid was
decanted and the precipitate was recovered by vacuum ﬁltration
and washed with demineralized water several times. Solids ob-
tained after washing were dried, crushed, and subsequently cal-
cined at 600 °C in air using a mufﬂe furnace. CZH obtained after
calcination was ground using a ceramic mortar and pestle and
stored in a dry atmosphere.

2.3. Thermochemical CO2 splitting cycles
The ability of the synthesized CZH materials to perform ther-
mochemical CO2 splitting cycles was examined in a thermogravi-
metric analyzer (TGA, Netzsch STA 409). The TGA furnace was
made up of an alumina tube surrounded by a silicon carbide
heating element (maximum temperature¼1500 °C). Ultra-high
purity Ar was used as a carrier gas and to maintain an inert at-
mosphere. During the CO2 splitting step, CO2 was fed and mixed
with Ar at the gas inlet of the furnace. The ﬂow rate of both, CO2
and Ar, was adjusted and controlled by mass ﬂow controllers
(MFC, BRONKHORST, Netherlands, precision 70.25%). A S-type
(Pt-Rh) thermocouple records the temperature and controls the
furnace. Off-gases from the TGA were continuously analyzed by a
gas chromatograph (GC, VARIAN, CP-4900, Micro GC 2 channel
system) with a time resolution of about 1 dataset every 2 min.
Approximately 50 mg of material was placed in an Al2O3 cru-
cible and was subjected to several consecutive thermal reduction
(1400 °C) and CO2 splitting (1000 °C) steps. During each thermal
reduction step, the temperature was ramped to 1400 °C (25 °C/
min) and then kept at this temperature for 60 min in the inert
atmosphere of Ar (100 ml/min). A mass loss was observed after
reaching a temperature of 1000 °C, and it was attributed to the
amount of O2 released by the CZH material [54–58]. After per-
forming the thermal reduction of CZH, the sample was cooled to
1000 °C at the natural cooling rate of 25 °C/min under inert at-
mosphere. To perform the CO2 splitting reaction, CO2 was injected,
and the CO2 splitting was performed under isothermal conditions
for 30 min. Flow rates of CO2 (50 ml/min) and Ar (50 ml/min) were
kept constant during the entire CO2 splitting step. The mass gain
observed was attributed to the oxygen uptake of the material, and
can be directly correlated to the CO produced [54–58]. The re-
duction and oxidation yields (Xred and Xoxd, respectively) of the
CZH materials were also calculated using the correlations pub-
lished by Le Gal et al. [58]. The reproducibility of the results was
examined and the error in all experimental measurements was
observed to be less than73%.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Characterization of the CexZryHfzO2 materials
The stoichiometry of the synthesized CZH materials is reported
in Table 1. In Fig. 3a we report the PXRD pattern of all synthesized
materials. All materials consist of a single phase i.e. they corre-
spond to solid solutions that exhibit the cubic ﬂuorite structure
(Fm-3m) identical to that of native CeO2. Peaks related to dopant
oxides such as ZrO2 or HfO2 are notably absent. We observe that
with increasing concentration of the dopants the peak shifts to
higher 2θ angles (see Fig., 3b), i.e. correspond to a cubic unit cell
with smaller a-parameter. The shifts observed conﬁrm the for-
mation of solid solutions of zirconia and hafnia in the cubic base
material. Ce4þ (crystall radius 1.11 Å) is partially replaced by Zr4þ
(0.97 Å) or Hf4þ (0.98 Å), but both ZrO2 and HfO2 exhibit the same
crystal structure as ceria which makes the formation of a solid
solution with a wide solution range possible. It is also evident from
the increase of the width of the peak in Fig. 4b that the size of the
crystalline domain decreases with increasing amounts of dopant.
The result of the quantitative analysis (Scherrer formula) is also
reported in Table 1.
The elemental composition (or ratios) of the CZH materials was
veriﬁed by an EDS analysis. Fig. 4 shows the EDS pattern of CZH5
as an example. The EDS data is summarized and reported in Ta-
ble 1. The close correspondence of the target elemental ratios to
the ones obtained by EDS proves that precipitation of all pre-
cursors at pH 9 was complete. Speciﬁc surface area (SSA), cumu-
lative pore volume (PV) and average pore diameter (PD) were de-
termined from a BET surface area analysis. The individual results
are reported in Table 2. The materials exhibit very similar numbers
and no clear trend can be observed. SSA lies in the range of
40–45 m2/g, PV in the range of 0.10–0.15 cm3/g, and PD in the range
of 12–14 nm. The fact that no clear trend of the SSA as function of
the total amount of dopant can be observed in contrast to the
crystallite size derived from the width of the PXRD peaks strongly
suggests that the latter is indicative of the crystallinity of the
material and not of the size of the particles.
The microstructural morphology of the CZH materials was
analyzed by SEM, and representative images are shown in Fig. 5.
All materials consist of larger agglomerates with a cauliﬂower-like
structure. The diameter of the primary particles is in the range of
10–50 nm. These primary particles exhibit a roundish morphology
and no evidence of being bound by distinct lattice planes is ob-
served. These images conﬁrm the data obtained by PXRD and BET
analysis. The materials exhibit similar values of the SSA as they
consist of aggregates with very similar structure. The primary
particles are not very well crystallized and become smaller with
increasing dopant concentration. The same trend is observed in
the crystallite sizes determined from the peak width in the PXRD
pattern.

3.2. Thermochemical CO2 splitting cycles using ceria–zirconia–haf-
nia materials
All CZH materials were subjected to an initial test consisting of
four consecutive redox cycles. Thermal reduction was performed
at 1400 °C while the following reoxidation in CO2 was performed
at 1000 °C. A baseline TGA proﬁle was acquired under identical
conditions as in the actual experiment but in absence of a sample.
This allows subtracting artifacts due to buoyancy effects or those
caused by changing the atmosphere.
In Fig. 6, we report the baseline corrected TGA graphs for CZH5,
CZH10, CZH15, CZH20, and CZH25. The black curve represents the
temperature program while the other ﬁve curves represent the
weight changes observed. The initial large weight loss observed
during ﬁrst heating segment (approximately the ﬁrst 50 min) is
neglected in the analysis because we assume that a fraction of
incompletely condensed, amorphous hydroxide phase survives
calcination. This fraction increases as the degree of crystallinity
decreases. During the initial heating the hydroxides are converted
to oxides and substantial amounts of water are released. Similar
observations during the thermal reduction are also reported by
others [56–58]. CO2 was admitted during the isothermal segment
at 1000 °C. Amounts of O2 released and CO produced during four
successive cycles were calculated from the mass losses and gains,
respectively, are summarized in Table 3.
It is evident form Fig. 6 that all materials except CZH5 exhibit a
very similar temporal behavior. Both, reduction and reoxidation
seem to be rather slow and do not run to completion. In contrast,
CZH5 exhibits much faster reaction rates. Thermal reduction runs to
completion assuming a slightly skewed baseline. Also reoxidation,

during 2nd, 3rd and 4th CO2 splitting steps amounts to 162 μmol/g
per cycle. Much smaller amounts are observed for the other sam-
ples (CZH10: 121 μmol/g per cycle, CZH15: 100 μmol/g per cycle,
CZH20: 83 μmol/g per cycle, CZH25: 59 μmol/g per cycle). With the
exception of CZH5 all materials exhibit a pronounced decrease of up
to 50% in the amounts of CO released during reoxidation.
In Fig. 7 we report the data for CZH5 together with the O2 and
CO signals obtained from gas chromatography. It is evident from
the ﬁgure that in all cycles the majority of the mass loss already
occurs during the heating from 1000 °C to 1400 °C. Once the
temperature has reached 1400 °C only a minor loss occurs during
the 45 min at 1400 °C. This is also reﬂected in the oxygen signal
obtained from gas chromatography. Once temperature reaches
1400 °C the O2 signal rapidly decreases to the background level.
The reoxidation step, however, is much slower. Once CO2 is ad-
mitted at 1000 °C the weight slowly increases and does not be-
come constant even after 30 min and CO is detected by GC
throughout the reoxidation segment. This conﬁrms that multiple
thermochemical cycles do not decrease the activity of the mate-
rials. It seems also that only a minor amount of sintering occurs
that does not affect the ability of the material to convert CO2 to CO.
Previously, Le Gal et al. [58] synthesized CeO2 by the co-pre-
cipitation of hydroxides method and tested its suitability for the
CO2 splitting cycle. They reported that CeO2 is releasing a constant
amount of O2 (53 μmol/g) in three successive thermal reduction
steps performed at 1400 °C. Their material produced 98, 102, and
105 μmol/g of CO during the reoxidation step performed at
1200 °C, 1100 °C, and 1000 °C in CO2, respectively.
Abanades and Le Gal [57,58] further reported that the redox
activity and thermal stability of CeO2 can be improved by doping
the ceria ﬂuorite crystal structure with zirconia. Ceria–zirconia
solid solutions were synthesized by the same co-precipitation of
hydroxides method and their thermochemical activity was as-
sessed. Among different ceria–zirconia solid solutions examined,
Ce0.75Zr0.25O2 powder exhibited the best performance. During
three complete cycles, respectively, Ce0.75Zr0.25O2 released 137, 96,
and 74 μmol/g of O2 during the reduction step at 1400 °C. In the
same experiment, 139, 132, and 99 μmol/g of CO evolved during
the reoxidation steps at 1200 °C, 1100 °C, and 1000 °C, respectively.
Thus, this material exhibited a severe loss of activity, where its
redox capacity has decreased by almost 50% after only three
complete cycles.
In contrast to the previous results, in this work, the ternary
oxide with the lowest dopant concentration (CZH5) exhibits the
highest redox reactivity. After some degradation in the ﬁrst cycle,
the amounts of O2 (118 μmol/g) and CO (167 μmol/g) released in
the next three redox cycles remained constant and higher as
compared to Zr doped ceria. Furthermore, virtually no further
degradation occurred after the ﬁrst cycle as shown in Fig.7 and
Table 3. Indeed, in case of CZH5, it was observed that the reduction
yield was virtually constant during the 2nd (7.63%), 3rd (8.16%), and
4th (7.63%) thermal reduction steps, which indicated no adverse
effect of thermal cycling on the thermal reduction ability of the
CZH5. It was also realized that the CZH5 oxidation yield was al-
ways higher than 70.00% during the 2nd (72.00%), 3rd (78.03%),
and 4th (91.00%) CO2 splitting steps.

production on a commercial level, it is imperative that its activity
is maintained even after being subjected to many more redox
cycles. The long-term stability of CZH5 was examined during 20
thermochemical cycles with the O2 release and CO production
amounts summarized in Fig. 8. Overall, the data reported in Fig. 8
conﬁrms that the amount of O2 released and CO produced by CZH5
was virtually unchanged in 20 thermochemical cycles. For in-
stance, CZH5 released, in average, 114 μmol/g of O2 and 162 μmol/

4. Summary and conclusions
CexZryHfzO2 (CZH) materials with different chemical stoichio-
metries were successfully synthesized by using the co-precipita-
tion of hydroxides method. The PXRD and EDS analyses conﬁrm
the formation of pure CZH materials phase with an absence of any
other oxides or metal impurities. The BET surface area analyses
indicate that all derived CZH materials possess high surface areas
in the range of 40–45 m2/g. The microstructural analyses per-
formed by SEM showed that the CZH materials derived in this
study acquire a nanoparticle morphology. CZH materials were
further examined towards high-temperature reduction and CO2
splitting thermochemical cycles in the temperature range of 1000
to 1400 °C. Among various CZH materials investigated (CZH5,
CZH10, CZH15, CZH20, and CZH25), CZH5 was observed to be the
best in terms of its redox reactivity during four thermochemical
cycles, which reﬂects into constant amounts of O2 release and CO
production. For instance, in average, steady amounts of O2
(118 μmol/g) and CO (167 μmol/g) were produced in four ther-
mochemical cycles. CZH5 was also observed to be promising as
compared to the previously investigated Zr doped ceria based re-
dox materials in terms of its thermochemical CO production
ability. The redox reactivity and thermal stability of CZH5 was
further examined in 20 thermochemical cycles. Results obtained
show that the thermal reduction (average of 114 μmol/g of O2) and
CO2 splitting (average of 162 μmol/g of CO) capacity of CZH5 re-
main constant for 20 thermochemical cycles. Likewise, the yields
of thermal reduction (8.72%) and oxidation (71.30%) shown by
CZH5 during multiple thermochemical cycles was also stable.
PXRD and BET characterization of the reacted CZH5 showed un-
changed chemical composition and pore characteristics from cycle
2 to cycle 20. The SEM images show a grain growth due to sin-
tering after the ﬁrst thermal reduction step. However, from cycle
2 to cycle 20, the grain size was observed to be constant. Results
obtained in this investigation indicate that the CZH5 material is
very promising towards the thermochemical splitting of CO2 due
to its sustained redox reactivity in multiple cycles.
