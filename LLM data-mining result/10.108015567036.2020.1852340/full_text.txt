Chemical looping co-gasification of lignite and rice husk for syngas 
generation with a Co decorated Cu-based oxygen carrier
Tianle Li, Yuchao Chen, Qiao Wu, and Wenju Wang
School of Energy and Power Engineering, Nanjing University of Science and Technology, Nanjing, China
ABSTRACT
In this work, the steam gasification and chemical looping gasification (CLG) 
of lignite and rice husk (LRH) over 5% Co3O4/CuO oxygen carrier were 
conducted on a fixed-bed system by non-isothermal method. The non- 
isothermal oxygen uncoupling experiments showed that the oxygen uncou­
pling temperature of 5% Co3O4/CuO (730°C) was lower than that of CuO 
(800°C). It was ascribed to the synergy of CuO and Co3O4. The gasification 
results showed that the yields of gas products (H2, CO, CO2 and CH4) in CLG 
of lignite or rice husk were higher than that in steam gasification because of 
the catalytic function of Co3O4/CuO oxygen carrier. Moreover, the catalysis of 
K and the transferring of active OH and H radicals from the biomass to the 
coal will show a synergetic effect between LRH. So the yields of gas products 
in chemical looping co-gasification (CLCG) were also higher than that in 
steam co-gasification or individual CLG. Furthermore, the carbon conversion 
rate in CLCG of LRH was 24.99% higher than that in steam co-gasification. It 
showed that CLCG of coal and biomass was effective for syngas generation, 
and the presence of 5% Co3O4/CuO oxygen carrier enhanced the generation 
of syngas.

Introduction
Syngas (consisting mainly of H2 and CO) can be produced from carbonaceous materials, and it is an 
important raw material for a number of different chemical products (Sheng et al. 2017; Yang et al.  
2019). Nowadays, people prefer to obtain syngas by solid fuels (Guo et al. 2014). However, direct 
combustion of solid fuels brought serious environmental problems (Wang et al. 2017b). Chemical 
looping gasification (CLG) has been suggested to be the most promising technology for its great 
potential to solve energy and environmental problems (Qi et al. 2017). In the CLG process, the oxygen 
carrier circulates in the fuel reactor to transfer oxygen and heat to the fuel, thereby avoiding direct 
contact between fuel and air (Li et al. 2020). Compared with other typical gasification process, CLG 
process saved the demand of oxygen and heat. (Siriwardane et al. 2016).
Because of the high volatile content, porosity, and reactivity (Long and Wang 2011; Wei et al. 2020), 
we chose lignite as the carbon source for CLG. It has been reported that biomass facilitated the 
gasification of coal and it increased the yields of syngas by the reason of a positive synergy between the 
active sites of the biomass and coal. The interaction was formed not only by the H and OH radicals 
formed during co-pyrolysis (Krerkkaiwan et al. 2013), but also by the catalysis of alkali metals such as 
potassium in biomass(Mallick, Mahanta, and Moholkar 2017). Co-gasification of biomass and coal 
was favorable because that it reduced the dependency on fossil fuels and improved the temperature of 
gasification and conversion of carbon. (Bhui and Vairakannu 2018) reported that lignite could 
produce syngas with calorific value of 226.8 kJ/mol under stream gasification, and the calorific value

biomass type, and there are about 7 × 107 tons of rice husks produced annually just in China (Jiang

The choice of oxygen carrier is crucial in the key chemical looping reactions. Its thermodynamics 
properties, oxygen transport capacity, reactivity, resistance to sintering, cost, and environmentally 
friendly characteristics should be considered in the selection process (Adanez et al. 2012; Li et al.  
2009). Abundant works have been reported on the development of high-performance stable oxygen 
carriers. Metal-based oxides (Cu, Ni, Co, Fe, Mn) have been widely used in chemical looping process. 
Among these oxygen carriers, Cu-based oxygen carriers have the advantages of high reactivity (Means 
et al. 2019), strong oxygen transfer capacity (Zheng et al. 2020), high lattice oxygen content (Liu and 
Wang 2018), good sintering resistance (Zhang et al. 2015), low price, and environmental friendliness 
(Dai and Whitty 2020). These characteristics make Cu-based oxygen carriers have promising applica­
tion prospects. Compared with other oxygen carriers, Co3O4 has received less attention due to its high 
cost and low decomposition temperature of 900°C(Fu and Grant 2015; Hossain and De Lasa 2008). 
However, Co3O4 has the highest available oxygen content (Wang et al. 2020), higher reactivity of 
oxidation and reduction reactions (Alalwan, Cwiertny, and Grassian 2017), and good resistance to 
sintering at high temperature (Li et al. 2017). All of these properties make Co3O4 as a good oxygen 
carrier. Studies of CuO and Co3O4 in chemical looping system have been reported in lots of literatures 
in recent years. (Kuang et al. 2020) investigated the reaction of CuO supported on iron ore, chrysolite, 
and limestone with biomass char, and the conversion reached over 90% under N2 atmosphere and 95% 
under CO2 atmosphere. (Alalwan, Cwiertny, and Grassian 2017) researched the chemical looping 
combustion of CH4 over Co3O4 oxygen carrier, they found that the oxidation rate and the degree of 
CH4 oxidation to CO2 reached the maximum at 700°C. However, few publications have been devoted 
to chemical looping system over a mixed oxygen carrier of CuO and Co3O4.
In this work, CLG of rice husk or lignite, and CLCG of LRH were conducted over 5% Co3O4/CuO 
oxygen carrier on a fixed-bed system by non-isothermal method. The gaseous products were analyzed 
by a gas chromatography finally.
Experimental
Materials preparation
The 5% Co3O4/CuO oxygen carrier was synthesized by using a sol-gel technique. Firstly, Co(NO3)2  
· 6H2O and Cu(NO3)2 · 3H2O (1:20) were added into deionized water to prepare the mixed solution. 
0.3 M citric acid solution was then added into the mixed solution. The molar ratio of metal ions to 
citric acid was 2:1. The final solution was stirred in a water bath at 80°C to get a viscous gel. The 
obtained solid was then put in an oven at 120°C to make a xerogel. Finally, the xerogel was calcined at 
800°C for 1 h with a heating rate of 10°C/min. After being ground into particles, the composite oxygen 
carrier (5% Co3O4/CuO) was prepared.

The schematic diagram of fixed-bed system for oxygen uncoupling experiments is illustrated in Figure

Results and discussion
Characterization of fresh catalyst
All kinds of single metal oxides have their own defects. The interaction between metal oxides can 
effectively suppress the phase transition at high temperatures. The non-isothermal oxygen uncoupling 
experiments of CuO and 5% Co3O4/CuO were conducted on a fixed-bed system, and the oxygen 
uncoupling curves were shown in Figure 3. The oxygen-uncoupling temperature of CuO is 800°C, 
while that of 5% Co3O4/CuO is 730°C. It has been reported that CuO released O2 and converted into 
Cu2O at about 800°C in Ar atmosphere (Hosseini et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2017a). The lower oxygen 
uncoupling temperature of 5% Co3O4/CuO oxygen carriers may be concluded as the synergy of CuO 
and Co3O4. In addition, the gaseous oxygen release of CuO and Co3O4 was 20% and 26.6%

CLG of lignite
The gasification process of coal includes pyrolysis, steam gasification, water-gas shift reaction, carbon 
gasification reaction, methanation reaction and so on. The outlet gaseous production in CLG included 
H2, CO, CO2 and CH4, and these gases were analyzed online by a gas chromatography. The gas yields 
with unit mass were obtained by integrating the flow rate values of produced gas. The changes of gas 
production rates with time are shown in Figure 6. The maximum yield of H2 is 0.03 mol/g in lignite 
char gasification and that is 0.05 mol/g in CLG of lignite. The H2 yield in CLG of lignite is 40% higher 
than that in steam gasification of lignite. H2 is mainly produced by the water-gas shift reaction 
(CO+H2O→CO2+ H2) and the gasification reaction between lignite and steam (char(C)+H2O→H2 
+ CO). The increased H2 yield is due to that the heat released from CLG improved the water gas shift 
reaction of CO and the gasification of tar. The maximum yields of CO and CO2 are 0.014 mol/g and 
0.025 mol/g in the gasification of lignite, respectively. It increases to 0.02 mol/g and 0.03 mol/g in CLG 
of lignite with 5% Co3O4/CuO as oxygen carrier. For the increase of CO yields, there are two reasons to 
explain it. On the one hand, the reduction reaction of oxygen carrier (C+ CuO→CO+Cu) provides the 
heat for the gasification of lignite. The equilibrium shift improves the CO yield. On the other hand, the 
reduction of CO2 (C+ CO2→2CO) obtained from chemical looping combustion (CLC) produces CO. 
CO2 was mainly produced by water-gas shift reaction, the reaction of C with CuO (C + 2CuO→CO2 
+ Cu) and the reaction of CO with CuO (CO+2CuO→CO2+ Cu2O). CO2 generates slowly in the initial 
stage of reaction due to the inadequate oxygen concentration. The ratio of H2 to CO in yield is 2.5:1. It 
was because the water-gas shift reaction led to a larger proportion and a higher product mass of H2. 
CH4 is mainly produced by the pyrolysis of lignite. However, the yield of CH4 is relatively low, and it is 
always below 0.0015 mol/g.

higher than that in the gasification of rice husk. The main reasons for the improvement in CLG are 
concluded as two aspects. First, the exothermic reaction of oxygen carrier provides more heat for rice 
husk gasification. According to the theory of Le Chatelier, the high temperature is beneficial to the 
equilibrium of exothermic reaction. The equilibrium shift improves the gasification of C with steam. 
Second, the secondary cracking and the catalysis of oxygen carrier also increase the H2 yield. The CO 
yield in CLG of rice husk is 0.005 mol/g, which is 66.67% higher than that in gasification of rice husk. 
The heat provided by the exothermic reaction of oxygen carrier in CLG promotes the conversion of 
semi-coke fixed carbon to CO. In the CLG process and gasification process of rice husk, the CO yield is 
33.33% and 27.27% of the H2 yield, respectively. The proportion of CO to H2 in the CLG of rice husk is 
higher than that in rice husk gasification, which is a disadvantage of the CLG process. The CH4 yield in 
CLG of rice husk is 4 × 10−4 mol/g, which is 166.7% higher than that of rice husk gasification. This 
situation is caused by the high temperature and the high yield of H2 and CO. In that condition, the 
methanation reaction of H2 and CO produces more CH4. The secondary cracking of tar interferes with 
the reforming reaction of CH4 with steam and the transition from CH4 to H2 and CO. The proportion 
of CH4 in available gases in CLG of rice husk is 1.27%, and 0.68% in rice husk gasification. It turned 
out that the presence of oxygen carrier could improve the proportion of CH4 in available gases. The 
CO2 yield in CLG of rice husk is 0.001 mol/g, which is 25% higher than that in rice husk gasification. In 
the steam gasification of rice husk, the H2O molecule adsorbed on the surface of carbon atom breaks 
down into a hydrogen atom and a hydroxyl group, and the hydroxyl groups with strong oxidizing 
property oxidize CO to CO2. Furthermore, the presence of oxygen carrier in CLG process improves 
the oxidation reaction of C. Hence, the yield of CO2 increases. In brief, the yields of four kinds of gases 
in the CLG of rice husk are higher than those in steam gasification.
Steam co-gasification and CLCG of LRH
Biomass showed high volatile content and good carbon sequestration activity. The co-gasification of 
coal and biomass can increase the gasification temperature, carbon conversion rate and the decom­
position of tar. Besides, the synergy in co-gasification improves the efficiency of gasification reactions. 
The synergy is explained as the stimulative effect of an alkali metal such as K in biomass.

Steam co-gasification of LRH
In the steam co-gasification experiments of LRH, the weight ratio of lignite/rice husk blend (0.5g) was 
50:50%, it was reported that the synergistic effect of biomass and coal is most significant when the ratio 
of biomass to coal is 1:1 (Krerkkaiwan et al. 2013). The gasification results were shown in Figure 8. It is 
found that the H2 yield in lignite gasification is 3.5 times of that in steam gasification rice husk 
gasification. The yield of H2 in steam co-gasification of LRH is 71.43% of that in gasification of 0.5g 
lignite, and the H2 yield in 0.5 g rice husk gasification is 40% of that in steam co-gasification. (Arifin 
et al. 2015) also found that co-gasification of LRH enhanced the hydrogen content in syngas when the 
mass yield of coal was around 50%. The yield of CO in co-gasification of LRH is 64.29% of that in 0.5 
g lignite gasification, and the CO yield in 0.5 g rice husk gasification is 33.33% of that in co-gasification. 
The yield of CH4 in co-gasification is 54.55% of that in 0.5 g lignite gasification. The CH4 yield in 0.5 
g rice husk gasification is 33.33% of that in co-gasification. The yield of CO2 in co-gasification is 
66.67% of that in 0.5 g lignite gasification, and the CO2 yield in 0.5 g rice husk gasification is 37.52% of 
that in co-gasification. Moreover, the mass of lignite in co-gasification is only half of that in individual 
gasification. It suggested that the yields of produced gases in steam co-gasification of LRH were higher 
than those in steam gasification of lignite, which was caused by the transferring of active OH and 
H radicals from rice husk to the lignite and a promoting effect of K released from biomass. Similar 
results have been reported that the K released from the cedar bark will condense on the surface of coal 
tar in the co-pyrolysis, and it will facilitate the reaction (Wannapeera, Fungtammasan, and 
Worasuwannarak 2011).

experimental results of CLCG and CLG of 0.5 g lignite or 0.5 g rice husk are shown in Figure 9.

g lignite is 70.52% of that in CLG, and the yield of CO in the CLG of 0.5 g rice husk is 35.71% of that in 
CLCG. The yield of CH4 in CLCG is 66.67% of that in CLG of 0.5 g lignite. The CH4 yield in CLG of

The carbon conversion in steam co-gasification and CLCG
The conversion of carbon in different gasification experiments was shown in Figure 10. The 
C conversion of lignite in steam gasification is 58.45%. In CLG process, the C conversion in of lignite 
is 80.9%, which is 22.45% higher than that in steam gasification. The C conversion of rice husk in 
steam gasification is 60.05%. The C conversion of rice husk in CLG is 82.5%, which is 22.45% higher 
than that in steam gasification. What is more, the C conversion in the steam co-gasification of LRH is 
62.43%, and in CLCG process, the C conversion of LRH is 87.42%, which is 24.99% higher than that in 
steam co-gasification. In addition, the C conversion in co-gasification is also higher than that in 
individual feed gasification. The higher C conversion in CLCG is due to the effect of 5%Co3O4/CuO 
oxygen carrier. There are also researchers reported that the carbon conversion rate and the yields of 
available gases in co-gasification of coal and biomass are larger than those in coal gasification (Song 
et al. 2006; Tursun et al. 2016). (Figueiredo, Orfão, and Ferraz 1984) found that Co improved the 
production of carbon with well-developed mesoporosity in gasification of biomass char. The porous 
carbon indicates the improvement on carbon gasification of Co catalyst. (Furusawa and Tsutsumi  
2005) reported that Co catalyst was a promising catalyst and it increases the energy efficiency of 
biomass gasification system.

was also higher than that in individual feed gasification, and in CLCG process, the C conversion of 
LRH is 24.99% higher than that in steam co-gasification. Given the advantages of CLCG, we will try 
more catalysts (such as spinel and perovskite) to increase the yields of syngas in the future.
