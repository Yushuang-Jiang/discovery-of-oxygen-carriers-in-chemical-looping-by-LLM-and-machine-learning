Summary
The synthesis, characterization, and application of the MgxFe3−xO4 (MF) redox
materials towards conversion of CO2 via thermochemical redox reactions are
reported. Sol‐gel method was utilized for the synthesis of MF materials. The
derived MF materials were characterized to determine the physicochemical
properties using various analytical techniques. PXRD results authorized the
phase pure composition, and the SEM analysis designated nanoparticulated
morphology of all the synthesized MF materials. The MF materials were fur-
ther tested to estimate their O2 releasing and CO production ability in multiple
thermochemical cycles using a high‐temperature thermogravimetric analyzer
(TGA). Attained outcomes specify that the MgFe2O4 (MF10) was superior in
terms of the thermal reduction and CO2 splitting capacity as compared with
the other MF materials. For instance, MF10 liberated 58.7 μmol of O2/g·cycle
and produced 79.6 μmol of CO/g·cycle with an average O2 recovery of 67.7%/
cycle in nine thermochemical cycles.

INTRODUCTION
CO2 is believed as one of the foremost greenhouse
gases,1,2 and its persistent release largely from the chem-
ical and power plants3 results into a prerequisite of a sus-
tainable technology that will reutilize the CO2. One of the
routes for the utilization of the CO2 is to convert it into
synthesis gas (syngas) via solar‐driven two‐step thermo-
chemical H2O/CO2 splitting cycle (using metal oxides).4
Solar syngas yielded by this cycle can be transformed into
gasoline, kerosene, and others by employing the catalytic
Fischer‐Tropsch process.5 Eventually, the solar fuels pro-
duced by utilizing the metal oxide–based thermochemical
splitting of H2O and CO2 offer an alternative sustainable
energy resource to fulfill the future fuel requirement.
Among the several metal oxides assessed for the solar
thermochemical H2O/CO2 splitting cycle,6-15 the ferrite‐
based redox materials are studied by numerous research
groups.16-20 For instance, Ni‐ferrite,21-23 Zn‐ferrite,24-26
Co‐ferrite,27-29 Sn‐ferrite,30 Mn‐ferrite,31-33 and others34,35
are some of the ferrites investigated. Table 1 reports the
key features of the previous investigations associated with
the ferrite‐based H2O/CO2 splitting cycle. In addition to
these studies, Randhir et al36 examined the blend of
MgFe2O4 and MgO towards the thermochemical conver-
sion of H2O/CO2. Results attained in this study indicated
that the MgFe2O4/MgO mixture produced higher sums of
fuel at lower reduction temperature as compared with the
CeO2. Nonetheless, the cyclability and the reaction kinet-
ics are the major issues in the case of the MgFe2O4/MgO
mixture that needs to be explored in detail. Moreover, we
believe that the finest combination of the Mg‐based fer-
rite, ie, MgxFe3 −xO4 (where x = 0.2 to 1), needs to be rec-
ognized to accomplish maximum fuel production.

2.1 | Synthesis of MF using a sol‐gel
approach
The sol‐gel methodology utilized for the development of
MF materials is presented in Figure 1. The sol‐gel synthe-
sis
primarily

molecular

and

specified

powder, narrow particle size distribution, and higher spe-

were synthesized by varying the x in the range of 0.2 to 1.
For instance, following MF materials were prepared in

Mg0.8Fe2.2O4 (MF8), and MgFe2O4 (MF10). In the begin-
ning, the nitrate precursors of magnesium and iron
[purchased from Sigma Aldrich, Mg (NO3)2·6H2O and
Fe (NO3)3·9H2O] were absolutely dissolved in 20 mL of
ethanol using an ultrasonic bath. Propylene oxide was
added to this solution (dropwise 15‐20 drop per min for
2‐4 min approximately) for the development of MF gel.
Synthesized gels were aged for 24 hours at ambient tem-
perature, then dried at 100°C for 2 hours (using a
temperature‐controlled hot plate), and in the end cal-
cined at 800°C for 2 hours (heating rate 10°C/min) in
the presence of atmospheric air (by using a muffle
furnace Nabertherm model RT 50‐250/11/P 330). The cal-
cined crystalline nanopowders of MF materials were
stored in a dry place and further characterized using mul-
tiple analytical techniques.
2.2 | Characterization of MgxFe3 −xO4
The crystallographic analysis of the derived MF materials
was performed using Panalytical XPert MPD/DY636 pow-
der X‐ray diffractometer. The microstructural morphol-
ogy and elemental composition of the MF materials
were analyzed using a scanning electron microscope
(SEM, Nova Nano 450, FEI) coupled with energy‐
dispersive X‐ray spectroscopy (EDX).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
PXRD patterns allied with the sol‐gel–derived MF mate-
rials are displayed in Figure 3. PXRD examination verified
that all the peaks are linked to the respective MF material
(spinel phase), and the strong reflections further approved
the crystalline nature of each MF sample. As the peaks
associated with the individual metal oxides (MgO and
Fe3O4) and metals (Mg and Fe) are absent, it can be con-
cluded that the Mg+2 was absolutely encompassed in the
iron oxide crystal structure. The results obtained via EDX
analysis corroborates this conclusion.
Table 2 reports the crystallite sizes of the sol‐gel–
derived MF materials, computed using Scherrer equation.
The numbers stated in Table 2 designate that, as the
molar concentration of Mg+2 increased, crystallite size
decreased. For example, as the molar concentration of
Mg+2 upsurges from 0.2 to 1.0, the crystallite size was
reduced from 85.5 to 79.2 nm.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
MgxFe3−xO4 (MF) materials, ie, Mg0.2Fe2.8O4 (MF2),
Mg0.4Fe2.6O4 (MF4), Mg0.5Fe2.5O4 (MF5), Mg0.6Fe2.4O4
(MF6), Mg0.8Fe2.2O4 (MF8), and MgFe2O4 (MF10), were
derived
using
the
propylene
oxide–assisted
sol‐gel
approach. The PXRD peaks obtained approved phase pure
composition of the MF materials with no traces of individ-
ual metal or metal oxides such as Mg, Fe, MgO, and others.
As expected, the crystallite size of MF materials was
observed to be reduced from 85.8 to 79.2 nm as the molar
concentration of Mg+2 in the iron oxide crystal structure
increased from 0.2 to 1. The SEM images obtained for all
the MF materials sanctioned nanoparticulated morphol-
ogy. Multiple CO2 splitting cycles were conducted to esti-
mate the redox reactivity of the derived MF materials.
The obtained results indicated that the MF10 material is
the best choice (among the MF materials investigated in
this study) in terms of the O2 releasing and CO2 splitting
capability. The overall results also concluded that the
incorporation of Mg+2 in the iron oxide crystal structure
was beneficial to improve the O2 releasing and CO produc-
tion competence of iron oxide. As the CO/O2 ratio is less
than 2, it clearly indicates that the reoxidation ability of
MF materials is poor. We are currently working towards
synthesizing ternary and quaternary ferrite materials
including Mg to improve the CO/O2 molar ratio. Likewise,
we are
also
involved
towards
the
investigation
of
cyclability and reaction kinetics associated with the
MF10‐based H2O/CO2 splitting reactions.
