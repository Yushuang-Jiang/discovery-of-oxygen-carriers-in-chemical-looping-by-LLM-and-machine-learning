A B S T R A C T
In chemical looping combustion (CLC), a necessary oxygen polishing step exerted an additional cost to the
technology due to an extra demand of pure O2. However, the additional cost can be lowered if the oxygen
carriers can produce gaseous oxygen, i.e. has a chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) property. A
CLC + CLOU scheme is proposed in the study. Production of gaseous O2 using a manganese ore and the Cu-
modiﬁed manganese ore as oxygen carriers were investigated. The reaction kinetics of the two oxygen carriers
were studied by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). Multiple O2 uptake and release cycles were carried out to
demonstrate the good stability of the oxygen carriers. Continuous tests with both oxygen carriers were carried
out in a dual ﬂuidized bed. Results showed that both oxygen carriers could be ﬂuidized well in the continuous
run and a stream of O2 could be obtained stably. The Cu-modiﬁed manganese ore showed higher oxidation rate
and oxygen transport capacity than the original manganese ore. Both materials have advantage economically
and could be promising for O2 production in the CLC + CLOU scheme.
1. Introduction
CO2 emission into atmosphere has been considered to contribute a
large part of the greenhouse eﬀect causing global warming [1]. Carbon
capture and sequestration (CCS) is a set of technologies aiming at
capturing waste CO2 from large point sources such as fossil fuel power
plants and depositing the CO2 into a storage site. The estimated costs
for CO2 transportation (USD 1–3/ton/100 km) [2] and sequestration
(USD 4–8/ton-CO2) [3] are small compared to that for CO2 capture,
estimated at USD 35–55/ton-CO2 [4]. Therefore, reducing the cost in
capturing CO2 is crucial to make CCS more economically attractive.
Chemical looping combustion (CLC) is one of the promising tech-
niques to obtain pure CO2 from a power plant allowing for CO2 se-
questration [5]. The main advantage of CLC compared to other CCS
technologies is its lower cost due to the inherent separation of CO2
without nitrogen dilution.
However, in CLC experiments with solid fuels [6–9], a complete
combustion can hardly be achieved due to the low reactivity of oxygen
carriers and/or coal segregation eﬀect [10,11]. Due to coal segregation,
part of the syngas from char gasiﬁcation is released in the upper regions
of the fuel reactor and therefore has insuﬃcient contact with the bed
material, which results an incompleted combustion. For example, in the
experiments conducted by Cuadrat et al. [8], 17 vol% syngas (CO and
H2) was found in the oﬀgas for the 125–200 μm coal and 49 vol% for
the 74–125 μm coal. The fuel conversion was also low in the test by
Chalmers on a 100 kWth unit with solid fuels, with the concentration of
unburnt gases (mainly H2, CO, and CH4) in the oﬀgas up to 32 vol% [6].
In order to achieve a complete or very high fuel conversion, an
oxygen polishing step is considered a necessary step [12]. In the oxygen
polishing step, pure oxygen is injected into a chamber to burn out the
unconverted gases, as shown in Fig. 1.
However, the oxygen polishing step would increase the cost of CLC
due to the extra demand of pure oxygen. In the operation of the 1 MWth
CLC plant in Darmstadt, an extra 26–38% oxygen demand was required
[13]. The cost of oxygen polishing step is estimated as 4–9 €/ton-CO2,
accounting for one third of the total cost of CLC [14].
If the oxygen carriers can release gaseous oxygen in the fuel reactor,
the total demand of O2 in the oxygen polishing step can be reduced.
Therefore, a CLC + CLOU scheme is proposed here, as shown in Fig. 2.
In this scheme, solid fuels react with the oxygen carriers at the lower
region of the fuel reactor in a normal CLC condition. At the upper re-
gion, the unburnt gases, including mainly H2, CO, CH4, react with
gaseous oxygen released by the oxygen carriers. Hopefully, a large part
of the unburnt gases can be burn out and thus minimize the demand of
pure O2 in the following oxygen polishing step. The reaction condition
at the upper region is slightly diﬀerent from that in the normal CLOU

condition. The normal chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling
(CLOU) process, ﬁrst proposed by Lyngfelt and his coworkers [15], was
based on the use of an oxygen carrier which releases gaseous O2 in the
fuel reactor allowing the solid fuel to burn with gaseous O2. In the
CLC + CLOU scheme, the gaseous O2 mainly react with unburnt gases
from coal gasiﬁcation rather than coal directly, aiming at reducing the
demand of pure oxygen in the following oxygen polishing step.
For the lower region of the scheme, a lot of oxygen carriers, such as
ilmenite, ores, artiﬁcial oxides, has been studied [16]. For the upper
region in the scheme, only copper, manganese and cobalt oxides are
capable of releasing oxygen. Copper oxide has a high tendency for
agglomeration and a high price. Cobalt oxide is more expensive and not
environmentally friendly. Manganese oxide is relatively cheap and do
much less harm to the environment but has a relatively low reactivity.
Research on improving the performance of manganese oxide was
usually carried out by adding diﬀerent inert materials. Mn-based
oxygen carriers with ZrO2 as an inert material stabilized by MgO, CaO,
or CeO2 showed high reactivity and avoidance of agglomeration [17].
Azad et al. examined an oxygen carrier made by mixing, extruding, and
calcining a 1:1 M mixture of CuO and Mn2O3 for CLOU, and the
Mn–Cu–O spinel system shows good performance [18]. However, the
manufacture procedure of synthetic oxygen carriers is usually complex,
and use of the large amount of copper oxide in most cases increases the
cost.
In this study, two materials were prepared as oxygen carriers. One is
the manganese ore. The other is manganese ore impregnated by copper.
The Cu-modiﬁed manganese ore has been examined with CO in a pre-
vious study [19] and showed a 6 times reactivity enhancement after
copper impregnation. This study will focus on the oxygen uptake and
release properties of the manganese ore before and after the copper
impregnation. Results showed that the Cu-modiﬁed manganese ore has
higher oxidation rate and oxygen capacity than the original manganese
ore. Both two materials showed good stability in the TGA test. Con-
tinuous test in the dual ﬂuidized bed reactor showed that the materials
presents no agglomeration or other ﬂuidization problems and these
materials could be suitable for use in the CLC + CLOU scheme as
oxygen carriers.
2. Experiments
2.1. Preparation and characterization of materials
The natural manganese ore (denoted as Mn) was provided by Mugui
Manganese Ore Company from Guangxi Province in China. The ore
particles were ﬁrst calcined in air for 4 h at 950 °C and then sieved to
125–300 μm. The Cu-modiﬁed oxygen carriers (denoted as CuMn) were
prepared by impregnation of Cu(NO3)2·3H2O solution. The natural
manganese ore particles were soaked in the solution for 6 h at ambient
temperature and calcined in air for 2 h at 500 °C. After cooling down, all
of the particles were resieved to 125–300 μm. For every 50 g of man-
ganese ore, solid Cu(NO3)2·3H2O (3.8 g) was used as a precursor and
was dissolved in water (15 ml) to give the solution. The composition of
the manganese ore was analyzed by X-ray ﬂuorescence (XRF) and
summarized in Table 1. The BET (Brunauer, Emmett and Teller) surface
area and BJH (Barrett, Joyner and Halenda) pore volume of the parti-
cles were measured with a Micromeritics micropore analyzer (Auto-
sorb-iQ2-MP, NOVA4000). Furthermore, the crushing strength of the
particles was determined using an HP-10 digital force gauge. The value
given is the average force in N from 30 individual measurements
needed to crush a particle in the size fraction of 125–300 µm. The bulk
density, BET surface area, BJH pore volume and crushing strength of
Mn and CuMn are also listed in Table 1.
Pure oxides were prepared to illustrate the copper addition to the
manganese oxide. Manganese oxide (Mn2O3) was prepared by calcining
solution of Mn(NO3)2 (50 wt%) in air for 3 h at 800 °C. Cu-Mn oxides
was prepared by calcining the mixture solution of Mn(NO3)2 and Cu
(NO3)2. Mixed oxides with diﬀerent mass ratios of copper to manganese
were prepared and denoted as Cu5%, Cu10%, Cu20% and Cu30%.

stabilization, multicycle tests were performed by exposing the sample
to alternating oxidizing and inert conditions. The oxidation reaction of
Mn-based oxygen carrier in air reactor is exothermic, while the de-
composition reaction of Mn-based oxygen carrier in fuel reactor is en-
dothermic; therefore, the average temperature of air reactor will be
higher than that of fuel reactor in order to satisfy the heat balance.
However, the air entering into air reactor is low temperature and this
inlet air has to be heated up to a high temperature by the heat released
from the exothermic oxidation reaction of Mn-based oxygen carrier,
and therefore there is a temperature rise step. From the chemical
equilibrium analysis, the oxidation of Mn-based oxygen carrier could be
enhanced at 750 °C and this value is selected as the experimental
temperature to simulate the temperature rise step occurring in air re-
actor. The oxygen release temperature was 950 °C in pure N2 with the
same ﬂow rate. The oxidation time was 60 min and the oxygen release
time was 90 min in each cycle. The heating and cooling rate between
800 and 950 °C was 10 °C/min.
2.3. Continuous test in the dual ﬂuidized bed reactor
The continuous test was performed in a dual ﬂuidized bed reactor,
as illustrated in Fig. 3. Basically, the system consisted of an air reactor
(AR), a fuel reactor (FR), a riser, a cyclone and two loop seals (LS). The
riser was used to transport oxygen carriers from the air reactor to the
fuel reactor. The solid circulation rate was controlled by the lower loop
seal. The fuel reactor was a bubbling ﬂuidized bed with a length of
1.3 m. The inner diameters of both the air reactor and the fuel reactor
were 50 mm. The inner diameter of the riser was 20 mm. The height of
the riser was 3.3 m. The secondary air was introduced at the lower part
of the riser to assist particle entrainment. The air and fuel reactors were
heated by electric furnaces. K-type thermocouples were used to monitor
the temperature. Fine particles from both reactors were collected by
ﬁlters. O1–O8 and I0–I2 were pressure measurement points used to
monitor the solid circulation of the entire loop. The system has been
used for a continuous test of ilmenite and cement-supported CuO
oxygen carriers, and more details about the reactor system can be found
in the previous publications [20,21]. Two continuous tests were con-
ducted in the dual ﬂuidized bed reactor. Each run lasted for about
400 min. The initial solid inventory of the system for both tests were
3500 g. N2 was used as ﬂuidizing gas for the fuel reactor (FR) and loop
seals. Air was used as ﬂuidizing gas for the air reactor (AR). The fuel
reactor was maintained at 950 °C and the air reactor was maintained at
750 °C.
The eﬀect of copper content on the performance was studied in TGA
and it was found that the copper content of 5% can still accelerate the
reaction kinetics, therefore this kind of oxygen carrier was used for dual
ﬂuidized bed study.

Five redox cycles were carried out for Mn and CuMn, as shown in
Fig. 4. As the temperature increased from room temperature to 950 °C,
the weight of both samples decreased due to O2 is released. The weight
loss of CuMn for the ﬁrst cycle is 3.3%, a little higher than 2.9% of Mn.
This suggested that impregnation of copper increased the amount of O2
released and lowered the O2 releasing temperature. Both materials
failed to get back to the original state after 60 min’ oxidation during the
successive redox cycles and this is because the original material con-
tains lots of MnO2, and this high valence state cannot be recovered from
other relatively lower valence state even after very long oxidation time
as shown in Fig. 4.
Fig. 5 presented the oxygen release rate of both oxygen carriers as a
function of temperature for the ﬁrst cycle. It is noteworthy that addition
of copper to the manganese ore reduced the decomposition temperature
of the material. Therefore, O2 was released at a lower temperature for
the Cu-modiﬁed manganese ore. As the temperature increased from the
room temperature to 950 °C, CuMn started to release O2 earlier than the
Mn. CuMn showed higher oxygen release rate than Mn when the tem-
perature is lower than 920 °C.
The weight loss in each cycle maintained at around 0.72% for Mn
and 0.82% for CuMn in the successive oxygen uptake and release cy-
cles, as shown in Fig. 6. Both materials showed good stability during the
ﬁve redox cycles. In conclusion, the copper addition contributes to a
higher oxygen transport capacity of the manganese ore.
Fig. 7 showed the oxygen uptake and release rates of both materials
in the third cycle. The conversion curve for both materials showed si-
milar proﬁles. However, the proﬁle of the oxidation rate curve is quite
diﬀerent from the oxygen release one. During reduction, the conversion
curve includes a very fast stage and a slow stage. Whereas the oxidation
rate was relatively slow throughout. Therefore, it is necessary to in-
crease the oxidation rate to match the oxygen release rate. Addition of
copper to the manganese ore improved the oxidation rate dramatically,

3.3. Agglomeration and attrition behaviour of oxygen carrier particles
Particle agglomeration should be avoided since it has a negative
eﬀect on gas solid contact and can lead to deﬂuidization. Particles
impregnated by copper should be taken special care because copper
oxide has a high tendency for agglomeration. In this study, both the
manganese ore and the Cu-modiﬁed manganese ore showed no signs of
agglomeration during the dual ﬂuidized bed tests. Particles are con-
tinuously lost in the reactor due to abrasive wear; or attrition and
fragmentation resulting from particle-particle interactions and particle-
wall interactions. Resistance to attrition and fragmentation is an im-
portant property of particles used as oxygen carriers. The crushing
strength was tested to evaluate the mechanical property of the material.
A crushing strength larger than 2 N is considered suﬃciently hard ac-
cording to a study by Ryden et al. [23]. The crushing strengths of both
materials are 1.9 N. The mass balance of the reactor system was also
established and summarized in Table 3. Actually, establishing exact
mass balance for the dual ﬂuidized bed reaction system turned out to be
diﬃcult. The total mass of particles collected after the test was less than
the amount added before the test. For Mn, 95 g was missing. For CuMn,
125 g was missing. This was partly due to the phase transformation of
the ore material (Mn2O3 →Mn3O4). Another reason should be that
some ﬁnes were stuck inside the loop seals or tubes where cleaning was

4. Conclusions
In this work, both the manganese ore and copper impregnated
manganese ore were tested in a TGA and a dual ﬂuidized bed as oxygen
carriers for their oxygen release and uptake properties. The oxidation
rate is much lower than the oxygen release rate for the manganese ore.
Impregnation the manganese ore with copper nitrite solution enhanced
the oxidation rate and oxygen transport capacity. The reaction rate and
oxygen transport capacity kept stable in redox cycles. The oxygen re-
lease temperature of manganese ore could be lowered by copper ad-
dition. The similar phenomena was also observed in the TGA test of
manganese oxide and Cu–Mn oxides. Two continuous tests were per-
formed in the dual ﬂuidized bed reactor. Oxygen was produced con-
tinuously and stably in two 6.5 h tests. The amount of O2 produced
increased with the temperature in the fuel reactor. No agglomeration
was found. Mechanical property was judged by the crushing strength
(1.9 N) and the mass balance of the reactor system.
