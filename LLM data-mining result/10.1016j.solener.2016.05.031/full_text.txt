Thermochemical redox processes involving novel oxygen ion conducting materials with perovskites
structure are studied to evaluate their application potential for solar energy storage in concentrated solar
power plants. A series of Ba and Sr containing perovskites was synthesized via modiﬁed Pechini method
and their crystal structure was characterized by XRD. The thermochemical reduction–oxidation steps of
the redox cycle and oxygen exchange capacity of the perovskites were investigated by thermogravimetric
(TG) analysis. The results revealed that Co-based perovskites are the most promising candidates for solar
thermochemical energy storage application. The O2 release/absorption of Co-based perovskites is com-
pleted in a reversible way when reaching a given temperature. BaCoO3 reduction occurs promptly when
the temperature reaches 900 C in Ar atmosphere (pO2 = 106 atm), and the oxidation proceeds com-
pletely as soon as the gas is switched from Ar to 20% O2 at 600 C. In these conditions, the amount of
monatomic oxygen released/captured reaches 0.47/0.49 mol per mol BaCoO3. Part substitution in A-
site improves the O2 exchange capacity of Ba0.5Sr0.5FeO3d but not Ba0.5Sr0.5CoO3d, while part substitu-
tion in B-site improves the O2 exchange capacity of SrCo0.8Fe0.2O3d and SrCo0.2Fe0.8O3d. Part substitution
together
in A-site and B-site of perovskites
does not improve
the O2 exchange capacity of
Ba0.5Sr0.5Co0.8Fe0.2O3d and Ba0.5Sr0.5Co0.2Fe0.8O3d. The Fe-based perovskites generally exhibit continu-
ous non-stoichiometry changes according to the temperature change suggesting continuous topotactic
evolution, while the non-stoichiometry of Mn-based systems is almost not changed at the considered
temperatures. Ba containing systems (BaCoO3, BaFeO3 and Ba0.5Sr0.5CoO3) show the largest oxygen
release ability under inert (pO2 = 106 atm) or oxidizing atmosphere (pO2 = 0.2 atm) up to 1050 C, while
only BaCoO3 can be fully re-oxidized at 600 C in a 20% O2 atmosphere, with an energy storage capacity of
292 J/g.

1. Introduction
Solar energy is considered to be an abundant, clean and renew-
able energy. Due to the low energy density and intermittent sup-
ply, concentrating and subsequent storage of solar energy is
thereby an attractive procedure for solar energy utilization, such
as photon to voltage, solar to thermal and solar to fuel conversion.
The latter procedure can be realized through electrochemical, pho-
tochemical, or thermochemical splitting of H2O and CO2 (Scheffe
and Steinfeld, 2014; Petrovic et al., 2015). Among these processes,
thermochemical splitting of CO2 and H2O attracts much interest
because it utilizes the entire spectrum of solar light to generate
CO and H2. The syngas mixture H2/CO can be further processed
to liquid hydrocarbon fuels via Fischer–Tropsch and other catalytic
processes.
A typical procedure for thermochemical redox processes often
consists of two-step cycles using reversible metal oxide redox reac-
tions (redox pair) (Agraﬁotis et al., 2015). In the ﬁrst step, the metal
oxide is heated by concentrated solar energy and thermal reduc-
tion releases O2 during temperature increase; in the second step,
the reduced metal oxide is re-oxidized by re-incorporating into
the lattice oxygen from CO2 and H2O, which produces CO and H2.
Alternatively, the stored energy during the endothermal reduc-
tion step can be recovered during the reversible exothermal oxida-
tion reaction with air. In this case, the exploitation of the endo/
exothermal effects of the reactions is favored over the production

As the thermochemical redox processes are all realized via the
oxygen exchange properties of redox materials, the redox pair sys-
tem has to be carefully chosen, and the oxygen exchange capacity
thereby becomes one of the important factors. Till now, different
single metal oxides such as ZnO/Zn, CdO/Cd, SnO2/SnO, GeO2/
GeO (volatile), and mixed metal oxides based on ceria, ferrites,
and
hercynite
(non-volatile)
have
been
extensively
studied
(Pardo et al., 2014; Levêque and Abanades, 2014; Alonso et al.,
2015; Loutzenhiser and Steinfeld, 2011; Koepf et al., 2012). The
volatile systems often meet the recombination problem of the
decomposition products, while the non-volatile systems such as
CeO2 and Fe3O4 may face the strong and non-ﬂexible binding
energy between metal and oxygen, which leads to low yields of
the either reduction or oxidation step. Mixed metal oxides systems
can then be considered in order to adjust the oxygen exchange
capacity and working temperature.
Among the mixed metal oxides redox materials, perovskites
with ABO3 formula may represent good candidates with high
potential for the solar thermochemical application. Due to high tol-
erance of distort structure, the materials can still keep the per-
ovskites crystal structure even after losing much oxygen ions,
yielding non-stoichiometric compounds (Royer et al., 2014). Per-
ovskites structure can be substituted in A or B sites via different
elements to obtain a large class of candidate redox systems with
ﬂexible metal–oxygen binding energies, which is favorable for
the adjustment of the oxygen exchange capacity and working tem-
peratures. Thermal reduction proceeds via the formation of oxygen
vacancies and the release of gaseous O2, resulting in the change in
materials stoichiometry.
Several studies have focused on the thermochemical behavior
of perovskites (Jiang et al., 2014; Demont et al., 2014; Babiniec
et al., 2015). The group of ETH/PSI investigated LaxSr1xMnO3 for
both H2O and CO2 splitting, which is a classic system with per-
ovskite structure (Scheffe et al., 2013). The results corroborated
the higher O2 yield of perovskites compared to that of ceria. Yang
et al. provided a quantitative assessment of the thermodynamic
and kinetic properties of La1xSrxMnO3d perovskites. Sr-doping
into lanthanum manganite increases the thermodynamic fuel pro-
duction capacity, which reaches 9 Nml g1 H2 with La0.6Sr0.4-
MnO3d for a thermochemical cycle operated between 1400 C in
Ar (containing 10 ppm O2) and 800 C (pH2O = 0.20 atm) (Yang
et al., 2014). A subsequent study of LaxSr1xMnyAl1yO3 on H2O
and CO2 splitting showed that this material exhibited much higher
reduction extent than ceria, achieved in addition at about 300 C
lower temperature, as well as multi-cyclic capability between
1400 C and 1000 C (McDaniel et al., 2013). A combination of
ab initio and experimental study based on SrxLa1xMnyAl1yO3 sys-
tem also demonstrated that the binding energy of metal–oxygen in
perovskite materials can be optimized through compositional tun-
ing (Deml et al., 2014). Dey et al. (2015) studied the Ln0.5A0.5MnO3
(Ln = lanthanide, A = Sr, Ca) systems for the thermochemical split-
ting of H2O and CO2 respectively and suggest that the amounts of
O2 and CO produced strongly depend on the size of the rare earth
and alkaline earth cations in A-site. Bork et al. (2015) then tuned
the B-site of La0.6Sr0.4Cr1xCoxO3d system and found that La0.6-
Sr0.4Cr0.8Co0.2O3d splits 25 times more CO2 compared to ceria for
two-step cycling between 1200 C in Ar and 800 C in 50% CO2.
Gálvez et al. (2015) studied the effect of A-site and B-site substitu-
tion (Ca, Sr and Al) on the LaMnO3 perovskites, and found that the
stability of LaMnO3 is detrimentally affected by sintering and by
the formation and eventual segregation of a carbonate phase dur-
ing oxidation by CO2. Carbonation of the Ca and Sr phase was
diminished by Al-substitution in the B-site.
Design principles of perovskites were then proposed by Ezbiri
and Michalsky et al., using electronic structure calculations and
thermogravimetric analysis (Ezbiri et al., 2015; Michalsky et al.,
2015). Furthermore, potential perovskites systems without lan-
thanum in A-site such as SrCoO3d, BaCoO3d and BaMnO3d were
introduced and SrCoO3d reached an oxygen exchange capacity of
44 mmolO2/mol SrCoO3d with speciﬁed pO2 (reduction at 900 K
and 20 vol% O2 and oxidation at 600 K and 3.5 vol% O2). The results
should be beneﬁcial for the application of perovskites in solar ther-
mochemical ﬁelds, as previous studies showed that the presence of
lanthanum in perovskites seems to be not favorable for its oxygen
exchange capacity (Babiniec et al., 2015).
The objective of this paper is to study the potential of per-
ovskites with Ba and/or Sr without La in A-site in solar thermo-
chemical
processes
for
energy
storage
application.
The
composition in materials oxygen content (non-stoichiometry) is
modiﬁed as a function of the temperature because of the ability
of the crystal structure to sustain large topotactic oxygen disincor-
poration. The study aimed to identify the materials that exhibit the
largest amounts of oxygen that can be produced over the course of
such topotactic reductions, and that can at the same time reincor-
porate the same amount of oxygen in a complete reversible way.
The reversibility of reduction and oxidation reactions is investi-
gated to screen the most attractive materials.

placed in an Al2O3 crucible. In general, the thermal reduction and

0 C, 1 atm). The detailed temperature and gas composition pro-

3. Results and discussion
The XRD patterns of perovskites with different compositions are
shown in Fig. 2. The main peaks observed in all diffractograms can
be attributed to the characteristic perovskite structure. The main
reﬂection difference can also be observed, which may be originated
from the distortions of the ideal cubic perovskites. SrFeO3d shows
the strongest intensity, while the BaFeO3d is the weakest among
all the samples. The weak peak at about 2h = 18.3 is reﬂection of
(100) surface, and the peaks at 2h = 32.8, 40.5, 47.1, 53.1,
58.6, 68.8, 78.3, 83.7, and 96.8 reﬂect (110), (111), (200),
(210), (211), (220), (310), (311) and (321) crystal surface of
SrFeO2.97 with cubic structure respectively. On the pattern of
SrCoO3d, the peak appearing at 2h = 28.5 is reﬂection of (101)
surface; 2h = 32.8 is reﬂection of (110) surface; 2h = 43.8 is
reﬂection of (201) surface; and the peak at 55.6 is from (112)
of SrCoO2.52 with hexagonal crystal structure. The peaks of SrMnO3
and BaMnO3 are somewhat complicated, but it still can be identi-
ﬁed as SrMnO3 and BaMnO3 with hexagonal structure. The other
samples are analyzed with the same procedure. Main crystal sys-
tem and crystal phase of these materials have been determined
from the PXRD diagram databases of ICDD, as listed in Table 1.
Thermochemical redox behavior of all the perovskites samples
was measured using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) after data
correction using a blank run. The whole experimental process con-
sisted of two redox cycles. In the ﬁrst redox cycle, the sample was
ﬁrst heated to 950 C, and then kept at 950 C for 10 min in the Ar
ﬂow (reduction step). In the following oxidation step, the sample
was ﬁrst cooled down to 600 C in Ar ﬂow (pO2 = 106 atm), and
then kept at 600 C in 20 vol% O2/80 vol% Ar ﬂow for 30 min, at last,
it was further cooled down to 300 C in the same O2/Ar ﬂow. In the
second cycle, the sample was heated from 300 C to 1050 C and
kept at 1050 C for 10 min (reduction step), then it was directly
cooled down to 300 C. The whole second cycle was carried out
in the 20 vol% O2/80 vol% Ar ﬂow, and all the cycles were carried
out at atmospheric pressure. The different gas atmospheres were
chosen to determine the effect of the oxygen partial pressure
(pO2) on the materials redox capacity. Reducing in Ar allows quan-
tifying the maximum amount of oxygen able to be exchanged at a
given temperature and further allows performing oxidation in
isothermal conditions (here 600 C) by simply switching the gas
atmosphere. The second cycle is performed to quantify the contin-
uous non-stoichiometry changes of the materials and to check if
the process is totally reversible or not. In this study, the maximum
temperature was limited to 1050 C to avoid materials sintering
and coarsening. Given the relatively low temperature of the pro-
cess, we assume that materials that exhibit completely reversible
reactions (i.e. the amount of oxygen released can be replenished
completely) could be cycled because the stability is usually lost
in the ﬁrst cycle. So we did not focus the study on the redox stabil-
ity during extensive cycling.

Fig. 3 shows the redox behavior of SrBO3d (B = Fe, Co, and Mn)
perovskites in the two cycles. It can be clearly seen that both the
redox pattern and oxygen exchange capacity of the three samples
are different from each other. SrCoO3d shows the most complex
redox pattern and the largest O2 exchange capacity. During the
two reduction steps, SrCoO3d releases about 2.79 and 1.82 mass
% of O2, respectively. The reduction pattern of SrCoO3d contains
three mass losses (attributed to the O2 release) stages in the ﬁrst
redox cycle. In the ﬁrst reduction stage (100 C to about 800 C),
the mass of SrCoO3d gradually decreases with temperature
increase; then it decreases sharply and quickly at about 800 C
and 900 C in sequence. If the differential method is used to obtain
the mass variation rate shown in Fig. 4, the reduction rate of the
three stages can be obtained as 0.13%/min, 0.21%/min and 0.19%/
min, respectively. In the reduction step of the second redox cycle,
the sharp mass loss at 800 C disappears; while the sharp mass loss
happens at 900 C again. The reduction rate reaches the maximum
of 0.34%/min. The disappearance of sharp mass loss at 800 C may
indicate the inﬂuence of the reduction atmosphere. The presence of
O2 in the ﬂow may suppress the O2 release of SrCoO3d in the sec-
ond redox cycle. At the same time, the sharp mass loss at 900 C in
the reduction step suggests that SrCoO3d shows at least two dif-
ferent O2 release mechanisms below and above 900 C. Below
900 C, it releases O2 at a steady rate, while after the temperature
reaches 900 C, it releases all the remaining exchangeable O2, and
does not release more O2 even if the temperature continually
increases.
In Fig. 3, the oxidation pattern of SrCoO3d is also distinct. In the
ﬁrst redox cycle, the reduced SrCoO3d captures a large amount of
O2 (1.72 mass%) as soon as the atmosphere is switched from pure
Ar to 20% O2 at 600 C. The oxidation rate in Fig. 4 reaches 1.30%/
min. It only further captures very little amount of O2 in the left
time. When the temperature further decreases from 600 C to
300 C, it captures 0.31 mass% of O2, but the oxidation process is
ﬁnished at about 400 C.
Next, SrFeO3d shows simple redox pattern but high O2
exchange capacity. The reduction step only contains one stage. It
releases 2.04 mass% O2 with a slow but steady rate of 0.07%/min
in the ﬁrst redox cycle. It captures about 1.33 mass% O2 as soon
as the atmosphere is switched from Ar to 20% O2 at 600 C. The oxi-
dation rate reaches 1.89%/min, which is higher than for SrCoO3d.
When the temperature decreases from 600 C to 300 C, it captures
more O2 (0.69 mass%), and the mass variation is corresponding to
the temperature decrease with a rate of about 0.046%/min. In the
second redox cycle, SrFeO3d keeps a relative stable O2 exchange
rate with the temperature increase/decrease. More importantly,
the O2 exchange capacity of SrFeO3d is perfectly reversible. It

equal) is one of the key characteristics that materials must exhibit
for energy storage application.
As for SrMnO3d, in the ﬁrst redox cycle, it keeps stable till
about 900 C and then only releases about 0.38 mass% O2. Though
about 0.11 mass% O2 is captured at 600 C, it cannot release or cap-
ture O2 again even when the temperature decreases to 300 C. The
poor redox behavior is probably caused by the well crystallized
structure, as shown in Fig. 2, which requires even higher tempera-
ture to reduce. The limited reduction inﬂuences its subsequent oxi-
dation. To conﬁrm this hypothesis, the reduction temperature of
SrMnO3d was heightened to 1150 C (Fig. S6). The material
released up to 1.88 mass% O2 in Ar (pO2 = 106 atm) at 1150 C
and it seems that the reduction process was not ﬁnished yet. In
contrast, reduction in 20% O2 at 1150 C remained weak because
the temperature was still not high enough for a favored reduction
at high pO2 (0.2 atm). The oxidation in 20% O2 was complete at
600 C, which means the process is reversible.
In general, SrCoO3d shows the largest O2 exchange capacity
and SrFeO3d shows the best reversible performance among all
the three samples. The results show that SrFeO3d and SrCoO3d
can be good candidates for oxygen exchange during solar thermo-
chemical conversion while SrMnO3d is not suitable for tempera-
tures below 1050 C.
Thermochemical redox behaviors of the BaBO3 series per-
ovskites are then discussed. Fig. 5 shows the O2 exchange pattern
and capacity of BaBO3d perovskites that were measured by TGA.
The BaCoO3d shows similar redox pattern but even better O2
exchange capacity than SrCoO3d. In the ﬁrst redox cycle, the
reduction step contains two stages. It does not release O2 until
the temperature increases to 400 C. In the range of 400–900 C,
it begins to release O2 with a rate of 0.15%/min, as shown in
Fig. 6. At 900 C, similar to SrCoO3d, a sharp mass loss suddenly
appears. The reduction rate yields to 0.50%/min. The total mass loss
reaches 3.13% and it can be almost fully oxidized as soon as the
atmosphere is switched from Ar to 20% O2 at 600 C. The oxidation
rate reaches 3.19%/min. Reasonably, little part (0.13 mass%) is fur-
ther oxidized when the temperature decreases to 300 C. In the
second redox cycle, the two stages of reduction step and one stage
of oxidation step appear again; while the oxidation degree and rate
are both decreased.
Next, interestingly, BaFeO3d shows more complex redox pat-
tern and larger O2 release ability than SrFeO3d in the ﬁrst redox
cycle; while it shows very poor O2 capture ability in the oxidation
step. In the ﬁrst redox cycle, four stages in the range of 130–300 C,
400–550 C, 550–900 C and at 900 C, appear during the reduction
step. The total O2 release reaches about 3.0 mass%, which makes
BaFeO3d a good candidate for reduction. Unfortunately, it shows
very poor oxidation ability and this restrains its potential. It only
captures 0.11 mass% O2 as soon as the atmosphere is switched
from Ar to 20% O2 at 600 C and keeps stable afterward. Only when
the temperature decreases from 600 C to 300 C, it captures some
more O2 (0.82 mass%). In the second cycle, the previously captured
O2 is lost again to reach the same ﬁnal reduction extent and the
same mass is subsequently recovered during oxidation upon cool-
ing in 20% O2.
Similar to SrMnO3, the BaMnO3d shows poor redox behavior
under the same reaction conditions. It seems that it cannot be
reduced or oxidized during the course of the temperature-
programmed experiment, and higher temperatures than 1050 C
may be required in order to favor reduction, as for the case of
SrMnO3d. The results thus indicate that Co-based perovskites are
the best candidates for oxygen exchange applied to solar thermo-
chemical energy storage, which is consistent with the prediction
of Ezbiri et al. (2015).
Besides, on the basis of BaBO3d and SrBO3d series results, it
can be observed that BaCoO3d and SrFeO3d exhibit good reversi-
ble redox property, while SrCoO3d and BaFeO3d show complex
reduction pattern with several stages. The complex reduction pat-
tern seems to be not favorable for the O2 capture ability during oxi-
dation. The different stages may be originated from the transition
of perovskites phases with different non-stoichiometry and crystal
structures (Fjellvåg et al., 1995). While SrFeO3d and BaCoO3d
might undergo less phase transitions, they are easier to be re-
oxidized.

4. Conclusions
A series of Ba or Sr perovskites with Fe, Co and Mn incorporated
on the B-site have been prepared by modiﬁed Pechini method and
their crystal structures characterized by XRD. The different per-
ovskite compositions have been considered to identify the most
attractive ones regarding oxygen storage capacity and reaction
reversibility. TG analysis reveals that the Ba induced systems show
enhanced O2 exchange capacity compared with Sr induced sys-
tems, and Co-based systems show the highest O2 exchange capac-
ity and reaction enthalpies among all the samples. The redox
patterns of Fe-, Co- and Mn-based systems are clearly different
from each other. The O2 exchange of Co-based perovskites is rapid
and seems like there is a transition in the oxidation state when
reaching a given temperature. In contrast, the O2 exchange rate
of Fe-based perovskites is corresponding to the temperature varia-
tion, which points out the rapid kinetics as the equilibrium state is
reached for each temperature of the non-isothermal proﬁle.
Finally, the Mn-based systems are nearly not changed during the
course of the redox cycles in the considered temperature range,
which denotes their high stability and unsuitability for thermo-
chemical redox cycling under the reaction conditions. Moreover,
according to the O2 exchange capacity, it seems that within the dis-
tortion tolerance range, the phase change of some perovskites is
still inclined to transform from one stable crystal structure to
another, which determines the O2 exchange capacity under certain
conditions.
