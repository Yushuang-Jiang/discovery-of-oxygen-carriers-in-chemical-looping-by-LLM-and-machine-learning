a b s t r a c t
Chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) is a novel combustion technology that utilizes the
decomposition reaction of an oxygen storage material to combust solid (hydro-)carbonaceous fuels.
CuO is an ideal candidate for CLOU owing to its high oxygen uncoupling capacity (10 wt.%) and its abil-
ity to release oxygen at temperatures relevant for chemical looping combustion (CLC), i.e. 850–1000 C.
Here, we investigate the feasibility of Cu-rich, Al2O3 or MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers containing
70–90 wt.% CuO for the CLOU process. Al2O3- and MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers were synthesized
using a co-precipitation technique and calcined either at 800 C or 1000 C. The CLOU performance of
the oxygen carriers was evaluated at 950 C in a ﬂuidized bed reactor using a N2 atmosphere for the
decomposition reaction and a mixture of 10.5 vol.% O2 and 89.5 vol.% N2 for the re-oxidation reaction.
It was found that the calcination temperature and the surface area had no signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the
oxygen carrying capacity of the synthesized oxygen carriers. The oxygen uncoupling capacity of Al2O3-
stabilized oxygen carriers was substantially below the theoretically expected values due to the formation
of the mixed oxides CuAl2O4 and CuAlO2. Based on thermo-gravimetric measurements, a reaction path-
way for the oxygen release and re-oxidation reactions of Al2O3-stabilized CuO was proposed. On the other
hand, no interaction between MgAl2O4 and the active phase, i.e. CuO was observed. As a consequence,
MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers possessed a high oxygen uncoupling capacity very close to the theo-
retical values. Owing to its stable and high oxygen release capacity, MgAl2O4-supported oxygen carriers
containing 80 wt.% CuO were identiﬁed as a promising material for CLOU.

The capture of CO2 using conventional absorption technologies
is very cost intensive [1]. On the other hand, emerging second and
third-generation CCS technologies such as carbonate looping and
chemical looping combustion (CLC) allow the capture of CO2 at
substantially reduced costs [2–6]. CLC, originally proposed by Rich-
ter and Knoche in 1983 [7], is a two stage process comprised of a
reduction and an oxidation step. In the reduction step, lattice
oxygen of a solid oxygen carrier is used to oxidize a (hydro-)carbo-
naceous fuel, viz.:

view, CLOU is performed either in a batch mode using a single ﬂu-

using bituminous coal as a fuel.
From a thermodynamic point of view the metal oxide systems,
CuO–Cu2O, Mn2O3–Mn3O4 and Co3O4–CoO, are very attractive
since they provide a high partial pressure of oxygen in the temper-
ature range of interest i.e. 700–1000 C, as plotted in Fig. 1. Another
important selection criterion for a suitable oxygen carrier material
is its oxygen carrying capacity. For example, the system CuO–Cu2O
has an oxygen carrying capacity of 0.1 g O2/g CuO whereas Co3O4–
CoO and Mn2O3-Mn3O4 have lower oxygen carrying capacities of
0.066 g O2/g Co3O4 and 0.034 g O2/g Mn2O3, respectively. Besides
metal oxides, also perovskite and spinels, e.g. CaMn0.875Ti0.125O3
and Mg2MnO4, have been proposed as promising-materials for
CLOU [12–14]. Overall, copper oxide is a very attractive oxygen
carrier for CLOU owing to its (i) high equilibrium partial pressure

(ii) high oxygen uncoupling capacity and (iii) fast kinetics for the

prone to thermal sintering and agglomeration because of the low
Tammann temperature of CuO, Cu2O and Cu of, respectively,
526 C, 481 C and 405 C [15]. Hence, CuO requires stabilization
by a high melting point support, e.g. ZrO2, Al2O3 or MgAl2O4 [16].
Over the past decade, strategies to synthesize CuO-based
oxygen carriers for CLOU have focused on freeze granulation
[10,17–19], spray drying [11,20–22] and mechanical mixing [23],
as summarized in a recent review by Imtiaz et al. [24]. For exam-
ple, Abad et al. [11] and Adánez-Rubio et al. [20–21] synthesized
MgAl2O4-stabilized,
CuO-based
oxygen
carriers
containing
60 wt.% CuO via a spray drying technique. In the temperature range
890–1000 C, the oxygen carrier showed a high reactivity with dif-
ferent solid fuels such as a bituminous Colombian coal. Oxygen
carriers synthesized using the aforementioned techniques pos-
sessed sufﬁcient mechanical strength for operation in a ﬂuidized
bed and excellent redox stability. However, the amount of CuO in
these materials was limited to 60 wt.%. We believe that the low
Tammann temperatures of CuO, Cu2O and Cu in combination with
the ‘‘macroscopic’’ synthesis techniques applied prohibited the
development of CuO-rich oxygen carriers, i.e. materials containing
P70 wt.% CuO for CLOU. One of the key challenges in the synthesis
of CuO-rich oxygen carriers is the requirement of a high degree of
dispersion of the stabilizing phase. ‘Conventional’ synthesis meth-
ods, such as mechanical mixing fail to provide the high degree of
dispersion required and, thus, produce materials which show poor
resistance to sintering when tested over repeated redox cycles
[15]. A synthesis technique that could potentially achieve homoge-
neous mixing between the active phase, CuO, and the support is
coprecipitation. For example, Chuang et al. [25] compared the re-
dox characteristics of Al2O3-stabilized, CuO-based oxygen carriers
containing 82.5 wt.% CuO synthesized using mechanical mixing,
wet impregnation and coprecipitation techniques. Chuang et al.
[25] found that the oxygen carriers prepared via coprecipitation
showed high redox stability over many cycles. Previously, also Im-
tiaz et al. [26,27] demonstrated that Al2O3-stabilized, CuO-rich
oxygen carriers (87.8 wt.% CuO) with excellent mechanical proper-
ties and stable redox performance could be synthesized by a judi-
cious choice of the pH value at which the coprecipitation was
performed.
Here, we report a systematic study of the inﬂuence of the CuO
content, the support material and the calcination temperature on
the redox stability of oxygen carriers synthesized via a precipita-
tion technique. The synthesized oxygen carriers were character-
ized in detail to elaborate a synthesis–performance relationship.
An important aspect of this work was the determination of the
maximal CuO content in the oxygen carriers that allowed stable
CLOU performance.

2.2. Material characterization
The surface area and pore volume of the unreacted oxygen car-
riers were determined using N2-physisorption (Quantachrome
NOVA 4000e analyzer). The Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) [28]
and Barrett–Joyner–Halenda (BJH) [29] models were used to calcu-
late the surface area and pore volume, respectively. Prior to the
acquisition of the N2 isotherms the oxygen carriers were degassed
at 300 C for at least 2 h.
The mechanical strength of the unreacted oxygen carriers was
determined by measuring the force required to break oxygen car-
rier particles (size range 300–425 lm) using a Shimpo breaking
force gauge. To obtain a representative value for the crushing
strength, 20 measurements on randomly chosen particles were
made.
A Bruker AXS D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer was used to ac-
quire powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the unreacted and
cycled oxygen carriers. The diffractometer was mounted with a
Lynx eye super speed detector and operated at 40 kV and 40 mA
using Cu Ka radiation. Each sample was scanned within the range
of 2h = 10–90 with a step size of 0.0275 per second.
High resolution scanning electron microscopy (HR-SEM) was
used to morphologically characterize the calcined oxygen carriers
(Zeiss Gemini 1530 FEG). The samples were sputter coated with
platinum before imaging.
To determine the content of CuO and the decomposition charac-
teristics of the oxygen carriers synthesized, temperature pro-
grammed decomposition (TPD) experiments were performed
using a thermo-gravimetric analyzer (TGA, Mettler Toledo TGA/
DSC 1). In a typical experiment, a small amount (<30 mg) of oxygen
carrier was heated from room temperature to 1100 C at a rate of
10 C/min under a ﬂow of N2 of 100 mL/min.
2.3. Cyclic CLOU characteristics
The cyclic CLOU performance of the oxygen carriers was studied
in a TGA at 950 C. In a typical experiment, a ﬁxed amount of an
oxygen carrier (30 mg) was loaded on an alumina pan and heated
up to 950 C at a rate of 10 C/min under a ﬂow of N2 (25 mL/min).
Once the reaction temperature was reached, the oxygen carrier
was allowed to decompose for 20 min in a N2 ﬂow of 25 mL/min.
Subsequently, the N2 ﬂow was stopped and the material was
reoxidized for 20 min in air (25 mL/min). A constant N2 ﬂow of
25 mL/min was used as purge ﬂow over the micro-balance. For
each oxygen carrier, ten CLOU cycles were performed. The oxygen
uncoupling capacity (Ro) and the fractional conversion (Xdec) were
calculated from the continuously recorded weight change using,
respectively, Eqs. (6) and (7):
Ro ¼ ðmox  mdecÞ=mox
ð6Þ
Xdec ¼ ðmox  mÞ=ðmox  mdecÞ
ð7Þ
Here mox, mdec and m is the weight of the oxygen carrier at the start
of the decomposition step (i.e. when it is fully oxidized), the weight
of the oxygen carrier at the end of the decomposition step (i.e. when
it is fully decomposed) and the instantaneous sample weight,
respectively.
The cyclic CLOU performance of the oxygen carriers calcined at
1000 C was also evaluated in a ﬂuidized bed operated at 950 C.
Detailed information on the experimental setup can be found in
the supporting information (Text S1 and Fig. S1 of the SI). The
experimental protocol to study the cyclic decomposition and re-
oxidation reactions in a ﬂuidized bed was as follows.
A batch of 20 g of Al2O3 (300–425 lm) comprised the bulk of
the ﬂuidized bed. The bed was ﬂuidized by N2 at a ﬂow rate of
3 L/min (u/Umf = 4.4) and operated at 950 C. Once the operating
temperature was reached, 1 g of an oxygen carrier was added to
the ﬂuidized bed and 25 CLOU cycles were performed. The dura-
tion of the decomposition and re-oxidation reactions were ﬁxed
to 120 s and 180 s, respectively. The ﬂow rate of the inlet gas
was set to 3 L/min, as measured at 25 C and 1 bar, in each segment
of a cycle. At the end of the experiment, the reactor was cooled
down to room temperature and the cycled oxygen carrier was
recovered for XRD analysis. Assuming complete decomposition of
the theoretical content of CuO in the oxygen carriers (i.e. 70, 80

3. Results
3.1. XRD analysis
XRD measurements were performed to determine the inﬂuence
of the calcination temperature on the crystallinity and the chemi-
cal composition of the synthesized materials. The X-ray diffracto-
grams of the Al2O3- and MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers are
given in Fig. 2(a) and (b), respectively. Fig. 2(a) shows only the
presence of CuO and CuAl2O4 in Al2O3-stabilized oxygen carriers,
independent of the calcination temperature. CuAl2O4 is a mixed
oxide of CuO and Al2O3 and has a cubic spinel structure. Al2O3
was not detected in the CuO–Al2O3 oxygen carriers, indicating
that either all the Al2O3 formed CuAl2O4 or that it was present in
an amorphous form. On the other hand, independent of the
calcination temperature, MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers only
contained CuO and MgAl2O4, as shown in Fig. 2(b). Unlike for
Al2O3-stabilized materials, the formation of mixed oxides between
the active compound, i.e. CuO, and the support, i.e. MgAl2O4, Al2O3
or MgO was not observed for MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers.
Using XRD the presence of alumina or magnesia could not be de-
tected indicating that all the Al2O3 and MgO formed MgAl2O4 or
existed in amorphous phases. The increase of the intensity and a
decrease in the full width at half maximum of the diffraction peaks
of the oxygen carriers calcined at 1000 C, when compared to
materials calcined at 800 C, indicates an increase in crystallinity
and/or the growth of the size of the crystallites with increasing
temperature [30]. The average crystallite size of the synthesized

3.2. Temperature programmed decomposition (TPD)
To determine the inﬂuence of the calcination temperature and
the CuO content on the thermal decomposition characteristics of
the oxygen carriers synthesized, temperature programmed decom-
position (TPD) experiments were performed in a TGA. Figs. 3 and 4
plot the normalized mass loss as a function of temperature for
Al2O3- and MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers, respectively. The
data in Fig. 3 show that the calcination temperature and the CuO
content in the oxygen carrier have a strong effect on the decompo-
sition characteristics of Al2O3-stabilized materials owing to the
interaction of CuO with Al2O3. The decomposition of CuO–Al2O3
based oxygen carriers calcined at 800 C occurred in the tempera-
ture range 735–970 C via a single step mechanism, irrespective of
the CuO content. However, it can be seen from Fig. 3(a) that for
temperatures exceeding 930 C the decomposition rates of the oxy-
gen carriers were very slow. This is probably due to slow decompo-
sition rates of CuAl2O4 present in the oxygen carriers. On the other
hand, the decomposition of oxygen carriers calcined at 1000 C oc-
curred in the temperature range 770–1050 C and revealed a clear
two step decomposition mechanism for Cu80Al-1000 and Cu90Al-
1000. In the ﬁrst decomposition step (770–950 C), bulk CuO forms
Cu2O. The comparatively slow decomposition of CuAl2O4 to CuAlO2
occurred in a second step in the temperature range 950–1050 C. A
kink in the normalized mass loss curve of Cu70Al-1000 at 985 C
suggests that also the decomposition of Cu70Al-1000 occurred
in two steps. However, the initial rate of decomposition of
Cu70Al-1000 was slow compared to the other two oxygen carriers
calcined at 1000 C. This behavior could be explained by the re-
duced amount of bulk CuO in Cu70Al-1000. Assuming that all the
Al2O3 in Cu70Al-1000 reacts with CuO, the amount of bulk CuO
would be 46.6 wt.%. Thus the dominant phase in Cu70Al-1000 is
CuAl2O4 and not CuO. The TPD results indicate that not only does
CuAl2O4 possess slow decomposition kinetics, but CuAl2O4 also
inﬂuences the rate of decomposition of bulk CuO, probably due
to the mixing of CuO and CuAl2O4 on a molecular level.

3.3. Structural characterization
Table 3 summarizes the BET surface area, the BJH pore volume
and the hardness of the oxygen carriers synthesized as a function
of (i) the CuO content, (ii) the calcination temperature and (iii)
the support material. The hardness of the oxygen carriers calcined
at 800 C was in the range of 1.4–3.5 N, irrespective of the support.
As expected, increasing the calcination temperature to 1000 C
increased the crushing strength of the materials (2.5–5.7 N). The
BET surface area and the BJH pore volume of the oxygen carriers
calcined at 800 C was in the range of 20–30 m2/g and 0.12–
0.17 cm3/g, respectively. Upon calcination at 1000 C, a drastic
decrease in the surface area and pore volume was observed. With
the exception of Cu70Al-1000, that possessed a surface area of
9 m2/g, the surface area of all oxygen carriers was 62 m2/g. The
measurements summarized in Table 3 indicate that the content
of CuO and the type of support, i.e. Al2O3 or MgAl2O4 had no
signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the surface area, pore volume and hardness
of the oxygen carriers. Indeed, the structural properties of the syn-
thesized oxygen carriers were inﬂuenced only by the calcination
temperature.

3.5. Thermo-gravimetric analysis
To determine the inﬂuence of the calcination temperature, the
support material and the CuO content on the CLOU characteristics
the cyclic oxygen uncoupling capacity, Ro, was determined from
TGA experiments performed at 950 C. The cyclic oxygen uncou-
pling capacity of Al2O3- and MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers
is plotted in Fig. 6(a) and (b), respectively. All Al2O3-stabilized oxy-
gen carriers revealed a substantial decrease in the amount of oxy-
gen released over the ﬁrst 3–4 cycles. The extent of the reduction
of the oxygen uncoupling capacity over the initial cycles was found
to decrease with increasing CuO content. Al2O3-stabilized oxygen
carriers containing 70, 80 and 90 wt.% CuO showed a decrease in
their oxygen carrying capacities by, respectively, 62%, 32% and
12% over 10 CLOU cycles. From Fig. 6(a) it can be seen that the cal-
cination temperature did not affect the CLOU characteristics sub-
stantially. On the other hand, the MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen
carriers possessed a high and stable oxygen uncoupling capacities
over the 10 cycles tested, irrespective of the CuO content and the
calcination temperature (Fig. 6(b)).

CuO was also determined from TGA experiments performed at

would indicate that 10 wt.% MgAl2O4 is not sufﬁcient to mechani-

of the cycled materials conﬁrmed that the composition of the
oxygen carriers was preserved over 25 cycles, i.e. only CuO and
MgAl2O4 were detected.
4. Discussion
4.1. Synthesis–performance relationship
An important facet of this work was to establish a synthesis–
performance relationship for the oxygen carriers synthesized. We
found that the phases present in the oxygen carriers were not af-
fected by the calcination temperatures. For Al2O3-stabilized oxy-
gen carriers, this observation is in agreement with the phase
diagram of the Cu2O–CuO–Al2O3 system which indicates the for-
mation of the CuAl2O4 spinel at both 800 C and 1000 C [33]. How-
ever, the calcination temperature of the oxygen carriers had a
signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the oxygen release characteristics of the
materials. For example, an increase in the calcination temperature
resulted in an increase in the decomposition temperature of CuO.
In addition, the apparent rate of the decomposition reaction de-
creased with increasing calcination temperature. It is conceivable
that a decrease in the surface area and pore volume of the oxygen
carriers in combination with an increase in the crystallite size with
increasing calcination temperature decreased the O2 mobility in
the material. However, more detailed studies are required to fur-
ther investigate this aspect. As expected the mechanical strength
of the oxygen carriers was increased with increasing calcination
temperatures, however, at the cost of a decrease in both surface
area and pore volume. This aspect is particularly important with
regards to a stable operation in ﬂuidized bed reactors. To summa-
rize, the surface area, pore volume, hardness, degree of crystallinity
and extent of interaction between the support material and the ac-
tive phase can be adjusted by a judicious choice of calcination
temperature.

As a consequence, the oxygen carrying capacity decreases with
cycle number, since bulk CuO forms CuAl2O4 which is irreversibly
decomposed to CuAlO2 and Al2O3. Reactions (9)–(13) continue to
occur until Al2O3 has been fully converted to CuAlO2. Based on
the proposed reaction scheme, a decrease of the theoretical oxygen
carrying capacity by 16%, 37% and 65% for oxygen carriers contain-
ing, respectively 10, 20 and 30 wt.% Al2O3, is expected. Indeed a de-
crease in the oxygen carrying capacity by 12%, 32% and 62% was
observed after 10 cycles for oxygen carriers containing, respec-
tively, 90 wt.%, 80 wt.% and 70 wt.% CuO. A possible strategy to cir-
cumvent this loss in oxygen carrying capacity is the complete
reduction of CuO to Cu prior to the reoxidation step. However, this
increases the risk of agglomeration for CuO-rich oxygen carriers. It
is, however, worth mentioning here that CuO-rich oxygen carriers
(87 wt.% CuO) which show a high resistance to agglomeration have
been previously developed by Imtiaz et al. [26,27]. Alternatively,
the formation of the CuAl2O4 spinel can be inhibited by the incor-
poration of Na+ or K+ ions [26].
4.3. Agglomeration and attrition
Agglomeration and attrition of the oxygen carriers are impor-
tant aspects of the CLOU process since operation in ﬂuidized beds
is most likely to be continuous. In the CLOU experiments reported
here agglomeration was not observed regardless of the CuO con-
tent of the material and the support material used. However, size
analysis of Al2O3-stablized oxygen carriers recovered after 25 CLOU
cycles revealed a signiﬁcant fraction of particles smaller than
300 lm. The high rate of attrition of Al2O3-stabilized oxygen
carriers was probably due to changes in their chemical composi-
tion accompanied by high mechanical stresses during cyclic
operation. On the other hand, only a small amount of particles with
dp < 300 lm were recovered when MgAl2O4-stabilzed oxygen
carrier (90 wt.% CuO) were used. These observations indicate that
MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers containing 80 wt.% CuO are
suitable oxygen carrier for the CLOU process.
5. Conclusions
We report the synthesis of Cu-rich, Al2O3- or MgAl2O4 stabilized
oxygen carriers for the CLOU process using a coprecipitation tech-
nique. The oxygen carriers were calcined at two different temper-
atures viz. 800 C and 1000 C. The results reported here showed
that the mechanical and structural properties of the oxygen carri-
ers can be adjusted via the calcination temperature. Furthermore,
we observed that calcination at high temperature shifted the tem-
perature at which CuO decomposition occurred towards higher
temperatures. Scanning electron micrographs of the oxygen carri-
ers calcined at 1000 C revealed a platelet morphology of CuO.
Using MgAl2O4 as a support, the surface of the oxygen carriers
was found to be uniformly covered with nano-sized MgAl2O4
grains. On the other hand, agglomerates of nano-sized Al2O3 grains
could be identiﬁed on the surface of Al2O3-stabilized CuO. For
Al2O3-stabilized oxygen carriers, the decomposition of CuAl2O4
was not fully reversible. As a consequence, the oxygen release
capacity of the Al2O3-stabilized oxygen carriers was signiﬁcantly
below its theoretical value. Based on thermo-gravimetric and
XRD measurements, a reaction mechanism for Al2O3-stabilized
CuO was proposed. On the other hand, the oxygen uncoupling
capacity of MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers was stable and
close to the theoretical values. MgAl2O4-stabilized oxygen carriers
containing 80 wt.% CuO were identiﬁed as a suitable material for
the CLOU process owing to their stability, attrition and agglomer-
ation resistance and high oxygen carrying capacity.
