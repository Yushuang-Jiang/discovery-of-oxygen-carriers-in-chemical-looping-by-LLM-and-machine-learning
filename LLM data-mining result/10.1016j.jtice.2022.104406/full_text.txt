A B S T R A C T   
Background: The current research of doping for metal oxide thermochemical energy storage (TCES) systems 
mainly focuses on the mutual doping among traditional metal oxides. However, it is too one-sided to solve the 
problem. In this work, SrCoO3-δ perovskite material is novelly doped into the cobalt oxide material system to 
solve the dehiscent problem of honeycombs, while compensating the energy storage density of materials system. 
Methods: Simultaneous thermal analysis (STA) is used to assess energy storage density, reaction kinetics, stability 
of materials. Thermo-mechanical stability assessment and thermal expansion tests are conducted to analyze the 
anti-crack performance of honeycombs. XRD, SEM, and XPS methods are used to analyze the characteristics of 
materials. 
Significant findings: The mass ratio of doping of 30% exhibits the best comprehensive performance. Based on this, 
the attenuation of energy storage density of SrCoO3-δ doping is narrowed from 27.1% to 22.9% at least compared 
with Al2O3 doping for anti-crack, and SrCoO3-δ doping has no negative effects on reaction kinetics of original 
cobalt oxide and a better stability. Beyond that, the SrCoO3-δ doping method of this work exhibits a better 
balance among various performance indicators of TCES than traditional doping methods based on improving 
TCES performance.   
1. Introduction 
Compared with traditional power generation technology, concen­
trated solar power (CSP) plants have great advantages in sustainability 
and environmental friendliness. But its power generation is affected by 
climate, seasons, and many other factors, which restrict its development. 
So, the energy storage system is considered as the key technology for 
CSP plants, which can improve the utilized level of solar energy [1]. 
Energy storage systems used for CSP can be divided into latent energy 
storage, sensible energy storage, and thermochemical energy storage 
(TCES). Compared with TCES, the sensible energy storage system ex­
hibits extremely low energy storage density. Although the latent energy 
storage technology has a higher energy storage density than the latent 
energy storage system, it is still lower than that of TCES, and latent 
energy storage system has disadvantages of corrosivity, needing heat 
preservation equipment, difficult heat and mass transfer performance 
[2]. TCES systems can be further divided into metal hydride, carbonate, 
metal oxide, hydroxide, and salt hydrate systems. Metal hydride energy 
storage is difficult to achieve due to the difficulty of hydrogen storage, 
carbonate systems have serious performance degradation problems, the 
temperature ranges of hydroxide systems is too low to completely satisfy 
the requirement of generators, and salt hydrated has flaws of heat and 
mass transfer performance. Compared with other materials systems, 
metal oxide systems have advantages of no need for gas storage, the air 
is used as the heat transfer fluid and the reactant, fast kinetic, and the 
heat can be stored at room temperature [1–3]. In the metal oxide TCES 
system, the heat is stored by the reduction process, and released by the 
oxidation process, the air is the heat transfer fluid and reactant. Among 
metal oxide TCES systems, cobalt oxide has faster reaction kinetics, 
better stability, and higher energy storage density than manganese 
oxide, barium oxide, and copper oxide [4,5]. The reversible reaction 
between Co3O4 and CoO during TCES processes is as follows [6].

materials is research-focused, which can adjust the reaction kinetics, 
energy storage density, and stability. At present, the doping method for

metal oxide TCES materials system mainly focuses on the mutual doping 
among traditional metal oxides, such as Mn2O3/Mn3O4 doped with 
Fe2O3, Mn2O3/Mn3O4 doped with SiO2, CoO/Co3O4 doped with Al2O3, 
CoO/Co3O4 doped with Fe2O3, or else. Among them, cobalt oxide system 
exhibits the best comprehensive performance. However, these tradi­
tional doping methods are too one-sided to solve the problem or has 
serious side effects. Beyond that, the metal oxide powder always is 
pressed into the honeycomb or pellets which can improve the heat and 
mass transfer performance of the materials system, and decrease the 
pressure drop of the fluid channel, to enhance the reaction processes [6, 
8,15–17]. However, this craft may cause the dehiscent problem of 
honeycomb, especially for the cobalt oxide system. Because of the great 
coefficient of thermal expansion of the cobalt oxide under high tem­
perature, using pure cobalt oxide to form the honeycomb or porous 
modules will cause the dehiscent phenomenon under the condition of 
repeated heating and cooling [18]. To overcome this problem, re­
searchers have proposed two solutions. Agrafiotis et al. use metal foam 
as the support to load the cobalt oxide, and they find that it can 
accommodate the thermal expansion of the materials to maintain the 
structural integrity during the TCES processes [19]. However, this 
method will result in the extra cost and the amount of cobalt oxide 
loaded by the carrier is not high. On the other hand, because Al2O3 and 
cobalt oxide can form CoAl2O4 spinel at a high temperature which is an 
inert and stable material [20]. Therefore, Pagkoura et al. add Al2O3 into 
the cobalt oxide TCES system, and the formation of the stable CoAl2O4 
can alleviate the thermal expansion performance under high tempera­
tures. So, the integrity of the honeycombs will be preserved. However, 
the existence of CoAl2O4 in the materials system will influence the mass 
transfer performance which results in a decrease in the reactivity of 
materials [21,22]. Moreover, the addition of Al2O3 into the cobalt oxide 
will decline the energy storage density by the reaction between Al2O3 
and Co3O4 [23]. To sum up, although there has been some methods to 
solve the dehiscent problem of cobalt oxide modulars, they will lead 
performance of energy storage density, reaction kinetics or cost deteri­
orative sharply [7–14]. Therefore, it is necessary to develop a better 
method to resist cracking while avoiding the decrease in reactivity and 
energy storage density. 
Due to the excellent stability and reactivity during the TCES process, 
metal oxide perovskite has been widely considered as a TCES material 
recent years. Nobuyuki et al. and Sean et al. explore the TCES perfor­
mance of the LaxSr1-xCoyM1-yO3-δ and BaxSr1-xCoO3-δ perovskites. They 
find that the Co used in the B-site of the perovskite can make the ma­
terials have a better energy storage density than Fe and Mn elements, 
and the Sr and La elements used in the A-site exhibit a good reactivity 
under some specific proportion. Beyond that, due to the high tempera­
ture of preparation (>1000 ◦C), the perovskite materials used in the 
TCES process have excellent cyclic stability [24,25]. Besides, the TCES 
performance of SrCoO3-δ perovskite also is demonstrated [26]. 
More than that, perovskite materials are widely used as the oxygen 
carrier in the chemical looping technology or the materials for the ox­
ygen permeable membrane, such as SrFeO3 and SrCoO3-δ perovskite [27, 
28]. Therefore, if some certain perovskites which have excellent per­
formance of oxygen transport performance were added into the tradi­
tional metal oxide TCES materials system, it may not decrease the 
reactivity of the TCES material. Beyond that, the change of crystal 
structure for SrCoO3-δ makes it exhibit special thermal expansion char­
acteristics between the room temperature and 1000 ◦C. Calle et al. 
investigate the crystal structures transition of SrCoO2.5 from the room 
temperature to 1000 ◦C, it is expounded that it will exhibit three 
different crystal structures and the thermal expansion performance be 
changed with crystal structure transition, the coefficient of thermal 
expansion of SrCoO2.5 is increased firstly and then decreased dramati­
cally when the temperature exceeds about 700 ◦C [29]. Therefore, it is 
possible to alleviate the thermal expansion performance of the cobalt 
oxide honeycomb at high temperatures by doping SrCoO3-δ perovskite. 
Moreover, because SrCoO3-δ perovskite has some oxygen transport 
performance and energy storage density, the disadvantages of 
decreasing reactivity and energy storage density greatly may be avoided 
simultaneously compared with the addition of Al2O3 into the cobalt 
oxide. Furthermore, the cobalt oxide TCES system always has a degra­
dation problem after long-term service [30]. Because the SrCoO3-δ has 
excellent stability during TCES processes, the addition of it into the 
cobalt oxide TCES system may have a positive influence on the sintering 
of the materials. For example, Bagherisereshki et al. introduce the per­
formance of CaSO4 and Sr3(PO4)2 as the physical barrier in the 
SrO/SrCO3 TCES system to improve the sintering of the materials [31]. 
Overall, the addition of SrCoO3-δ may adjust the thermal expansion 
characteristic of the cobalt oxide TCES system, while maintaining the 
energy storage density and fast reaction kinetics of the materials to some 
extent. So, in this work, the structural stability of the honeycomb for the 
cobalt oxide/ SrCoO3-δmaterials system is tested firstly, then further 
investigating the energy storage density, reaction kinetics, and sintering 
after 30 cycles of the composite system. We hope this work can open up a 
new collocation route of the materials system in the field of metal oxide 
thermochemical energy storage systems, and promoting the develop­
ment of this technology. 
2. Experiment 
2.1. Materials preparation 
SrCoO3-δ and cobalt oxide are used as the raw materials in this work. 
The SrCoO3-δ 
powder is synthesized by a combined ethyl­
enediaminetetraacetic (EDTA) acid and citric acid method [28]. Stoi­
chiometric amounts of the EDTA and citric acid are dissolved in the 
deionized water, then Sr(NO3)2 and Co(NO3)2⋅6H2O are dissolved in the 
solution. The molar ratio of the metal ions: EDTA: critic acid is 1: 1: 1.5. 
The pH value of the solution is adjusted to 9 by NH3⋅H2O. Then the 
solution is evaporated at 100 ◦C on a magnetic heating agitator to form a 
dark purple gel. Then the gel is transferred into a drying oven and heated 
to self-combustion under 250 ◦C to get the precursor. Finally, the pre­
cursor is calcined at 600 ◦C for 1 h and 1000 ◦C for 5 h, respectively to 
obtain the SrCoO3-δ powder. The cobalt oxide is bought from the com­
mercial company (>99.9%, Co3O4, Shanghai st-nano science, and 
technology Co., Ltd). The honeycomb is prepared by pressing the 
mixture of materials in a die directly as Fig. 2 shows. Firstly, the cobalt 
oxide and SrCoO3-δ powder are mixed in a ball mill for 30 min to obtain 
the dual materials system. 2% PVA solution is used as the binder. The 
material is mixed with the binder to obtain the mixture. Then, the 
mixture is transferred into the die and pressed in a hydraulic machine 
under 20 MPa to obtain the honeycomb. Finally, the honeycomb is 
calcined at 700 ◦C for 2 h to remove the organic matter. The flow chart of 
preparation for honeycombs is shown in Fig. S1. Each resulting honey­
comb weighed about 10 g, the diameter is 3 cm, the height is around 1 
cm. Each honeycomb will be evenly arranged with 5 holes with a 
diameter of 5 mm. 
2.2. Performance test 
The crack test of the honeycombs is conducted in a tubular furnace at 
a airflow rate of 1 L/min. The temperature range is 700–1000 ◦C , the 
heating and cooling rate is 15 ◦C/min. The sketch map of the equipment 
is shown in Fig. S2. 
Experimental data of thermochemical energy storage performance 
concerning transition temperatures during the redox process, reaction 
enthalpies, and oxygen storage capacity are obtained by thermogravi­
metric (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis. The 
relevant experiments are conducted in a Netzsch STA 449 C System.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Thermo-mechanical stability assessment 
Because our main target is improving the dehiscent phenomenon of 
the honeycombs for cobalt oxide TCES materials system by doping 
SrCoO3-δ perovskite in this work. Therefore, the thermo-mechanical 
stability assessment is conducted firstly in this section. The mass ratio 
of SrCoO3-δ doping is 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5, respectively, and the 
mass ratio of materials for each sample is shown in Table S1. Fig. 1 in­
dicates the XRD patterns for each fresh sample, the obvious diffraction 
peaks for cobalt oxide and Sr6Co5O15 oxide are identified. From the 
previous research, the SrCoO3-δ oxide existed as Sr6Co5O15 phase at 
room temperature, and the perovskite structure is formed when the 
temperature exceeds about 920 ◦C [26,29]. Beyond that, the thermal 
expansion test and TGA-DSC test also can verify the formation of 
SrCoO3-δ in this section and Section 3.2. 
During the crack test, the temperature range is 700–1000 ◦C, a total 
of 10 cycles is conducted, and the heating and cooling rate is 15 ◦C/min. 
22 Fig. 2 indicates the structural integrity of honeycombs before and 
after being tested. After the test, honeycombs of Co and SC0.1 still 
exhibit obvious cracking, but the dehiscent phenomenon of the honey­
comb of SC0.2 is significantly alleviated after the test. Moreover, the 
cracking of honeycombs is well inhibited when the mass ratio of doping 
reaches and exceeds 30%. 
Previous studies suggest that the cracking of honeycombs for cobalt 
oxide TCES materials system is mainly caused by the “chemically”- 
induced stresses due to the redox reaction of cobalt oxide [19,22]. To 
further explore the reason of SrCoO3-δ doping on improving the cracking 
of honeycombs, the thermal expansion test is conducted. Fig. 3 indicates 
the elongation of workpieces of SC0.3, Co, and SC (SrCoO3-δ) from room 
temperature to 1050 ◦C. As Fig. 3 shows, the degree of thermal expan­
sion of cobalt oxide is lower than that of SC below 750 ◦C, but it will 
increase dramatically when the temperature exceeds about 816.9 ◦C and 
reaches the maximum at about 900 ◦C, and the cracking of honeycombs 
mainly appeared in this stage. Moreover, because the onset temperature 
of the reduction reaction of cobalt oxide is about 920 ◦C, so, it can be 
seen that the violent thermal expansion phenomenon of cobalt oxide is 
not led by the reduction reaction itself but the lattice expansion before 
reduction begins. Sequentially, as the reduction reaction continues, 
oxygen species are removed, and the degree of thermal expansion de­
creases sharply. As for SC, the thermal expansion of SrCoO3-δ exhibits 
three stages and the trend is similar to the curve in previous studies, and 
the crystal structure of SrCoO3-δ is marked from the room temperature to 
1050 ◦C in Fig. 3 [29,32,33]. This also can verify the formation of 
SrCoO3-δ. The thermal expansion property of SrCoO3-δ is decreased in 
the hexagonal phase which is coincided with the temperature range at 
which cobalt oxide expands violently, and when the temperature ex­
ceeds 896 ◦C, the thermal expansion of the SrCoO3-δ also will increase 
until the temperature reaches about 922 ◦C. 
Synthesizing the thermal expansion performance of SC and Co, it is 
demonstrated that the thermal expansion property of Co at high tem­
peratures is much larger than that of SC. Beyond that, there is a severe 
thermal expansion phenomenon of Co from 816.9 to 900 ◦C. However, 
because of the crystal transition, the thermal expansion property of SC is 
declined between 700 and 900 ◦C. Moreover, although the thermal 
expansion of SC will increase between 900 and 920 ◦C, it still stays at a 
lower level compared with Co, and the thermal expansion property of Co 
also decreases in this stage. Therefore, the thermal expansion of SC0.3 is 
improved significantly between 700 and 1000 ◦C, and this is the reason 
why the cracking of the SC0.3 honeycomb is inhibited after the test. 
Fig. 4 displays the mechanism of adding SrCoO3-δ into cobalt oxide 
TCES materials to maintain the integrity of honeycombs. As Fig. 4 
shows, SrCoO3-δ particles has lower thermal expansion property at high 
temperature. Therefore, adding SrCoO3-δ into cobalt oxide can offer 
more space to alleviate the internal stress caused by thermal expansion 
among cobalt oxide particles in honeycombs at high temperatures, so as

3.2. Redox performance 
The effects of SrCoO3-δ doping on compensating the energy storage 
density and reactivity for the original cobalt oxide are focused on in this 
section. TGA-DSC test is conducted for each sample between 100 and 
1000 ◦C to analyze their TCES properties with a heating or cooling rate 
of 15 ◦C/min. Further, the onset temperatures and duration of redox 
processes are identified to explore the effects of doping SrCoO3-δ on the 
reactivity of cobalt oxide. 
Fig. 5 exhibits the TGA-DSC curves of all samples. Simultaneous 
thermal analysis (STA) is mainly used to analyze the conversion of 
materials and the corresponding heat absorption/heat release during the 
TCES processes. However, because the DSC data of simultaneous ther­
mal analysis is not accurate at high temperatures, the DSC data 
measured can only be used for comparison among experimental groups. 
The actual enthalpies of cobalt oxide are calculated from the conversion 
rate exhibited by the TGA data. As Fig. 5(a) shows, the TGA data 
indicates an almost complete conversion between Co3O4 and CoO (the 
conversion of 91.78%), which corresponds to the theoretical enthalpy of 
734.2 J/g. But the enthalpies measured of cobalt oxide during redox 
processes are 126 J/g and 160 J/g, respectively. These errors are caused 
by the measurement and calculation errors of the DSC method in heating 
and cooling processes, respectively, especially for the conditions of high 
temperatures. However, our objectives are verifying effects of perovskite 
doping on performance TCES rather than standardization, so it will not 
affect the analysis and experiments, and this is a common problem in 
this industry. 
For SrCoO3-δ, due to the change of crystal structure, the endothermic 
and exothermic processes of it proceed unsteadily as Fig. 5(b) shows. In 
the previous research, the enthalpies of SrCoO3-δ have been reported 
based on an isothermal process test which is different from the actual 
condition [26]. However, by synthesizing the thermal expansion anal­
ysis and crystal structure of SrCoO3-δ, the enthalpies measured by the 
DSC method under the condition of variable temperature are not accu­
rate, and the DSC peaks only appear when the crystal structure is 
changed [34]. Therefore, the actual enthalpies of SrCoO3-δ are still 
needed to be further explored. In Fig. 5(b), there are two obvious DSC 
peaks in the oxidation process which corresponded to two transitions of 
crystal structure, but there is only one peak in the reduction process 
which indicates that the crystal structure transition from brownmillerite 
to the hexagonal structure will not cause an obvious endothermic peak. 
Therefore, the obvious DSC peaks captured in heating and cooling 
processes only reflect the enthalpies of crystal structure transformation. 
However, according to the TG curve of Fig. 5(b), in addition to crystal 
structure transformation, another process also has continuous unstable 
weight changes which is corresponding to the heat absorb/release to 
some extent. Therefore, the enthalpies statistical will be lower than the 
actual values and they only can be used to assess whether the SrCoO3-δ 
has TCES performance in the cobalt oxide system in this work. 
Fig. 6(a–e) indicate the TGA-DSC curves of doped samples. Among 
them, except for the SC0.1, an obvious DSC peak is recognized at about 
440 ◦C during the cooling process. As Figs. 5 and 6 show, the temper­
ature ranges of DSC peaks appearing for Co and SC are coincident during 
the heating process, but there are two distinguishable DSC peaks of Co 
and SC in the oxidation process at about 800 ◦C. On this basis, the 
change of energy storage density of single cobalt oxide can be discerned 
from doped materials systems. Therefore, the enthalpy calculated from 
the DSC data during the heating process is recognized as the total energy 
storage density which is used to assess the change of energy storage 
density of materials system after adding SrCoO3-δ. It should be noted

In the previous research, doping Al2O3 into cobalt oxide will decrease 
the energy storage density and reaction kinetics, and the doping ratio of 
10% exhibited an acceptable performance of anti-crack [21,22]. On this 
basis, the energy storage density, and relevant parameters of the reac­

It can be seen from Table 1, the enthalpies measured of SrCoO3-δ are 
lower than that of cobalt oxide, so the energy storage density is still 
decreased with the increase of doping ratio of SrCoO3-δ. However, 
SrCoO3-δ has an obvious compensation effect on energy storage density

density, reaction kinetic, and anti-crack performance. Hence, the 
attenuation of energy storage density of SrCoO3-δ doping is lower than 
that of the Al2O3 doping under the same anti-crack performance, the 
attenuation of energy storage density is narrowed from 27.1 to 22.9% at 
least. Moreover, because of the limitation of the DSC method, the actual 
attenuation of energy storage density of materials system doped with the 
SrCoO3-δ may be further narrowed. 
In conclusion, compared with Al2O3 doping, SrCoO3-δ doping can 
maintain the material system with a higher energy storage density based 
on the same anti-crack performance and has no obvious side-effects.

3.3. Cyclic performance 
To comprehensively analyze the performance of cobalt oxide/ 
SrCoO3-δ TCES materials system, the cyclic test is necessary. A total of 30 
cycles is conducted in this work, and the scanning electron microscope, 
XRD analysis, and XPS analysis are conducted for the fresh and used 
samples. SEM images at different magnifications are taken for the 
samples after 10 and 30 cycles, respectively. By synthesizing the results 
in Sections 3.1 and 3.2, the SC0.3 is chosen to take the cyclic test due to 
the best comprehensive TCES performance from the viewpoints of anti- 
crack property and energy storage density. Beyond that, the SC0.5 is 
tested to further discuss the effects of extra SrCoO3-δ doping on the 
stability, and pure cobalt oxide is chosen as the control group in this 
section. The attenuation of TCES performance for the cobalt oxide sys­
tem is mainly caused by the sintering of the particles under high 
temperatures. The sintering can be divided into liquid-phase and solid- 
phase sintering. Liquid-phase sintering is mainly caused by the uneven 
temperature field and the low melting point of materials under TCES 
processes, while solid-phase sintering is mainly caused by the mass 
transport on the boundary of grains which is influenced by the elements, 
size, and structure of grains [35]. Because of the high melting points of 
cobalt oxide and SrCoO3-δ, the sintering of materials is mainly attributed 
to solid-phase sintering in this work. Solid-phase sintering of materials 
can be represented as coarsening and agglomeration. Coarsening refers 
to the phagocytosis of small particles by large particles, agglomeration 
mainly occurs in systems with small particle sizes.

phase in SC0.3 and SC0.5. However, the single SrCoO3-δ is stable during 
the TCES processes, so coarsening of particles is caused by the coexis­
tence of SrCoO3-δ and cobalt oxide [26]. By synthesizing the previous 
research about the analysis of the crystal structure of SrCoO3-δ, the 
coarsening can be attributed to the specific crystal transition of SrCoO3-δ 
from room temperature to 1000 ◦C. When SrCoO3-δ exhibits hexagonal 
structure, the stoichiometric ratio of Sr: Co: O is 2:3:3, and some cobalt 
oxide will be merged into the Sr-Co-O phase [27]. Then some cobalt 
oxide will be isolated under other crystal structures again. Because of the 
existence of this process in the materials system, the bonding of cobalt 
oxide and SrCoO3-δ is enhanced, and this is the reason of SC0.3 and 
SC0.5 sinter more severely. However, the coarsening of SC0.3 and SC0.5 
is more serious, but the attenuation of energy storage density is lower. 
This can be attributed to the oxygen transport performance of SrCoO3-δ 
and the better stability of SrCoO3-δ itself. Because the oxygen transport 
performance of SrCoO3-δ will be enhanced under higher temperature

surface of Sr6Co5O15(Srsurface). This also is caused by the crystal transi­

SrCoO3-δ in room temperatures. Moreover, the area ratio of Srsurface: 
Srlattice can represent the relative amounts of Srsurface and Srlattice, the 
proportion of Srsurface: Srlattice is decreased from 0.626 to 0.31 after the 
cyclic test [40,41]. This means some Sr elements move inside the 
materials.

4. Conclusion 
In the high-temperature thermochemical energy storage applica­
tions, the metal oxide doping in the energy storage materials is research 
focus. However, current researches are still limited to the doping of 
traditional metal oxides. In this work, SrCoO3-δ perovskite is doped into 
the cobalt oxide TCES system to solve the dehiscent problem of 
honeycombs, while maintaining the energy storage density and reaction 
kinetics of the material system as much as possible. 
According to the thermo-mechanical assessment, doping SrCoO3-δ 
into Co3O4/CoO TCES materials system can significantly mitigate the 
cracking of honeycombs when the doping mass ratio reaches and ex­
ceeds 30%. Through simultaneous thermal analysis of the doped system, 
SrCoO3-δ doping has obvious compensating effect on the energy storage 
density of the doped system and no negative effects on reaction kinetics, 
which shows great advantages compared with Al2O3 doping and using 
metal foam as the support. Especially, due to the good oxygen transport 
performance of SrCoO3-δ, the SC0.3 shows lower attenuation than Co 
after 30 cyclic tests. Overall, the SC0.3 exhibits the optimal compre­
hensive performance from viewpoints of TCES and anti-crack perfor­
mance. Moreover, SrCoO3-δ 
doping method balances various 
performance indicators of TCES by a comprehensive assessment of other 
doping methods. 
As the properties of perovskite materials can be flexibly adjusted by 
selecting and collocating A-site and B-site elements, perovskite doping 
method for metal oxide TCES systems deserves further investigation.
