A B S T R A C T
Chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) is promising for carbon capture in fossil fuel combustion.
Oxygen carriers (OC) are the key aspect for the development of CLOU technology. Four Cu-based bimetallic OC
(80Cu20Mn, 40Cu60Fe, 60Cu40Co and 60Cu40Ni) were prepared by sol-gel method in this work. The oxygen
release and adsorption behavior of the OC were examined in alternating Ar and air. Chemical looping com-
bustion (CLC) with both gaseous and char were conducted to investigate the performance of the OC. The oxygen
transport capacity remained stable in 20 cycles for 80Cu20Mn, 40Cu60Fe and 60Cu40Ni, but a remarkable
decrease was observed for 60Cu40Co. For the CLC tests with gaseous fuel, only 80Cu20Mn exhibited stable CO
conversion (XCO), while the other three OC showed reducing XCO in the redox cycles. Both 60Cu40Co and
60Cu40Ni were sintered, and insufficient oxidation was confirmed after CLC with 11.3%CO/N2. In CLC with coal
char, all the OC significantly enhanced carbon conversion (XCO), reaction rate(γC) as well as CO2 selectivity
(YCO2). However, γC decreased prominently in cycling tests indicating reduced reactivity. Agglomeration, sin-
tering and transformation of active components into composite salts such as CuFe2O4 and CuMnO2 were re-
sponsible for the deteriorated performance of the Cu-based bimetallic OC.
1. Introduction
With the increasing concern of global warming and rising atmo-
spheric CO2 concentration caused by the fossil fuel (especially coal)
combustion, the demand of effective and efficient carbon capture and
storage (CCS) technologies is becoming more stringent than ever before
[1]. Chemical looping combustion (CLC) has arisen as a promising and
innovative combustion technology recently due to its inherent CO2
separation without any energy penalty [2,3]. The working principle of
CLC is illustrated in Fig. 1, where fuel is fully oxidized in a fuel reactor
(FR) with oxygen provided by the solid oxygen carriers (OC, MexOy),
and the reduced OC (MexOy-1) are then oxidized and recovered with air
in an air reactor (AR). Following this scheme, the combustion of fuel is
divided into one reduction and one oxidation reaction that occur in
different reactors, thus direct contact of fuel and air is avoided. After
the reaction only CO2 and H2O (steam) are produced, and pure CO2 is
obtained after condensation of steam [4]. The OC used in CLC are
mostly metal oxides, and it can be envisioned that the contact between
OC and fuel, especially solid fuel such as coal, is significant for efficient
conversion. The concept of CLC was firstly proposed with gaseous fuels
(natural gas, H2, etc.), and then extended to solid fuels such as coal,
biomass, solid wastes, etc., while CLC using coal as fuel is undoubtedly
the most noticeable [5–8].
For the CLC with coal, which is also known as in-situ gasification
chemical looping combustion (iG-CLC), several types of reactions are
involved as described in Fig. 2 by Adanez et al. [6]. When coal is fed
into the FR, pyrolysis reactions first take place and produce steam,
volatiles and char. Volatiles are directly oxidized by OC into CO2 and
H2O, while char is first gasified with the gasifying agent (steam or CO2),
and the gasification intermediates (CO and H2) are then oxidized into
CO2 and H2O by OC. The direct reactions between OC and char are
negligible. The used OC restore their oxygen transport capacity by air in
AR and reused in the next cycle. An important technical challenge for
iG-CLC is the rate enhancement of coal gasification, as it is the rate-
controlling step and much slower than the OC oxidation [9,10]. This
slow coal gasification significantly lowers the efficiency and limits the
application of the technology. Common practices for addressing the
issue are to use reactive or catalytic OC that can accelerate coal gasi-
fication. For instance, Gu et al. used K2CO3-decorated iron ores in iG-
CLC with anthracite, and they found that coal gasification and water-

It is well recognized that OC are the crucial factor for the devel-
opment of iG-CLC as they are the carriers for both oxygen and heat to
fuel (coal) by circulating between FR and AR. Because of the different
temperatures and atmospheres in FR and AR, several important prop-
erties are required for OC including sufficient oxygen transport capa-
city, favorable thermodynamic property for oxidation fuel into CO2 and
H2O, high reactivity in both oxidation and reduction, resistant to at-
trition, agglomeration, carbon deposition, limited cost and environ-
mental friendly [12]. In the past decades, numerous studies have been
performed to explore efficient and cost-effective OC. It is reported that
more than 700 materials based on Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Cu, and low cost
materials (natural ores, red mud, perovskites etc.) have been tested to
verify their feasibility [6]. Among the tested OC, the Cu-based OC are
particularly remarkable as they could release gaseous oxygen in FR,
thus saving the rate-controlling coal gasification step and effectively
enhancing reaction rate [13]. This process is also known as chemical
looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU), and the reaction paths of
CLOU are also illustrated in Fig. 2. In the CLOU process, several reac-

Despite of the aforementioned merits, CLOU process is less mature
than CLC due to its higher requirements of OC. Up to now only several
metal oxides (CuO/Cu2O, Mn2O3/Mn3O4, Co3O4/CoO) were proved to
be potential for the CLOU process at 800-1200°C. Among these candi-
dates, the oxygen transport capacity of Mn2O3/Mn3O4 is too small (only
0.033), while Co3O4/CoO is too expensive for practical use. CuO/Cu2O
has higher oxygen transport capacity than Mn2O3/Mn3O4 and Co3O4/
CoO and it is relative cheaper, but it tend to be deactivated due to
sintering, agglomeration or ash deposition when using coal as fuel. It
was reported that CuO was highly reactive towards reduction but
substantial deactivation was observed after redox cycles [14,15]. Be-
cause of the low melting point of Cu (1089°C), CuO tend to be sintered
or agglomerated, thus modification of CuO are necessary to improve its
performance. Introducing inert materials such as SiO2, Al2O3, ZrO2,
TiO2 could effectively improve sintering resistance of CuO [16], but the
oxygen transport capacity of the modified CuO was also reduced. This
can be overcome by combining two CLOU metal oxides such as CuO
with Mn2O3 or Co3O4. Alternatively, One can combine one CLOU metal

Based on the above considerations, this work aims to verify the
feasibility of combining different metal oxides that could provide gas-
eous or lattice oxygen to prepare potential OC with high performance in
CLC tests. Four Cu-based bimetallic OC were prepared by combining
CuO with other metal oxides (Mn2O3, Co3O4, Fe2O3 and NiO) using the
sol-gel method. The CLC performance of the Cu-based bimetallic OC
with both gaseous and solid fuels was investigated. Cycling CLC tests
were conducted with different fuels to examine the reactivity, stability
and recyclability of the OC with indexes including carbon conversion,
reaction rate and CO2 selectivity. The fresh and used OC were char-
acterized by SEM-EDS and XRD to reveal the mineralogical and mor-
phological characteristics and interpret the deactivation of the OC. The
relevant results and information are expected to provide useful re-
ference for the preparation of efficient Cu-based OC that are potential
for practical CLC processes with both gaseous and solid fuels.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials
In this work, Cu-based OC were prepared with the sol-gel method by
using different nitrates precursors including Cu(NO3)2•3H2O, Mn
(NO3)2•4H2O, Fe(NO3)3•9H2O, Co(NO3)2•6H2O and Ni(NO3)2•6H2O.
The procedures for preparation of 30g of targeted OC were briefly in-
troduced here. Cu(NO3)2•3H2O and another nitrate with the pre-cal-
culated mass (shown in Table 1) were mixed and dissolved in de-io-
nized water, and then added with equimolar amount of critic acid. The
mixture was kept standing in a water bath of 80°C while stirring to
evaporate water, and the dehydrated wet gel was then dried at 120°C in
an oven overnight to obtain a completely dehydrated xerogel. The
xerogel was calcinated in air at 500°C for 5h and then 800°C for 1h. The
solid products were used as OC after crushing and grounding to a
particle size 450–1000μm. Images of the OC were shown in Fig. 3. The
resultant Cu-based OC were denoted as xCuyM, where x and y indicate
the weight percentage of CuO and another metal oxide (Mn2O3, Fe2O3,
Co3O4 or NiO). The four Cu-based OC thus prepared were denoted as
80Cu20Mn, 40Cu60Fe, 60Cu40Co and 60Cu40Ni, respectively. As can
be seen from Fig. 3 that there were no obvious agglomerations for the
OC after calcination.
A gaseous fuel (11.3%CO/N2) and a Shenhua (SH) bituminous coal
with a particle size 2–3mm were used to evaluate the performance of
the Cu-based OC. To eliminate the interference of volatile matter on
coal gasification and iG-CLC reaction, pyrolysis was conducted at 950°C
for 2h beforehand to remove the volatile matter and the resultant char
was used as the feeding material for the iG-CLC tests. The proximate
and ultimate analyses of SH coal and its char were listed in Table 2, and
the ash compositions of SH coal were shown in Table 3.

transition of crystal phases. Whatever factors, the reducing Ro is adverse
to their usage in long-term CLC operations. In the following section, the
performances of the OC in CLC with both gaseous and solid fuels are
discussed to examine their feasibility to be used as OC in smooth and
stable CLC operations.

of the other OC reduces obviously, especially in the first two or three

Fig. 11 shows the carbon conversion (XC), reaction rate (γc) and CO2
selectivity (YCO2) during iG-CLC with SH char in the first cycle. Clearly,
all the Cu-based OC enhance both XC and γc of SH char in iG-CLC
compared with SiO2. XC is only about 0.8 in iG-CLC with SiO2 at 950°C
for 30min, while it reaches almost 1.0 at 13min when 60Cu40Co is
used. The γc with 60Cu40Co is about five times greater than that with
SiO2. The values of γc with other OC are also much larger than that with
SiO2. This is expected as coal gasification is the rate limiting step for iG-
CLC. When Cu-based OC are presented, they release gaseous oxygen
and convert the C-H2O into CeO2 reaction therefore significantly en-
hance γc. High YCO2 is desired for iG-CLC as concentrated CO2 can be
readily captured. It is shown in Fig. 11 that YCO2 rapidly rises to 0.98
within 1min, indicating complete oxidation at the initial stage. This is
due to the relatively higher concentration of oxygen released at this
stage. As the iG-CLC reactions progress, because of the less gaseous
oxygen available and the reduced OC reactivity caused by the sintering
and transitions of crystalline phase (as discussed later in this section),
both γc and YCO2 gradually deminish after the peak values. As expected,
the YCO2 of SiO2 is very small (about 0.3) and changes little with time
extending, as the dominant gaseous products are CO and H2 rather than

3.4. Characterization of the Cu-based OC before and after CLC cycling tests
Fig. 14 shows the XRD patterns of the used OC after 10 cycles of iG-
CLC with SH char. Compared with the XRD patterns of the fresh OC,
more peaks are observed for the used OC. For instance, CuMnO2 and
CuFe2O4 are observed for the used 80Cu20Mn and 40Cu60Fe, respec-
tively. These composites are not presented in the fresh OC or in the used
OC derived from CLC cycles with 11.3%CO/N2. In addition, TiO2 is
observed in the used 60Cu40Ni due to the ash deposition, which is
consistent with the ash composition of SH coal (shown in Table 3). The
deposition of ash and agglomeration/sintering of the OC are visible in
the SEM-EDS images shown in Figs. 15–18. Apparently, the particle
sizes of all the OC grow with varying degrees, confirming the occur-
rence of agglomeration during iG-CLC with char. Moreover, the particle
surfaces of 80Cu20Mn and 60Cu40Ni become more smooth after cy-
cling tests compared with the other two OC. The sintering and ag-
glomeration inhibit the contact between OC particles and the gases,
that is, gasification intermediates in OC reduction or air in OC oxida-
tion, thus the reactivity of the OC are reduced. This is the reason why γc
and YCO2 are reduced in the iG-CLC cycles.
Furthermore, the fresh OC show only the active components, while
the used OC present noticeable elements such as Si, Al and Ca, which is
consistent with the ash composition of SH coal in Table 3. Combining
the EDS and XRD analyses, it is reasonable to speculate that ash de-
posited on the OC during iG-CLC would react with the active compo-
sitions (such as CuO) to produce composite salts with reduced oxygen
uncoupling capacity.
These results could interpret the decreasing γc in the repeated cycles
of iG-CLC. On the one hand, the transformations of active components
into composite salts (CuO + Fe2O3 →CuFe2O4, CuO + Mn2O3 →
CuMnO2) potentially reduced the reactivity of the OC [23]. On the
other hand, the ash deposited on the OC surfaces further accelerates
sintering, thus deteriorating the oxygen supply to the char and reducing
conversion rate [24]. This implies the necessity for introduction of
supporting components that could resist sintering and ash deposition in
the design and preparation of Cu-based OC, so as to achieve high per-
formance in iG-CLC process.
Therefore, it can be summarized that the OC used in this work are
still less satisfying in resisting sintering and maintaining high reactivity
and stability in CLC cycling tests, whatever type of fuels (gaseous or
solid) are used. Modifications of the Cu-based OC are required to im-
prove their performance before they can be potential OC candidates for
practical chemical-looping processes.
4. Conclusions
In this work, four Cu-based OC including 80Cu20Mn, 40Cu60Fe,
60Cu40Co and 60Cu40Ni were prepared by sol-gel method, and their
performance in oxygen release and adsorption, CLC with gaseous fuel
(11.3%CO/N2) and iG-CLC with SH char were evaluated. The main
conclusions are summarized as follows.
(1) The oxygen transport capacity of 80Cu20Mn, 40Cu60Fe and
60Cu40Ni remained stable in 20 cycles of oxygen release and ad-
sorption, while that of 60Cu40Co deceased rapidly. This result in-
dicates the higher stability of the former three OC in transporting
oxygen from air to fuels.
(2) In the CLC with gaseous fuel (11.3%CO/N2), the CO conversion
(XCO) of 80Cu20Mn remained relatively stable while that of
40Cu60Fe, 60Cu40Co and 60Cu40Ni decreased remarkably in the
cycling tests. Insufficient oxidation and sintering of 60Cu40Co and
60Cu40Ni surfaces were confirmed from the XRD and SEM ana-
lyses, and the sintering was responsible for the reducing XCO in CLC
with gaseous fuel.
(3) In the iG-CLC with SH char, all the Cu-based OC accelerated the
reaction rate (γc) and improved carbon conversion (XC) of char,
especially 60Cu40Co. The CO2 selectivity (YCO2) was very high at
the initial stage but gradually reduced with the reduction duration.
All the OC exhibited decreasing γc in the cycling tests. The ash
deposition, agglomeration and sintering, as well as the transfor-
mation of active components into composite salts were confirmed
to be responsible for the deterioration of reactivity.
All the above results indicate that the Cu-based OC are potential
oxygen carriers for CLC reactions, but further modifications such as
introducing support or adding other elements are required to improve
their resistance of ash fouling, and to maintain high and stable re-
activity in practical chemical-looping processes.
