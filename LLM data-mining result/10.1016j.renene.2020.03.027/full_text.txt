This effort is to improve CuO’s chemical-looping characteristics by introducing iron ore, chrysolite and
limestone to obtain high power efﬁciency in chemical looping combustion (CLC) power plants. The
reactivity and oxygen uncoupling behavior were evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) in N2
and CO2 atmosphere under different heating rate. CuO modiﬁed by chrysolite and limestone showed
better performance than iron ore-added CuO oxygen carrier (OC) in a CLC system. CO2 could play an
important role in the gasiﬁcation of char only when the temperature is above 800 C. Two peaks were
found in the Differential thermal gravity (DTG) curve when modiﬁed CuO reacted with char. That could
be the solid-solid reaction turned into gas-solid reaction with temperature increasing. Scanning electron
microscopy (SEM)/energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) technique analysis indicated an enhancement of high
temperature tolerance of Cu-based OCs, while CuO occurred sever sinter and agglomeration. Higher
temperature tolerance means higher temperature off-gas from the fuel reactor entering the heat utili-
zation power generation system, which could obtain higher efﬁciency in CLC power plants.

1. Introduction
Climate change and global warming is caused by an increasing
concentration of CO2 in the atmosphere drawing people’s attention
widely. Researchers from world wild are endeavoring on the solu-
tions to reduce the CO2 emission. The utilization of biomass is one
of the methods to reduce CO2 emissions because the carbon dioxide
released during biomass combustion has previously been removed
from the atmosphere during biomass growth [1]. The global
warming is getting deteriorated by the higher CO2 emission, CO2
capture and storage (CCS) technologies become a popular topic for
many researchers. Several different technologies, pre-combustion,
oxy-fuel combustion, and post-combustion, have been used to
capture CO2. These techniques involving the separation and storage
of CO2 are energy-intensive, resulting in a signiﬁcant decrease in
net thermal efﬁciency of the conventional coal-ﬁred power plant
(CFPP) by 7e13% point [2]. However, chemical looping combustion
(CLC) combining fuel combustion and pure CO2 production without
extra energy supplemented has been deemed as one of the lowest
energy consumption potential technology to capture CO2.
The technology discussed in this paper uses oxygen carrier (OC)
particles to transfer oxygen from the air to the fuel. The OC particles
are circulated between the fuel reactor and the interconnected air
reactor. In the fuel reactor, fuel is oxidized by the OC particles to
produce CO2 and H2O, allowing high-purity CO2 to be produced for
storage or utilization after steam condensation. In the air reactor,
the OC particles are re-oxidized by the air to gain poor oxygen and
rich nitrogen stream. This process is ﬂameless combustion and has
no direct contact between fuel and air, producing no nitrogen
oxide.
The use of solid fuel in the CLC has been reported by many re-
searchers. However, solid-solid reaction between OC and char
could be the limiting step of the whole process. The ﬁrst option to
use solid fuel to obtain high carbon conversion and CO2 capture
efﬁciency in a CLC system is to use synthetic gas generated from a
previous gasifying step. A CO2 capture ratio of 90.1% was obtained
in Wang’s study [3] which built a system of coal gasiﬁcation CLC
combined cycle. Zhu et al. [4] evaluated the performance of inte-
grated gasiﬁcation combined cycle (IGCC) system with CO2 capture
using CLC and calcium looping process (CLP) for power generation.
A net power efﬁciency of 39.78% with the overall CO2 capture ef-
ﬁciencies of 99.9% was obtained. However, the gasiﬁcation process
has an important energy penalty to provide the oxygen needed for

the gasiﬁcation process as well as extra investment in gasiﬁcation
equipment [5]. Spallina et al. and Mukherjee et al. presented ana-
lyses of coal-fed, integrated gasiﬁcation CLC plants with energy
penalty as high as 4.5% for gasiﬁcation step [6]. The second option is
the direct coal chemical looping combustion where the solid fuel is
directly brought into to the fuel reactor. As the fuel and oxygen
carriers are physically mixed, the reaction rate and combustion
efﬁciency are restricted by solid-solid reaction. In-situ gasiﬁcation
chemical looping combustion (iG-CLC) and chemical looping with
oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) are two proposed solid fuel CLC pro-
cesses that transform the solid -solid reaction into gas-solid reac-
tion to increase the reaction rate and CO2 capture efﬁciency. Design
and operation of 10-100KWth unit with coal have been demon-
strated in some researches [7,8]. However, The limiting step in iG-
CLC is the reaction rate of syngas with OC which is faster than
the gasiﬁcation reaction rate [9]. While for CLOU process, the idea
was proposed by Mattisson et al. [10] making use of the idea ﬁrst
proposed by Lewis and Gilliand to produce CO2 from solid carbo-
naceous fuels by utilizing the gaseous oxygen released from some
oxygen carriers [11]. This process is based on the utilization of
oxygen carrier materials which release gaseous oxygen at high
temperature that could burn solid fuel. During this process, solid
fuels are burned with gas-phase oxygen released from OC at high
temperature, avoiding the slow gasiﬁcation step, giving a much
faster solid conversion [10e13].
CuO/Cu2O, Mn2O3/Mn3O4 and Co3O4/CoO have been identiﬁed
as redox pairs with the capacity to release oxygen and could be
regenerated at high temperature [14]. The investigation of using
Mn-based oxygen carriers have been studied by many researchers
[15e17]. Thermogravimetric experiments and bench-scale ﬁxed-
bed reactor tests were conducted to investigate the oxygen transfer
capacity, reaction kinetics, and stability during cyclic reduction/
oxidation reaction based on CuO and Fe2O3 [18]. Besides, Cao et al.
[19] established a theoretical frame of oxygen carriers among Cu,
Mn, Co, Fe and Ni and demonstrated copper to be a better choice as
an oxygen carrier based on the oxygen transfer capability, energy-
balance analysis, and thermodynamic analysis. Cu-based oxygen
carriers have also been tested with solid fuel in many studies
[14,19e21]. The primary advantages of Cu-based OCs are the fast
reaction rate, high oxygen transport capacity and favorable com-
bustion thermodynamics that convert fuel to CO2 and H2O [14].
However, agglomeration is a potential issue due to the low melting
of Cu (1085 C). Slightly melting phenomenon of reduced CuO by
coal and wood in the SEM images at 800 C was found in the
research [22]. It is essential for the OCs to meet the characteristics
of chemical and mechanical stability during successive redox cycles
and high resistance to attrition in a ﬂuidized bed.
In order to improve the mechanical stability of CuO at high
temperature, supports of SiO2, TiO2 or ZrO2 on CuO have been
studied by many researchers [14,20,23]. Synthetic OCs allow for CLC
operation at high temperatures and pressures, which in turn allows
for higher efﬁciencies and smaller carbon footprint per energy unit
produced, compared to their inexpensive equivalents [6]. ICB-CSIC
research group performed a screening study of 25 different Cu-
based oxygen carriers to select appropriate materials of 60 wt%
CuO supported on MgAl2O4 and 40 wt% CuO supported on ZrO2
[11,12]. Other than the OC’s reactivity, its high ash-resistance was
also studied [24]. In an actual CLC system, separating system of ash
from the OCs is often installed to avoid the accumulation of ash
[19]. This would lead to appreciable loss of OCs with ash stream.
Thus, low cost OC materials, especially natural materials are more
favorable for the combustion of solid fuel [24]. Iron is often used in
CLC due to its high temperature stability, low cost, expensive
sources and environmentally friendly but exhibits low reactivity
when used with solid fuels [25]. The agglomeration resistance of
CuO species and the lower reactivity of the Fe2O3 species were both
improved by the synergetic effect of the bimetallic CuOeFe2O3 [26].
Mitsuoka et al. [27,28] found that the alkali and alkaline earth metal
compounds showed attractive catalytic effect on the gasiﬁcation
process of solid fuels. Zhifeng Zhang [28] concluded that Ca-based
catalysts had more application prospects than other alkali-metal-
based additives because they were cheaper and more widely
available. Besides, Erik Jerndal et al. also [29] found that the addi-
tion of Mg could help get better fuel conversion. However, the
literature contains relatively few studies devoted to the use of Mg-
based ore as a bed material in chemical looping processes.
Furthermore, a direct comparison of performance among the CuO
modiﬁed by chrysolite, iron ore and limestone has also not been
reported.
Additionally, the reaction temperature has great inﬂuence on
the reaction taking place in iG-CLC and CLOU. Some researcher [30]
obtained a lower carbon conversion in the CLC process using Cu-
based oxygen carrier reacted with solid fuel with a result of a
high carbon capture efﬁciency (99.29%) in a CLOU test at 950 C.
Ping et al. [21] concluded that the reaction rate in the CLOU process
were three times faster than with iG-CLC for CuO and found a CLOU
process started at 600
C. Thereby, the reaction should be
controlled in CLOU to obtain higher carbon conversion and reaction
rate. However, the support has great inﬂuence on the reaction. In a
iG-CLC process, the operating temperature was 850 C using CuO
supported on g-Al2O3 and a CLC test was also operated at 900 C of
Cu-based oxygen carriers [30]. Hence, it is important for CuO-ore to
deﬁne the reaction temperature taking place in CLOU.
In addition to the inﬂuence of oxygen carriers, the reaction
condition of heating rate and ﬂuidized gas in chemical looping
combustion also shows noticeable effects on the combustion of
char with OCs [19]. Hence, the objective of the study is to improve
the performance of CuO by introducing of iron ore, chrysolite and
limestone. The reactivity and oxygen uncoupling behavior of the
CuO-based OC were investigated by thermogravimetric analysis
(TGA) to investigate the reaction temperature in CLOU. The CO2
gasiﬁcation of char on the OC’s performance in biomass char-fueled
CLC was further investigated to improve the carbon conversion.
Finally, physical properties of OCs were characterized using various
techniques. The utilization of Cu-based oxygen carriers for CLC
power generation was also discussed.
2. Experimental and data processing methods
2.1. Oxygen-carrier material
The CuO-based oxygen carriers were prepared by mechanical
mixing method. CuO, natural iron ore produced from Mongolia,
chrysolite and limestone were used as raw materials and the par-
ticle size of all the materials were all smaller than 50 mm. The
composition analysis of these ores is listed in Table 1.
A certain amount of active metal oxide and the inert support
were mixed in a beaker to obtain the power mixture, the deionized
water was then added to the beaker to obtain a paste. After stirred,
the paste was extruded in a tablet machine (FW-4A, produced by
Tianjin TP instrument company) to obtain cylindrical extrudates.

2.2. Char preparation
The biomass char used in the study was prepared by rapid py-
rolysis of straw (from Shanxi province) in a ﬁxed-bed reactor which
is shown in Fig. 1. Biomass after crushing and screening with a size
range of 150e200 mm was loaded into a crucible and then placed on
the cold side of the quartz tube. After the reactor reached the
speciﬁed temperature 950 C, the gas switched to N2 to purge to
exhaust air in the reactor and then the crucible was quickly pushed
into the heated zone of the reactor. Particles were pyrolyzed rapidly
for about 10 min. The ultimate analysis of biomass char is listed in
Table 2.
2.3. Experimental setup and procedure
A thermo-gravimetrical analysis (TGA) experiments were con-
ducted in a TGA system (WCT-2C) produced by Beijing Optical In-
strument Factory. TGA experiments were used to analyze the
reactivity of the sample under the determined conditions in the
absence of the complex ﬂuidizing factors, such as those derived
from particle attrition and interphase mass-transfer processes. The
reactor consisted of a quartz tube (22 mm inner diameter) placed in
an oven, allowing to heat up till 1400 C. The samples were placed
in a 5 mm deep and 10 mm diameter crucible. The schematic dia-
gram of the TGA is shown in Fig. 2.
Before the experiment began, air was ﬁrstly removed by purging
nitrogen for about 10 min and the sample was heated at different
heating rate of 10, 15, 20 and 40 C/min from ambient temperature
to 950 C. In order to get fully conversion of samples, the reaction
was kept at 950 C for about 30 min. A computer was used to
continuously record and monitor the weight change of the sample.
The inlet gas, controlled by a ﬂow meter, was injected and
discharged from the upper and middle of the tube, respectively. The
samples were heated in N2 and CO2 environments to investigate
CuO-based oxygen carriers uncoupling, CLOU, iG-CLC, char pyrol-
ysis and gasiﬁcation.
Considering the thermodynamic limitations, external mass
transfer and inter-particle diffusion, the gas ﬂow rate was set to
60 mL/min [20] and the weight of the sample varied in 20e60 mg,
except for the study on the char pyrolysis and gasiﬁcation.
2.3.1. Characterization of the oxygen carriers
The surface morphology and composition of both fresh and used
oxygen carrier particles were analyzed by a (SEM) scanning elec-
tron microscopy (FESEM, JSM7800F, JEOL, Japan) and energy-
dispersive X-ray (EDX) technique (Oxford 50 mm2). The X-ray
diffraction (XRD) patterns for crystal structure of fresh and used
particles were conducted on a diffractometer (PANalytical, the
Netherlands).

3. Results and discussion
3.1. TGA test for the oxygen releasing process of oxygen carriers at
950 C in N2 and CO2 atmosphere under different heating rate
When temperature rose, the oxygen carrier conversion in the
reduction process was characterized as a function of time, shown in
Fig. 3. The effect of heating rate on the oxygen release process is
also displayed in this ﬁgure. As oxygen carrier heated from room
temperature to 800 C in N2 or CO2 atmosphere, there was almost
no weight loss from 50 to 800 C which implied that no reaction
happened in this period. After the temperatures went beyond
800 C, apparent weight loss was observed in the TG curve by CuO
reduction to Cu2O (Reaction (R1)). Detailed analysis is illustrated in
the following paragraphs.
3.1.1. Effect of heating rate
Due to the similar oxygen releasing process of OCs in CO2 at-
mosphere, only the conversion verse time in N2 is shown in Fig. 3.
The speciﬁc parameters, such as, initial-end temperature, peak
temperature, reaction time are summarized in Table 3 (the data of
60Cu-L is not included in Table 3 because it differs from the other
two OCs and the effect of heating rate on the oxygen release process
will be discussed later). For 60Cu-Ch and 60CueFe, it was apparent
in Fig. 3 that the TG curves moved toward higher temperature re-
gion as the heating rate increased. Experiments results also
demonstrated that the reaction time and the peak value were
improved with increased heating rate. The reaction time at 10 C/
min was almost two times longer than the time at 40 C/min. This
indicated that the heating rate had a conspicuous effect on the
oxygen release process. The reason for the phenomenon was that
oxygen release process was a chemical reaction process accompa-
nied by heat and mass transfer. With the increase of the heating
rate, the temperature difference between the ambience and parti-
cle became larger, which led to a thermal hysteresis phenomenon.
That explained why the initial temperature of the reaction moved
to the high temperature region. In addition, it can be concluded
from Table 3 that maximum oxygen release rate was accelerated
with the increasing of heating rate. That could be attributed to the
increased activity at high temperature. Different from CuO modi-
ﬁed by chrysolite and iron ore, the oxygen uncoupling process of
60Cu-L had two oxygen release process in N2 atmosphere, shown in
Fig. 3. The effect of heating rate on the initial temperature was the
same as the 60Cu-Ch and 60CueFe due to thermal hysteresis,
especially at 40 C/min. In order to ﬁgure out what happened
during the ﬁrst stage, an experiment with terminal temperature of
720 C was conducted in TGA with a heating rate of 15 C/min under
N2 atmosphere. The presence of Cu2O, shown by XRD analysis
(Supplementary Material Fig. S7), conﬁrmed that both stages may

3.1.2. Effect of atmosphere
From the experimental results showed in Table 3, it was obvious
that TG and Differential thermal gravity (DTG) curves of oxygen
release process in CO2 atmosphere shifted to higher temperature
zone. At the same heating rate, the initial temperature and reaction
time increased signiﬁcantly in CO2 atmosphere. Experimental re-
sults also indicated that the peak reaction rate under CO2 atmo-
sphere was lower than this in N2 atmosphere. Oxygen release
process in CO2 atmosphere with lower initial temperature and peak
temperature was possibly resulted from the higher speciﬁc heat of
CO2 [32]. In addition, it was worth noting that the amount of oxy-
gen released was the same in both atmosphere, which showed that
CO2 only affected the reaction process and had no effect on the
reactant itself.
3.1.3. Effect of support
The main component in the modiﬁed oxygen carrier was CuO
which was conﬁrmed in XRD analysis, shown in Fig. 7. For oxygen
carrier modiﬁed by chrysolite and iron ore, only one stage was
observed in Fig. 3. The oxygen release process showed a similar
trend with the increasing heating rate. The difference in reaction
time and the maximum oxygen release rate was not signiﬁcant
between 60Cu-Ch and 60CueFe under the same reaction condition.
However, notice that the initial temperature and peak temperature
of 60Cu-Ch was lower than that of 60CueFe. The higher peak value
and shorter reaction time demonstrated that the reactivity of 60Cu-
Ch was stronger than 60CueFe. This was mainly due to the new
phase of CuFe2O4 in 60CueFe which was stable enough even under
high temperature. Some studies [33] concluded that in the process
of oxygen release from CuO, the formation of OeO bond and the
desorption of O2 were controlled by surface chemical reaction. The
process of oxygen release was not affected by oxygen atom
migration in crystals. While for spinel structure, the internal oxy-
gen atom migration process was the speed control step of oxygen
release process. The stable structure of crystal and densely arranged
oxygen atoms led to the difﬁculty of oxygen migration and the
formation of OeO structure which resulted in the difference of
oxygen release rate.
For 60Cu-L, the oxygen release process in CO2 was different from
that in N2 atmospheres. As shown in Fig. 4 (only the TG and DTG
curves of the heating rate at 40 C/min are shown here), it can be
found that oxygen carriers showed a weight gain process in the
atmosphere of CO2 between 600 C and 868 C. This might be the
presence of CaO in 60Cu-L which could react with CO2 to produce
CaCO3. As the temperature increased further, the oxygen release
rate curve had two peaks, which may be the decomposition of
CaCO3 (R2) and CuO (R1).

could ﬁnish the oxygen release process independently. The
maximum oxygen release rate was observed at approximately

where gas-solid (R1, R3, R4, R5) did not occur due to the low
reactivity of oxygen release and gasiﬁcation of char in this period.

into gas-solid reaction could accelerate the oxidation of biomass
char. Ping Wang [22] deduced similar conclusions using CuO as
oxygen carriers and found that the combustion rate of char was
increased with the oxygen releasing rate of CuO. There were dif-

carrier reported by investigators. This could be contributed to three
aspects. First, the oxygen carriers amongst the literature studies
varied obviously, especially in the compositions of the inert sup-

impacts on the reaction of char with OC. Second, experimental
parameters, such as stoichiometric ratio, heating rates etc., may
also partially inﬂuence the observed experimental phenomenon.
Third, differences in fuel reactivity in different studies should also
be considered.
The reactivity of oxygen carrier can be evaluated by the maximum
weight loss rate [36]. In other words, if the weight loss rate was low
and its corresponding temperature was high, the reactivity of oxygen
carrier would be poor. As shown in the DTG curves in Fig. 6, the

limestone during char gasiﬁcation improved the combustion of char.
The higher reactivity of 60Cu-Ch reacted with char also showed that
chrysolite is a better support material for CuO. Additionally, notice
that the conversion of char-OCs reached over 90% and 95% under N2
and CO2 atmosphere, respectively.

In order to illustrate the effect of the support of three different

3.5. Power generation
The main purpose of solid fuel in CLC is to generate power and
achieve high CO2 capture efﬁciency. Design and operating param-
eters of the CLC system, such as the pressure drops, temperature,
and combustion efﬁciency etc., will affect the overall performance
of the plant. The heat inside the CLC reactor is removed for further
utilization in downstream turbines. Thus, reactor temperature
plays a signiﬁcant role in improving the overall plant efﬁciency
[37]. In the case that a combined cycle was used i.e. air based, CO2
and steam turbines, the overall efﬁciency enhanced as the fuel-
reactor temperature increases [38]. Shiyi Chen [39] performed
joint simulation of CLC reactors at 850 Ce910 C using Fe2O3 as
oxygen carrier and a Supercritical CO2 (sCO2) Brayton cycle for
power generation. The results showed that the net power efﬁciency
increased from 41.3% to 43.3% as the turbine inlet temperature
increased about 50 C. Chen et al. [6] also concluded that the main
factor that limited CLC combined cycle for power plant efﬁciency
was the reactor temperature which was constrained by the oxygen
carrier material. They estimated that a 100 C increase in turbine
inlet temperature could translate into a net electrical efﬁciency gain
of ~2%.
In our study, the results indicated that the optimum reaction
temperature of the CuO-based oxygen carriers was 950 C which
was higher than the temperature in the literature [40,41] due to the
support of chrysolite, iron ore and limestone. Moreover, higher
temperature determined a better char conversion [42] and a larger
fraction of low heat value (LHV) energy of exploited fuel, leading to
an increment of the electric power. Thus, higher temperature off-
gas from the fuel reactor sucked in the heat utilization power
generation system could obtain higher efﬁciency in CLC power
plants.
4. Conclusion
The CuO supported on iron ore, chrysolite and limestone were
investigated as an OC using TGA in CLC process at 950 C in N2 and
CO2 atmosphere at different heating rate. The oxygen uncoupling
properties and the reactivity of the Cu-ore were evaluated. The
following conclusions are drawn:
1) The oxygen release process of Cu-based OCs occurred at around
800 C and the reaction was improved with the increasing
temperature. The formation of CuFe2O4 in 60CueFe had a
negative effect on the oxygen release process due to the stable
spinel structure. 60Cu-L had two oxygen release process in N2
atmosphere. The XRD analysis showed the weight change be-
tween 500 and 720 C resulted from CuO uncoupling.

2) Ch-modiﬁed CuO and limestone-modiﬁed CuO presented better

char gasiﬁcation. The higher reactivity of CuO modiﬁed by

reached over 90% under N2 atmosphere and 95% under CO2

3) For the OC-char reaction, the process during 750e850 C should
be controlled by the solid-solid reaction due to the low reac-
tivity of oxygen release. With temperature increasing to 950 C,
the second period should be caused by the combustion of char
with the oxygen released from the OCs. Gasiﬁcation of char in
CO2 atmosphere has positive effect on the chemical looping
combustion of solid fuel when the temperature is above 800 C.
4) SEM analysis proved that the no sintering and agglomeration
phenomenon occurred in Cu-ore. No carbon deposition was
found on the surface of the OCs. These results indicated that the
addition of ores inhibits the agglomeration of CuO at 950 C.
