Experimental and theoretical insights into the reaction mechanism of spinel 
CuMn2O4 with CO in chemical-looping combustion 
Ruixue Fang , Feng Liu , Jing Liu *, Yu Li 
State Key Laboratory of Coal Combustion, School of Energy and Power Engineering, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Wuhan 430074, China

A B S T R A C T   
Chemical-looping combustion has been regarded as a promising technology to realize low-cost CO2 separation. 
CuMn2O4 homogeneously combining Cu and Mn into spinel matrix is a potential oxygen carrier owing to the 
synergistically improved performance. The involved reaction mechanism of spinel CuMn2O4 with CO was sys­
tematically studied by experiments and theoretical calculations. Thermo-gravimetric analysis results suggested 
that CuMn2O4 can be reduced into Cu and MnO. The reactivity of CuMn2O4 is superior than that of Mn2O3 due to 
the existence of Cu. To further understand the improved reactivity of CuMn2O4, the interaction between CO and 
spinel CuMn2O4 was studied by density functional theory calculations. The results indicated that CO prefers to 
chemisorb at Mn site. The oxidation of CO on CuMn2O4 surface is found to be a three-step reaction including CO 
adsorption, CO diffusion and CO2 desorption. CO oxidation on CuMn2O4 surface is an exothermic reaction mainly 
limited by the CO diffusion step. The activation energy for oxidizing CO upon Mn-terminated surface is 64.59 kJ/ 
mol. However, the rate-limiting-step possesses a lower activation energy (58.83 kJ/mol) over Cu-terminated 
surface due to the high reactivity of Cu. The calculated results can well explain the improved reactivity of 
spinel CuMn2O4 observed in the experiments.   
1. Introduction 
Chemical-looping combustion (CLC) has been regarded as a prom­
ising combustion technology that can realize the efficient fuel utilization 
and reduce the CO2 separation cost [1]. In the CLC process, metal oxides 
function as the oxygen carrier to provide lattice oxygen for the fuel 
combustion instead of air [2,3]. Generally, fuel is oxidized to CO2 and 
H2O by oxygen carrier in the fuel reactor, and then the reduced oxygen 
carrier interacts with air in the air reactor to regain its lattice oxygen. 
The oxygen carrier is continuously circulated between fuel reactor and 
air reactor to deliver lattice oxygen for fuel conversion [4]. Compared 
with the conventional combustion, fuel and air in CLC are indirect 
contact, therefore avoiding the dilution of CO2 and lowering the for­
mation of NOx. 
In the CLC system, oxygen carrier not only transfers the lattice ox­
ygen and reaction heat, but also acts as the catalyst for fuel conversion 
[5]. Therefore, oxygen carrier with high performance is the key for the 
successful deployment of CLC [6–9]. In the past few years, many studies 
have focused on the screening and development of oxygen carriers with 
the improved redox and mechanical performance. Many researches have 
been conducted using various materials based on Mn, Ni, Cu and Fe as 
well as other mixed-metal oxides [10–18]. Among these materials, Mn- 
based oxygen carriers exhibit a number of advantages such as low cost, 
good thermodynamic properties and less toxicity [19–22]. Moreover, 
Mn-based oxygen carrier can release oxygen at high temperature, which 
is suitable for the chemical-looping with oxygen uncoupling process 
[23,24]. Nevertheless, Mn-based oxygen carrier shows low fuel con­
version rates and easily react with the support oxides. On the other 
hand, CuO exhibits superior redox reactivity and high oxygen carrying 
capacity, but it tends to agglomeration at high temperature as compared 
to other metal oxides [25]. 
Each Mn- and Cu-based oxygen carrier shows some disadvantages. 
However, bimetallic Mn-Cu oxygen carriers have better performance 
because of its synergistic effect which utilizes the favorable character­
istics of respective metal oxides. Xu et al. found that the reactivity of the 
manganese ore could be greatly improved by copper impregnation [26]. 
Further, Wang et al. reported that CuMn2O4 showed superior reactivity 
and desirable cyclic redox performance with biochar as compared with 
the single metal oxides of Mn2O3 and CuO [27]. Sandra et al. found that 
90% of the oxygen carrying capacity of CuMn2O4 can be used for syngas

had slight change as the redox cycle numbers increased [29]. Hence,

The knowledge of the reaction mechanism of CuMn2O4 with various 
fuels and the activity of sites on spinel CuMn2O4 oxygen carrier surface 
is closely associated with the fuel conversion efficiency. To date, some 
researches have been conducted to identify the reaction characteristics 
of CuMn2O4 under CH4 or syngas (CO + H2) atmosphere in CLC process 
[28,30]. However, no attempts have been made to reveal the synergis­
tically improved reactivity of CuMn2O4 with fuel gas such as CO. 
Furthermore, the specific reaction mechanism of CO oxidation upon the 
CuMn2O4 surface is still unknown. In view of inadequate existing in­

about the reaction mechanism of CuMn2O4 oxygen carrier with CO as 
fuel gas is necessary. 
In this work, the reactivity of CuMn2O4 with CO and the reaction 
mechanism of CO oxidation on CuMn2O4 surface were studied by ex­
periments and theoretical calculations. The reduction behavior of 
CuMn2O4 with CO was determined by thermogravimetric analysis 
(TGA). Furthermore, the possible reaction pathways for the CO oxida­
tion upon CuMn2O4 surface were identified by density functional theory 
(DFT) calculations. The results of this study can be beneficial to 
comprehensively understand the reaction mechanism of CuMn2O4 ox­
ygen carrier with CO, and thus guiding the development of highly effi­
cient Cu-Mn oxygen carrier in CLC process. 
2. Material and methods 
2.1. Sample synthesis and characterization 
Oxygen carriers (CuMn2O4 and Mn2O3) were synthesized using the 
sol–gel combustion method. At first, the Cu(NO3)2⋅3H2O, 50 wt% Mn 
(NO3)2 solution and citric acid (C6H8O7⋅H2O) were dissolved in deion­
ized water, and the molar ratio was set to 1: 4: 1 so as to obtain the aimed 
spinel CuMn2O4. Afterward the mixture solution was stirred at 90 ◦C for 
6 h to obtain sol–gel. Then, the sol–gel was dried at 100 ◦C until the 
remaining water was evaporated. Thereafter the dried gel was calcined 
in a muffle furnace at 450 ◦C for about 1 h, followed by calcination at 
950 ◦C for 5 h. The selection of temperature program is based on the 
knowledge that the precursor starts to convert into spinel-type crystal­
line structure at about 400 ◦C and the crystallization degree of spinel 
structure increases with the increasing calcination temperature. Finally, 
oxygen carriers were ground to smaller than 125 µm. 
X-ray diffraction (XRD, Shimadzu XRD-7000) analysis was per­
formed to measure the crystalline phase composition of the oxygen 
carriers [31–34]. The XRD patterns were recorded in the range of 10◦to 
80◦with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5418 Å) operating at 40 kV and 30 mA. 
The scanning rate was 5 ◦/min. The XRD results can display the crys­
tallographic phases and crystal plane of fresh, oxygen uncoupling and 
reduced oxygen carriers. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, 
Thermo Fisher Escalab 250Xi) was used to characterize the chemical 
state of O, Cu and Mn elements on oxygen carrier surface, which was 
equipped with standard Al Kα excitation source and operated at 15 kV. 
The binding energy (BE) scale was calibrated using C 1 s peak at 284.6 
eV. The surface morphology of CuMn2O4 of fresh and reduced oxygen 
carriers were investigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 
SU8010). 
2.2. Thermogravimetric experiments 
Thermogravimetric experiment can analyze the weight loss rate at a 
certain temperature [31,35,36]. The reaction behavior of CuMn2O4 in 
CO or N2 atmosphere were performed under atmospheric pressure using 
a TGA instrument (TA-SDT-Q600). It mainly consists of several parts 
such as thermal balance, furnace, program temperature control device, 
recording instrument and balance supporter. The sweep gas enters the 
balance room to keep it in an inert environment, and CO is introduced 
into the furnace body from the reactive gas channel of the instrument. At 
first, about 20 mg of the CuMn2O4 material was loaded to an alumina 
pan. Then, the CO-N2 (N2 serves as balance) gas mixture with a total 
flow rate of 100 mL/min was introduced into the reaction chamber. CO 
temperature programmed reduction was carried out with a heating rate 
of 20 ◦C/min and from the room temperature to 900 ◦C to study the 
reduction behavior of CuMn2O4 and Mn2O3. For consideration of the 
oxygen uncoupling property of CuMn2O4, the temperature programmed 
decomposition test was carried out in a pure N2 atmosphere. The tem­
perature was heated from the room temperature to 1000 ◦C with a 
heating rate of 20 ◦C/min. The effects of CO concentration and heating 
rate on the reduction performance of CuMn2O4 were also studied. The 
CO concentrations of 6%, 9%, 12% and the heating rate of 10, 20, 30 ◦C/ 
min were considered in this investigation. 
2.3. Models and calculated details 
All the calculations were performed by using the CASTEP codes [37]. 
The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with Perdew-Burke- 
Ernzerhoff (PBE) functional was selected to describe the exchan­
ge–correlation potential. The plane-wave kinetic cutoff energy was set to 
be 340 eV. A grid of 3 × 3 × 3 for k-point sampling was used for bulk 
optimization and a 3 × 3 × 1 was chosen for the surface as well as for CO 
adsorption [38]. The convergence criteria for the self-consistent field 
(SCF), energy, force, and maximum displacement were 1.0 × 10–6 eV/ 
atom, 1.0 × 10–5 eV/atom, 0.03 eV/Å, and 0.001 Å, respectively. 
CuMn2O4 is known as having a normal spinel structure, where the 
tetrahedral interstices are occupied by the Cu2+ cations and the octa­
hedral interstices are occupied by the Mn3+ cations. Fig. 1a presents the 
unit cell structure of CuMn2O4 which is used in our calculations. The 
optimized lattice constants of CuMn2O4 are a = b = c = 8.317 Å, which is 
well consistence with the experimental values of a = b = c = 8.327 Å 
[39]. The results suggest that the calculations in this study are 
dependable. 
The CuMn2O4 (100) surface is a catalytically active and the most 
thermodynamically stable low-index surface, which easily exposes to 
ambient atmosphere [40]. The representative low-index surface of 
CuMn2O4 (100) was adopted to investigate the reaction mechanism of 
CuMn2O4 oxygen carrier with CO. Two modeled slabs, labeled as Mn- 
terminated surface and Cu-terminated surface, were constructed (see 
Fig. 1b). 
The adsorption energy (Eads) of CO molecule on CuMn2O4 surface is 
defined as follows: 
Eads = E(CO−CuMn2O4) −
(ECO + ECuMn2O4)
(1) 
where E(CO-CuMn2O4), ECO, and ECuMn2O4 represent the total energies of 
adsorption system, single CO molecule, and optimized CuMn2O4(100) 
surface, respectively. A more negative Eads value suggests that the sys­
tem experiences stronger adsorption. 
The activation energy barrier (Ea) is defined as following equation: 
Ea = ETS −
EIM
(2) 
where ETS and EIM denote the total energies of transition state (TS) 
and intermediate (IM), respectively.

3.2. Influences of CO concentration and heating rate 
The influences of gas concentration and heating rate were considered 
to further study the reaction characteristics of CuMn2O4. As shown in 
Fig. 3, the influence of CO concentration (6%, 9% and 12%) on reduc­
tion characteristics was analyzed. It can be observed an increase of 
reduction rate as the CO concentration increasing. The weight loss of 
CuMn2O4 that starts at 339 ◦C and ends at 700 ◦C is about 13.66% when 
the CO concentration is 6%. However, the temperature range of weight 
loss shift to a lower temperature range for the CO concentration of 9% 
(from 293 to 555 ◦C) and 12% (from 293 to 512 ◦C). For the CO con­
centration of 9% and 12%, the weight loss of CuMn2O4 are 14.7% and 
14.9%, respectively. It should be noticed that the weight loss peak shifts 
from 568 ◦C to 442 ◦C as the CO concentration increases from 6% to 9% 
or 12%. It primarily because the higher CO concentration corresponds to 
more efficient molecular collision, so CO can react with CuMn2O4 at the 
relative low temperature as the CO concentration increasing. It can be 
concluded that the CO concentrations have apparent influence on the 
reaction rate. In particular, low CO concentration (less than6%) is not 
recommended in practical application of CLC process.

Fig. 1c demonstrates the possible active adsorption sites of Cu- 
terminated surface, which includes the five-fold Mn site (Mnb), two- 
fold Cu site (Cub), three-fold O site (Oc) and coordinately saturated O 
site (Od). The calculated adsorption energies and the corresponding 
structural parameters for CO molecule adsorbed on the Cu-terminated 
surface are shown in Fig. 9. The adsorption stability based on the Eads

For configuration 2A, CO strongly chemisorbs at Cub site with the C

For configuration 2B, the CO adsorbs on Mnb site having an adsorbed 
energy of −83.88 kJ/mol. As compared to configuration 1A, the 
adsorption strength of configuration 2B exhibits lower stability. The 
newly generated C−Mnb bond distance is 1.891 Å and the C−O bond 
length of CO is stretched to 1.166 Å. 
As displayed in configuration 2C, CO molecule adsorbed on the 
Mnb−Oc bridge site generating a O−C−Mn geometry. This configuration

3.5. CO oxidation mechanism on CuMn2O4 (100) surface 
In view of the CO adsorption behavior over CuMn2O4 (100) surface, 
the CO oxidation pathways were proposed in this section. Obviously, the 
free CO molecule and CuMn2O4 surface can be taken as an initial state 
(IS), as well as the O-defective surface with the departed CO2 molecule 
are regarded as a final state (FS). It should be noted that the formation of 
CO2 species via CO adsorption on the bridge site is an important inter­
mediate (IM) for CO oxidation revealing the reaction between CO and 
lattice O. Afterward, the transition states (TSs) between IS and IM, as 
well as IM and FS were located. The energy diagram and optimized 
geometries for CO oxidation over both Mn- and Cu-terminated surfaces 
are presented in Fig. 10 and Fig. 11, respectively. It can be seen that the 
CO2 can be formed from CO oxidation over CuMn2O4 surface via the 
following possible elementary reaction steps: CO adsorption, CO diffu­
sion and CO2 desorption. 
As revealed in Fig. 8, there were two possible reaction pathways on 
Mn-terminated surface, which can be distinguished based on the 
different activities of O atom. CO adsorbs on the Mna top site is the first 
step (IS→1A) for both pathways. The CO adsorption step is exothermic 
by 97.16 kJ/mol.

From above analysis, it can be seen that the surface Mn ion is the 
active center for CO adsorption. CO is oxidized by the lattice O upon 
CuMn2O4 surface through Mars van Krevelen pathways. Firstly, CO 
chemisorbs on the surface Mn atom site. Afterward, the adsorbed CO 
tilts to a neighboring O atom to form a surface CO2 species with a bent 
equilibrium geometry. Eventually, CO2 molecule departs from the sur­
face by surmounting an energy barrier. 
It is worth noting that the metal atomic layer on surface shows steric 
hindrance for the interaction of CO molecule with surface O atom [11]. 
Nevertheless, CO oxidation upon the Cu-terminated surface is more 
kinetically and thermodynamically favorable than that upon the Mn- 
terminated surface in this investigation. This is caused by the differ­
ence in oxygen activity that are directly related with the coordinated 
metal atoms. The Oc (on Cu-terminated surface) coordinated with two 5- 
fold Mn and one 4-fold Cu exhibits superior reactivity than the Oa (on 
Mn-terminated surface) coordinated with three 5-fold Mn. This can 
explain the higher reactivity of CuMn2O4 than that of Mn2O3 observed in 
the TGA experiments. 
In our previous works, the reactivity of Fe-based composite oxygen 
carriers (CuFe2O4, MnFe2O4 and CoFe2O4) and the related reaction 
mechanism with CO have been studied [10,11,45]. Compared with 
CuMn2O4, the energy barrier of the minimum energy path for the re­
action of CuFe2O4, CoFe2O4, and MnFe2O4 with CO is 10.84, 69.47, and 
90.19 kJ/mol, respectively. It suggested that the order of surface oxygen 
activity of these four composite oxygen carriers is: CuFe2O4 > CuMn2O4 
> CoFe2O4 > MnFe2O4. Meanwhile, the reactivity of single metal oxy­
gen carriers is promoted by the existence of Cu, Co and Mn elements, and 
the existence of Cu shows the highest improvement in the reactivity. By 
comparing the coordination of active oxygen on the surface of these 
composite oxygen carriers, it can be found that the synergistic effect is 
closely associated with the coordination between oxygen and metal 
atoms. The surface oxygen coordinated with tetrahedral metal atoms 
shows the lowest reactivity. 
4. Conclusion 
The reaction mechanism between CuMn2O4 oxygen carrier and CO 
during CLC was investigated via TGA experiments and DFT calculations. 
XRD was performed to characterize the phases of fresh, oxygen release 
and reduced oxygen carriers. CuMn2O4 shows oxygen release property, 
and the oxygen release temperature is in the range from 626 to 900 ◦C. 
The transformation of CuMn2O4 into CuMnO2 and Mn3O4 accompanies 
the oxygen release. CuMn2O4 can be reduced into Cu and MnO by CO. 
The reduction of CuMn2O4 shows a higher reactivity as compared to 
Mn2O3, suggesting that the existence of Cu can enhance the reactivity of 
Mn2O3. The CO concentration and heating rate show influence on the 
reaction rate. Mn atom on the surface is identified as the active center for 
CO adsorption. CO oxidation over the CuMn2O4 surface proceeds by 
Mars van Krevelen pathway: CO initially chemisorbs on the Mn site, it 
then moves to an adjacent O atom forming a surface CO2 species which 
still adsorbs at Mn site, CO2 molecule finally departs from the surface by 
overcoming an energy barrier. It can be found that the CO oxidation on 
spinel CuMn2O4 surface is exothermic. The reaction is limited by CO 
diffusion on the Mn-terminated surface with an activation energy of 
64.59 kJ/mol, while on the Cu-terminated surface shows lower activa­
tion energy (58.83 kJ/mol) because of the high reactivity of Cu. The 
calculated results show good agreements with the experimental anal­
ysis. These results provide a fundamental understanding for the 
improved reactivity of spinel CuMn2O4 due to the existence of Cu.
