Solar thermochemical CO2 splitting is a promising route to reduce environment pollution and mitigate
energy crisis. However, traditional redox materials are limited by low CO production, slow reaction ki-
netics, and poor cycling stability, which results in low solar-to-fuel efﬁciency. Here, Ca- and Al- doped
SmMnO3 perovskites are proposed for high-performance solar thermochemical CO2 splitting. The
average CO yield of Sm0.6Ca0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3 reaches a record-high value of 595.56 mmol g1 when
swinging between 1350 C and 1100 C. The CO yield is about 4.22 times as high as that of undoped
SmMnO3 (140.97 mmol g1) under the same conditions, and remains stable over multiple cycling. The
ultrahigh CO yield can be attributed to the transformation of reaction mechanism from solid-state sur-
face reaction (F2) to bulk diffusion model (D2). Besides, Ca- and Al- doped SmMnO3 perovskites possess
high solar absorptance over 86.5% (in stark contrast to 12.1% of CeO2), so that only solar concentration
ratio of 898 (Sm0.6Ca0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3) is required to drive thermochemical reactions while benchmark
CeO2 needs 1936. This work provides novel approaches for high-performance solar thermochemical CO2
splitting with high CO yield, good cycle stability, small temperature swing, and low solar concentration
required.

1. Introduction
Over the past decades, the massive use of traditional fossil fuels
has led to serious global warming and energy crisis problems while
the demand of human activities for clean and sustainable fuels is
constantly increasing [1e7]. CO2 conversion into fuels is an effective
route to solve this current dilemma, and various CO2 conversion
paths have been investigated all over the world, such as electro-
chemical [8,9], photochemical [10,11], thermochemical [12e20]
and so on. Among these approaches, solar thermochemical CO2
splitting over two-step redox cycles has attracted much attention
owing to high selectivity, wide solar spectrum utilization, and
simple operation condition, which are described as following
[21e27]:

then produces CO by ﬁlling the oxygen vacancies formed in the ﬁrst

treated as benchmarks. For example, Jacot et al. pointed out Hf-, Zr-,
and Ta- doped ceria display higher oxygen exchange capacity than
pristine ceria to release more oxygen during reduction process [34].
Zhu et al. reported that 15% Zr- doped CeO2 exhibited high perfor-
mance with the CO production of 315.4 mmol g1 (1500/1000 C)
[35]. Nevertheless, there is still a large gap between currently re-
ported solar-to-fuel efﬁciency with the theoretical limit [21,36].
Low CO production, high operation temperature, and high solar
concentration required are three important reasons. To tackle
aforementioned challenges, perovskite oxides (ABO3/ABO3-d) have
drawn increasing attention recently due to their unprecedented
tunability of thermochemical CO2 splitting performance via doping
different elements at A- and B- sites [36e42]. For example, Wang
et al. pointed out LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3 can achieve CO production of
1066.6 mmol g1 when swinging between 1300 C and 800 C.
Nevertheless, under such a wide temperature swing range (500 C),
the sensible heat loss increases signiﬁcantly during cycling process
[43]. Therefore, achieving high CO yield within a small swing
temperature difference is highly desired [44e47].
Efﬁcient system design is also vital for high-performance solar
thermochemical CO2 splitting. To reduce radiative heat losses and
enhance solar-to-fuel energy efﬁciency, various direct solar-driven
reactors have been proposed. For example, Furler et al. designed a
solar cavity-receiver containing porous CeO2 foams, and reported a
peak solar-to-fuel efﬁciency of 3.53% [48]. Based on a similar sys-
tem design, Abanades et al. replaced porous foams via ﬁbrous pel-
lets and obtained a higher peak solar-to-fuel efﬁciency of 9.40%
[49]. Nevertheless, redox cycling is inevitably accomplished by
frequently switching ﬂow of inert gas and CO2 reactants for these
ﬁxed bed reactors. Recently, Milanese et al. reported a new solar
reactor based on a double-loop ﬂuidized bed and achieved
continuous operation [50]. They further employed multi-parameter
optimization to obtain the best conﬁguration of the double-loop
ﬂuidized bed solar reactor, and predicted an average efﬁciency of
29.96% over one year, with a maximum value of 59.46% [51]. For
aforementioned direct solar-driven reactors whether ﬁxed bed or
ﬂuidized bed, a high solar absorptance of materials is always sig-
niﬁcant to achieve a high solar-to-fuel efﬁciency. Therefore, ideal
redox materials should possess high CO yield, small swing tem-
perature difference, and high solar absorptance simultaneously.
In this work, Ca- and Al- doped SmMnO3 are proposed for high-
performance thermochemical CO2 splitting. Redox properties of
different Ca and Al doping ratios based on SmMnO3 are studied, and
the optimal Sm/Ca and Mn/Al ratio are found to be 0.6:0.4 and
0.8:0.2, Sm0.6Ca0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3 namely. A record-high CO yield of
595.56 mmol g1 is demonstrated over small temperature swing
between 1350 C and 1100 C, which is about 4.22 times as high as
that of undoped SmMnO3. And, no obvious degradation is found
over multiply cycles. Effects of different reduction and oxidation

2. Experimental section
2.1. Material synthesis
All perovskite powder samples based on SmMnO3 are synthe-
sized via a sol-gel method using citric acid as the complexing agent.
Sm(NO3)3$6H2O,
Ca(NO3)2$4H2O,
Al(NO3)3$9H2O
and
Mn(CH3COO)2$4H2O are used as metal precursors without further
puriﬁcation. The molar ratio of citric acid (C6H8O7$H2O) and metal
cations is kept at 1.5:1. Firstly, precursors are calculated and
weighted according to the corresponding chemical formula, fol-
lowed by dissolving precursors and citric acid together in a beaker
with 100 mL of deionized water. The mixed solution is then heated
to 90 C with continuous stirring. The mixture becomes sol after DI
water being evaporated. After that, sol is dried at 120 C for 24 h,
and then milled into powders and calcined in a high temperature
furnace at 1400 C in air for 6 h to eliminate organics. Finally, the
perovskite sample is transferred into an agate mortar and collected
for subsequent thermochemical tests and characterizations.
2.2. Material characterization
X-ray diffraction patterns (XRD) are recorded via the Bruker D8
ADVANCE, and the operating current and voltage are 40 mA and
40 kV, respectively. All samples are scanned at a scanning rate of
0.02 s1 in the 2q ranging from 10 to 90. The morphologies of all
perovskite powders are observed via scanning electron microscopy
(GeminiSEM 300) at an acceleration voltage of 2 kV. The element
composition and distribution of samples are investigated by using
the XFlash 6160. The proportions of each metal element for all
samples are analyzed by the Inductively Coupled Plasma (ICP,
Varian 720-ES). The solar absorptance of all samples at different
wavelengths is observed on PerkinElmer (Lambda 1050þ) and
Thermo SCIENTIFIC (iS50 FT-IR). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy
(XPS) measurement is conducted by an ESCALAB 250Xi spectrom-
eter (THERMO Scientiﬁc), using a non-monochromatized AlKa X-
ray (hn ¼ 1486.65 eV) source.

2.3. Thermochemical performance measurements
All samples are weighted as 0.2 g and tested for four cycles in the
experiment setup, which is shown in Fig. 1b. The air in the high-
temperature tubular furnace is ﬁrst removed by a vacuum pump.
After that, the large ﬂow rate of high purity Ar (99.999%) is purged
into a tubular furnace for 1 h to reduce the partial pressure of ox-
ygen and then set the ﬂow rate of Ar to 200 mL min1 for 2 h. Then,
the thermochemical cycle can be run. After that, each sample is
heated to 1350 C at a heating rate of 10 C min1 and kept at the
same temperature for 60 min with a constant ﬂow rate of
200 mL min1 of Ar. The reoxidation process is performed at
1100 C for 90 min with Ar and CO2 (99.999%) controlled at the ﬂow
rate of 100 mL min1 individually (50% CO2 and 50% Ar), then CO2
reacted with oxygen-deﬁcient powders to release CO. The testing
concentrations of O2 and CO produced in the reduction and
oxidation process are detected by O2 detector (YF-A01) and CO
detector (CHeHeCO1000), both of which are calibrated via stan-
dard gas to guarantee the measurement accuracy.
Both the amount of O2 produced in the reduction step and CO
released in the reoxidation step can be calculated by integrating the
measured concentration curve, as follows [42,46]:

4. Conclusions
In this work, direct solar-driven thermochemical CO2 splitting
based on Ca- and Al- doped perovskites are proposed to achieve
high CO yield within small temperature swing. After optimizing the
doping ratio of Ca and Al elements, the highest CO yield reaches
595.56 mmol g1 for Sm0.6Ca0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3, which is about 4.22
times as high as that of undoped SmMnO3 (140.97 mmol g1) under
the same conditions, and remains stable over multiple cycling. The
excellent redox performance of Sm0.6Ca0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3 can be
attributed to the conversion of reaction mechanism from solid-
state surface reaction to bulk diffusion model. Additionally, the
Ca- and Al- doped SmMnO3 perovskites present full-spectrum solar
absorption (higher than 86.5%), which is about 7.15 times as high as
that of CeO2 (12.1%), making solar concentration ratio required to
achieve reduction temperature of 1350 C reduce by 53.62%. High
CO yield and high solar absorptance enable Sm0.6Ca0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3
to possess a high solar-to-fuel efﬁciency of 10.01%, which is 3.81
times as high as that of undoped SmMnO3. This work provides
novel approaches for achieving high-performance solar thermo-
chemical CO2 splitting with high CO yield, good cycle stability, small
temperature swing, and high solar-to-fuel efﬁciency.
