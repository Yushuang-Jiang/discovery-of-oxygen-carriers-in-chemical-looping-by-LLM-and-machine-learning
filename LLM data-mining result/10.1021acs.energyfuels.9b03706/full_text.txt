Improvement of the Oxygen Uncoupling Properties of Copper-
Based Composite Oxygen Carriers for Chemical Looping Air
Separation

Cite This: Energy Fuels 2020, 34, 3449−3457

ABSTRACT: To increase the oxygen concentration in the product gas of a chemical looping air separation (CLAS) system, it is
important to reduce the oxygen uncoupling temperature of the oxygen carrier (OC). In this paper, it is investigated whether and to
what extent the addition of Mn2O3, Co3O4, Pb3O4, and Cr2O3, all of which have oxygen uncoupling propensity according to
thermodynamics, to CuO improves the oxygen uncoupling behavior of the mixed-oxide OC at lower temperatures. Using
thermogravimetric analyses, it is shown that the oxygen uncoupling temperature of a Cu−Mn composite OC is decreased by
approximately 200 °C relative to a Cu OC. In addition, the oxygen transport capacity of this composite OC can reach up to 0.056 g
O2/g OC. Density functional theory calculations have been carried out to analyze the changes in reactivity and stability of the mixed-
oxide OC. When a Mn3O4(001) surface adsorbs a Cu4O4 cluster, the average bond length of Cu−O in the Cu4O4 cluster increases
from 1.983 to 2.047 Å and the charge is transferred from Cu4O4 to Mn3O4(001). Chemical reaction between the cluster and the
surface occurs, and Cu−Mn composite oxides are formed after adsorption. Moreover, the adsorption energy is −3.749 eV, which is
higher than that of Cu4O4 on a CuO(111) surface (−2.92 eV), and the composite OC has better stability and sintering resistance
than the Cu OC. The stability of the composite OC was also investigated at 900 °C under reducing conditions and at 700 °C under
oxidizing conditions. The results show that the Cu−Mn/Zr composite OC remains stable over 15 consecutive cycles. Moreover, the
Cu−Mn composite does not only reduce the oxygen uncoupling temperature of CuO but it also overcomes thermodynamic
limitations of the oxidation of Mn3O4 to Mn2O3, where the redox reaction of the Cu−Mn composite OC can be expressed as
follows: CuxMn3−xO4 ⇋CuxMn2−x + O2(g). From the results, it can be concluded that Mn2O3 is the most suitable composite oxide
to improve the oxygen uncoupling properties of a Cu OC for oxygen uncoupling in the CLAS system.
1. INTRODUCTION
Oxygen is widely applied in chemical industries, and the
demand for oxygen has grown signiﬁcantly in recent years.1
However, the current oxygen production technologies, viz.
cryogenic distillation, swing adsorption, and membrane
separation, are complex, expensive, and energy-intensive.
Chemical looping air separation (CLAS) is a novel technology
to produce high-purity oxygen or oxygen-enriched gases based
on the chemical looping concept. The introduction of the
concept of chemical looping for the production of oxygen can
be dated back to as early as 1811, with the development of the
Brin process. In this process, BaO/Ba2O is used to produce
oxygen. However, the formation of BaCO3 during the
production process poses the biggest problem for this process.2
In 2000, the ceramic autothermal recovery (CAR) technology
was proposed by the BOC Company, where perovskite oxides,
such as LSCF, SCCF, LBCF, BSCF, and SCF, are used to
produce oxygen-enriched gases.3−5 In 2010, the feasibility of
the CLAS technology using metal oxides as oxygen carriers was
ascertained by Moghtaderi to produce pure oxygen or oxygen-
enriched gases.6 Figure 1 shows the oxygen production
principle of the CLAS.
In the CLAS system, the separation of oxygen from air is
achieved by the cyclic reduction−oxidation of a solid oxygen
carrier (OC). The system includes two separated reactors. In
the reduction reactor, the oxidized OC releases oxygen into a

The oxidation of the OC is exothermic, which can provide the
heat necessary for the reduction process. The advantages of the
CLAS system are its energy savings, being relatively simple,
and ease of operation when compared to the cryogenic air
separation system.6,9
A good performance of the OC is key in the development of
the CLAS process. In addition to the usual properties of OCs
for all CL technologies, such as a high oxygen transport
capability, antisintering ability, high reactivity, low cost, and
environmental friendliness, the OC for the CLAS system must
also have the capability to release oxygen into an atmosphere
of inert gases, such as steam and CO2.10−12 Thus, the
development of an eﬀective OC for CLAS is most important.
Many research studies have been performed on the selection of
a suitable OC for CLAS through thermodynamics and
experimental studies. Wang et al. carried out a thermodynamic
study for the selection of a suitable OC by analyzing the Gibbs
free energy changes of the reduction reactions of the metal
oxides.13 The results showed that CrO3, Pb3O4, SrO2, Mn2O3,
Co3O4, and CuO all possess the capacities of releasing oxygen
at suitable temperatures and oxygen partial pressures.
However, despite their thermodynamic feasibility, the metal
oxides of CrO3, Pb3O4, and SrO2 face kinetic problems with
low reaction rates. CuO/Cu2O, Mn2O3/Mn3O4, and Co3O4/
CoO have been identiﬁed as redox pairs with the ability to
release oxygen at temperatures of 800−1200 °C,14−16 and
oxygen carriers with CuO, Mn2O3, and Co3O4 as the active
phases have been widely investigated for chemical looping with
oxygen uncoupling (CLOU).17,18 The reduction reactions of

Of the three oxygen carriers indicated above, the oxygen
transport capability of CuO is the largest, achieving 0.1 g O2/g
CuO. Although the transport capacity of Co3O4 is relatively
high (0.066 g O2/g Co3O4), the large endothermicity of the
reduction reaction makes it diﬃcult to maintain high
temperatures in the reduction reactor. The transport capacity
of Mn2O3 is the smallest (0.033 g O2/g Mn2O3). More
importantly, the oxidation of Mn3O4 to Mn2O3 is restricted
due to the thermodynamic limitations of manganese
oxides.19,20 Based on the above analysis, CuO/Cu2O seems
to be the most suitable metal oxide system for CLAS
technology. However, CuO used as OC for CLAS suﬀers
from some restrictive issues that should be solved. First, the
oxygen uncoupling temperature is high, usually above 850 °C
in inert atmospheres, and the operating temperature should
even be as high as 1025 °C to obtain a product gas with an
oxygen concentration of 20.0%.13 The high oxygen uncoupling
temperature complicates the operation of the system. More-
over, agglomeration issues of Cu-based OCs at these very high
temperatures are very serious, caused by the low melting points
of copper oxides (1450 °C for CuO, 1235 °C for Cu2O, and
1075 °C for CuO−Cu2O).21
At present, most investigations are focused on the
improvement of the antisintering properties of CuO, and the
most common method is the addition of binders in the
preparation of the Cu-based OC. Adánez-Rubio et al.
evaluated the stability of an OC consisting of CuO with 40
wt % MgAl2O4 for a CLOU process. Constant reactivity was
observed at 950 °C in a batch ﬂuidized-bed reactor for six
consecutive redox periods, and no agglomeration problems
were encountered during 40 h of continuous operation.22
Arjmand et al. also found that the OC with MgAl2O4 as
support exhibited a stable oxygen-releasing behavior.23
Mattisson et al. investigated the stability of the CuO/ZrO2
OC by exposing it to petroleum coke and oxygen alternately in
a laboratory ﬂuidized-bed reactor. Neither deﬂuidization nor
agglomeration was observed during the experiments.24 Song et
al. found that the OC with SiO2 as binder showed the highest
redox stability in their thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)
experiments.25 Moreover, when MgO was added, the stability
of the Cu/Si OC was further improved.26 In our previous
studies, we also found that the redox reactivity of a Cu-based
OC remained stable at 1000 °C when 60 wt % ZrO2 or 50 wt
% MgAl2O4 was added.27,28
From the above investigations, it can be concluded that the
agglomeration tendency of copper oxides can be greatly
reduced by the addition of binders. However, the high binder
addition ratio decreases the oxygen transport capacity and
increases the OC inventory in the reactor, while also the power
consumption for OC cycling between the reduction and
oxidation reactors is increased. Even though binders can
increase the reaction rates to some extent, the required redox
temperatures of the Cu-based OC are still very high.29,30
Unlike chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) and
chemical looping combustion (CLC) technologies, the
purpose of CLAS is to produce oxygen, and it is desired to
maintain the temperatures of the reduction and oxidation
reactors at a relatively low level. Based on our previous
research, the oxidation rates of the Cu-based OC remain high,
within the oxidation temperature range of 500−800 °C.31
Thus, reducing the oxygen uncoupling temperature is key and
is the focus of this study. In addition, agglomeration issues of
copper-based OCs can be avoided when operating at low
temperatures.
Elemental composition or substitution is an eﬀective method
to improve the thermodynamic properties of oxides, and this
method has been commonly used to overcome thermodynamic
restrictions in the oxidation of Mn3O4 to Mn2O3 and to
improve the redox properties of perovskite oxides.32−34 Azimi
et al. conﬁrmed the feasibility of Mn−Fe OC in CLOU. At 850
°C, the oxygen concentration in an inert atmosphere was
around 0.2−0.5% in a ﬂuidized-bed reactor, and the oxygen
transport capacity was 0.032−0.033 g/g (Mn, Fe)2O3;35
Mattisson et al. investigated six combined manganese oxide
systems and found that MnyMgOx particles likely release
oxygen through Mg2MnO4;36 Hou et al. investigated the
feasibility of YBaCo4O7+δ as OC and found that the δ value can
be increased using the Dy element to substitute the Y element.
The oxygen transport capacity reached up to 0.027 g/g
YxDy1−xBaCo4O7+δ.37 In this study, the oxygen uncoupling
property of the Cu-based OC was improved by the
composition method. Due to the high oxygen transport
capacity of CuO/Cu2O, the composite Cu-based OCs may
retain this property. Mn2O3, Co3O4, Pb3O4, and Cr2O3 were
selected as candidate materials to prepare the copper

2. METHODS
2.1. Preparation of the Composite OC. Mechanical mixing is a
simple, low-cost, and high-yield method for the preparation of OCs,
and it was also used to prepare the composite Cu-based OCs in this
paper. Commercial CuO, Mn2O3 (Co3O4, Pb3O4, Cr2O3), and ZrO2
obtained from the Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Company were used
as raw materials. ZrO2, as a binder in all of the samples, was used to
increase the antisintering properties of OCs. Graphite (10 wt %) was
added as a pore-forming agent during the mixing process. Using
graphite, the mechanical strength of the prepared OCs was also
improved.27 The preparation process was as follows: the powders of
the raw materials were mixed thoroughly in an agate mortar; distilled
water was added to form a paste; the paste was dried at 80 °C for 12 h
in a dryer, and then calcined at 950 °C for 6 h in a furnace in an air
atmosphere. Finally, the samples were ground up and sieved to obtain
particles within a size range of 150−180 μm.
2.2. Characterization of the Composite OC. When binders,
such as MgAl2O4, ZrO2, and SiO2, are added to CuO, the phases of
CuO and binders remain stable during the preparation proc-
ess,17−21,27,28 and the thermodynamic properties of CuO do not
change. However, when doping oxides are added to CuO, these will
react with CuO and form new phases, thereby potentially modifying
the oxygen uncoupling property of CuO. Therefore, X-ray
diﬀractometer (XRD) (PANalytical, PW 3040/60) measurements
were carried out to determine the phases of the four prepared types of
composite OCs.
In addition, stability is very important for the composite copper
OCs, where agglomeration is considered to be the main cause for a
decrease in the stability of the oxygen carrier. The morphology and
surface microstructure of the prepared OCs after consecutive redox
cycles were investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
(ZEISS, ULTRA 55). Whether in a ﬂuidized bed or a ﬁxed bed, good
mechanical strength is necessary to avoid attrition of the OC.37 The
mechanical strength was measured using a press apparatus (SHIMPO,
FGV-50). The Brunauer−Emmett−Teller (BET) surface area and
Barrett−Joyner−Halenda (BJH) pore volume of the prepared sample
were also measured (QUANTACHROME, NOVA 1200e).
2.3. Experimental Set-Up. Experiments were performed in a
thermal analyzer (Netzsch, Model STA409PC). Temperature-
programmed TGA experiments (room temperature to 1000 °C with
a heating rate of 15 °C/min) were used to determine the starting
oxygen uncoupling temperature as well as the oxygen transfer capacity
of the OC prepared. Subsequently, isothermal TGA experiments were
used to investigate the reactivity and stability of the OCs at constant
reduction−oxidation temperatures. In all experiments, nitrogen was
used as the carrier gas in the reduction step and air was used to
oxidize the reduced oxygen carrier. All of the samples, about 10 mg
for each experiment, were loaded in a crucible (6.8 mm diameter and
4 mm height).

the weight changes during the reduction and oxidation steps. The
weight of the OC after complete oxidation and reduction is deﬁned as
mox and mred. The mass of oxygen released per unit mass of OC is
deﬁned as the oxygen transport capacity (Ro), and it can be calculated

2.5. DFT Analyses. DFT calculations were performed using the
DMol3 module. First, the Cu4O4 cluster and the Mn3O4(001) surface
were optimized. Subsequently, the Cu4O4 cluster was loaded onto the
Mn3O4 model. In the calculations, the generalized gradient
approximation (GGA) method with Perdew−Burk−Ernzerhof
(PBE) was used, having selected the double numeric with polarization
(DNP) basic set and eﬀective core potentials (ECP). Electron spin
was always considered due to the special properties of the transition
metal. The convergence tolerance for energy, force, maximum
displacement, and self-consistent ﬁeld (SCF) were set as 1.0 × 10−5
Ha, 0.002 Ha/Å, 0.005 Å, and 1.0 × 10−6, respectively.
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Determination of Suitable Composite Oxide. In
this paper, Cu−Mn/Zr, Cu−Co/Zr, Cu−Pb/Zr, and Cu−Cr/
Zr composite OCs were prepared. The oxygen uncoupling
properties of the four types of OCs measured in the TGA are
presented in Figure 2. The ﬁgure shows that Cu−Cr/Zr hardly
exhibits any oxygen uncoupling ability, whereas Cu−Mn/Zr,
Cu−Co/Zr, and Cu−Pb/Zr all present oxygen uncoupling
activity. Among these three OCs, the initial oxygen uncoupling
temperature (Tred) of Cu−Mn/Zr is the lowest. Compared to
Cu/Zr, Tred of the Cu−Mn composite OC decreases by about
200 °C.21 The result corresponds to the result in the literature.
Driessens and Rieck suggested that a Cu/Mn composite
oxygen carrier could reduce the oxygen-releasing temperature
and improve the oxidative regeneration ability of a pure Mn-
based oxygen carrier.38 Azad et al. found that the spinel Cu−
Mn−O system could release O2 in an inert atmosphere at the
tested temperature range of 800−850 °C.39 The Cu−Co/Zr
also presents good oxygen uncoupling activity. For this OC,
the oxygen uncoupling process can be divided into two steps:
the ﬁrst step takes place in the temperature range of 750−820
°C and the second step between 820 and 910 °C, which is
attributed to two diﬀerent active phases in the Cu−Co/Zr OC
with diﬀerent reduction temperatures. For the Cu−Pb
composite OC, the value of Tred is the highest and this value
is almost the same as for the Cu-based OC.
The oxygen uncoupling characteristics depend greatly on the
composition compared to the Cu-based OC, and the diﬀerent

reaction steps are shown in the oxygen uncoupling process.
Table 1 summarizes the composition characteristics of the
four OCs. The eﬀects of composite oxides can be divided into
three types: (i) reaction with CuO to form a new phase with or
without oxygen uncoupling activity, where the oxygen
uncoupling activity of the Cu-based oxygen carrier is enhanced
greatly if the new phase shows oxygen uncoupling activity; (ii)
reaction with the binder forming a new binder phase; and (iii)
no reaction occurs and the active phases are CuO and the
added composite oxide. It should be pointed out that only
oxides with thermodynamic potential for oxygen release have
been selected in this study to combine with the copper oxide.
Other oxides such as Fe2O3, NiO, CeO2, etc. will be
investigated in a future study. The oxygen transport capacity
of Cu−Co/Zr was found to be the largest. The reason is that
both Co3O4 and CuO have the ability to releasing oxygen. The
value of Ro of Cu−Mn/Zr is also high. On the other hand, the
Ro of Cu−Pb/Zr is the lowest due to the low CuO mass ratio.
Despite the high value of Ro, the thermodynamic properties of
Co3O4 and CuO remain unaltered. This type of oxygen carrier
is thus only the mixing of active phases.
Based on the analysis of the oxygen uncoupling activity of
the four types of OCs, the oxygen uncoupling temperature of
the Cu−Mn composite OC is about 200 °C lower than the
copper OC (referring to Figure 2), while the oxygen transport
capacity can reach up to 0.056 g O2/g AP. According to the
focus of this study, Mn2O3 is the most suitable oxide to
improve the oxygen uncoupling properties of a composite OC
with CuO, and the active phase in the Cu−Mn OC is
determined as CuxMn3−xO4.
Subsequently, the addition ratio of Mn2O3 to CuO on the
oxygen uncoupling activity of the Cu−Mn/Zr composite OC
was investigated. Four diﬀerent Mn2O3 adding ratios (in the
molar ratio CuO/Mn2O3 of 0.5:1, 1:1, 1.5:1, and 2:1) were
used in the preparation of the composite OCs. Figure 4 shows
the chemical species presented in the OCs with diﬀerent
Mn2O3 addition ratios. Within the tested range, the
CuxMn3−xO4 phase is formed and the main phases are
CuxMn3−xO4 and ZrO2 in all of the samples. The reason is
that the value of x in CuxMn3−xO4 is variable. When the molar
ratio of CuO/Mn2O3 is 0.5:1, Mn3O4 is presented in this
sample. The preparation conditions are thus easy to control,
which is important to decrease the cost of the OC in CL
technologies.

3.2. DFT Analysis. DFT calculations, also known as ﬁrst-
principle calculations, were used in CL technologies to reveal
the interaction of phases in the OC particle and the
microscopic mechanism of the redox reaction in recent
years.40−44 In this study, the Cu4O4 cluster was loaded on
the Mn3O4 models, and the adsorption energy was calculated.
The reactivity and stability of the composite model were also
analyzed.
Mn3O4 is a typical Jahn−Teller twisted spinel compound.
The space group is 141/AMD, and the ionic structure is
Mn2+(Mn3+)2O4, where Mn2+ is located at the center of the
tetrahedron and Mn3+ is located at the center of the
octahedron. Mn3O4 is also an antiferromagnetic substance
with a spin direction of ↑↓↑↓↑↓.45 Figure 6a shows the
structure diagram of the Mn3O4 unit cell. The theoretical
calculation of the optimized cell parameters is a = b = 5.762 Å,
c = 9.439 Å, which is close to the experimental value (a = b =
5.76 Å, c = 9.46 Å).46 The surface of Mn3O4 belongs to the
polar surface, and the surface structure of Mn3O4(001) is the
most stable.47 Figure 6b,c shows the surface structure of
Mn3O4(001). The surface atoms are repeatedly arranged in
eight layers as one cycle in the Z-direction. Thus, an 8-layer (2
× 1) Mn3O4(001) surface structure with a total of 81 atoms
was constructed. To eliminate the interaction between the
layers, the thickness of the vacuum gap was set at 20 Å.

ensure the accuracy of the calculation results. In this study, a

to 2.047 Å, indicating that the cluster has been activated. In a
free Cu4O4 cluster, the charge numbers of Cu and O atoms are
0.547 and −0.547, respectively, while the charge numbers of
each atom after adsorption are 0.504, 0.504, 0.503, 0.503,
−0.502, −0.502, −0.468, and −0.468, respectively. The total
charge number also changes from 0 to 0.074, which indicates
that the charge of atoms in the Cu4O4 cluster has been
transferred to Mn3O4(001). A chemical reaction between the
cluster and the surface occurs, and a Cu−Mn composite OC is
obtained. After adsorption, the electrons are redistributed, in
which the O atom loses part of the electrons and carries less
negative charges, which is more conducive to the oxygen
uncoupling reaction in the CLAS system.
3.3. Stability Tests. In accordance with the results from
the DFT calculations, a chemical reaction between copper and
manganese oxides to form the Cu−Mn composite oxides had
already been conﬁrmed. Moreover, the DFT calculation results
indicate that the composite oxide has better stability and
sintering resistance than copper oxides due to the high
adsorption energy. In this section, the stability of the Cu−Mn
composite OC was investigated experimentally. Cyclic redox
experiments were carried out in alternate N2 and air
atmospheres at 900 °C reduction and 700 °C oxidation
temperatures, where 15 complete cycles were performed.
Figure 9 shows the mass changes of Cu−Mn/Zr OC during
the 15 cycles. In each cycle, the amount of oxygen released and
absorbed by the OC is basically the same. The reduced OC
can be completely reoxidized. The composite of Cu−Mn not
only reduces the oxygen uncoupling temperature of CuO but
also overcomes thermodynamic limitations in the oxidation of
Mn3O4 to Mn2O3. Moreover, the oxygen transport capacity
and reaction rates have changed very little over the consecutive
cycles. This type of OC retains its high stability when
employed at high temperatures.
The chemical species in the OCs before and after cycles
were also determined by XRD. Figure 10 shows the XRD
phases of the fresh, reduced, and oxidized OCs. In the fresh
and oxidized samples, the peaks have good correspondence,
which reﬂects that the reduced state can be completely
converted back to the oxidized state in an air atmosphere.

CuO to construct a copper composite OC to reduce the
oxygen uncoupling temperature of CuO. The main results

(1) Cu−Cr composite OC does not exhibit any oxygen

oxygen uncoupling temperature as the Cu-based OC.
No reaction occurs during the preparation process when
CuO and Co3O4 are combined, and this type of OC
retains the oxygen uncoupling properties of CuO and
Co3O4. However, the oxygen uncoupling temperature of
a Cu−Mn composite OC decreases by about 200 °C
compared with the copper OC. The active phase
CuxMn3−xO4 is formed during the preparation process.
(2) DFT calculations were carried out to analyze changes in
the reactivity and stability of the composite OC. When a
Mn3O4(001) surface adsorbs a Cu4O4 cluster, the O−
Mn bond length is 1.821 Å and the Cu−O bond length
is 1.962 Å, and the charge transfers from Cu4O4 to
Mn3O4(001), which indicates a chemical reaction
between the cluster and the surface, forming a Cu−
Mn composite oxide. Moreover, the adsorption energy is
−3.749 eV, which is higher than that of Cu4O4 on the
CuO(111) surface (−2.92 eV), which indicates that the
composite OC has better stability and sintering
resistance than the copper OC.
(3) The Cu−Mn/Zr composite OC remains stable during
15 redox cycles and the composite of Cu−Mn not only
reduces the oxygen uncoupling temperature of CuO but
also overcomes thermodynamic limitations in the
oxidation of Mn3O4 to Mn2O3, where the redox reaction
of the Cu−Mn composite OC can be expressed as
