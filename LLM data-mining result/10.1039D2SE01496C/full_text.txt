Remarkable solar thermochemical CO2 splitting
performances based on Ce- and Al-doped SrMnO3
perovskites†
Ke Gao,a Xianglei Liu,
*abc Qi Wang,
a Zhixing Jiang,a Cheng Tian,a Nan Suna
and Yimin Xuanabc
Solar thermochemical CO2 splitting has emerged as a promising strategy for solar fuel production to alleviate
climate change challenges and reduce the energy crisis. However, traditional redox materials still suﬀer from
low CO production, poor cycle stability, high operating temperature, and large temperature swing, leading to
low solar-to-fuel eﬃciency. Herein, Ce- and Al-doped SrMnO3 perovskites are proposed for high-
performance solar thermochemical CO2 splitting within moderate temperature swing (1350/1100 °C).
Sr0.6Ce0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3 demonstrates a remarkable CO yield of 799.34 mmol g−1, which is ∼6.67 times as
high as that of benchmark CeO2. Fast CO2 splitting kinetics of SCMA20 is demonstrated by the CO peak
production rate enhanced by 142% compared with undoped SrMnO3. The proposed Sr0.6Ce0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3
also exhibits excellent stability with no obvious deactivation or degradation over multiple cycles. The
ultrahigh CO yield can be attributed to lower oxygen vacancy formation (3.65 eV) and migration energy
(2.14 eV) as conﬁrmed by DFT calculations, and the transformation of the ﬁrst-order reaction (F1) to the
second-order reaction (F2) compared to SrMnO3. This work provides a new path for high-performance
solar thermochemical CO2 splitting with high CO production, fast reaction kinetics, good cycling stability,
moderate temperature swing, and high solar absorptance.
1.
Introduction
The excessive use and massive combustion of traditional fossil fuels
have resulted in huge anthropogenic emissions of CO2 to the
atmosphere,1–3 which is now considered to be responsible for energy
and climate change crises.4–7 As a result, humans' attention to the
development and utilization of sustainable clean energy continues
to rise.8–11 Massive and ongoing eﬀorts have been made to convert
CO2 into solar fuels in order to solve this quandary.12–14 Especially,
solar thermochemical CO2 splitting via redox cycles has received
much attention due to ease of implementation, utilization of the full
solar spectrum, high solar-to-fuel eﬃciencies, and great promise for
CO2 emission and utilization.15–18 Meanwhile, this reaction process
can make the Gibbs free energy less than zero at a lower tempera-
ture to reduce the reaction temperature signicantly, compared
with the traditional direct CO2 splitting process (over 3000 °C).18 The
general reaction in each step can be listed as follows:19–21

To deal with the above challenges, perovskite redox materials
(ABO3/ABO3−d) have received much attention widely owing to
their exible and adjustable structure,42–44 which can be achieved
by doping one or more metal elements at the A site or the B
site.45–48 For instance, Dey et al. pointed out that Y0.5Sr0.5MnO3
possesses a remarkable CO production activity of 757 mmol g−1 in
the 1400/900 °C redox cycle. Nevertheless, the high operating
temperature during reduction steps results in very high energy
demand and thus low solar-to-fuel eﬃciency.19 Very recently, Gao
et al. reported the redox performance of Sm0.6Sr0.4MnO3 in the
1300 °C isothermal cycle; however, the average CO production is
only 376.1 mmol g−1. Meanwhile, the CO yield rate of Sm0.6-
Sr0.4MnO3 (2.0 mmol g−1) is only 20.8% of that of the benchmark
(CeO2), and such slow kinetics will lead to high energy losses and
thus low solar-to-fuel eﬃciencies.49 Therefore, it is still a formi-
dable challenge to achieve high CO yield and fast reaction
kinetics at moderate temperature swing and low reduction
temperature for obtaining high solar-to-fuel eﬃciency.
Herein, we propose a novel redox material, Ce- and Al-doped
SrMnO3, for solar thermochemical CO2 splitting with remark-
able redox performances. A series of Ce- and Al-doped SrMnO3
perovskite samples are investigated for CO2 splitting, and the
optimal redox material, Sr0.6Ce0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3, shows the high-
est CO yield of 799.34 mmol g−1 over moderate temperature
swing (1350/1100 °C), which is ∼6.67 times as high as that of the
benchmark material, CeO2 (119.82 mmol g−1). Meanwhile, the
total CO yield only decays by 6.23% aer 20 redox cycles.
Moreover, the eﬀects of diﬀerent reduction and oxidation
temperatures for Sr0.6Ce0.4Mn0.8Al0.2O3 are studied, and their
kinetic reaction mechanisms during the oxidation process are
revealed. Additionally, the high solar absorptance enables the
required solar concentration ratio to be reduced signicantly by
using Sr1−xCexMn1−yAlyMnO3 perovskites.

2.4
DFT calculation
All calculations are performed within the DFT framework using
the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of Perdew–
Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE).50 The VASP (Vienna Ab initio Simulation
Package) package is used together with the projection-enhanced
wave method.51,52 The kinetic cutoﬀenergy of the plane wave
basis is set to 480 eV. As shown in Fig. S2,† when the cutoﬀ
energy for the plane-wave-basis is set to 480 eV, the calculation
example has fully converged. Brillouin zone integration is per-
formed on a Gamma-centered K-mesh (1 × 1 × 1). The self-
consistent eld (SCF) procedure is performed with a conver-
gence criterion of 10−8 eV, with all atoms fully relaxed until the
force on each atom is less than 0.02 eV ˚A−1. Prior to this, a rough
calculation is performed using a 10−5 eV convergence criterion.
The ground state energy is calculated using the DFT+U
method,53 where the U value of Mn is 3.8 eV.54 To analyze the
eﬀect of Al doping in the SrMnO3 crystal, a 2 × 1 × 1 supercell
of Sr0.6Ce0.4Mn1−xAlxO3 is constructed. A total of 98 atoms,
including 10 Sr atoms, 6 Ce atoms and the corresponding
proportion of Al atoms (3, 6, 10, and 13), are used. The oxygen
vacancy formation and migration energies are calculated for the
four models, and the calculation of the oxygen vacancy forma-
tion energy is determined by using:54

range by comparing with other materials. The average solar

3.4
Eﬀects of reduction and oxidation temperature
According to the previous analysis and discussion, it can be
found that SCMA20 presents the best redox performance. The
eﬀects of diﬀerent reduction and oxidation temperatures are
investigated further while other parameters remain constant.
Then, in order to nd out the eﬀects of reduction temperature
on redox capacity, the reduction temperature is set to 1100 °C,
1150 °C, 1250 °C, 1350 °C, and 1400 °C (Fig. S13†), respectively,
and the oxidation temperature is maintained at 1100 °C, as
shown in Fig. 8a and b. As illustrated in Fig. 8a and Table S1,†
both total O2 and CO yields are the lowest (56.00 mmol g−1 s−1
and 78.09 mmol g−1 s−1) at a reduction temperature of 1100 °C.
It can also be noticed that as the reduction temperature
increases, SCMA20 is more likely to form oxygen vacancies and
thus release more O2. For example, the corresponding O2
production is enhanced continuously from 56.00 mmol g−1 to
479.34 mmol g−1, when the temperature increases from 1100 °C
to 1400 °C. The CO yield, on the other hand, reaches the highest
value of 799.34 mmol g−1 at a reduction temperature of 1350 °C,
and then drops to 674.53 mmol g−1 at 1400 °C. This dropping
phenomenon is attributed to severe sintering, resulting in
a signicant reduction in the contact area between CO2 and
material particles (Fig. S14†). Additionally, the reoxidation
extent (0.5 × total CO yield/total O2 yield) reects the relation-
ship between O2 and CO production, and the higher the value,
the higher the utilization rate for releasing O2. It can be found
that the reoxidation extent of SCMA20 at 1400/1100 °C is only
70.36%, showing that a large number of oxygen vacancies
formed in the reduction process are not lled well during the
oxidation reaction. As displayed in Fig. 8b, for diﬀerent reaction
conditions, the CO peak production rate increases with reduc-
tion temperature, and reaches a maximum (0.607 mmol g−1 s−1)
at 1350 °C, and decreases to 0.57 mmol g−1 s−1 at 1400 °C. This
change trend of the CO peak production rate is basically the
same as that of the total CO yield. Therefore, 1350 °C is the
optimal reduction temperature for SCMA20 during solar ther-
mochemical CO2 splitting.

n power of the reactant concentration, and the change of the
reaction rate is more obvious with the increase of reaction order

the normalized CO production rate has the same trend as the
second order reaction model (F2) at rst, and subsequently,

lattice defects.64,65 Compared to undoped SrMnO3, the kinetic

F2, indicating that the kinetic reaction rate can be enhanced by
the increase of the reaction order n. Therefore, the ultrahigh CO
yield and fast kinetics of SCMA20 can be mainly attributed to
the transition from the rst-order reaction (F1) to the second-
order reaction (F2).

4.
Conclusions
In this work, Ce- and Al-doped SrMnO3 perovskites are
proposed for high-performance solar thermochemical CO2
splitting with a high CO yield, moderate temperature swing, fast
reaction kinetics, high solar absorptance, and good cycle
stability. Aer optimizing doping contents, Sr0.6Ce0.4Mn0.8-
Al0.2O3 is regarded as the best with an average CO yield of 799.34
mmol g−1, which is ∼6.67 times as high as that of benchmark
CeO2 (119.82 mmol g−1), and the CO peak production rate (0.607
mmol g−1 s−1) is ∼2.42 times as high as that of undoped
SrMnO3. And no obvious downward trend is found aer 20
redox cycles. The eﬀects of reduction and oxidation tempera-
tures on the redox performance are elucidated, and the optimal
operation conditions of 1350/1100 °C are suggested. Mean-
while, it has the lowest oxygen vacancy formation (3.65 eV) and
migration energy (2.14 eV) based on DFT calculation during the
reduction process, which results in more O2 production and
thus releases more CO. And its reaction mechanism of the
oxidation process is revealed, and compared to undoped
SrMnO3, the fast kinetics of SCMA20 can be mainly attributed to
the transition from the rst-order reaction (F1) to the second-
order reaction (F2) at the beginning of the oxidation process.
Moreover, the average solar absorptance of the proposed
perovskite samples is over 86.18%, which is much higher (∼6.50
times) than that of CeO2 (13.3%), enabling thermochemical CO2
splitting reactions to be operated at moderate solar concentra-
tion ratios. This work provides novel perovskites for direct solar-
driven thermochemical CO2 splitting with remarkable redox
performances,
excellent
solar
absorptance,
fast
reaction
kinetics, and moderate temperature swing, and gives excellent
perspectives to photothermal reaction systems.
