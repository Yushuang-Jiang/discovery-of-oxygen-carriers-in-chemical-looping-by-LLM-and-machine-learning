a b s t r a c t
Sewage sludge is the by-product from the wastewater treatment plant. It can be utilized by chemical
looping combustion (CLC) for energy conversion and phosphorus recovery. Hematite is an attractive
material for CLC due to its abundance, low cost and environmental compatibility. However, hematite
shows low reactivity for CLC of solid fuel. The reactivity of hematite can be improved by synthesizing
with CuO. This work is to evaluate the combustion performance and NO emission in CLC of sewage sludge
in a batch ﬂuidized-bed reactor. The oxygen carrier materials consisted of a mixture of hematite and CuO,
which were synthesized using mechanical mixing method. The effect of reduction temperature and the
CuO/hematite mass ratio on combustion efﬁciency and NO emission were investigated. The results show
that the mixture of CuO and hematite can give signiﬁcant improvement in sludge conversion in compar-
ison to only hematite. The oxygen carrier which consisted of 40% CuO and 60% hematite gave the highest
sludge conversion with or without steam in ﬂuidizing gas. Most of the nitrogen in sewage sludge was
released as N2 during the reduction period. About 0.4% of N in sewage sludge was converted to NO using
the materials containing 10% CuO and 90% hematite. The conversion of fuel-N to NO decreased with the
mass ratio of CuO/(CuO + hematite) from 10% to 40%. Additionally, the structural and morphological sta-
bility of the oxygen carriers have been examined. The formation of CuFe2O4 could improve both the reac-
tivity of Fe2O3 species through synergetic effect and the physical stability of CuO during redox reactions.
Therefore, mixing hematite with CuO could be suitable for CLC of sewage sludge.

1. Introduction
Sewage sludge is the by-product from the wastewater treat-
ment plant [1]. It is a complex mixture of organic and inorganic
materials and contains a wide variety of substances and microor-
ganisms in suspended or dissolved form [2]. Some ingredients in
the sludge such as phosphorus and organic matter are valuable
components. With the growing global urbanization in recent years,
the quantity of sewage sludge around the world has increased
rapidly
[3].
The
increasingly
stringent
sludge
reuse/disposal
regulations and increasing public concerns demand the reliable
and lasting sludge management strategies [4]. Due to the organic
nature of sewage sludge, thermochemical process is one of the
most attractive methods to produce energy and valuable products
from sewage sludge. Chemical looping combustion (CLC) is an
innovative combustion technology that could intrinsically separate
CO2 within the process. CLC offers a promising solution for sludge
treatment with the target for both energy reuse and phosphorus
recovery [5,6].
The CLC system consists of two reactors, a fuel reactor and an
air reactor with the oxygen carrier continuously circulating
between them. In the fuel reactor, the oxygen carrier in a higher
state is reduced by the fuel, which in turn is oxidized to CO2 and

H2O. Subsequently, the reduced oxygen carrier is oxidized to its
initial state in the air reactor [7]. In this way, the fuel reactor efﬂu-
ent is comprised of CO2 and H2O, therefore a concentrated stream
of CO2 suitable for sequestration can be obtained simply by water
condensation. If a solid fuel is used, the main approach is in situ
gasiﬁcation chemical looping combustion (iG-CLC). In this process,
the solid fuel is physically mixed with the oxygen carrier in the fuel
reactor and gasiﬁed by CO2 or steam. Then the gaseous compounds
(volatiles and gasiﬁcation products) react with the oxygen carrier.
The selection of efﬁcient oxygen carriers is an essential compo-
nent of the CLC process. The oxygen carrier should exhibit sufﬁ-
cient oxygen transport capacity, high reactivity towards both fuel
and air. It should also be mechanically durable, inexpensive and
environment-benign [7]. Additionally, it should present resistance
to attrition to minimize losses of elutriated solids. Both nickel
oxide and hematite have been investigated as oxygen carrier for
CLC of sewage sludge [5,6]. Although higher carbon capture efﬁ-
ciency is obtained, the main drawback is the lower CO2 capture
efﬁciency. When solid fuel is used in CLC process, the CO2 capture
efﬁciency will be reduced if char particles enter the air reactor with
the oxygen carrier stream [8]. Fortunately, our previous work
revealed that there were no char particles by-passed to air reactor
during CLC of sewage sludge using hematite as oxygen carrier in a
1 kW continuous unit [6]. Thus, it is not necessary to deploy a car-
bon stripper to separate the char from oxygen carrier. Sewage
sludge contains a high volatile content. It may leads to insufﬁcient
carrier stability and poor redox kinetics if a low reactivity oxygen
carrier is selected.
Among the various metal oxides (CuO, NiO, Fe2O3, etc.) tested as
oxygen carriers for CLC, iron oxide remains the most favorable due
to its abundance, low cost and environmental compatibility [9].
Hematite as a kind of iron oxides has commonly been used as an
oxygen carrier [10–12]. However, its low fuel conversion, weak
redox characteristics, and low oxygen transport capacity (because
of thermodynamic limitation) constitute signiﬁcant drawbacks. It
is possible to resolve the shortcomings by combining hematite
with a more reactive metal, such as Cu, Mn and Ni [9,13–15].
The mixed oxides show a good balance between cost, toxicity
and reactivity for CLC. One interesting category among these is
the combined Cu–Fe oxide as oxygen carrier. The combined Cu–
Fe oxygen carriers have low cost, high reactivity and favorable
environmental and thermodynamic properties. Thus, the Cu–Fe
oxide is signiﬁcantly relevant for CLC applications.
The phase diagram of CuO–Fe2O3 bimetallic system is shown in
Fig. 1 [16]. It can be seen that a solid solution of CuO–Fe2O3 is
formed at the temperature above 761 K. If the mole concentration
of Fe2O3 is higher than CuO, Fe2O3 and CuFe2O4 can coexist.
Similarly, CuO can be observed when CuO concentration is higher
than Fe2O3.
Several combined Cu–Fe oxides have been examined as oxygen
carriers in both CLC and CLOU process [17,18]. Compared with the
single metal oxygen carriers, the combined Cu–Fe oxides displayed
high reaction rate, better solid conversion, greater oxygen usage
and improved physical stability [16]. Additionally, Cu–Fe mixed
oxides has a higher catalytic activity for CO oxidation even at
low temperature [19]. The addition of Cu into iron oxide promoted
the structure and enhanced the catalytic activity through a
synergistic effect. The results by Yang et al. indicate that 6% Cu
(6CuHem) has better reactivity with the gasiﬁcation products
and can accelerate the gasiﬁcation rate of anthracite char [17].
It is a crucial technical obstacle that high nitrogen content in
sewage sludge could cause a high NOx emission during combustion
process. The high NOx emissions from sludge combustion are not
only because of their high nitrogen content but also the high
contents of metal oxides in the ash [20,21].
Shimizu et al. [20,21] investigated the emissions of NOx and N2O
during combustion of dried sewage sludge in both bubbling
ﬂuidized bed combustor (BFBC) and circulating ﬂuidized bed com-
bustor (CFBC). The results show that the increase in NOx with the
sludge ash accumulation in the reactor is less for the CFBC than
the BFBC. It can be due to the higher attrition rate of sludge ash
in CFBC resulting from the higher gas velocity. The combustion
process in the CFBC presents a high concentration of NOx
(400–600 mg/Nm3) at 1123 K. To reduce NOx emission, a combined
gasiﬁcation and incineration process for the disposal of sewage
sludge was proposed [22]. The results indicate that the conversion
of fuel-N to NOx is 1 ± 0.04%. Even with the combined gasiﬁcation
and incineration process, high NOx concentrations are emitted in
sewage sludge air-combustion process. However, during CLC of
coal in a 1 kW unit, N2 is the sole product of fuel-N conversion in
the fuel reactor using either hematite or Ni-based oxide as oxygen
carrier [12,23]. Thus, it can be speculated that both the inlet fuels
and the combustion conditions have a great inﬂuence on the NO
emission [24]. As a result, the viability of sewage sludge for CLC,
to a large extent, will be determined by the NO emission level.
The objective of this work is to explore the combustion perfor-
mance and NO emission in CLC of sewage sludge using bimetallic
Cu–Fe oxide as oxygen carrier. The experiment was conducted in
a batch ﬂuidized-bed reactor. The effects of operating conditions
including reduction temperature and the CuO/hematite mass ratio
on the combustion characteristics were investigated. The combus-
tion characteristics such as carbon conversion and NO emission
were analyzed. SEM and XRD analyses were also conducted to
reveal the reaction mechanism of during sewage sludge in CLC.
2. Experimental
2.1. Manufacture and characterization of oxygen carriers
The oxygen carriers used in this work were particles with a
CuO:hematite mass ratio of 10:90, 20:80, 30:70 and 40:60. The
particles were manufactured by mechanical mixing method in
Southeast University. Hematite (Nanjing Steel Manufacturing
Company, <0.05 mm) and CuO (Jiangsu Teho Metal Industrial Com-

0.2–0.3 mm. After this, the samples were calcined at 980 C for

De-watered sewage sludge supplied by Nanjing wastewater

2.3. Experimental setup
The combustion performance of sewage sludge with bimetallic
Cu–Fe oxygen carrier was examined in a batch ﬂuidized-bed
reactor system. The reactor was made of stainless steel and the
schematic view of the reactor was shown in Fig. 3. The same
system has been used in earlier studies for other kinds of oxygen
carrier materials [5]. This system is not a circulating ﬂuidized
bed, but instead emulates circulation by exposing the oxygen car-
riers alternatingly to oxidation with O2/Ar and a reduction by fuel.
The system is ﬂushed in between cycles by inert gas ﬂow (Ar). The
reactor is 1340 mm long and 32 mm in diameter. The oxygen car-
rier particles were placed on a porous plate which is located
450 mm from the bottom of the reactor. The reactor temperature
was monitored by two K-type thermocouples, one between the
tube and the heater, and the other inside the tube 10 mm above
the porous plate. The gas was injected from the bottom of the reac-
tor. The ﬂue gas leaving from the top of the reactor was cooled and
then to sampled by gas bags. CO2, CO, O2 and CH4 concentration is
analyzed by a NGA2000 type gas analyzer. The concentration of NO
is analyzed by an ECOM-J2KN analyzer.
The gas ﬂow in the ﬂuidized bed reactor was 3 L/min (STP),
which corresponded to a gas velocity of 0.27 m/s at 900 C and
0.28 m/s at 950 C, respectively. The minimum ﬂuidization gas
velocity Umf of the oxygen carrier was around 0.068 m/s at 900 C
and 0.066 m/s at 950 C. A sample of 50 g oxygen carrier particles
was placed on the porous plate and heated to the desired
temperature in a gas mixture consisting of 5% O2 and 95% Ar. As
the desired temperature reached and kept stable, the particles
were ﬂuidized by Ar. Subsequently, 1 g of sewage sludge was
introduced to the reactor and the ﬂuidization gas was switched
to either 100% Ar or 30% H2O in Ar for reactivity testes with sewage
sludge.
The oxidation and the reduction periods were separated by an
inert period with Ar for 5 min. The temperatures during reducing,
inert and oxidizing period were identical. Additionally, the reaction

3. Results and conclusion
3.1. Sewage sludge combustion with bimetallic Cu–Fe oxides
The combustion performance of sewage sludge is a crucial fac-
tor to determine the activity of bimetallic Cu–Fe oxygen carriers. A
wide range of process parameters was examined in CLC of sewage
sludge experiments.
Fig. 4 shows the carbon conversion efﬁciency of sewage sludge
as a function of the oxygen carrier composition at two different
reactor temperatures. The ﬂuidizing gases are 30 vol.% steam with
Ar as balance gas. For all oxygen carriers, it can be said that
increased reactor temperature resulted in increased carbon con-
version efﬁciency of sewage sludge. The carbon conversion efﬁ-
ciency reached almost 90% at 950 C for the oxygen carrier with
a CuO content higher than 30%. This could be expected since the
oxygen carrier reduction reactivity was higher at higher tempera-
tures, but also because sewage sludge gasiﬁcation reactions pro-
ceed faster at higher temperatures [25]. In Fig. 4, it is worth
noting that the addition of CuO could signiﬁcantly improve the
sludge conversion. The carbon conversion for the pure hematite
was only 81.73%. This value for Cu40Fe60 could reach 86.83% at
900 C and 91.07% at 950 C, respectively. This can be also proved
that Cu-based oxygen carrier has a higher reactivity towards CO
and H2, compared to the Fe-based counterparts [26]. At the lower
reaction temperature (900 C), the carbon conversion efﬁciency
increased slightly when the CuO ratio increased from 10% to 20%;
but the carbon conversion efﬁciency increased drastically with
the CuO ratio rise from 20% to 40%. At higher reaction temperature
(950 C), the carbon conversion is in a marginal increase when the
CuO content higher than 30%.
Fig. 5 shows the dry gas concentrations for a reduction period as
a function of CuO mass fraction with sewage sludge as fuel at two
different reactor temperatures. The inlet gas is 30% H2O in Ar. It can
be noted that the CO2 concentration is higher at 950 C compared
to the one at 900 C for all of the investigated materials. This is
likely due to the higher reactivity of oxygen carrier at higher tem-
perature. For either temperatures, the oxygen carrier with a higher
CuO content exhibit a higher CO2 concentration. As the CuO mass
ratio increased from 0% to 40%, the CO2 concentration increased
from 63.39% to 75.06% at 900 C and from 69.12% to 79.17% at
950 C, respectively. One explanation for the higher CO2 conversion
with the rise of CuO content is the contribution by release of oxy-
gen from CuO, meaning that some of the fuel is converted through
chemical looping with oxygen uncoupling (CLOU) effect. It is a
challenge to determine whether the conversion process is achieved
via CLC or CLOU. So, the bimetallic Cu–Fe oxides were tested under
Ar to investigate the sludge CLOU performance.

decomposition of the investigated bimetallic Cu–Fe oxides at
950 C. The initial slight weight increase results from the oxidation
of oxygen carrier. The weight increase did not affect the decompo-

3.2. NO emission during CLC of sewage sludge
A salient problem of sludge combustion is its high nitrogen con-
tent, which may engender high NO emission. The effect of reduc-
tion temperature on the formation and distribution of nitrogen
compounds was discussed. The ﬂuidizing gas is 30 vol.% steam
with Ar as balance gas.
Fig. 8(a) and (b) shows the progress of NO concentration during
CLC of sewage sludge at 900 C and 950 C, respectively. At such
temperature levels, the formations of thermic NO and prompt NO
are negligible. NO formation mainly results from the oxidation of
the nitrogen present in sewage sludge. In the reduction period,
N2 and NO were the only nitrogenous species detected at all the
temperatures tested. All of the investigated oxygen carriers have
similar NO emission proﬁles. Increasing reduction temperature
has a noticeable decrease on the NO concentration. It can be also
observed in Fig. 8(a) and (b) that the CuO/hematite mass ratio is
very sensitive to NO emission. In both tested temperatures, the
NO emission is higher at a lower CuO/hematite mass ratio.
Fig. 9 presents the conversion ratio of fuel-N to NO as a function
of CuO/hematite mass ratios at two different reaction tempera-
tures. It can be seen that the increased of reduction temperature
resulted in decreased conversion ratio of fuel-N to NO. For exam-
ple, when Cu30Fe70 was used as oxygen carrier, the process emit-
ted 0.31% NO at 900 C and with a temperature increase to 950 C
the NO concentration was 0.26%. It is also interesting to note that,
for both reaction temperatures, the conversion ratio of fuel-N to
NO decreased with increasing CuO/hematite mass ratio. When
the Cu/hematite mass ratio reached 40%, the conversion ratio of
fuel-N to NO was only 0.18% at 950 C. Compared to NO formation
in CLOU of lignite using Cu50Fe/MgAl [27], NO concentration in
reduction process was much lower during sewage sludge combus-
tion by CLC. Additionally, these results was appreciably higher
compared to NO emission in CLC of bituminous coal or anthracite
with hematite as oxygen carrier [12].
During sewage sludge devolatilization process, part of fuel
nitrogen is released with volatiles, whereas the remaining part is
retained in char [28]. The split of nitrogen between volatiles and
the remaining char is roughly in proportion to the volatiles in sew-
age sludge [29]. As shown in Table 2, the weight ratio of volatile to
ﬁxed carbon corresponded to 9.36, indicating that the nitrogen in
volatiles accounted for a major portion in NO emission. The evolu-
tion of NO in Fig. 8 can also prove that the volatile conversion path
determines the NO emission.
Because sewage sludge is mainly composed of microbes, the
major part of nitrogen in sewage sludge is in the form of amino
(–NH2) and peptide bond (–(CO)–(NH)–) [30,31]. In sewage sludge
combustion process, volatile-N species are mainly released as light
gas-N [29]. The intermediate NH3 is the predominant N-containing
gas present in the light gas-N in sludge combustion process
[30,32]. In CLC process, H2O provides the effective source of H
and OH radicals, which would react with NH3 to form NO.
In NH3 oxidation process, NH3 can react with H and OH radicals
to form NH2, and subsequently NH and N radicals. The NH2, NH and
N radicals are subsequently further oxidized to NO [30,33]. The NO
formation mechanism described above can be shown as (R1).

Another aspect affecting the nitrogen chemistry in CLC process
is the oxygen carrier used. Fe2O3 demonstrates positive effect for
NO decomposition [35]. Thus, the presence of important quantities
of Fe2O3 is favorable for NO reduction.
Hence, NO generation is the competition between the formation
through NH3 oxidation and the destruction channels of NO to N2 in
CLC process. The NO emission in CLC of sewage sludge process
below the legal limit. It can be speculated that CLC is an appropri-
ate technology for conversion sewage sludge to energy with simul-
taneous the reduction of NO emission.

EDX analyses of the studied materials were conducted in order

The crystalline phase composition of the fresh and used oxygen

4. Conclusions
The combustion performance and the fate of nitrogen were
evaluated in CLC of sewage sludge process in a batch ﬂuidized
bed reactor using bimetallic Cu–Fe oxygen carrier. The results indi-
cate that the mixture of CuO and hematite as oxygen carrier signif-
icantly improved carbon conversion and CO2 conversion in CLC of
sewage sludge process. More strikingly, both carbon conversion
and CO2 concentration increased with CuO mass ratio. Although
there are some sludge ash deposited on the surface of oxygen car-
rier, the effect of ash on the characteristic of oxygen carrier can be
ignored. The XRD data shows that CuFe2O4 is formed during oxy-
gen carrier manufactured and the reduced phase is CuFeO2 after
reaction with sewage sludge. The formation of CuFe2O4 could
improve both the reactivity of Fe2O3 species through synergetic
effect and the physical stability of CuO during redox reactions. As
regards the fate of nitrogen in sewage sludge, N2 and NO were
the main nitrogenous species observed in the reduction period.
NO concentration was below 0.4% of the total nitrogen fed with
sewage sludge. Higher the mass ratio of CuO/hematite appeared
to decrease the conversion ratio of fuel-N to NO. NO formation is
the competition between the formation through NH3 oxidation
and the destruction channels of NO to N2 in CLC process. Therefore,
mixing hematite with a more reactive CuO can improve combus-
tion performance and decrease NO emission as compared to the
case with only hematite as oxygen carrier.
