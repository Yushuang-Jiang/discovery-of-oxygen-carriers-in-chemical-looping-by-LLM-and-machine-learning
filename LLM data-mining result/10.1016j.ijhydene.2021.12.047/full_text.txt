a b s t r a c t
Thermochemical water splitting using perovskite oxides as redox materials is one of the
important way to use solar energy to produce green hydrogen. Thus, it is hence important
to discover new materials that can be used for this purpose. In this regard, we focused on
Al-substituted La0.4Sr0.6Mn1-xAlxO3 (x ¼ 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6) perovskite oxides, namely as
La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.6Al0.4 (LSMA4664), La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.5Al0.5 (LSMA4655), and La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.4Al0.6
(LSMA4646) which have been successfully synthesized. Herein, synthesized LSMA4664,
LSMA4655, and LSMA4646 were subjected to three consecutive thermochemical cycles in
order to determine their oxygen capacity, hydrogen capacity, re-oxidation capability and
structural stability following three cycles. Thermochemical cycles were carried out at
1400 C for reduction and 800 C for the oxidation reaction. LSMA4646 exhibited the highest
O2 production capacity with 275 mmol/g among the other perovskites employed in the
study. Moreover, LSMA4646 has also the highest H2 production, 144 mmol/g, with 90% of re-
oxidation capability by the end of three thermochemical water splitting cycles. On the
other hand, LSMA4664 has the lowest H2 production and only kept approximately one-
third of its hydrogen production capacity by the end of cycles. Thus, the current study
provides insight that the increase in the Al-substitution enhances both oxygen and
hydrogen production capacity. Besides, increasing the Al amount increases the structural
stability during the redox reactions, the re-oxidation capability was also increased from
38% to 89% after thermochemical cycles.

Introduction
Nowadays, the increase in electrical devices results in more
energy consumption. The use of fossil fuels is inevitably
increasing
to
meet
this
energy
demand.
This
causes
increasing greenhouse gas emissions that released to nature,
and thus, results in environmental pollution and climate
change. In addition, it is conjectured that current fossil fuel
reserves existing in nature can preserve as a time period for 40
years, 60 years, and 156 years for petroleum, natural gas, and
coal, respectively [1,2]. Consequently, the concern of fossil
fuels depletion has led researchers to focus on alternative
energy sources in the last decades [3]. This reveals once again
the importance of the need for renewable energy sources and
hydrogen appears to be a valid solution [4].
Hydrogen can be considered a renewable and sustainable
energy solution due to its high energy density per mass (120 MJ/
kg) and also as clean energy [5,6]. Especially, when it is used as
fuel in fuel cells, only water and heat are released as a by-
product [7e10]. Apart from being an energy source candidate,
hydrogen is currently needed in many different industries such
as crude oil reﬁning, chemical production (ammonia, etc.),
aviation, metal reﬁning, food processing, and electronics
manufacturing applications [11e17]. Moreover, independently
of all these usages, hydrogen has shown that it has the po-
tential to be used to mitigate the CO2 emission resulting from
iron and steel production [18]. Nevertheless, the inability to
produce pure H2, e.g. steam reforming process, formation of
CO2, O2, H2O, H2S ve NOx as a by the product does not ﬁt the
clean, low-cost and sustainable perspective of hydrogen. For
this reason, signiﬁcant researches have been devoted to pro-
ducing hydrogen with a cost-effective and clean route [19].
Many methods can be used to obtain hydrogen from
renewable resources such as biologically routes (including
fermentation and photosynthesis), electrolytic methods (e.g.
electrolysis of water), and also solar energy-based production
methods (thermolysis, thermochemical cycles, reforming,
cracking,
gasiﬁcation)
[20e23].
Among
the
solar-based
hydrogen production methods, thermochemical water split-
ting have been considered as a long-term solution for green
hydrogen production. The limiting drawback for the wide-
spread usage of this method is the development of low
reduction temperature materials is yet to be sufﬁcient.
Although, some oxides which are reduced at lower tempera-
ture e.g. Mn3O4, cannot be taken place in two-step thermo-
chemical water splitting (TWS) redox reactions due to a too
high regeneration temperature, still TWS using the metal ox-
ides as redox material has attracted remarkable attention due
to its reversibility of the method, its reasonable H2 production
levels among the other hydrogen production methods [23e26].
As shown in Fig. 1, TWS cycles begin with a reduction re-
action. Here, metal oxide (MOx) is thermally reduced and be-
comes an oxygen deﬁcient phase, MOx-d. This reduction
reaction yields O2 formation at a temperature typically lower
than 2000 C, Eq. (1). When the oxygen deﬁcient phase is
exposed to H2O vapor, it oxidizes to its original state, i.e. MOx
and produces H2 through water splitting at slightly lower
temperatures between 500 and 1000 C Eq. (2). The production
of O2 and H2 at different steps makes the TWS ideal, sus-
tainable, and promising to produce pure H2 [27e29].

Redox materials to be used in TWS play critical roles in terms
of solar-to-fuel efﬁciency. Therefore, an ideal redox material
should demonstrate high oxygen exchange capacity, fast reac-
tion kinetics, high structural and thermal stability for efﬁcient
H2 production. Up to now, a variety of metal oxides has been
proposed as active material that can be categorized as stoi-
chiometric and non-stoichiometric [30,31]. Although stoichio-
metric metal oxide such as ZnO [32,33], SnO2 [34], In2O3 [35], and
Fe3O4 [33,36,37] showed high oxygen exchange capacity, they
suffer from recombination, phase transformation, sintering,
and sluggish reaction kinetics. Moreover, according to Steinfeld
et al., the unit cost of 5 $/kg H2 from stoichiometric metal oxides
is not competitive compared to other hydrogen production
methods [38]. Thus, these problems make the stoichiometric
oxides less preferable. In contrast, non-stoichiometric metal
oxides such as CeO2 exhibits higher cycling stability and high
reaction kinetics. Even though CeO2 has been regarded as a
benchmark material for TWS, its high-temperature require-
ment (>1500 C) for reduction reaction increases the interest in
looking for alternative metal oxides [23,39e41].
Currently,
perovskite-type
metal
oxides
have
been
demonstrated as a promising alternative to CeO2 owing to their
unique features such as non-stoichiometric oxygen vacancy
formation, high structural stability, ﬂexible doping/alloying,
composition variety, and low-cost. An ideal perovskite struc-
ture with a chemical formula of ABO3 present a cubic structure
[31,42]. Different elements can occupy the A-site such as lan-
thanides (e.g. La, Pr, and Gd) or alkaline earth metals (e.g. Ba
and Sr) with 12-fold coordination. On the other hand, B-site is
generally composed of transition metals (e.g. Mn, Co, Ni, Cr,
and Fe) or other metals such as Al and Ga in 6-fold coordination
with the surrounding oxygen anion [43]. It is stated that the
mechanism of redox cycle activity that improves the water
splitting behavior of the perovskite material, as it allows the
formation of redox-active pairs that activate the water splitting
reaction, is developed by substituting the trivalent cations (e.g.
La3þ) with the divalent (e.g. Sr2þ) cations [48]. That is why
numerous perovskite oxides families have been studied for
thermochemical water splitting cycles. LaMnO3 perovskite
family has received great interest due to its redox cycles ac-
tivity among the other perovskite oxides. A site substitution of
LaMnO3 with different divalent elements such as Ca [44,45], Sr
[46,47] has revealed promising water splitting results. It has
been observed that Sr-substituted LaMnO3 perovskite exhibi-
ted an increased oxygen production capacity. The study of
Scheffe et al. showed that the presence of Sr in the structure
has increased the reduction capacity at a lower temperature
compared to that state-of-art CeO2 [49,50]. Moreover, it was
reported the increase of Sr substitution from 0.1 to 0.4
enhanced the O2 production capacity of LaxSr1xMnO3 from
24 mmol/g O2 to 215 mmol/g. That is approximately ten times
higher O2 production for Sr ¼ 0.4 when it is compared to
Sr ¼ 0.1. In addition, it was also observed that Sr substitution in

Demont and Abanades showed that re-oxidation capability

has a crucial role in the re-oxidation efﬁciency. Simultaneous
substitution of B-site cation is a widely used approach to
improve the re-oxidation efﬁciency, structural stability, cycling
kinetics. It was primarily focused on the substitution of the Mn,
which is the B-site cation, with elements such as Al, Ga, Mg, Fe,
Sc, and Co [52e56]. In this regard, Luciani et al. exhibited that
the reduction temperatures can be reduced down to 1350 C.
The reduction capacity can also be increased by Fe substitution
in La1-xSrxMnO3 perovskite oxide. O2 production capacity has
improved by Fe substitution (0.2e0.8) from 286 mmol/g to
333 mmol/g in the La0.6Sr0.4Mn1-xFexO3 at 1350 C [57]. Demont
et al. reported that Mg2þ substitution in La1-xSrxMnO3 prevents
sintering and increases thermal stability [58]. In another study,
Dey et al. showed that Ga3þ and Sc3þ substitution has a
remarkable effect on having higher O2 production capacity and
cycling performance in La1-xSrxMnO3 perovskite system for the
two-step water splitting cycles [56]. On the other hand,
aluminum is one of the feasible B-site substitution elements
since its partial substitution has an impact on the structural
stability for La1-xSrxMnO3 perovskite oxides. This is the result
of the decrease in the crystal lattice due to the ionic radius
difference between Mn and Al cations leading to strong atomic
interactions. Thus, Al can provide better structural stability by
lowering the reduction temperature and reducing the possi-
bility of sintering problems [59].
In this study, several perovskite-type oxides based on
La0.4Sr0.6Mn1-xAlxO3 were investigated to reveal the effect of Al
substitution as a B-site dopant. The thermochemical water
splitting has been performed with multiple cycles in a series of
oxides, i.e. La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.6Al0.4 (LSMA4664), La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.5Al0.5
(LSMA4655), and La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.4Al0.6 (LSMA4646). The effect of
Al was evaluated according to oxygen production, hydrogen
production, re-oxidation efﬁciency, and the structural stabil-
ity of the compositions in question.
Experimental
Chemicals
La0.4Sr0.6Mn1-xAlx (LSMA) perovskite oxides were synthesized
using La(NO3)3$6H2O (99.99%, Alfa Aesar), Sr(NO3)2 (99%,
Carlo Erba), Mn(NO3)2$4H2O (98.5%, Merck) Al(NO3)3$9H2O
(99%, Carlo Erba), citric acid anhydrous (99.5%, Carlo Erba)
and ammonium solution (25%) precursors without any further
puriﬁcation.
Perovskite synthesis
LSMA4664, LSMA4655, and LSMA4646 were synthesized via
the Pechini Method [60]. The desired stoichiometric amount of
nitrates
precursors
including,
La(NO3)3$6H2O,
Sr(NO3)2,
Mn(NO3)2$4H2O, Al(NO3)3$9H2O were dissolved in deionized
water. Citric acid was added to the solution as a chelating
agent. Obtained solution heated to 70 C with continuous
stirring at 300 rpm on the magnetic stirrer. The pH of the so-
lution was adjusted to 8 by adding ammonium solution (25%)
dropwise and held at 70
C and 300 rpm until a gel-like
structure was obtained. The gel-like structure was kept at
70 C for ~24 h to remove the water completely in the drying
oven. The drying process was completed by keeping them at
250 C for 2 h remove any remaining organics and/or nitrates.
Subsequently, synthesized powders were calcined at 900 C
for 6 h to obtain a crystalline perovskite oxide structure.

with a step size of 0.02 and a scanning rate of 2/min. The

Thermochemical water splitting test
Thermochemical water splitting cycles were performed to
investigate the oxygen and hydrogen capacity, re-oxidation
capability, and structural stability of the LSMA4664, LSMA4655,
and LSMA4646 perovskite oxides. For that purpose, TWS cycles
were carried out in a high temperature horizontal tubular
furnace, coupled with a gas analyzer, Fig. 2. 0.5 g of powders
producedwastransferredintoanaluminacrucibleandplacedin
the heat zone of the horizontal furnace with an inner diameter
of 50 mm. High purity argon (Ar) gas (~99.999%) was used as a
carrier gas duringthe cycles and the ﬂow rate of theArwasset to
100 sccm by a mass ﬂow controller integrated into the system.
LSMA powders were subjected to the thermal reduction
(TR) step by heating the furnace up to 1400 C at a rate of 5 C/
min. The powders were held at that temperature for 2 h under
continuous Ar ﬂow. To perform the water splitting (WS) step,
the temperature was decreased to 800 C with a cooling rate of
5 C/min. Water was then fed to a reactor by a syringe pump
(New Era NE-300) with a rate of 150 ml/min. The temperature of
the reactor was set to 150 C to evaporate the water fed to the
system. H2 produced as a result of the reaction was trans-
ported to the water trap to be puriﬁed from water vapor before
reaching the analyzer. The gas mixture, which was free from
water vapor, was transferred to the gas analyzer for the
determination of H2 production. The whole H2O splitting
process was kept for 2 h to complete the re-oxidation process.
During the TR and WS steps, O2 and H2 produced were
determined by a mass spectrometer (HIDEN QGA) and a gas
chromatograph (Shimadzu Tracera).
Results and discussion
Structural characterization
Fig. 3 a shows XRD patterns of LSMA4664, LSMA4655, and
LSMA4646 perovskite oxides synthesized. It was observed that
the main peaks observed in all LSMA belong to characteristics
of cubic perovskite structure with a space group of Pm3m
(COD-1542145). Rietveld reﬁnements of the LSMA perovskites,
Fig. 3b-d, showed that SrCO3 existed as an impurity phase
with a ratio of 1%e2% wt.
The particle morphology was similar for all as-synthesized
and calcined perovskite oxides which can be seen in Fig. 4. All
LSMA samples have an equiaxed and ﬁne grain structure with
an average primary particle size of <100 nm. Especially, it is
seen that LSMA4655 and LSMA4646 have relatively smaller
particles causing the formation of agglomerates. The ﬁndings
in SEM analyses were conﬁrmed by BET results. The relatively
higher surface area was obtained which were 33.52 m2/g,
30.13 m2/g, 28.52 m2/g for LSMA4664, LSMA4655, LSMA4646,
respectively. The results showed that an increase in the
amount of Al substitution in the perovskite oxides makes the
primary particle size smaller yielding the formation of ag-
glomerates. Thus, it causes a slight decrease in the surface
area of oxides.
XPS was performed to determine the chemical states of
elements in LSMA4664, LSMA4655, and LSMA4646 perovskite
oxides. Here, carbon peak, C1s, at 284.8 eV was used as a
reference to diagnose all other peaks belonging to relative
elements. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy spectrum of

multiplet splitting, DE, can be used as an indicator to conﬁrm
the chemical state of La3þ in the LSMA4664, LSMA4655, and
LSMA4646 compositions, which is calculated as DE is around
4 eV [61]. Strontium has two chemical states at around 131 eV,

Thermochemical water splitting tests
A consecutive three thermochemical water splitting cycles
were performed for each LSMA powder to investigate their
oxygen
and
hydrogen
production
capacity,
re-oxidation
capability, and structural stability. The reduction reaction
temperature was employed at 1400 C for 2 h while water
splitting reaction was carried out at 800 C for another 2 h. The
re-oxidation capability of perovskite oxides was calculated by
considering how much hydrogen production amount in the
last cycle was preserved compared to the ﬁrst cycle. A sum-
mary of the results showing O2 and H2 production for each
cycle is given in Table 1.
Fig. 6 a. shows the O2 production capacities of LSMA pe-
rovskites in 1st cycle. During the 1st cycle, O2 production was
achieved in the order of LSMA4646>LSMA4664>LSMA4655
from 275 to 221 mmol/g. LSMA perovskites with various Al
content had a signiﬁcant reduction capacity at the end of the
1st cycle when their O2 production capacity was compared.
Herein, with increasing Al amount in the structure from 0.4 to
0.5, it was observed that oxygen production decreased from
246 mmol/g to 221 mmol/g. In contrary to the expectations, a
further increase in the amount of Al from 0.5 to 0.6 was
resulted
in
increasing
the
oxygen
production
capacity.
LSMA4646 exhibited the highest oxygen production, 275 mmol/
g, among the other LSMA perovskite oxides. LSMA4646 has
also the highest H2 production, 162 mmol/g, followed by
LSMA4655, and then LSMA4664, Fig. 6 b. Unlike the oxygen
release amount, the result for the H2 production amount was
quite similar and low for the perovskite oxides. This result
indicates that the reversible redox efﬁciencies of the perov-
skite oxides are different. In addition to that, an increase in
the Al substitution slightly enhances the hydrogen production
capacity.
During the 2nd cycle, it was observed that the non-
stoichiometric oxygen production capacity of the studied
perovskite oxides decreased. The effect of the decrease in the
O2 production was clearly observed in the hydrogen produc-
tion capacities of the perovskite oxides. During the 2nd cycle,
H2 production capacities were in the order of LSMA4646>LS-
MA4655>LSMA4664. The same behavior was obtained during
the 2nd cycle for hydrogen production as in the case of the 1st
cycle. During the 3rd cycle, all LSMA perovskite oxides exam-
ined lower O2 production except LSMA4655. In contrary to the

Conclusion
Al-doped La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.6Al0.4 (LSMA4664), La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.5Al0.5
(LSMA4655), and La0.4Sr0.6Mn0.4Al0.6 (LSMA4646) perovskite
oxides were synthesized via the Pechini method to be able to
use as redox materials in thermochemical water splitting
cycles. Cubic crystal structure was predominantly obtained
following the calcination at 900 C for 6 h. It was observed
that increasing Al-doping in the structure slightly reduced
the particle size obtained in perovskite oxides. All composi-
tions have similar surface morphology with the average
particle size of <100 nm after calcination. O2 capacity of
LSMA4646 was determined as 275 mmol/g which was the
highest
capacity
obtained
as
compared
to
others.
Re-
oxidation capability after the 3rd cycle (%) was 38, 73, and
89 for LSMA4664, LSMA4655, and LSMA4646, respectively.
While LSMA4646 highly preserved its hydrogen production
capacity, LSMA4664 only kept approximately one-third of its
hydrogen production capacity by the end of three consecu-
tive cycles. On the other hand, the ﬁndings showed that there
were phase transformations in LSMA oxides after consecu-
tive three thermochemical water splitting cycles. Small
amount of orthorhombic and oxygen deﬁcient phase, namely
as ABO3-d were observed together with dominant cubic
phase. The formation of this oxygen-deﬁcient phase can be
strongly correlated with having an incomplete reversible
oxygen release-uptake reaction during cycling. The results
showed that not only the hydrogen and oxygen capacity but
also the re-oxidation capacity increase by the increasing of
Al-dopant in LSMA oxides. Thus, the current study provides
insight that the increase in the Al-dopant enhances both
oxygen and hydrogen production capacity, as well as the re-
oxidation capability during cycling although there is a need
for further investigation under more consecutive cycles.
