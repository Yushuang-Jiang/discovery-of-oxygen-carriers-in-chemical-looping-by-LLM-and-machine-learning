Enhanced Thermochemical Water Splitting through
Formation of Oxygen Vacancy in La0.6Sr0.4BO3@d (B=Cr, Mn,
Fe, Co, and Ni) Perovskites
Lulu Wang,[a] Mohammad Al-Mamun,*[a] Yu Lin Zhong,*[a] Porun Liu,[a] Yun Wang,[a]
Hua Gui Yang,[b] and Huijun Zhao*[a]
Oxygen vacancies in catalyst systems play a crucial role in ma-
nipulating pivotal redox properties that are strongly depen-
dent on the composition of the material. Herein, for the first
time, experimental evidence of a linear correlation between
the extent of oxygen vacancy formation in the La0.6Sr0.4BO3
(B=Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni) perovskite series and H2 generation
in two-step thermochemical water splitting is reported, with
detailed materials characterization by means of thermogravi-
metric analysis, XRD, SEM, TEM, and energy-dispersive X-ray
spectroscopy.
Noteworthy
O2
(718 mmolg@1)
and
H2
(514 mmolg@1) production was achieved by the La0.6Sr0.4CoO3
perovskite in the thermochemical water-splitting process con-
ducted between 1300 and 9008C.
Oxygen deficiencies in metal oxides result in the formation of
oxygen vacancies (VO), which can greatly affect the behavior of
materials, such as ionic and electronic conductivity, optical
properties, and catalytic activity.[1,2] Recently, redox catalysts
operating through the VO mechanism have attracted significant
attention for a solar-driven, two-step, thermochemical water-
splitting reaction, which is one of the most efficient routes to
convert concentrated solar power into clean and renewable
chemical fuels, namely, H2. Unlike traditional stoichiometric
redox pairs, such as ZnO/Zn,[3,4] Fe3O4/FeO,[5,6] and CeO2/
Ce2O3,[7] which
have distinct structural compositions and
phases in the reduced and oxidized states, off-stoichiometric
oxygen catalysts conduct their thermochemical catalytic activi-
ties through the formation of VO within the crystal structure.
This permits maintenance of a constant phase during the ther-
mochemical water-splitting process, which leads to simple op-
erational requirements and rapid reaction kinetics. In a VO
mechanism based, two-step, thermochemical water-splitting
process, oxygen vacancies in a metal oxide (MOx) are first
formed through a high-temperature endothermic reduction re-
action [Eq. (1)], which leads to an off-stoichiometric oxygen
state (MOx@d). Subsequently, MOx@d can be recovered to the
original MOx through an exothermal oxidation reaction with
water [Eq. (2)], which produces hydrogen. Therefore, the over-
all thermochemical performance strongly depends on the
extent of VO formation (d) and the redox properties in the cata-
lysts, which determine the theoretical solar fuel production ca-
pacity.

As a classical type of off-stoichiometric oxygen catalyst, the
La1@xSrxBO3 (LSB; B=Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni) perovskite series have
been widely exploited in various catalytic fields, for example,
fuel cells,[8–10] electrocatalysis,[11–15] and chemical sensors[16–18],
owing to their structural tunability, rapid ion mobility, and re-
markable VO formation ability. Recently, some LSB perovskite
oxides with a Sr doping content of 0.4, such as La0.6Sr0.4MnO3
[19]
and La0.6Sr0.4FeO3,[20] were proposed as promising alternative
catalysts for thermochemical water-splitting applications, with
impressive VO formation and solar H2 production performances
at low temperature. As such, the thermochemical catalytic
properties of LSB-type catalysts are strongly related to their
material compositions. DFT calculation results reported by
O’Hayre et al. revealed that the LSB perovskite series displayed
different VO formation energies with the incorporation of vari-
ous transition-metal ions into the B site, which differed in the
extent of VO formation under the same conditions.[21] To date,
however, the experimental validation of the VO formation per-
formances of LSB perovskites in two-step thermochemical
water-splitting cycles can rarely be found. Therefore, to further
develop advanced redox catalysts, it is important to draw an
experimental relationship between VO formation during ther-
mal reduction and H2 production in the subsequent water-
splitting step.
Herein, we systematically investigated the VO formation be-
havior of the LSB perovskite series with different transition-

LSCo, and LSNi, respectively) introduced into the B site for the

with reported theoretical calculations,[22] the experimentally
observed VO formation performances of the LSB perovskite
series produced a series converse to that of the required VO

LSFe>LSCr>LSMn. More importantly, the observed VO forma-

the preliminary assessment of the catalyst activity for water

yields followed the same trend as that of the extent of VO for-
mation in the perovskites.
To perform the two-step thermochemical water-splitting in-
vestigation, a modified Pechini method was employed to pre-
pare the required LSB perovskite series (details can be found in
the Supporting Information).[23,24] As shown in Figure 1a, the
XRD pattern of the LSB perovskites (LSCo is presented here as
a representative sample; the other samples are shown in Fig-
ure S1 in the Supporting Information) represent a typical per-
ovskite crystal structure with no impure phases. The surface
morphology of La0.6Sr0.4CoO3 was observed by SEM and a hier-
archical porous structure composed of small particles was ob-
served (Figure 1b). From the inset in Figure 1b, we can clearly
observe abundant pores that are formed by the interconnect-
ed nanosized particles (<100 nm). Similar surface morpholo-
gies were also observed in the other LSB perovskites (Figure S2
in the Supporting Information). Owing to the similar structural
morphologies of all perovskites prepared in this study, the sole
impact of chemical composition on the overall water-splitting
performances could be evaluated and compared.
The interconnected porous structure and particle sizes of
LSCo were further confirmed by means of TEM. As shown in
Figure 1c, the as-synthesized perovskite showed high crystal-
linity, with particle sizes ranging from 50 to 80 nm. The HRTEM
result (Figure 1c, inset) showed a lattice fringe distance of
0.27 nm, which was consistent with the (110) plane, as evi-
denced from the peak observed in the XRD pattern (Figure 1a).
The elemental distribution of LSB perovskites was also mea-
sured by means of EDX mapping. As shown in Figure 1d, a ho-
mogeneous distribution of the corresponding metal cations
(La, Sr, and Co) in the LSCo sample was detected. Thus, based
on XRD, SEM, TEM, and EDX results, it is evident that the LSB
perovskite series with different transition-metal ions (Cr, Mn,
Fe, Co, and Ni) introduced into the B site had been successfully
synthesized.
Considering the critical role played by VO in the thermo-
chemical solar fuel production process, the VO formation and
oxygen evolution behaviors of the LSB perovskites were care-
fully analyzed by means of thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)
and in a temperature-programmed tubular furnace based
fixed-bed reactor.[25] TGA was first introduced to detect the VO
formation of LSB perovskites from the measured weight loss
during the high-temperature reduction step. As shown in Fig-
ure 2a, the weight of LSB perovskites started to decrease with
increasing temperature. The TGA curves revealed a rapid loss
once the temperature reached 13008C, and finally stabilized
after being held at 13008C for 1 h. LSMn and LSCr showed the
smallest weight losses, LSFe and LSNi were in the middle, and
the largest weight loss was observed for the LSCo sample. The
different weight-loss profiles in the TGA measurements indi-
cate different extents of VO (d) within the tested LSB perov-

After converting the weight-loss results into the correspond-
ing extents of VO formation in LSB perovskites, the following
trend of extents of VO formation was found: LSCo (0.33)>LSNi
(0.20)>LSFe (0.17)>LSCr (0.072)>LSMn (0.058; Figure 2b).
Hence, the experimental VO formation results displayed in this
study strongly concur with theoretical predictions.[21] According
to DFT calculations, the introduction of different B-site transi-
tion-metal ions (B=Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni) in LSB perovskites
led to various strengths of the B@O bond and thermal reduc-
tion enthalpy, which collectively determined the overall VO for-
mation energies. The perovskites of LSCo, LSNi, and LSFe had
significantly lower VO formation energetics than those of LSCr
and LSMn, which could greatly promote the extent of VO for-
mation under the same experimental conditions. From the
thermochemical catalysis perspective, the greater extent of VO
formation in redox catalysts results in higher amounts of solar
fuel production, which leads to a higher solar energy conver-
sion efficiency.[21,25,26]
To investigate the VO formation performances in different
perovskite compositions, the LSB perovskites were thermally
reduced in a temperature-programmed tubular furnace up to
13008C, with a slow heating ramp (108Cmin@1). Evolved O2
was instantaneously recorded by a mass spectrometer gas ana-
lyzer connected to the tubular furnace.[27–33] Figure 3a shows
the O2 evolution profiles of different LSB perovskites under the
same experimental conditions. LSB perovskites with Fe, Ni, or
Co on the B site had significantly increased O2 evolution per-
formances than those with Mn and Cr, owing to higher oxygen
formation energies.
This can be further demonstrated by analyzing the onset
temperature required to initiate oxygen evolution, which can
be clearly seen by enlarging the initial O2 evolution curves of
the La0.6Sr0.4BO3 perovskite series (Figure 3a, inset). LSB perov-
skites with lower VO formation energies start to release O2 at a
lower heating temperature, that is, the onset temperature of
LSCo (&9008C) is much lower than that required by LSMn
(&10208C). Furthermore, the final oxygen evolution amounts
of LSB perovskites, which are summarized in Figure 3b, exhibit
the same sequence as that of the aforementioned TGA results,
namely,
LSCo
(718 mmolg@1)>LSNi
(437 mmolg@1)>LSFe
(375 mmolg@1)>LSCr (166 mmolg@1)>LSMn (131 mmolg@1). No-
tably, the amount of oxygen released from each perovskite
sample in the fixed-bed reactor system is almost consistent
with the weight loss obtained in the TGA experiments. The
cross-matching of experimental results from both techniques
indicates that no additional gaseous species, apart from O2, are
emitted during the thermal reduction step. Based on the com-
bined TGA and oxygen evolution results, we can conclude that
the thermal reduction behavior of LSB perovskites is strongly
dependent on the transition-metal ions incorporated into the
B site, because of different VO formation energies. Thus, a
lower VO formation energy led to significantly increased
oxygen evolution and a higher extent of VO formation. More

To establish the relationship between the H2 production per-
formance and the extent of VO formation, water-splitting reac-
tions were performed once the temperature was decreased to
9008C by introducing a mixture of steam/Ar (v/v 40%) gas into
a homemade fixed-bed reactor system (Figure S3 in the Sup-
porting Information). The H2 production signal was immediate-
ly detected through water splitting by the off-stoichiometric
oxygen LSB perovskite series. As shown in Figure 4a, the H2
production profiles reached the maximum rate within approxi-
mately 10 min followed by a slow decrease. As expected, LSCo
showed the best H2 production; this H2 production trend is
presented in Figure 4b, and correlates well with the VO forma-
tion and O2 evolution trends in the thermal reduction step. We
further compared the extent of oxygen vacancy and H2 pro-
duction of our studied catalysts with those reported for other
thermochemical water-splitting perovskite catalysts, including
ceria[34] (see Table S1 in the Supporting Information). From the
results in Table S1 in the Supporting Information, there is a
clear linear relationship between the extent of oxygen vacan-
cies (d) and H2 generation, which is consistent with our find-
ings. Therefore, VO can be considered as an indicator in the
search for efficient perovskite-based thermochemical water-
splitting catalysts.
Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, the H2 production
performance of LSCo achieved in our study is superior to that
of all other perovskite-type redox catalysts reported to date
(Table 1). The cyclic stability of the La0.6Sr0.4CoO3 sample was
also assessed by measuring the O2 and H2 evolution responses
over five consecutive cycles. Stable O2 and H2 production (Fig-
ure S5 in the Supporting Information) was observed from the
second cycle onward, whereas a low reoxidation yield was wit-
nessed in the first cycle. Low recovery in the first cycle is possi-
bly related to structural deformation (Figure S6 in the Support-
ing Information) and chemical decomposition (Figure S7 in the
Supporting Information) to form CoO in the high-temperature
thermal reduction step. As indicated, stable O2 and H2 produc-
tion was recorded from the second cycle; this indicated a con-
stant extent of recovery. This observation is coherent with the
results obtained for other cobalt-based catalysts reported for
the thermochemical water-splitting reaction.[20,38,39] Further-
more, the thermochemical performances of the investigated
LSB perovskites could be further improved by optimising the
operating factors during the two-step thermochemical H2O
splitting process, such as the reduction and reoxidation tem-
peratures, dopant composition, and the water vapor percent-
age.
In summary, a series of La0.6Sr0.4BO3 perovskites doped with
various transition-metal ions (Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni) in the B
site were experimentally investigated, for the first time, and re-
sulted in different extents of VO formation. Higher extents of
VO formation and oxygen evolution were achieved by the LSB
perovskites with lower VO formation energies, in the order of
LSCo>LSNi>LSFe>LSCr>LSMn. More importantly, the subse-
quent water-splitting performances of the LSB perovskites
were shown to be dependent on the VO formation trends in
the thermal reduction step. Specifically, the maximum O2
(718 mmolg@1) and H2 production (514 mmolg@1) values ach-
ieved by LSCo are highly promising and superior to those of
previously reported perovskite-type redox catalysts tested for
two-step thermochemical water-splitting processes. In a broad-
er context, this initial screening of the extent of VO formation
in perovskite oxides can be a very useful indicator to identify
high-performance candidates for solar thermochemical fuel
production.
