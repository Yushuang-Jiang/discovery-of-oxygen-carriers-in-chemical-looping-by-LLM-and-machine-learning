A B S T R A C T
In this investigation, the redox reactivity of CoxFe3−xO4 materials was examined in multiple thermochemical
CO2 splitting cycles. Sol-gel method was applied for the synthesis of CoxFe3−xO4 materials and the prepared gels
of CoxFe3−xO4 materials were aged, dried, and further annealed up to 600 °C in air. The characterization results
indicate formation of phase pure CoxFe3−xO4 materials. The thermal reduction (at 1400 °C for 60 min in the
presence of inert Ar) and CO2 splitting (at 1000 °C for 30 min in the presence of CO2/Ar mixture) aptitude of each
CoxFe3−xO4 material was estimated by using a high temperature set-up consisting of a thermogravimetric
analyzer. From the TGA analysis, it was understood that the amounts of O2 released and CO produced by
CoFe2O4 was higher and stable as compared to the other Co-ferrite materials. CoFe2O4 was capable of producing
76.2 µmol of O2/g·cycle and 139 µmol of CO/g·cycle in ten thermochemical. The thermal reduction and CO2
splitting aptitude of the CoFe2O4 was spotted to be greater than the CeO2 material.
1. Introduction
Incessant emission of CO2 (mainly via combustion of fossil fuels)
and its accumulation in the earth’s atmosphere is deemed as one of the
main causes of global warming. To avoid these unfavorable penalties,
the translation of CO2 into treasured products is currently the prime
focus of a plentiful research teams [1,2]. The chemical transformation
of thermodynamically stable CO2 into CO, utilization of this CO to form
syngas, and conversion of the syngas into liquid fuels via Fisher Tropshe
technology is considered as one of the favorable processes for the
conversion of CO2 [3–6]. A metal oxide (MO) based thermocatalytic
conversion of H2O/CO2 into H2 and syngas is judged, by several re-
searchers, as one of the most efficient ways to convert CO2 into fuels.
This process runs as a two-step cycle, and it is more advantageous over
direct splitting of H2O/CO2 due to the lower reaction temperatures and
the elimination of the problem associated with the separation of H2/CO
and O2.
Several MOs, such as zinc oxide [6–10], tin oxide [11–14], iron
oxide [15–18], ceria and doped ceria [19–29], perovskites [4,30–38],
ferrites [39–49], and others [50–53] were utilized for the thermo-
chemical conversion of H2O/CO2. Especially, in case of ferrites, Co-
ferrite appears to be a remarkable option for the production of H2 via
thermochemical splitting of H2O. Kodama et al. [54] examined the
ZrO2-supported CoxFe3-xO4 for H2O splitting (WS) application and

Allendorf et al. [56] thermodynamically concluded that the Co-ferrite is
thermally more stable than Ni-ferrite and Zn-ferrite towards H2 pro-
duction via WS reaction. A spinel ferrite/hercynite based H2O splitting
cycle, by depositing the Co-ferrite on the Al2O3 substrates via the
atomic layer deposition method, was explored by Scheffe et al. [43] and
reported that this combination can be recycled for multiple thermal
reduction (TR) of WS steps (without any deactivation) at 1200 °C. By
using the same atomic layer deposition method, Scheffe et al. [17]
synthesized the thin films of Co-ferrite on the ZrO2 support and stated
that these thin films can produce elevated amounts of H2 in multiple WS
cycles. Muhich et al. [57] testified that the Hercynite cycle is proficient
enough to produce upper levels of H2 as compared to ceria at iso-
thermal operating conditions (1350 °C). Agrafiotis et al. [58] reported
that the combustion synthesized Co-ferrite has the potential to produce
higher and steady quantities of H2 as compared to the other ferrite
materials. By conducting the WS experiments in a stagnant flow reactor
(900 to 1450 °C), Scheffe et al. [40] stated that the Co-ferrite/YSZ
material follows a second order reaction kinetics with a diffusion-con-
trolled mechanism.

As per the literature, the WS ability of the Co-ferrite (CoF) was
significantly explored, however; its utilization towards thermocatalytic
conversion of CO2 was very limited [59–63]. To produce a high quality
syngas, in addition to the H2 production via WS, generation of CO via
CO2 splitting (CS) is also important. Furthermore, to explore the long-
term redox reactivity and thermal stability, a methodical estimation of
the H2O/CO2 splitting ability of the ferrites in multiple cycles is es-
sential. Therefore, in this study, the CoF materials were thermo-
chemically scrutinized towards CO2 splitting reactions by performing
multiple cycles. The CoF materials examined for the thermocatalytic
conversion of CO2 were prepared via sol-gel method.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Chemicals, synthesis approach and characterization
The Co and Fe nitrate precursors, ethanol as the solvent, and the
proton scavenger i.e., propylene oxide (PO) were procured from Sigma
Aldrich. Inert Ar (99.999%) and feed gas mixture (50% CO2 in Ar) were
purchased from Buzwair Scientific and Technical Gases supplier, Qatar.
The sol-gel approach utilized for the synthesis of CoF materials is al-
ready reported in detail in our previous study (for the production of Ni-
ferrite) [63]. The process flow diagram associated with the synthesis of
CoF materials is presented in Fig. 1. Following are the key steps allied
with the sol-gel synthesis of CoF materials:
• Dissolution of the metal precursors in ethanol
• Dropwise addition of the PO in the metal precursor – ethanol solu-
tion
• Attainment of CoF gel formation
• Aging of the CoF gel for 24 h
• Drying of the aged gel at 120 °C for 4 h
• Calcination of the dried CoF powder at 600 °C for 4 h

3. Results and discussion
3.1. PXRD and SEM analysis
The phase composition and the morphology of the CoF powders,
acquired after annealing at 600 °C, was determined via PXRD and SEM
analysis. The PXRD results of the calcined CoF, displayed in Fig. 3,
indicate following three key features that endorsed the formation of
CoF materials with a phase pure composition.
a) The PXRD patterns of all the CoF materials matches very well with
the reflections associated with a cubic structure of the spinel phase.
b) Absence of any peaks associated with any metal/metal oxide im-
purities such as Co, Fe, CoO, FeO, Fe3O4, and Fe2O3.
c) A shift in the PXRD peaks towards the lower 2θ angles and increase
in the peak intensity of the CoF as the atomic concentration of Co+2
was increased from 0.2 to 1.0.
In addition to the phase composition, by applying the Scherrer’s
equation [64], the average crystallite size of each sol-gel derived CoF
material was computed and detected to be in the range of 21–35 nm.
The results obtained via the SEM analysis further confirmed the num-
bers obtained via the Scherrer’s equation. The SEM image of each CoF
material synthesized in this study is presented in Fig. 4. A close in-
spection of each SEM image indicate formation of CoF nanoparticles
with average particles size in the range of 25 to 100 nm. Additional
confirmation of the formation of the ferrite nanoparticles was obtained
by TEM analysis and exemplified TEM image for the CoF10 material is
presented in Fig. 5.
The chemical composition of all the CoF materials were also de-
termined by performing the EDS analysis and the obtained results are
reported in Table 1. The results obtained via EDS analysis confirmed
formation of CoF materials.
3.2. Thermocatalytic splitting of CO2
As shown in Fig. 6, the sol-gel synthesized CoF materials were first
tested in three successive thermochemical cycles to identify their redox
reactivity towards TR and CS. The amounts of mass reduced and gained
during each TR and CS step was determined by using the TGA profiles
(presented in Fig. 6). This mass variation was further converted into the
nO2 and nCO by each CoF material via Eqs. (1) and (2). The values ob-
tained after computations are listed in Table 2. The nO2 by all CoF
materials in the 1st cycle was considerably higher than the nCO. It was
also observed that the nO2 was decreased and nCO was increased with
the rise in the Co+2 atomic concentration. In the 1st cycle, the highest
nO2 was observed in case of CoF2 (855.5 μmol/g) and the maximum CO
production was noticed for CoF10 (143.2 μmol/g).
In cycle 2, the nO2 and nCO by each CoF material was reduced
considerable as compared to the 1st thermochemical cycle. The CoF2,
CoF4, CoF5, CoF6, CoF8, and CoF10 materials lost their TR capacity (in
terms of nO2) by 98.7%, 96.7%, 97.0%, 91.1%, 86.3%, and 63.4%, re-
spectively in the 2nd cycle as compared to the 1st cycle. Likewise, the
nCO by CoF2, CoF4, CoF5, CoF6, CoF8, and CoF10 materials was also
diminished by 78.8%, 57.7%, 69.9%, 25.0%, 35.6%, and 1.8%, in-
dividually in cycle 2 in comparison to the cycle 1. In cycle 3, the TR
aptitude of all the CoF materials was further dropped but the magnitude
of decrease was less as compared to the reduction observed in the 2nd
cycle. For instance, the nO2 by CoF2, CoF4, CoF5, CoF6, CoF8, and
CoF10 materials was lessened in cycle 3 by 18.2%, 13.6%, 10.0%,
5.2%, 4.0%, and 11.3%, correspondingly as compared to the cycle 2.
Opposite to this, the CO production capacity of all the CoF materials in
the 3rd cycle was identical to that of the cycle 2.

4. Summary and conclusions
By employing the PO-assisted sol-gel method, CoxFe3−xO4 (CoF)
materials (where x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, and 1.0) were successfully
synthesized. The PXRD analysis indicate formation of phase pure CoF
materials and the SEM analysis confirms formation of CoF nanoparticles
(25–100 nm). The TR and CS capacity of each CoF material was as-
sessed by performing multiple thermochemical cycles in two sets i.e.,
set-1: three cycles and set-2: ten cycles. The results associated with the
first three cycles indicate that the CoF materials showed signs of sta-
bility in terms of nCO in the 2nd and 3rd cycle, however; a continuous
decline in the nO2 for all the CoF materials was recorded. Set-2 (ten
cycles) was performed in order to explore the long-term redox activity
and thermal stability of CoF materials. Among all the CoF materials
examined, only CoF8 and CoF10 indicate stable nO2 and nCO in ten
consecutive thermochemical cycles. In terms of numbers, CoF8 and
CoF10 were capable of releasing 37.5 µmol of O2/g·cycle and 72.7 µmol
of O2/g·cycle, individually. Likewise, the nCO by CoF8 and CoF10 was
