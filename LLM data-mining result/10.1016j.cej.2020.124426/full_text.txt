H I G H L I G H T S
• Zr doped LaCoO3 perovskite was ﬁrstly used for solar thermochemical fuel production.
• An excellent perovskite LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3 was obtained and produced exceptional high CO.
• Eﬀects of heating rate and redox temperatures on the thermochemical redox properties were studied.
• Kinetics of the isothermal reduction process was studied.

A B S T R A C T
Solar thermochemical CO production via CO2 splitting is an eﬀective route to achieve carbon cycle and reduce
carbon emissions. One of the most important issues for thermochemical fuel production technology is to ﬁnd a
material with high oxygen release capacity and fuel production as well as excellent thermal stability. Due to the
excellent oxygen releasing capacity and limited fuel production and durability of LaCoO3, the feasibility of
elemental doping for enhancing the redox capacity were studied in detail. Novel and high eﬃcient redox ma-
terials, Zr doped LaCoO3 perovskites obtained were ﬁrstly used for solar thermochemical CO production. Zr
doping is beneﬁcial to improve CO yield and thermochemical redox stability. LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3, an optimum in
doping for CO production, produced an average of 1066.6 μmol/g CO, which was an inspiring performance in
the current perovskite based thermochemical ﬁeld. The degree of reduction of LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3 was positively
correlated with temperature and heating rate. Kinetics study shows that the activation energy of reduction
reaction is 107.1 kJ/mol, which is much lower than that of CeO2, and the reduction process is a ﬁrst order
reaction. This material has better performances by comparison with CeO2 and other similar materials reported in
literatures. The excellent CO production performance, pretty redox stability and kinetics manifested by
LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3 provided a new approach for exploiting higher performing materials for solar thermochemical
CO2 splitting.
1. Introduction
In the face of the current world energy situation, in order to solve
the current dilemma, renewable energy research and development has
brought dawn and hope to the world [1,2]. The solar energy-based
renewable energy development route has attracted the attention of
researchers all over the world because of the huge resource advantages
[2–5]. Solar fuel production via a two-step solar thermochemical redox
cycle is a promising alternative to the current solar-based photoelec-
trolysis and photocatalysis due to its potentially high eﬃciency and low
pollution characteristics [6–10]. This fascinating route, which directly
converts solar energy into gaseous fuels and can be further processed
into liquid fuel through existing technologies, has received the atten-
tion of laboratories worldwide [3,11–13]. A schematic diagram of a
typical two-step solar thermochemical fuel production redox cycle is
illustrated in Fig. 1(a) [14–16]. In this process, a metal oxide is em-
ployed as the reaction medium and suﬀering thermal reduction (TR) at
a high temperature in the ﬁrst step. In the following step, the reduced
metal oxide is reoxidized to its initial oxidation state by performing CO2
splitting (CDS), yielding CO [14,17–23]. In the ﬁeld of two-step

thermochemical fuel production, CeO2 is used as the benchmark for
rapid kinetics and excellent long term cycle stability [8,23–25]. How-
ever, the high operation temperature [11,24–26] and limited fuel
production [12] have impeded the practical application [26–29].
Due to the moderate operation temperature and higher fuel pro-
duction potential, perovskite has received extensive attention very re-
cently [29–39]. Perovskite with ideal cubic structure and the general
formula ABO3 was shown in Fig. 1(b). Since there are diﬀerent metal
elements in the A and B sites, the perovskite material has a wider
regulatory space than some simple metal oxides. We can perform ele-
ments doping on both A and B sites to manipulate the operation tem-
perature and thermodynamic properties [37,40,41]. Ramos, A. E. et al
investigated the application of Co doped LaBO3, and found that Co-
doping is favorable for the formation of oxygen vacancies [42]. Also,
Demont A. found that Co doped La1-xSrxFeO3 was more favorable for
the reduction than the undoped one and larger O2 produced during the
reduction of LSC [43]. Despite the great enhancement of CO production
induced by Co-doping, the current CO production performance is still
insuﬃcient to meet the requirements of practical applications. More-
over, Co-containing perovskites are easily decomposed into cobalt
oxides [42,43], which would greatly aﬀect the cyclic stability.
As for the improvement of material stability, element doping has
been shown an eﬀective approach. In recent works, Zr doping favors the
cyclic stability of CeO2 [44–50]. To our knowledge, the eﬀects of Zr
doping on perovskite-based thermochemical cycle durability as well as
CO production performance have not been studied.
In this work, the solar thermochemical redox properties and kinetics
of Zr doped LaCoO3 perovskits were detailed studied. The studied
materials were synthesized via citrate sol–gel method. The crystal
structure was characterized by X-ray diﬀraction, and the thermo-
chemical cycle performances were veriﬁed by thermogravimetric ex-
periments. The eﬀects of single doping of Sr and Zr and double doping
of both elements in A and B sites in LaCoO3 on thermochemical cycle
performances were studied. In order to obtain the optimal strategy for
doped LaCoO3 series, the doping concentration was optimized to obtain
the optimal thermochemical cycle performances of this system and the
kinetics of optimized material was studied to provide some theoretical
guidance and experimental reference for the application of the per-
ovskite system in solar thermochemical fuel production.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Preparation of the materials
The sol–gel method with citric acid as a complexing agent was
employed to prepare perovskite powders based on LaCoO3. Four
diﬀerent perovskite samples of LaCoO3, La0.9Sr0.1CoO3, LaCo0.9Zr0.1O3
and La0.9Sr0.1Co0.9Zr0.1O3 were obtained. All the chemical reagents
used were of analytical grade and were not subject to any processing.
Firstly, selecting the corresponding precursors according to the desired
synthesized products, the calculated amounts of La(NO3)3·6H2O, Zr
(NO3)4·5H2O, Co(NO3)2·6H2O and Sr(NO3)2 were weighed according to
the stoichiometry of the chemical formula. 50 ml of deionized water
was added to a clean beaker. Then these materials were dissolved in the
beaker, citric acid (C6H8O7·H2O) was added to the mixture with a
1:1.5 mol ratio of total metal to citric acid. The mixed solution was
allowed to evaporate the excess water at 70 °C with continuous stirring,
about ten minutes later, ethylene glycol was added to the mixed solu-
tion at a molar ratio of ethylene glycol (C2H6O2): citric acid = 1:1 and
stirring was continued until a fuchsia gel was formed. After that, the
formed gel was dried at 120 °C for 24 h and a red xerogel was obtained.
Then, the produced xerogel was ground into powder and calcined in air
atmosphere in a muﬄe furnace at 1300 °C for 10 h, the sample was then
cooled to room temperature in the furnace. Finally the sample was
pulverized ﬁnely in an agate mortar for characterization and sub-
sequent thermochemical cycles.

3.1.2. Thermochemical application for CO production
The CDS thermochemical cycle reactivity of the above samples was
investigated by TGA. Fig. 5 represents the temporal evolution of TG
curves during three consecutive thermochemical CDS cycles performed
between 1300 and 800 °C. The commercial CeO2 (99.9%) was used as
the benchmark and was tested under the same conditions. It can be
noted that a fast weight loss for all the samples is observed due to the
thermal reduction during each cycle and since the reduction time is
only 20 min, the reduction reaction does not reach equilibrium. The fast
weight loss may be attributed to the fast surface reaction [44]. Com-
pared with CeO2, much more O2 released for all perovskites, which
conﬁrmed that the Co-containing perovskites are more favorable for
reduction. As CO2 was injected, the weight of the reduced samples in-
creased rapidly and then it proceeded slowly until the reaction reached
equilibrium [52]. Similarly, the weight gain of perovskite samples was
more pronounced than CeO2, which meant more CO was produced, this
was also conﬁrmed in Table 2. Meanwhile, we also found that Sr doped
in A site had the worst thermochemical redox performance among all
tested perovskites, but still outperformed CeO2 in terms of redox ca-
pacity.
Table 2 shows the O2 released in the reduction step and CO pro-
duced in the CDS step with respect to each cycle, which were calculated
based on the weight change of the samples during three successive
thermochemical cycles. It can be found that the O2 evolution in the
reduction step was much higher for all perovskites during the three
cycles than that of CeO2, the same was true for the subsequent CDS to
produce CO. The undoped LaCoO3 released the most O2, Sr doped in A
site and/or Zr doped in B site separately and/or simultaneously de-
creased the oxygen release capacity more or less. Comparing Sr doped
LC with the undoped one, the oxygen evolution was slightly decreased.
After three successive thermochemical CDS processes, the yields of CO
for LC and LSC-91 decreased from 205.0 to 76.9 μmol/g and 108.8 to
74.8 μmol/g, respectively, which indicated poor fuel production and
thermochemical cycle potential of the above two samples. For LCZ-91
and LSCZ-9191, the O2 evolution was gradually decreased but still
higher than CeO2 during the cycles, the amounts of CO were 210.3,
140.2 and 142.4 μmol/g for the former during three CDS processes and
178.9, 111.1 and 116.4 μmol/g for the latter, respectively. The amounts
of CO for the second and third cycles remained stable, showing the

3.2. Potential for further optimization
3.2.1. Preparation and characterization of LaCo1-xZrxO3-δ perovskites
Based on the above analysis, further optimizations of LaCo1-xZrxO3-δ
(x = 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7) perovskites were conducted. Synthesis and
characterization of diﬀerent concentrations of Zr doped LaCoO3 per-

tion methods as previously mentioned. XRD patterns of these newly
prepared samples were illustrated in Fig. 6. The comparison of the
diﬀractograms with the database of powder crystal diﬀraction indicated
that the perovskite structure was obtained in all the samples. As the Zr
content increases, the perovskite structure transforms into a La2Zr2O7

the sample with high Zr [58]. The characterization results of the LaCo1-
xZrxO3-δ from EDS are presented in Table 3, the molar ratios of metal
ions as prepared and from EDS are almost identical, which shows that
the materials we get are in conformity with the expected requirements.

3.3. The thermochemical cycle performance for LCZ-73
3.3.1. Inﬂuence of oxidation temperature
Based on the analysis of diﬀerent content of Zr on the thermo-
chemical cycle performance of LCZ, LCZ-73 was screened and identiﬁed
as the optimal dopant among the studied materials with diﬀerent
contents of Zr. For the sake of further discussion, the appropriate oxi-
dation temperature will be discussed and selected here. The reduction
temperature maintained at 1300 °C for 60 min, and the oxidation tem-
perature varied from 800 to 1300 ℃and retained for 60 min. The
heating rate of 10℃/min was adopted here. In order to avoid the in-
ﬂuence between experiments, a new crucible and the same amount of
powder samples (∼10 mg) were weighed for each experiment. The
evolution of O2 and CO produced as functions of oxidation temperature
varying from 800 to 1300 °C with the screened material LCZ-73 was
given in Fig. 9, after a 1300 °C reduction process for 60 min. As shown
in the ﬁgure, in the ﬁrst thermochemical redox process, the amount of
O2 changes relatively stable with the increase of oxidation temperature,
and the order of magnitude ﬂuctuates around 2500 μmol/g. However,
in the second thermochemical process, with the increase of oxidation
temperature, the amount of O2 produced in the reduction step decreases
gradually, and the order of magnitude decreases from 1680 μmol/g at
800 °C to 150 μmol/g at 1300 °C. Meanwhile, the CO production of the
two thermochemical cycles decreases with the increase of oxidation
temperature, which is lower than 1000 μmol/g, but the diﬀerence be-
tween the two oxidation processes is not very signiﬁcant. And the CO
production of both oxidation processes reaches the highest when the
oxidation temperature is 800 °C. Further observation shows that the CO
production of the second oxidation reaction is slightly higher than that
of the ﬁrst one in the oxidation temperature range of 800–1100 °C. At
1200 °C, the diﬀerence of CO production between the two cycles is
further narrowed, but the second cycle is still slightly higher than the
ﬁrst cycle. At 1300 °C, the CO production of the second cycle is sig-
niﬁcantly lower than that of the ﬁrst one, which might be due to the
error in the detection process, but this would not aﬀect the overall
conclusion. Therefore, it is more favorable for CO2 oxidation when the
oxidation temperature is 800 °C for LCZ-73 during the oxidation which
is carried out at 800–1300 °C.

3.4. Comparing with similar materials in literatures
Comparison of the CO yield and the activation energy Ea with that
reported in literatures using the similar materials is performed in
Fig. 14. As seen in Fig. 14(a), a lot of studies on two-step thermo-
chemical CDS for CO production based on CeO2 [37,52,62], CeO2-based
dopants [52,62] and perovskites [37,39,40,63–66] have been searched
and compared. CO production reported in most literatures based on
CeO2 and its dopants [37,52,62] is less than 200 μmol/g. The same
value reported with Mn-based perovskites [63–66] is signiﬁcantly
higher than that of CeO2 and its dopants. The highest CO yield reached
757 μmol/g [64]. Studies on Co-based perovskites [37,40] have lower
CO yield, and the reported values are all below 200 μmol/g. However,
the average yield of CO for the optimized LCZ-73 perovskite in this
paper reached 1066.6 μmol/g, which is the highest value reported so
far.
Although there are many studies reported on thermochemical fuel
preparation, few studies have been done on kinetics. This can also be
clearly seen from the number of documents we obtained in Fig. 14(b).
The kinetic data of thermochemical CDS based on CeO2 [59] and per-
ovskite LFC-73 [67] reported in literatures are given. Obviously, CeO2
has the highest activation energy, which is consistent with the need for
reduction at higher temperatures. It should be noted that the activation
energy Ea corresponding to the ﬁrst-order and the second-order reaction
models for LFC-73 are given in Fig. 14(b). Correspondingly, the cor-
responding values for LCZ-73 studied in this paper are also given. By
comparison, it is found that the results in this paper are lower than
those reported in literatures. Overall, therefore, the LCZ-73 perovskite
obtained in this paper can be used as another good choice for solar
thermochemical fuel production.
4. Conclusions
In the present research, several doped LaCoO3 perovskites were
prepared via citric acid sol–gel method and tested through thermo-
chemical CDS experiments to explore an eﬀective elemental doping
strategy to obtain a practically applicable thermochemical material. As
an excellent candidate, Zr doped LaCoO3 was found and further opti-
mized to obtain the best performing material for thermochemical ap-
plications. The eﬀects of heating rate and redox temperatures on ther-
mochemical CDS process were analyzed for the best performance
perovskite obtained. Finally, kinetic parameters during isothermal re-
duction process were also studied. Crystal structure, lattice parameters,
surface morphology and chemical composition were characterized and
identiﬁed by XRD, SEM and EDS. The results indicated that Zr doped
LaCoO3 lowered the oxygen releasing capacity compared with the un-
doped one, but greatly improved CO production performance and
thermochemical CDS durability. An optimum of doping for CO pro-
duction, LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3, produced an average of 1066.6 μmol/g CO,
which was an encouraging performance in the ﬁeld of thermochemical
fuel production. The degree of reduction of LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3 was posi-
tively correlated with temperature and heating rate. 1.4 times O2 and
more than twice as much CO were produced, as the heating rate
changed from 10 °C/min to 30 °C/min, meanwhile, the degree of re-
oxidation was close to 100%. The activation energy of LaCo0.7Zr0.3O3
thermochemical reduction is 107.1 kJ/mol, which is much less than
