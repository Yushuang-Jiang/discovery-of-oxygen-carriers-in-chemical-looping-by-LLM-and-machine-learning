Thermochemical energy storage (TCES) using redox cycles of reducible perovskite oxides can potentially
provide higher speciﬁc energy capacities and storage temperatures than molten-salt systems for large-
scale energy storage in concentrating solar power (CSP) and other applications. Perovskites from earth
abundant cations, such as CaMnO3, are needed for cost-effective solutions, but such materials must
demonstrate favorable thermodynamics for high speciﬁc TCES (chemical + sensible) and favorable kinet-
ics for heat-driven reduction and exothermic re-oxidation in the redox cycle. This paper explores the
thermodynamics and kinetics of Ca1xSrxMnO3d ðx ¼ 0:05 and 0:1Þ particles for TCES redox cycles where
the particles are heated and reduced in N2 (PO2  104 bar) to high temperatures TH up to 1000 C in a
solid-particle solar receiver. Chemical and sensible energy stored in the reduced perovskite particles is
released as needed to a power cycle via re-oxidation and cooling of the material. Variation of oxygen
non-stoichiometry (d) of Ca1xSrxMnO3d with temperature and PO2 is measured via thermogravimetric
analysis. Reaction enthalpy for reduction and re-oxidation is determined by ﬁtting the variation of d with
temperature and PO2 to a two-reaction point defect model. The ﬁts compare favorably to differential scan-
ning calorimetry with overall reaction enthalpies varying signiﬁcantly with d. For CSP applications, lim-
ited time in the solar receiver for perovskite reduction requires fast kinetics to achieve high speciﬁc TCES.
Once the material is characterized thermodynamically, kinetic measurements for particle reduction and
re-oxidation are performed in a packed-bed reactor to assess rates of O2 release and uptake at various
temperatures and PO2 with particles of diameter between250 and 425 lm. Packed-bed experiments indi-
cate that the low values of A-site Sr-doping stabilize the CaMnO3d structure and allow large d at high

1. Introduction
As energy demands worldwide continue to increase, high pene-
tration of renewable energy resources for power generation
becomes an increasingly compelling approach to reduce global
greenhouse gas emissions. However, studies show that the inter-
mittent and non-dispatchable nature of solar and wind resources
limits the degree of penetration of these technologies without inte-
gration of massive storage facilities on the order of GW h (Denholm
and Hand, 2011; Evans et al., 2012). Concentrating solar power
(CSP) has the potential of utilizing large-scale, cost effective ther-
mal energy storage to provide dispatchable power from renewable
energy (Kuravi et al., 2013). Thermal energy storage using molten
salts for CSP has been incorporated into many new CSP facilities
with projects at various stages of completion worldwide (Dunn
et al., 2012b; Liu et al., 2016; Prieto et al., 2016). The molten salt
storage systems in current-day, large-scale CSP applications, based
on nitrate salt mixtures, have limitations with maximum storage
temperatures below 600 C and minimum storage temperatures
due to freezing well above 100 C (Bauer et al., 2013; Fernandez
et al., 2015). Over a typical operating temperature range, these
nitrate-salt mixtures can provide speciﬁc energy storage of about
500 kJ kg1, but they can only drive low-temperature (i.e., low-
efﬁciency) conventional Rankine cycles.
The temperature and speciﬁc energy limitations of current-day
molten nitrate salts have driven research and development to

tem complexity. Researchers have been developing TCES systems
based on carbonate decomposition (Edwards and Materic, 2012),
reversible hydrides (Ward et al., 2015), ammonia decomposition
(Dunn et al., 2012a) and sulfur dioxide disproportionation (Wong
et al., 2015). Most of these TCES systems require storage of gaseous
products and/or reactants in one form or another and as such, pre-
sent system design challenges that increase costs and reduce effec-
tive speciﬁc storage capacities (Cot-Gores et al., 2012). An
alternative TCES approach that motivates the current study uses
reducible oxide materials where the gaseous product/reactant O2
can be recovered from air without necessary storage. Various oxide
particles including simple oxides with multivalent bonds (General
Atomics, 2011; Carrillo et al., 2014; Agraﬁotis et al., 2015;
Agraﬁotis et al., 2016b) and mixed oxides such as perovskites
(Babiniec et al., 2015; Albrecht et al., 2016) have been explored to
provide chemical as well as sensible energy storage with total speci-
ﬁc storage densities that exceed molten salts and with peak storage
temperatures over 900 C. By enabling higher storage densities and
temperatures, the reducible oxide TCES systems as shown in Fig. 1
can enable the integration of storage with more efﬁcient power
cycles, such as air-Brayton cycles or supercritical CO2 cycles, for
CSP applications (Stekli et al., 2013).
Previous studies on TCES using redox cycles of simple oxides
with multivalent cations such as Co3O4/CoO or Mn2O3/Mn3O4 have
shown thus far, that these oxides may have limited applicability for
large-scale CSP storage applications because of either high costs or
poor reversibility due to inadequate re-oxidation kinetics (General
Atomics, 2011; Carrillo et al., 2014). To improve the reversibility of
these oxides, researchers have explored doped or mixed oxides
(General Atomics, 2011; Carrillo et al., 2015; Agraﬁotis et al.,
2016b; Block and Schmücker, 2016; Karagiannakis et al., 2016;
Varsano et al., 2016). While Co3O4/CoO has shown much better
promise for full system implementation (Schrader et al., 2015;
Karagiannakis et al., 2016) than the MnOx and other single-
cation redox couples, Co3O4/CoO has not yet demonstrated the
long-term cyclability necessary for large-scale CSP applications.
While cyclability of the cobalt oxide redox can be improved by
lowering the extent of Co3O4 reduction and thus speciﬁc TCES
(Karagiannakis et al., 2016; Agraﬁotis et al., 2016b), the high cost
of cobalt oxide also raises questions about its applicability at the
utility scale. Recent studies have begun to explore perovskite oxide
structures of the form ABO3d where A and B are primarily earth-
abundant cations for cost-effective storage (Babiniec et al., 2015;
Albrecht et al., 2016; Babiniec et al., 2016). Perovskite composi-
tions with multivalent cations like Mn, Co, or Fe on the B-site,
can permit large swings in O non-stoichiometry d as high as 0.3
without irreversible phase transitions. Such compositions have
been widely explored for applications such as chemical looping
combustion (Arjmand et al., 2013; Hallberg et al., 2013; Ksepko,
2014; Pishahang et al., 2014) where low-cost and good redox
cyclability are also important. The chemical looping studies have
largely targeted doped calcium manganites CaMnO3d or doped
strontium manganites SrMnO3d where the dopant displaces a
small fraction of the Mn on the B-site to reduce the tendency for
irreversible phase transitions observed in reduction of undoped
CaMnO3d (Bakken et al., 2005b; Leonidova et al., 2011). Variations

The current paper builds on earlier studies to explore the use of
doped CaMnO3d for high-temperature TCES with oxide reduction

2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Synthesis and characterization
All perovskite materials were synthesized using a solid-state
reaction (SSR) approach reported in the literature (Tong et al.,
2010; Nikodemski et al., 2013). Stoichiometric amounts of precur-
sor powders: manganese dioxide (MnO2 purity P 99.9%), calcium
carbonate (CaCO3 P 99.5%), and strontium carbonate (SrCO3 -
P 99.9%) were mixed in isopropyl alcohol and ball-milled for
48 h. The solution was subsequently dried, and the resulting slurry
calcined twice at 1200 C for 10 h without the use of any sintering
agents. The sintered material, Ca1xSrxMnO3d with x = 0.0, 0.05, or
0.1, was ground to particles after each calcination cycle.
To assess the extent of non-perovskite phases in undoped and
Sr-doped CaMnO3d samples, X-ray diffraction (XRD) scans were
performed on a PANalytical X’pert PRO MRD using Cu  Ka radia-
tion. Fig. 2 shows a comparison of the room-temperature diffrac-
tograms for the solid-state reactive sintered Sr-doped CaMnO3d
and for a reference of undoped CaMnO3d (Jorge et al., 2001). The
comparison shows good major peak agreement for the orthorhom-
bic phase for CaMnO3d (Goldyreva et al., 2012) with only minor
shifts due to lattice strain in the A-site-doped Ca0:95Sr0:05MnO3d
and
Ca0:9Sr0:1MnO3d.

Calcination at lower temperatures (notably 1100 C) for the Sr-

2.2. Thermodynamic measurements
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning
calorimetry (DSC) measurements were carried out on a Setaram
Labsys TGA/DSC instrument using approximately 40 mg of pow-
dered sample. O2 partial pressure, PO2, in the TGA/DSC chamber
was controlled by ﬂowing a combination of UHP N2 (with O2 mole
fraction XO2  104), dry air (XO2 ¼ 0:209), and 5% O2 in N2. A Bosch
LSU4.2 sensor with a Innovate Motorsports LC-2 controller mea-
sured the sweep gas ﬂow PO2 after leaving the TGA, and with the
UHP N2 maintained an average PO2 = 104 bar. The sweep gas ﬂow
rate was maintained at 100 standard mL min1 for all TGA/DSC
experiments.

stoichiometry, d, of the perovskites as a function of T and PO2. To

were used for the DSC measurements.
2.3. Kinetic measurements
Kinetic measurements of the reduction and re-oxidation of the
Sr-doped CaMnO3d were performed in an annular packed bed
reactor. Approximately 1 g of perovskite particles with particle
diameters between 250 and 425 lm were packed into an annular
bed 20 mm long. The packed bed was pressed up against a quartz
frit in a 10.2 mm inner diameter quartz tube. Along the central axis
of the quartz tube was an alumina tube (6.2 mm outer diameter)
with grooves cut into its surface. The grooves held three K-type
thermocouples, which provided temperatures at the bed inlet,
midpoint, and outlet. The quartz tube with the packed bed was
heated inside a near-IR radiant furnace (Ulvac Riko VHT-44), which
focused radiant energy along a 10-cm long heated zone to permit
rapid heating of the bed. An upstream region of packed quartz
wool and the downstream quartz frit provided conﬁnement for
the reactive particles and thermal equilibration of the gases before
they reached the reactive bed.

The relatively large particle diameters (between 250 and
425 lm) were chosen to simulate particle sizes needed for ade-
quate transport in a large-scale redox cycle with a central solar
receiver for particle heating. This range of relatively large diame-
ters can cause bulk oxide-ion diffusion to impact kinetic rates.
The impacts of bulk diffusion are not discussed here in detail, but
kinetic modeling of the packed bed experiments with and without
bulk oxide-ion diffusion indicated that bulk diffusion is primarily
important only for reduction at the lower temperatures in this
study (Albrecht, 2016). These modeling studies, not reported in
this paper, further indicated that the packed bed measurements
can be ﬁtted well to axially discretized reacting ﬂow models, and
thereby provide a valuable tool for assessing kinetic parameters.
However, the results from the packed bed experiment due not rep-
resent general kinetic limits on the achievable degree of reduction
or speciﬁc TCES in a full-scale system, but rather provide a mea-
sured limit tied to the speciﬁc geometry and ﬂow conﬁgurations
of this reactor.

3. Results & discussion
3.1. Thermodynamic measurements and modeling
Initial TGA screening tests provided an evaluation of the
undoped and Sr-doped CaMnO3d redox potential under cycling
conditions targeted for a large-scale TCES system in a CSP plant
with an advanced supercritical-CO2 power cycle. For such a TCES
system, the representative operating cycle conditions involve par-
ticle reduction for energy storage at temperatures TH, from 800 C
up to 1000 C. The lower TH is near the minimum needed for heat
input to supercritical-CO2 power cycles with ﬁring temperatures of
P700 C (Turchi et al., 2013). The upper limit represents a TH
above which exergy losses for the heat release to the power cycle
become signiﬁcant due to unreasonably high heat recovery
approach temperatures (Padilla et al., 2015). To improve the extent
of reduction at TH in an industrial-scale TCES system for a CSP
plant, a reduced PO2 using a pressure-swing absorption (PSA) sys-
tem can increase d and thus speciﬁc TCES for the perovskite
(Albrecht et al., 2016). For large scale PSA, PO2 ¼ 104 bar provides
an approximate limit for commercially viable PO2 for reduction
(Shirley and Lemcoff, 1997), and this value was set as the baseline
PO2 for high-temperature reduction in this study.
For perovskite composition screening tests, material samples
were cycled from a fully-oxidized state at 500–900 C in a
PO2;red ¼ 104 and then re-oxidized in air while cooling, as shown
in Fig. 6. The evolution of d in Fig. 6 shows large degrees of rever-
sible reduction (d  0:2) for the two Ca1xSrxMnO3d compositions
for these cycle conditions. These tests thus provide a good indica-
tor of the effectiveness of speciﬁc perovskites as a TCES media.
Sample TGA screening plots in Fig. 6 show that the A-site Sr-
doped CaMnO3d demonstrate signiﬁcantly higher extent of reduc-
tion than the undoped CaMnO3d over the 3.5 h of exposure to N2.
These results were repeatable over several samples and show that
the Sr-doped CaMnO3d demonstrates reversibility of the redox
cycle in the TGA (shown for only 2 cycles in Fig. 6) unlike the
undoped CaMnO3d, which fails to re-oxidize fully upon exposure
to air and subsequent cooling for the temperature and times allot-
ted in the screening cycle. Hysteresis with delayed re-oxidation in
redox cycling at 1000 C of undoped CaMnO3d has been attributed
to partial decomposition of the high-temperature cubic perovskite

3.2. Kinetic measurements
For large-scale commercial applications, the time to reduce,
heat, and therefore store energy in materials may be limited by
various factors. In a CSP plant, A central solar receiver for reactive
materials may likely involve a falling particle stream (Oles and
Jackson, 2015) or a limited on-sun time for a ﬁxed bed reactor
(Agraﬁotis et al., 2016a). Thus, it is critical for the receiver to effec-
tively transfer solar radiation to the material and maintaining a
low oxygen environment (Prieto et al., 2016). For these reasons,
understanding redox kinetics of these perovskites will be essential
for receiver design.
Isothermal kinetic tests are useful for extracting kinetic and
bulk diffusion parameters at various temperatures. These tests
are accomplished by allowing the packed bed to approach equilib-
rium at a speciﬁed T in air and switching in 300 s intervals between
reducing and oxidizing gas compositions, as described previously
in the experimental section. Fig. 10 shows the evolution of d and
speciﬁc chemical energy stored for reduction at PO2  104 bar
and re-oxidation in air. Each curve is the average of 10 isothermal
cycles. The change in d increases monotonically with T for both
materials, and in particular Ca0:95Sr0:05MnO3d shows signiﬁcantly
more reduction for T P 1000 C. On the other hand, the 10% Sr
doped sample actually shows greater reducibility compared to 5%
Sr doping at lower T 6 800 C.
Time scales for reduction are much slower than re-oxidation in
all cases and after 300 s reduction, neither composition reached its
deq at any of the tested T in Fig. 10. Typically the d obtained in these
cyclic reductions for each T ranged from 60 to 80% of the equilib-
rium value. Interestingly, for the higher 10% Sr-doping, reduction
was faster at T 6 900 C and re-oxidation was faster at all T tested.
However, because Ca0:95Sr0:05MnO3d shows higher d in air at
1000 C, it has a higher maximum d of  0.3 after 300 s of
reduction.
The chemical energy stored (relative to the fully-oxidized state)
in these isothermal redox cycles is less than the thermodynamic
limits provided in Table 2. As illustrated by the values at the very
beginning of the re-oxidation shown in Fig. 10(d), Dhchem after
300 s of reduction is around 65–85% of the equilibrium values at
PO2 = 104 bar. The sensible energy is principally a function of DT,
and thus does not change from the thermodynamic values derived
from Table 2. Thus, adding sensible energy to the maximum chem-
ical energy stored in the cycles, a kinetically limited speciﬁc TCES
values based on the 300 s reduction in this reactor conﬁguration
are calculated. Cycle-limited speciﬁc TCES values at 800, 900, and
1000 C are 450, 580, and 770 kJ kg1 for Ca0:9Sr0:1MnO3d respec-
tively and 440, 620, and 880 kJ kg1 for Ca0:95Sr0:05MnO3d respec-
tively.

3.3. Redox cycling performance
The durability of the Sr-doped CaMnO3d in terms of morpho-
logical structure and phase purity was evaluated in the same
packed bed experimental setup with 1000 non-isothermal redox
cycles. The non-isothermal redox cycles, as illustrated earlier in
Fig. 5, simulated conditions expected in a CSP plant with a
supercritical-CO2 cycle with reduction during rapid heating from
500 to 900 C and re-oxidation in air during cooling back to
500 C. Near-IR quartz lamps with focusing gold-plated mirrors
provided packed bed heating rates of 100 C per min for these tests.
Faster ramp rates may be attained in a solar receiver which could
effect the transient partitioning of energy into chemical and sensi-
ble components, but the effect of heating rates was reserved for
future studies.
Fig. 11 shows the variation in d (averaged over 100 cycles) as a
function of time for both Ca1xSrxMnO3d compositions during the
heating and reduction. The consistency in the curves over 1000
cycles is evident for both compositions, although more so for the
10% Sr-doped composition with a standard deviation in maximum
d for the cycles of 0.0025 (1.6%). The 5% Sr-doped composition
shows an increased standard deviation for maximum d of 0.0056
(2.9%). The increased variation is largely due to lower reduction
in the ﬁrst few hundred cycles. This may indicate slow equilibra-
tion of partial phase transitions as observed in undoped CaMnO3
(Leonidov et al., 2016), and the lower Sr-doping may be less effec-
tive at suppressing these transitions. However. after 400 cycles the
variation in d has stabilized such that its maximum is consistently
around 0.20. This corresponds to a speciﬁc Dhchem of 275 kJ kg1
and a total speciﬁc TCES of 620 kJ kg1. These values are about
20% lower than the thermodynamic limits in Table 2, since the
reduction reaction does not reach equilibrium during the time on
steam in the low PO2 ﬂow for these speciﬁc cycling experiments.
Faster reduction might be achieved with smaller particles or a
shorter bed, but those parameter variations were not explored in
this study. In general, the higher 10% Sr-doped CaMnO3 provided
less maximum d ( 0:16) in the time limitations of the redox cycle
and this corresponded to a speciﬁc Dhchem of 235 kJ kg1 a total
speciﬁc TCES of 570 kJ kg1. For both perovskite compositions,
the speciﬁc packed bed conﬁgurations achieved just over 60% of
the thermodynamic limits for chemical energy stored and close
to 80% of the equilibrium limit of total TCES (chemical + sensible
energy).

3.4. System considerations
The reversible redox cycling experiments demonstrated 1000
cycles for both Sr-doped CaMnO3d compositions suggests that
these perovskites have the potential to provide a relatively simple
oxide-based TCES system for CSP applications, in particular with
supercritical
CO2
power
cycles.
Like
other

high-

chemical energy storage (listed in Table 2). Higher percentages of
the chemical energy limit may be achieved with smaller beds or
alternative ﬂow conﬁgurations, and thus while these tests show
the challenge of kinetically limited receiver/reactors approaching
the material thermodynamic limits for TCES, the cyclic test TCES
values do not represent a general result that may be achieved in
a well-designed full-scale receiver. Such a design effort is reserved

Co3O4 redox cycles in kinetically limited reactors in recent studies
(Agraﬁotis et al., 2016b; Agraﬁotis et al., 2016a; Karagiannakis
et al., 2016). While the Co3O4 redox cycles have similarly exhibited
between 50 and 65% of their thermodynamic limits for chemical
energy storage, the very high speciﬁc chemical storage capacity
of Co3O4 (Dhchem  844 kJ kg1) make it attractive as a TCES mate-
rial (Karagiannakis et al., 2016). Furthermore, because Co3O4
reduction to CoO occurs in air at 885 C, it does not require a
reduced PO2 sweep gas in the receiver. The simplicity of full
reduction-oxidation cycle in air eliminates parasitic losses required
to generate the low PO2 sweep gas for reducing the perovskites in
the receiver. System-level modeling of the Sr-doped CaMnO3-
redox TCES system have shown that the penalty for producing
the low-PO2 receiver sweep gas with realistic pressure-swing
adsorption (PSA) units rises to 20% of the power cycle output
for providing adequate sweep gas ﬂow at PO2 = 104 bar and
TH = 900 C (Albrecht, 2016). Those system modeling studies show
that PSA power requirements drop off signiﬁcantly to below 5% of
the power cycle output if the sweep gas PO2 is raised to 102 bar at
TH = 900 C. The higher PO2 lowers the speciﬁc chemical storage
potential by 100 kJ kg1 but leads to slight improvements in
overall solar-to-electric efﬁciencies (Albrecht, 2016). The compli-
cations and energy efﬁciency penalty associated with providing
the low PO2 sweep gas for the Sr-doped CaMnO3d reduction must
be weighed against the substantial cost advantage of these per-
ovskites from earth-abundant cations relative to Co3O4.
There are additional advantages that make the Sr-doped
CaMnO3d worthy of consideration as a potential TCES material.
The long-term stable cyclability of the Sr-doped CaMnO3 shown
in the current study has not to the authors’ knowledge been
demonstrated by Co3O4 or other simple-oxide redox couples. Fur-
thermore, the perovskite unlike single-cation oxides provide
chemical energy storage (and similarly energy release) over a
range of temperatures, and this offers advantages for operational
ﬂexibility with respect to temperature for receivers and heat
recovery reactors. Further system modeling and techno-economic
analysis is vital for evaluating perovskites relative to Co3O4 and
other simple oxides in full-scale TCES systems.
4. Conclusions
Particles of Ca1xSrxMnO3d, with x = 0.05 and 0.1, were charac-
terized thermochemically as a viable option for large-scale ther-
mochemical energy storage in CSP applications. TGA and DSC
measurements provided the basis for ﬁtting the material proper-
ties to a two-reaction point defect model that captures the varia-
tion in speciﬁc chemical energy storage as a function of T and
PO2. The material characterization showed that small levels of A-
site Sr-doping eliminated phase decomposition observed for
undoped CaMnO3d at conditions relevant for coupling a TCES sys-
tem up to 1000 C to an advanced supercritical-CO2 cycle. Mea-
surements of thermodynamic limits for both Ca1xSrxMnO3d
compositions suggest signiﬁcantly higher speciﬁc energy storage
potential compared to inert thermal energy storage materials, such
as Al2O3 and high-temperature salts, for storage at T P 700 C.
Ca0:95Sr0:05MnO3d shows higher speciﬁc TCES than the 10% Sr-
doped composition, but may be more prone to phase decomposi-
tion for TH above 1000 C. While these perovskites show lower
speciﬁc TCES than the high values of Co3O4, the perovskites have
demonstrated the promise of long-term stability that has yet to
be duplicated in Co3O4, and the substantial cost advantages and
their ﬂexible high-temperature range may make them more attrac-
tive for coupling to supercritical-CO2 cycles.
The perovskite-based TCES has the ability to operate at higher
temperatures with high speciﬁc energy storage for smaller storage
tanks than required for inert oxide materials. For the high temper-
atures needed to integrate CSP with supercritical CO2 power cycles,
chemical energy storage from reduction in PO2 = 104 bar can pro-
vide more than half of the total thermodynamic limits of speciﬁc
energy stored for the two Sr-doped CaMnO3d compositions stud-
ied. In redox cycling studies, reduction kinetics are slower than
re-oxidation kinetics, and the full thermodynamic limits of chem-
ical energy storage are not reached in times scales of a few min-
utes. The reduction process is in part controlled by mass transfer
limiting kinetics in the packed bed conﬁguration, but reduction
rates are fast enough to achieve  80% of the limit within 300 s
of reduction.
Signiﬁcant challenges remain for establishing the commercial
feasibility of TCES storage based on Ca1xSrxMnO3d for CSP appli-
cations. The limited residence times for solar receivers and the
need for rapid energy capture places demands on the material
kinetics and receiver design to ensure adequately rapid solar
energy capture. The kinetic tests presented here, in a packed bed
reactor with rapid switching between oxidizing and reducing envi-
ronments, show that the reduction kinetics may require on-sun
residence times of several minutes. Furthermore, management of
the released gaseous O2 is critical to sustain a low PO2 around the
particles to continue driving reduction. The provision of low PO2
via PSA or other technology represents a system integration chal-
