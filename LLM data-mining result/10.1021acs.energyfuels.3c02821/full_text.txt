CuCo2O4@SiO2 Oxygen Carrier with Strong Oxygen Release and

ABSTRACT: Oxygen carriers transfer lattice oxygen and oxidize
elemental mercury in chemical-looping combustion (CLC). This
study aims to design a core−shell CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carrier
to enhance the removal of mercury during CLC. CuCo2O4@SiO2
was prapered by combining a salt-assisted combustion method and
an impregnation method. The SiO2:CuCo2O4 ratio was optimized
to achieve a high mercury removal efficiency. Over 10 cycles,
CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carriers have a stable oxygen release. At
900 °C, in comparison with CuCo2O4, mercury removal efficiency
over CuCo2O4@SiO2 through oxidation increased from 63.46 to 82.21%. The mercury removal over CuCo2O4@SiO2 through
chemical adsorption decreased accordingly. This indicated that CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carriers promoted the oxidation efficiency of
Hg0 and reduced the adsorption of mercury on its surface. The DFT calculations revealed that SiO2 loading over CuCo2O4 surface
provided part of the lattice oxygen to supplement the oxygen vacancies on the CuCo2O4 surface, promoting the oxygen release from
the oxygen carrier. However, the loading of SiO2 inhibited the formation of surrounding oxygen vacancies to a certain extent, and the
right amount of SiO2 improved the oxygen release rate. Therefore, CuCo2O4@SiO2 is a suitable oxygen carrier that can be applied in
CLC.
1. INTRODUCTION
Chemical-looping combustion (CLC) has been regarded as
one of the most promising technologies to reduce carbon
emissions from fossil fuel combustion.1,2 Unlike conventional
combustion, which relies on air as the source of oxygen, CLC
uses oxygen carriers to provide oxygen to the fuel, allowing for
more efficient combustion and in situ CO2 capture.3 Oxygen
carriers are a key component of the CLC process and play a
critical role in the overall efficiency and effectiveness of the
technology. When the oxygen carrier is exposed to high
temperatures and reducing atmosphere of the CLC process, it
undergoes a reduction reaction, releasing oxygen to the fuel
and becoming reduced. As the reduced oxygen carrier
circulates back to the air reactor, it is exposed to air, which
oxidizes the carrier and restores its oxygen-carrying capacity.
This cyclic process of reduction and oxidation allows for
efficient and continuous fuel combustion, while also capturing
the CO2 generated in the process.4 In addition to its role as an
oxygen transport carrier, oxygen carriers have the ability to
remove pollutants from the combustion process, including
elemental mercury, a highly toxic substance often found in coal
combustion exhaust gases.5−7 The gas composition in the
process of CLC has a significant impact on the removal of Hg0
and the oxidation of Hg0 during flue gas cooling. Studies have
shown that NO, HCl, and O2 have a positive effect on the
oxidation of Hg0,8 while SO2 has a negative effect on Hg0
oxidation.9 H2S positively affects the oxidation of Hg0 through
heterogeneous reactions.10 CO and H2 are reducing gases that
may indirectly affect the oxidation of Hg0, leading to the
reduction of oxygen carriers. In CLC, the reasons for mercury
removal are related to four main aspects. First, mercury
emissions are higher in air reactors compared to coal-fired
power plants. The mercury emission from ultralow emission
coal-fired power plants accounts for 4.8% of the total mercury
in the furnace, while the mercury emission from air reactors
accounts for more than 5% of the total mercury in the
furnace.11 Second, the mercury released in the fuel reactor has
an impact on the quality of CO2.12 Third, the adsorption of
elemental mercury on oxygen carriers causes the decom-
position of CO to C, which results in carbon deposition on the
surface of oxygen carriers, leading to a reduction in the active
sites and surface reactivity of oxygen carriers.13 Zhang et al.6
found that the adsorption of Hg0 reduces the adsorption
energy of CO on the reduced Fe2O3 surface but promotes the
charge transfer from the surface to the adsorbed CO molecule.

effect on aluminum heat exchangers in a high-pressure
environment, so that the standard of 0.01−0.1 μg/N m3 of

There are very few studies on the migration and trans-
formation of mercury during CLC. Liu et al.15 discussed the
underlining mechanisms for the transformation of mercury-
related species both in combustion and postcombustion
process, in which oxygen carriers promote the conversion of
Hg0 to Hg2+ and Hgp, which contributes to the efficiency of
mercury removal. Mendiara et al.16 used the Fe-enriched sand
fraction (58 wt % Fe2O3) as an oxygen carrier in CLC. The
molar ratio of Hg0/Hg2+ was 86:14 in the fuel reactor and
38:62 in the air reactor for the combustion of anthracite at 920
°C. An et al.17 found that chemical-looping gasification of coal
promoted the conversion of Hg0 to Hgp and Hg2+ compared to
conventional coal gasification using CuFe2O4 as the oxygen
carrier. Ma et al.12 studied hematite as an oxygen carrier and
found that oxygen carriers during in situ gasification chemical-
looping combustion of coal had a less effect on the release rate
and amount of mercury released, but the oxygen carriers
promoted the conversion of Hg0 to Hg2+. The removal
efficiency of Hg0(g) by CuO@TiO2@Al2O3 with oxygen
uncoupling ability in CLC was 60.71%. Previously, when
evaluating oxygen carriers, it was found that the oxidation
efficiency of metal oxides for Hg0 in a HCl atmosphere was in
the following order: Co3O4(Mn2O3)>Fe2O3>CuO(CeO2)
>SiO2(Al2O3),18,19 with the lattice oxygen release in the
oxygen carrier playing an important role in the removal of
mercury.20,21 Oxygen carriers can effectively reduce the
emission of Hg0, and the conversion of Hg0 to Hg2+ is still
possible under high-temperature conditions.17 Therefore,
oxygen carriers with good mercury removal performance
need to be further developed to improve the efficiency of Hg0
oxidation in fuel reactors.
The three common metal oxides with oxygen uncoupling
ability are CuO, Co3O4, and MnO2, which have oxygen release
capacities of 10, 6.6, and 3.3 wt %, respectively. CuO has the
strongest oxygen release capacity, but CuO is prone to
sintering at high temperatures, agglomeration after reduction,
and thus has poor recycling performance.22 In recent years,
spinel (AB2X4) oxides have been continuously applied in
several research fields, such as energy storage and environ-
mental treatment, due to their magnetic property, high
catalytic activity, and good chemical stability.23−31 A series of
spinel oxides have also been widely used as oxygen carriers in
CLC processes due to the synergistic effect of their complex
metal oxides.32−36 Among the spinel-type oxides, cobalt-based
spinel oxides have good activity, low cost, high stability, and
environmental benefits.37−39 CuCo2O4, constructed by replac-
ing Co2+ in the tetrahedral position in Co3O4 with Cu2+, is a
typical spinel structure.40,41 At 900 °C, the mercury removal
efficiency of CuCo2O4 is 16% higher than that of Co3O4, and it
reaches the maximum mercury removal efficiency faster (as
shown in Figure S1). CuCo2O4 has better oxygen release
properties than Co3O4 and can further improve the oxidation
of mercury in the fuel reactor. Most of the mercury in the coal
is released in the fuel reactor, and some are adsorbed on the
surface of the oxygen carrier and carried into the air reactor.
Perez-Vega et al.42 found a mercury concentration of 1 μg/N
m3 in the air reactor with copper-based oxygen carriers in
CLC. The mercury released in the air reactor was originated
from the reduced oxygen carrier and residual char. Ji et al.43
found that the release of mercury in the air reactor was about
10% of the total mercury.
Although the mercury in coal was mainly released in the fuel
reactor, some of the mercury was still released in the air
reactor, causing the secondary pollution of air. Therefore, the
oxygen carrier should also have the property of weak
adsorption of mercury to avoid the chemical adsorption of
mercury onto the surface of the oxygen carrier in the fuel
reactor. Based on the strong oxygen release capacity of
CuCo2O4 oxygen carriers, a layer of SiO2,44 which is inert for
mercury adsorption, is further loaded on the surface of
CuCo2O4 to form CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carriers with a
strong oxygen-releasing capacity and weak adsorption capacity.

does not change the mercury oxidation performance of the

In this study, CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carriers with strong
oxygen release and weak adsorption capacity were designed by
the salt-assisted combustion and impregnation methods. The
physicochemical properties of CuCo2O4@SiO2 were charac-
terized by different analytical methods. The optimum loading
amount of SiO2 was determined. The oxygen release and
mercury removal performance, as well as the cycling stability,
of CuCo2O4@SiO2 were investigated. In addition, the
microscopic mechanism of oxygen transfer on the surface of
CuCo2O4@SiO2 was revealed by DFT calculations.
2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
2.1. Preparation of Oxygen Carriers. Figure 1 shows the
preparation process of CuCo2O4 and CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen
carriers. CuCo2O4 was prepared by the salt-assisted combustion
method. Co(NO3)2·6H2O, Cu(NO3)2 ·3H2O, glycine, and KCl were
uniformly mixed and dissolved in deionized water (molar ratio of
Co2+/Cu2+ was chosen as 2:1; molar ratio of KCl/metal ions was
chosen as 1:1; and the amount of glycine was determined by chemical
eq 1, while stoichiometric ratio λ was chosen as 7:9). The mixed
solution was further heated and stirred on a magnetic stirrer to form a
viscous gel. The gel precursor was placed in a drying oven and dried at
95 °C for 48 h to form a porous precursor. The precursor was burned
in a muffle furnace at 300 °C to form salt-containing solid particles,
which were washed and filtered with deionized water and anhydrous
ethanol to form a salt-free solid. The salt-free solid continued to be
dried in a drying oven at 120 °C for 24 h to remove water, and the
dried particle precursor was then calcined at 800 °C for 2 h.
CuCo2O4@SiO2 was prepared by impregnation using tetraethyl
orthosilicate (TEOS) as the silicon source. A certain molar ratio of
CuCo2O4 and TEOS was dissolved in ethylene glycol solution, heated
until a viscous mixture was formed, and then dried in a desiccator at
120 °C for 24 h. The resulted material continued to be placed in a
muffle furnace and was calcined at 800 °C for 2 h to obtain the
CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carrier.

2.2. Method of Experiment. The experimental system used in
the study was a horizontal tube furnace system, as shown in Figure 2,
which mainly consisted of a gas supply unit, an oxygen carrier reaction
unit, and a gas analysis unit. The Hg0 vapor in the gas supply unit was
obtained by heating the mercury permeation tube in the water bath
and brought out by the carrier gas. Different mercury vapor
concentrations were obtained at different temperatures under control.
The oxygen carrier reaction unit mainly consisted of a horizontal tube
furnace and a quartz tube reactor, in which the experimental sample
was placed in an internal quartz tube, while quartz wool was plugged
at both ends to prevent the sample from blowing away. The gas
analysis unit mainly detected and analyzed the mercury in the flue gas,
which needed to be pretreated before entering the mercury analyzer
for testing. The moisture was absorbed by color-changing silica gel to
prevent damage to the instrument. At the same time, the flue gas was
filtered and purified by activated carbon before being discharged into
the atmosphere to prevent pollution of the atmosphere. The
experimental temperatures included 800 and 900 °C. The mass of
the oxygen carrier was 1 g. The flow rate of the mercury removal
experimental gas was 1 L/min; and the concentration of mercury
vapor was 100 μg/m3. The carrier gas was 20% N2 + 80% CO2.
The detection and analysis of gaseous mercury mainly included the
analysis of Hg0 and Hg2+. Hg0 was obtained directly by the
measurement of VM3000 (Mercury Instruments, Germany) online
mercury analyzer. The measurement of Hg2+ was obtained according

Carriers. Oxygen release is an important index to evaluate
the performance of oxygen carriers. The thermogravimetric-
differential scanning calorimetry (TG-DSC) curve of the
oxygen release reaction of the prepared CuCo2O4 oxygen

oxygen release of the CuCo2O4 oxygen carrier was about 7.2

release is shown in Figure 11. CuCo2O4 was reduced to Cu2O
and CoO after oxygen decoupling. The theoretical oxygen
release of (Cu2O+CoO)/CuCo2O4 was 9.7 wt %. It failed to

The initial oxygen release temperature of CuCo2O4 was about
750 °C, while the initial temperatures of Co3O4 and CuO were

3.4. Simulation Study on the Performance of
CuCo2O4 and CuCo2O4@SiO2 Oxygen Carriers for
Mercury Removal. The adsorption of Hg0 on the
CuCo2O4 oxygen carrier surface is the first step in the
heterogeneous oxidation of mercury. Thus, the interaction of
Hg0 with the CuCo2O4 surface was first investigated,
considering the Co, Cu, O, and bridge sites. The optimized
stable configurations and related geometrical parameters are
shown in Figure 19. The most stable bonding structure is 1-
Cu, with Hg and the Cu sites on the surface generating the
Cu−Hg bond. The Co site on the CuCo2O4 surface can form
two stable adsorption configurations, 2-CoCo and 3-Co, with
adsorption energies of −55.26 and −42.51 kJ/mol, respec-
tively, which are lower than that of the Cu2+ site. Compared to
Co3O4, Cu2+, which replaces Co2+, becomes the best Hg0
adsorption site, while the original Co2+ does not form a stable
Hg0 adsorption, indicating that CuCo2O4 has a better Hg0
adsorption capacity, which is beneficial for the removal of
mercury. At the same time, the Co site (−111.72 kJ/mol) is
stronger than the Cu site (−69.71 kJ/mol) in O2 adsorption.
Therefore, CuCo2O4 can achieve the synergistic effect of
oxygen adsorption at the Co site and mercury adsorption at the
Cu site.
The optimized stable configurations and their geometric
parameters are shown in Figure 20, considering Co, Cu, O, and
bridge adsorption sites on the CuCo2O4@SiO2 surface. When
SiO2 is adsorbed on the O site of the CuCo2O4 surface, the
most stable structure is 1-CuO. Hg forms Cu−Hg bond (2.75
Å) with the surface Cu and forms O−Hg bond (2.24 Å) with
O from SiO2, with an adsorption energy of −109.52 kJ/mol.
The adsorption energies of the other two types of sites are
−50.03 and −53.90 kJ/mol. When SiO2 is adsorbed on the Cu
site of the CuCo2O4 surface, the most stable structure is 4-
CoO. Hg forms Co−Hg bond (2.77 Å) with the surface Co
and forms O−Hg bond (2.42 Å) with O from SiO2, with an
adsorption energy of −105.90 kJ/mol. The Hg adsorption
energies on the 5-Co and 6-Cu3 sites are −29.94 and −27.16
kJ/mol, respectively.
Table 1 presents the bond configuration analysis of Hg0
adsorption on CuCo2O4@SiO2, with bond occupancy values
ranging from 0 to 1. A higher value indicates a stronger bond,
while negative values indicate nonbonding interactions. When
Hg is close to 1-CuO near the silica, the bond occupancy
numbers for the bonding between Hg and O18 and O4 are
0.27 and 0.21, respectively. The bond occupancy numbers for
the bonding between Hg and Cu1 and Cu4 are 0.12 and 0.08,
respectively. A comparison with other individually adsorbed
Cu sites reveals that when Hg forms bonds with O, it leads to
weakening of the adsorption capacity of Cu for Hg. This may
be due to the formation of a relatively stable mercury
compound of HgO through the bonding between Hg and
oxygen.
From the above discussion, it can be inferred that when Hg
is adsorbed near SiO2 on the CuCo2O4@SiO2 surface, it
simultaneously forms bonds with the oxygen atom from SiO2
and Cu or Co sites on CuCo2O4, with a higher adsorption
energy and more stable configuration. However, when Hg is
adsorbed on other sites, its adsorption energy is lower than
that on the CuCo2O4 surface, especially when SiO2 is adsorbed
on the Cu site of CuCo2O4. The reason for the higher
adsorption energy of 1-CuO and 4-CoO may be due to the
combined adsorption of CuCo2O4 and SiO2 for Hg.
HgO is an important product in the process of mercury
removal from oxygen carriers, and it is also particularly
important to study the interaction of HgO on the surface of
CuCo2O4 and CuCo2O4@SiO2. Figure 21a shows the stable
configuration and adsorption energy of HgO adsorption. Two
stable adsorption configurations can be formed. In 1-Co-O
configuration, Hg0 combines with Co and O on the surface to
form a Co−Hg−O bond with an adsorption energy of −23.13
kJ/mol. In 2-Co configuration, Hg0 only combines with Co on
the surface to form a Co−Hg bond with an adsorption energy
of −21.35 kJ/mol.

The oxygen transport processes of CuCo2O4 oxygen carriers

second lattice layer of CuCo2O4 to the surface oxygen

surface oxygen vacancies of CuCo2O4. The oxygen vacancy
formation energy and the maximum reaction energy barrier for
this process are 220.75 and 171.10 kJ/mol, respectively. Path 3

adsorbed at the O site on the CuCo2O4 (110) surface to the
oxygen vacancy on the CuCo2O4 (110) surface. The oxygen
vacancy formation energy of this process is 152.38 kJ/mol, and
the reaction energy barrier to be overcome is 147.51 kJ/mol.

SiO2 at the Cu site and O site on the CuCo2O4 surface is
higher than the oxygen vacancy formation energy on the
CuCo2O4 surface. Therefore, the SiO2 adsorbed on the surface
of the CuCo2O4 oxygen carrier may be detrimental to the
formation of surrounding oxygen vacancies. The difference in
energy barriers between path 3 and path 1 is 32.13 kJ/mol, and
the difference in energy barriers between path 2 and path 1 is
55.72 kJ/mol. The difference in reaction energy barriers
between path 3 and path 1 is not significant, and this indicates
that it is possible for the oxygen in SiO2 to supplement the
oxygen vacancies on the CuCo2O4 surface. This simulation is
consistent with experimental results as shown in Figure 9 and
Figure 17.
Figure 23 shows the oxygen migration process during the
oxidation of Hg0 by adsorption of CuCo2O4@SiO2. First, Hg0
is adsorbed on the surface of CuCo2O4@SiO2, and the oxygen
on the surface is consumed by the adsorbed Hg0 to generate
HgO, which leads to the generation of oxygen vacancies on the
surface of CuCo2O4. There are two different oxygen migration
paths for the replenishment of surface oxygen vacancies, the
first path is the migration of oxygen from the second lattice
layer of CuCo2O4 to the surface to replenish the surface
oxygen vacancies. The second path is the migration of oxygen
from SiO2 to the CuCo2O4 surface oxygen vacancies. Most of
the oxygen vacancies are replenished by the first migration
path, with oxygen migration in SiO2 accounting for a small
fraction. Compared to the single oxygen migration pathway on
the CuCo2O4 surface, two oxygen migration pathways in
CuCo2O4@SiO2 may exist, and this in turn promotes the
oxygen release from the oxygen carrier.
4. CONCLUSION
In CLC, elemental mercury is better released and oxidized in
the fuel reactor with subsequent pollution control apparatus
compared to that in the air reactor with pollutants directly
emitted to atmosphere. In this paper, a novel kind of oxygen
carriers with strong oxygen release and weak adsorption
performance CuCo2O4@SiO2 has been proposed. Investiga-
tions on elemental mercury removal over CuCo2O4@SiO2
have been conducted based on both experiments and
simulations, and the following conclusions can be drawn.
(1) The salt-assisted combustion impregnation method was
used to prepare the CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carriers with
strong oxygen release and weak adsorption performance. The
optimal ratio of SiO2 to CuCo2O4 was 1:1, and the oxygen
release was 6.89 wt %. SiO2 did not reduce the oxygen release
of CuCo2O4 but provided some oxygen for the reduction
process of CuCo2O4. During 10 cycles, the oxygen release of
the CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carrier is stable, indicating that it
has good cycling performance.
(2) The peak mercury removal efficiencies of CuCo2O4@
SiO2 at 800 and 900 °C were 42% and 45%, respectively, and
they were slightly lower than CuCo2O4. Specifically, the
contribution of mercury removal decreased to 34 and 17.79%
at 800 and 900 °C, respectively. The Hgp production was also
reduced and was only 42.58 ng within 1 h before the reaction
of 1 g CuCo2O4@SiO2 oxygen carrier at 900 °C, achieving the
research goal of strong oxygen release and weak adsorption.
(3) Based on DFT calculations, it is found that the
adsorption of SiO2 has a certain inhibitory effect on the
formation of oxygen vacancies on the surface of CuCo2O4;
however, SiO2 can replenish the oxygen vacancies on the
surface. Therefore, an appropriate SiO2/CuCo2O4 molar ratio
can promote the oxygen release rate, while a high SiO2 ratio
can seriously hinder the formation of oxygen vacancies on the
surface of CuCo2O4, thus reducing the oxygen release rate of
CuCo2O4.
